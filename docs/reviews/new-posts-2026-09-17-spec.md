### A1. boi-reporting-foreign-owned-us-llc-2026
Query: "BOI report foreign-owned LLC 2026" / "do I need to file BOI". Answer: a US-formed LLC — even one wholly owned by a non-US person — has NO BOI filing obligation after FinCEN's August 2026 final rule. Cover: the timeline (2024 rule → litigation → March 2025 interim final rule → 11 August 2026 final rule) stated only with fetched dates; who still reports (foreign-formed entities registered to do business in a US state) with a clear "US-formed vs foreign-formed" table; what FinCEN said about deleting previously filed US-person data; how to spot BOI scam letters/emails and fake "compliance deadline" invoices — use FinCEN's own fraud-alert page if it exists (fetch it; otherwise describe red flags generically without claiming FinCEN said them); what obligations remain for a foreign-owned LLC (state annual report, Form 5472). ORIGINAL ELEMENT: "Is this BOI letter real?" checklist. Scenarios: Wyoming LLC owned by a UK resident; Delaware LLC owned by a foreign parent company; a UK Ltd registered to do business in Texas (foreign reporting company); an owner who filed BOI in 2024 and wants it removed.

### A2. registered-agent-foreign-owned-llc
Query: "registered agent for foreign-owned LLC" / "can a non-resident be their own registered agent". Answer: every US LLC must keep a registered agent with a physical street address in the formation state; a non-resident living abroad generally cannot serve as their own because they have no in-state address. Verify the requirement on at least one state primary source (Wyoming or Delaware Secretary of State or statute) and say requirements are set by each state. Cover: what the agent does (receives service of process and state mail) and does NOT do (no federal tax filing — the agent does not file Form 5472; many owners assume otherwise); registered agent vs mailing address vs virtual office; what happens if the agent resigns or the address lapses (loss of good standing — describe generally, cite a state page if fetched); changing agents (state filing, generally a form with the Secretary of State). ORIGINAL ELEMENT: "what your registered agent covers vs what it doesn't" table. Four scenarios.

### B1. single-member-llc-operating-agreement-foreign-owner
Query: "operating agreement single-member LLC foreign owner". Answer: most states do not require you to file an operating agreement with the state, but banks, payment processors and marketplaces often ask for one, and it is the owner's own document of how the LLC is run and funded. **State-by-state legal requirements are the highest-error area: only state a specific state requires or does not require an operating agreement if you fetched that state's statute or official page; otherwise say "check your formation state's LLC act".** Cover: what a single-member agreement typically contains (member identity, management, capital contributions, distributions, dissolution) as general practice; why a non-resident owner benefits (bank KYC, documenting the single-member disregarded-entity status, evidencing capital contributions); the link to Form 5472 — documented contributions and distributions are exactly what Part V reports; what an operating agreement does NOT change (it does not change federal tax classification — Form 8832 does; link that post if live: form-8832-election-foreign-owned-llc). We do not draft operating agreements — say so; do not give legal advice. ORIGINAL ELEMENT: "clause → why a bank asks → what it evidences for Form 5472" table. Four scenarios.

### B2. certificate-of-good-standing-foreign-owned-llc
Query: "certificate of good standing foreign-owned LLC". Answer: a certificate of good standing (names vary by state — e.g. certificate of existence, certificate of status) is issued by the formation state's Secretary of State and confirms the LLC exists and is current on state filings; banks, Stripe/Amazon/marketplaces, and foreign registrations commonly ask for it. Verify the certificate name and ordering route on at least two state Secretary of State pages (Wyoming, Delaware, or New Mexico) before naming them; do not state certificate fees unless fetched. Cover: what keeps good standing (annual report / franchise or license tax on time, registered agent maintained); what causes loss of good standing and administrative dissolution (general, cite a state page); reinstatement exists but varies; **good standing with the state says nothing about IRS compliance** — Form 5472 is a separate federal obligation. ORIGINAL ELEMENT: "who asks for it → what they are checking → what to send" table. Four scenarios.

### C1. sales-tax-nexus-foreign-owned-llc
Query: "sales tax foreign-owned LLC selling in the US" / "sales tax nexus non-resident LLC". Answer: US sales tax is imposed by states, not the federal government; after South Dakota v. Wayfair (21 June 2018) states can require remote sellers to collect sales tax once they exceed an economic-nexus threshold, and marketplace facilitator laws usually make platforms like Amazon and Etsy collect on marketplace sales. Fetch the Wayfair opinion (supremecourt.gov) for the date and holding. **Thresholds differ by state and change: state a specific threshold ONLY for states whose Department of Revenue page you fetched (at most 2–3 examples, cited); otherwise say "varies by state".** Cover: economic nexus vs physical nexus (inventory in US warehouses, e.g. Amazon FBA, can create physical nexus — say "can", cite if fetched); marketplace sales vs own-website (Shopify) sales; that sales tax is separate from federal income tax and from Form 5472; registering for a sales tax permit is a state process; we do not handle sales tax. ORIGINAL ELEMENT: decision table "where you sell → who usually collects → what you must check". Scenarios: Amazon FBA seller with US inventory; Shopify store shipping from abroad; Etsy/POD seller; SaaS/digital products (say taxability of digital goods varies by state — do not generalise).
