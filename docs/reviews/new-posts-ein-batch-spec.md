## Facts you MUST verify by curl before stating (cite inline; if a page will not load, omit the claim rather than guessing)
- The IRS charges **$0** for an EIN (https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online).
- International applicants (no legal residence/principal office in the US) apply by phone on **+1-267-941-1099**, Mon–Fri 6 a.m.–11 p.m. ET; fax **855-215-1627** (from within the US) or **304-707-9471** (from outside); mail to Internal Revenue Service, Attn: EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999. Verify on the Form SS-4 instructions (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4) and the "Apply by phone/fax/mail" IRS pages.
- The **online** EIN assistant requires the responsible party to have an SSN or ITIN — foreign responsible parties cannot use it; line 7b takes "foreign" / "N/A" (SS-4 instructions).
- The IRS limits EIN issuance to **one per responsible party per day**.
- **Letter 147C** is the EIN verification letter that replaces a lost **CP 575** (CP 575 is issued once and not reissued). Requested from the IRS Business & Specialty Tax Line — verify the number and hours on irs.gov, and note explicitly that the toll-free 800 line is generally not reachable from outside the US, so a foreign owner uses the international line instead; verify which number irs.gov gives international callers before stating it.
- **Third-party designee**: the SS-4 has a Third Party Designee block authorising a named person to receive the EIN and answer questions about the application; verify its scope and that the authorisation expires once the EIN is assigned (SS-4 instructions).
