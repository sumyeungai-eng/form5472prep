## L9a - How long it takes to rank (6 keyword timelines)

- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Typical arc on a DA 19-20 site: nothing for a few weeks, then appears at #3, holds 2-3 for weeks, touches #1 many weeks later, falls back, settles at #1 much later with the page untouched (playbook section 10). (src: 9 full ~[00:00:00-00:01:00])
- [NEW] The arc has more texture than the playbook says: #3, then 2, then 3, then "touched" 1, back down, then #1 again, then occasional dips. Sturm calls the up-and-down "very normal" and says it repeats on every keyword he shows. Expect oscillation of 1-3 positions week to week; do not read it as a failure. (src: 9 full ~[00:00:30-00:01:30])
- [NEW] Fast case: "Students record themselves reading" appeared 1-2 weeks after launch at #10, then 11, 11, 9, 5, and kept climbing to #1 where it stayed. He says "this is why you track your keywords. It's so useful." Rule: track weekly, and only intervene if it is NOT moving at the expected pace; if "everything was expected, everything was normal," touch nothing. (src: 9 full ~[00:02:00-00:03:00])
- [NEW] Existing-page-ranks-poorly case: "NYC website migration SEO services" (high-ticket service, DA 34 site). One of his pages already ranked badly for it, so he built a dedicated SEO landing page. Week 1 = #25, then 27, then 22 (2 wks), 16 (3 wks), and #1 about 6 weeks after publishing; stayed there. Timeline template for a service page. (src: 9 full ~[00:03:00-00:04:30])
- [NEW] Directory submissions accelerate a keyword: "Growth hacking newsletter" climbed fast to #2 partly because he had submitted the newsletter to newsletter directories "with the language around growth hacking newsletter" (i.e. the target keyword appears in the directory blurb). Reaching #1 then took longer because the top competitor's page was stronger (#1, #2, #1, #2, then consistent #1). (src: 9 full ~[00:04:30-00:05:30])
- [NEW] Low-DA data point: a hidden client site at DA 14 (vs. his own site DA 34, Reverb DA 29 now / ~20 then) ranked #1 for a classic BOFU page, tracked against a named competitor. Slow start: "many weeks after launching the page to even start showing up", first showed at #9, then #2, dropped to #4, spiked to #1, back to #4, then #1 mostly consistently. Page Optimization Score was 100: "not ideal, 96 better, but enough." (src: 9 full ~[00:05:30-00:06:30])
- [NEW] Track each target keyword in your rank tracker AGAINST a named competitor so you can compare trajectories (he did this for Loom on "students record themselves reading"; he found that keyword originally by running a competitor keyword analysis of Loom, which ranked for it, and later Loom stopped ranking while he stayed #1). Compact keywords can be harvested from a bigger known competitor's ranking list. (src: 9 full ~[00:02:30-00:03:30])
- [NEW] Spotty indexing is normal on a higher-volume keyword: sixth example started at #2 "many weeks after launch", with some weeks not ranking at all, then rose "really fast" to #1, then flipped #2/#1, and finally held #1 untouched. A gap week (no ranking) is not a signal to rewrite. (src: 9 full ~[00:06:30-00:07:30])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Ranking #1 for one BOFU keyword also ranks you for many similar phrases, so the page gets far more than the "0-10 clicks" the tools predict (playbook section 3). (src: 9 full ~[00:01:30-00:02:30])

## L9b - What if you don't rank (months 3-4, page 2-3, stuck at #6)

- [NEW] Trigger definition: act when after ~3-4 months you either do not show at all, or sit on page 2-3 / stuck at a position (e.g. #6) without moving. Earlier than that, follow L9a and wait. (src: 9 full ~[00:08:00-00:09:00])
- [NEW] Cause 1, above-the-fold fails to capture attention, so the searcher bounces. Fixes: keyword used directly in the opening sentence; satisfy the intent you judged; add more detail to the opening hook; use a more relevant image. Warning: a too-irrelevant AI image, or one that "clearly looks generated with ChatGPT", makes searchers doubt the product/brand; either replace it or make it relevant and good-looking. (src: 9 full ~[00:08:30-00:10:00])
- [NEW] Cause 2, page title not competitive on a contested SERP. If everyone has the keyword in the title you get fewer clicks and fewer clicks = worse rank. Fix: add an extra call-to-action or benefit after the keyword. His example phrases: "Free, no login required", "Use now", "Use free", or use-case specifics like "lossless quality, up to one hour recording". (src: 9 full ~[00:10:00-00:11:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Aim for 96 not 100: Google increasingly sensitive to keyword stuffing; Moz may not flag stuffing at 100; he has de-optimised pages by removing the keyword from specific sections and they ranked better, "multiple times". Still keep the keyword in opening sentence, H1, page title, URL, meta description. (src: 9 full ~[00:11:00-00:12:30])
- [NEW] Cause 4, fragment keyword used verbatim sounds awkward as H1/title. Example: keyword "students record themselves reading" became page title/H1 "Have your students record themselves reading". Rule: searchers type fragments; work out WHY they search it and phrase the H1/title as natural language that still contains the fragment words in order. (Compatible with the playbook's Moz exact-word-order trap, since the fragment stays intact inside the sentence.) (src: 9 full ~[00:12:30-00:14:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] If the keyword is grammatically impossible or misspelled, write it correctly and accept the lower Page Score; it still ranks (playbook section 8 trap 5). (src: 9 full ~[00:13:30-00:14:00])
- [NEW] Cause 5, spelling/grammar errors. Searchers and Google both penalise pages with "tons of errors"; a stray minor typo is fine, many errors hurt. Exact prompt for ChatGPT/Gemini: "Check for spelling and grammar. Don't change anything, just tell me the errors." Run it on the page copy, AND separately on the page title and meta description (paste them in); and put the title in title case. (src: 9 full ~[00:14:00-00:15:00])
- [NEW] Cause 6, thin internal linking on a key page. For a really important/competitive keyword, link to it from MORE pages than just the hub, using related (not necessarily exact-match) anchor text; Sturm's main rule remains "link from the hub page, hub in the footer". Optionally add the single most important SEO landing page itself to the footer (SociableKit example), but mainly for human users. Caveat: if searchers bounce or don't click, extra internal links will not save it, because Google reads user behaviour. (src: 9 full ~[00:15:00-00:16:30])
- [NEW] Cause 7, intent misjudged (you thought BOFU, it is mixed or middle/top funnel). Worked example keyword "AI wedding photos", page "AI Wedding Photos Enhancer", brand "ProPhoto". Exact 3-line opening template: line 1 "Use ProPhoto as an AI wedding photos enhancer to make your wedding photos look flawless." / line 2 "Remove imperfections, color correct, remove objects and people, and more." / line 3 "Make your wedding photos look the way you want them to be remembered." then CALL TO ACTION ("use now / try now / use free"). (src: 9 full ~[00:16:30-00:18:00])
- [NEW] Mixed-intent fix: keep the BOFU opening but add top-of-funnel sections BELOW the CTA: (a) "why you want to do this / benefits", (b) "all the different ways it's done"; and add the secondary-intent word to title and meta description, e.g. "AI Wedding Photos Enhancer and Generator". Page stays BOFU while also serving the learners/explorers Google wants to see addressed. The intro should also briefly say why and how it's done. Detect the mix by reading the SERP: half the searchers want the solution, half want to learn. (src: 9 full ~[00:17:00-00:19:00])
- [NEW] Keep a CHANGE LOG for every page change. Fields: date, why you changed it (what you hoped to fix), what you changed. Lets you reverse a tweak days or months later and learn what works. He calls himself "super scrupulous" about this because tweaking is common. (src: 9 full ~[00:19:00-00:19:30])
- [NEW] Late bloomers: a page made "many months ago", not ranking at all (position 50+) and not being improved, jumped to #1 in a single week. He says Google "adds random variance" to stop marketers predicting it. Do not delete or give up on a clean page; (playbook says "weeks to appear, months to settle", this extends it to many months). (src: 9 full ~[00:19:30-00:21:00])
- [NEW] If ALL pages fail to rank, suspect a site-wide technical fault. His list: pages not crawlable; `noindex` on pages; robots.txt blocking the hub or pages; super cluttered/messy site; staging site being indexed (duplicate site floating around); pages accidentally orphaned. Revisit technical + site-audit lessons. "If you were very diligent up to this point, that shouldn't be the case." (Playbook has crawl depth/orphans, but not the explicit noindex / robots / indexed-staging checks. For Next.js on Vercel: confirm production has NO noindex and previews DO.) (src: 9 full ~[00:21:00-00:22:30])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Defensive-before-offensive framing: offensive pages are ignored by Google if the fundamentals are not in place (playbook section 2). (src: 9 full ~[00:21:30-00:22:00])

## Lesson - ColorBliss case study (programmatic + AI SEO) and the Dominic win

- [NEW] "$3M" win used as proof of method: friend Dominic, consulting for a leader in fintech, spent 2 days seeing what they already ranked for, found 3-5 pages "already pretty much written" but aimed at the wrong keywords. He retargeted them to compact keywords: put the keyword in the URL, 301-redirected the OLD URL to the NEW URL, keyword at the beginning of page title, beginning of meta description, beginning of H1, and in the first sentence. Barely changed copy (maybe the intro). Also improved internal links so pages were reachable from the homepage. Ranked #1 quickly and still years later; Dominic estimates $3M/year from those pages. Pages were transactional: searchers who know what they want but not who provides it. Procedure to copy for existing pages: re-slug to keyword + 301 + five placements + intro tweak + link from homepage. (src: 10-11 ~[00:01:00-00:04:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Compact keywords can represent a ton of users or very high order values, and "fix beats write" for pages that already exist (playbook section 9 step 7, trap 8). (src: 10-11 ~[00:03:00-00:04:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Low authority is not a blocker: ColorBliss found at DA 8, ranking #1-3 for many BOFU keywords; now DA 17, ~2.1k ranking keywords, 152 in positions 1-3. "Getting past 8 to 20 is really easy" (link-building lessons). (src: 10-11 ~[00:00:00-00:05:00])
- [NEW] Programmatic template (stagetimer.io-style rather than the standard BOFU template): one page template per item, with variables in the sentences, some human-written and some AI-written. Exact template sentences observed: "Check out our collection of 34 ice cream coloring pages that are free to download and print." / "Check out our collection of 63 Spider-Man coloring pages that are free to download and print." / "To get started, simply click on the coloring sheet thumbnail." Variables: item name, item COUNT (63, 84, 34), AI-written description, AI-generated images inserted programmatically. Counts in the first sentence change per page and make pages distinct. (src: 10-11 ~[00:05:00-00:08:30])
- [NEW] Programmatic hub: `/coloring-pages` hub ("Free printable coloring pages. Discover the fun with our free printable coloring pages."), with example tiles, each linking to a category/list page ("Browse Baby Coloring Pages", "16 baby coloring pages"). Same hub-and-spoke as the playbook's `/uses` hub but generated at scale. (src: 10-11 ~[00:11:00-00:12:00])
- [NEW] Long-tail rank-by-association proof: the "dragon adult coloring pages" page (660 searches/mo, non-competitive SERP, ColorBliss #1) also ranks #1 for black Spider-Man coloring pages, cute shark coloring page, bucket of ice cream coloring page, etc. A #1 on one compact keyword drags in dozens of variants. (src: 10-11 ~[00:05:00-00:06:30])
- [NEW] Competitive counter-example: "dragon coloring pages" = 16,600/mo. #1 page scores 97/100; #7 scores 95 at DA 60; one at DA 28 scores 97; ColorBliss scores 87 at DA 17 and "should be 96-97". Rule of thumb: the higher the volume, the more competitive; there you need Page Score 96-97 AND more authority ("it would still be easier ... if it had more domain authority"). Link building matters most for the most competitive keywords. (src: 10-11 ~[00:06:30-00:09:30])
- [NEW] QA warnings from the weak ColorBliss pages (it ranks anyway, which shows the bar is low, but do better): AI images with melded limbs and detached hands, a single "Baby Yoda" page listed as a collection, a giant unformatted text block, and the keyword repeated too many times ("Craft ideas to do with coloring with dragon coloring pages"). Moz flagged "Avoid keyword stuffing in document" and that was "the biggest mistake" on the page. Lesson: templatise, but QA every page, vary the phrasing, never ship a collection page with 1 item. (src: 10-11 ~[00:07:30-00:13:30])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Misconception that you need tons of backlinks or DA to get BOFU traffic: "the bar for what is necessary is so low it is inspiring"; most people ranking for these keywords are "kind of lucky" and do not know the proper method. (src: 10-11 ~[00:08:30-00:13:30])
- [NEW] Programmatic SEO needs the monetisation hook in place: ColorBliss had a pricing tier on the homepage ("Create printable custom coloring sheets in seconds") to monetise the traffic. SEO pages must lead into a product/CTA. (src: 10-11 ~[00:04:00-00:05:00])

## Lesson - Using video to rank for keywords (YouTube Shorts, Reels, TikTok)

- [NEW] Why it works: Google is pushing user-generated content (Reddit's SERP visibility rising from ~June 2023; TikTok/Reels/YouTube video in SERPs). Sturm's theory: webmasters pollute sites with false/hallucinated AI content, so searchers append "reddit" or want video/comments as a source of truth. Real comments challenge false posters. (src: 10-11 ~[00:14:00-00:17:00])
- [NEW] Each social platform is its own search engine: he found Repurpose.io by searching a BOFU phrase ("repurpose TikTok videos...") ON TikTok and became its biggest affiliate (bought reusevideo.com, 301 to affiliate link, easier to say in video); a major SEO firm found him by searching YouTube "local SEO expert" and landing on his podcast where "local SEO" was all over the description. Takeaway: put the keyword in descriptions on every platform. (src: 10-11 ~[00:17:00-00:20:30])
- [NEW] Volatility warning: a BOFU web page can hold #1 for years; a video ranking can last a day, months, or years depending on competition, video performance, other platforms' content for that keyword, and searchers' intent. But compact keywords are overlooked, so longevity is plausible. (src: 10-11 ~[00:20:00-00:21:30])
- [NEW] Worked example, "free backlink checker" Short: made in a few minutes, posted everywhere (Shorts, Reels, TikTok, Snapchat Spotlight, Facebook Reels). Showed on YouTube search the next day for "free backlink checker" AND "backlink checker", on a competitive keyword with many SEO companies. Later YouTube SERP layout changed (2 long-form videos above, his Short #1 in the Shorts shelf; sometimes Shorts rank above long form because YouTube "constantly tests"). (src: 10-11 ~[00:21:00-00:24:30])
- [NEW] Short-form SEO recipe (exact): keyword at the beginning of the TITLE ("The best free backlink checker for any website is not what you'd expect, #SEO" style); same text as the first sentence of the DESCRIPTION; rest of description = the video transcript (he edits in Descript, copies transcript, proofreads and fixes things that read badly); custom-write only title and first sentence; use LONG descriptions; make them easy to read (mostly single-sentence paragraphs) which also raises featured-snippet odds. YouTube auto-transcribes too, but the written description still helps Google recommend it. (src: 10-11 ~[00:34:00-00:37:30])
- [NEW] Long-form YouTube: same rule (keyword "digital marketing newsletter" at the beginning of title and description), but write the description yourself because transcripts are too long. (src: 10-11 ~[00:37:00-00:38:00])
- [NEW] Instagram Reels has NO title field; the DESCRIPTION is the SEO field: put the keyword at its very start, then a one-line hook, e.g. "Ryan Reynolds marketing strategy. His secret: he always chooses storytelling." then a quote from the video. TikTok uses the same description. That Reel got the Google featured snippet for "Ryan Reynolds marketing strategy" 8 hours after posting (12 hours on a repeat test); the snippet quote came from the description. (src: 10-11 ~[00:25:00-00:29:00], ~[00:38:00-00:39:00])
- [NEW] Caveats on that result: mobile and desktop SERPs differ (desktop put a LinkedIn post above him, mobile showed the video on several platforms); the Instagram result was later removed after he publicised the trick. Theory: it only works if the video gets views/engagement. Nobody can rely on description being used as the snippet. (src: 10-11 ~[00:26:00-00:29:00])
- [NEW] "Link echoes SEO" example: videos with the keyword at the start of title/description took 3 of the top 4 Google video results; on YouTube Shorts #1 with 842 views and #2 with 540 views, so view count does not need to be high. Disambiguation tip: "link echoes" alone returns the Zelda character, so he added the niche word "SEO" to the keyword. Add a context word to ambiguous keywords. (src: 10-11 ~[00:39:00-00:41:00])
- [NEW] Posting mechanics: free guide edwardsturm.com/articles "social media posting strategy" (step-by-step for YouTube Shorts, Reels, TikTok, Snapchat, Pinterest, Facebook Reels; tools; high-frequency posting; keyword usage; he updates it and changes the publish date). Meta platforms dislike third-party automation tools (Repurpose is not used for everything). He has posted >=1 short video daily since 1 Nov 2022 (~2,000 videos). (src: 10-11 ~[00:41:00-00:42:30])
- [NEW] Planning sheet: the internal SEO template has tabs "YouTube SEO" and "Instagram Reels and TikTok SEO" with columns: target keywords, video title, description (first sentence + any extra planned keywords/content beyond the transcript), hashtags, content notes, pushed-out status. YouTube: only 1-3 hashtags. Description for a 1-1.5 minute video = transcript is enough; extra length is good "unless too long". File name: put keyword in the file name (use the title as the file name for video and thumbnail), minor factor. Thumbnails only for long-form YouTube. (src: 10-11 ~[00:42:30-00:44:00])
- [NEW] Trap: do NOT use an engaging but off-topic clip with the keyword pasted in. It may show briefly, but searchers bounce or pogo-stick (go result to result) and the platform/Google learn "this person makes bad content for search results; stop showing it." Satisfy intent, and use the video to show your brand as the solution so people convert. (src: 10-11 ~[00:44:00-00:46:30])
- [NEW] Unlike web pages (never publish several landing pages for the SAME BOFU keyword), you CAN post many videos on the same keyword and many variations; platforms test them against each other, recency is prioritised, and the best performer shows first. Slop with no engagement gets you shadow-banned and Google stops ranking you. (src: 10-11 ~[00:47:00-00:49:00])
- [NEW] Advantages list: more engaging and faster to understand than text; comments act as a source of truth/extra detail; organic algorithmic reach (no press needed, unlike 2009-2012); builds brand while doing SEO; less competition because legacy SEOs are shy about making video; cost is minutes per video. Disadvantage: video reach is "barely qualified" compared to BOFU page traffic. (src: 10-11 ~[00:29:00-00:34:00])
- [NEW] Free competitor-backlink tool: Bing Webmaster Tools, Backlinks in the sidebar. Shows backlinks to your site OR any site (use it to see who links to competitors, what press they get, poach their links). Limitation: it underreports (his site: 99 linking domains in Bing vs 332 in Moz). (src: 10-11 ~[00:21:30-00:23:00])
- [NEW] "Link echoes" claim (Rand Fishkin test, as cited by Sturm): two sites each given 22 equal-authority backlinks both rose a lot; after all links were removed, months later little had changed. Implication he draws: earned links keep working even if a publisher removes them. Treat as a secondary, unverified claim. (src: 10-11 ~[00:39:00-00:40:00])

## Lesson - Reddit and app stores (compact keywords on other platforms)

- [NEW] General rule that survives platform changes: the closer your keyword is to the START of the FIRST thing people read, the better optimised you are. Applies to page title, H1, first sentence, Reddit post/comment first sentence, video title/description. Compact keywords are so non-competitive and high-converting they can rank on virtually any platform. (src: 10-11 ~[00:50:00-00:52:00]; [01:04:30-01:05:00])
- [NEW] Search = relevance + authority. Keyword at the start = relevance. On Google, backlinks = authority. On platforms authority = account age, followers, post popularity (likes, comments, upvotes, shares = "equivalent to backlinks"), and Google pulls those platform metrics. A viral Reddit post containing the keyword will likely appear on Google. (src: 10-11 ~[00:52:00-00:53:30])
- [NEW] Reddit "best coffee" example: ranks #1 because the top comment has "best coffee" in its FIRST sentence, with Google "so generous with Reddit that it doesn't need the exact keyword match, but it helps." Legit lesson: when answering genuinely on Reddit, lead the comment with the phrase. (src: 10-11 ~[00:54:00-00:55:30])
- [NEW] Warning, parasite SEO: Sturm shows the #2 comment was AI-written (gptzero.me rated 100% AI), had exactly 50 bought upvotes, linked with a keyword anchor to a product; related accounts' posts were removed. Also: black hats hire Fiverr/Upwork workers as "brand ambassadors" who comment daily with keywords, rarely link to the brand, make only every 3rd-10th post about it for plausible deniability. He explicitly does NOT recommend it: ban, site blacklisting ("any post that mentions the website automatically removed"), possible Google manual penalty. Different subreddits require karma/age before posting. (src: 10-11 ~[00:53:30-01:01:30])
- [NEW] Reddit content trains ChatGPT: OpenAI has a Reddit deal giving real-time access to Reddit posts feeding ChatGPT; so genuine Reddit presence is also an AI-search (LLM citation) input. (He raises this as the reason black hats flood Reddit; the white-hat takeaway is that real, helpful Reddit answers can feed ChatGPT.) (src: 10-11 ~[01:00:00-01:01:00])
- [NEW] App stores/Amazon: all searches there are BOFU ("I have a problem, I hope an app solves it"), so a standard ASO strategy is compact keywords: main keyword at the start of the title; keyword variations in the subtitle (e.g. "remove background, AI object eraser, photo remover"); long detailed description using variations, no stuffing. Example: "background remover" 25,000/mo; remove.bg puts the keyword first and the brand last in the title. (src: 10-11 ~[01:01:30-01:04:30])
- [NEW] Authority beats on-page optimisation on very competitive BOFU SERPs: for "background remover" the #1 page was NOT the best optimised, but had the most backlinks saying it's a background remover tool and DA 70; all pages in that SERP were well optimised. (src: 10-11 ~[01:03:30-01:04:30])
- [NEW] Closing principle: platforms and authority metrics will change, but authority will always be valued and keyword-at-the-start will always matter; don't bet on one platform. (src: 10-11 ~[01:04:30-01:05:00])

# FILE: 13.md - What I'd do if I were designing your SEO strategy (full prioritised sequence)

Sturm's own caveat: "I'm going to generalize a lot... every business has its own needs and amount of resources." (src: 13 ~[00:00:00])

## Step 1 - Audit (existing site)
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Start with Screaming Frog + the site audit template; go through the technical lesson "like it was a checklist". Defensive SEO must be solid before offensive. (src: 13 ~[00:00:00-00:01:00])
- [NEW] Gate: "Assuming everything looks good, time to move on to research", i.e. do not start page building until the audit passes. (src: 13 ~[00:01:00])

## Step 2 - Keyword research
- [NEW] Sources: competitor keywords, competitor pages, "different AI tools to bounce ideas off", SERP reading to find keyword gaps and untargeted keywords; "writing everything down" in the Keywords tab (a big list). (src: 13 ~[00:01:00-00:02:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Prefer HIGH-LEVERAGE keywords: represent more than one acquisition (many users/customers), earn brand clout/reputation, or earn backlinks just by ranking, which then lets you rank for more of them. (src: 13 ~[00:01:00-00:02:00])

## Step 3 - Information architecture
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] One or two hub pages that list all BOFU keywords; hub must look good, not a plain HTML list; link hub from the footer. (src: 13 ~[00:02:00-00:03:00])
- [NEW] Optional extra links to the hub: header navigation or the main page "if I could find a reason for it"; footer is the non-negotiable one. (src: 13 ~[00:02:30-00:03:00])

## Step 4 - Build BOFU pages in volume
- [NEW] Cadence: "a ton" of BOFU pages, as many as wanted, "probably one every day or every two days". (src: 13 ~[00:02:30-00:03:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Exact keyword at the start of page title; keyword at start of meta description; in URL; in H1; at the start of the first sentence; use the structure from the on-page lesson. (src: 13 ~[00:03:00-00:03:45])
- [NEW] Title formula: keyword FIRST (unless grammatically wrong), then an optional extra benefit or CTA, then the brand name, separated with divider characters (e.g. `|`). So benefit text goes between keyword and brand. (src: 13 ~[00:03:00-00:03:30])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Short, punchy sentences/paragraphs; neutral tone; emphasise features; no sensationalising, no superlatives. (src: 13 ~[00:03:30-00:04:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Submit each finished page to Google Search Console and track the keyword (all of them if the page has several). (src: 13 ~[00:04:00])

## Step 5 - Post-batch re-audit
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] After a batch, re-run Screaming Frog and compare with the plan, because even careful people get typos, wrong titles, wrong meta descriptions, wrong H1s. (src: 13 ~[00:04:00-00:05:00])
- [NEW] The plan to compare against is the "site SEO layout" tab where every page's title/meta/H1/URL was planned in advance; keep that tab as the source of truth. Re-submit changed pages to GSC; if a URL changed, 301 it ("301 redirects pass SEO link juice"). (src: 13 ~[00:04:00-00:05:30])

## Step 6 - Video SEO in parallel (if bandwidth or an employee)
- [NEW] Do video SEO on every platform, keyword at the beginning of the title (where a title field exists) and at the beginning of the description, targeting the SAME keywords you build BOFU pages for; "further guarantees coverage" and builds brand.
