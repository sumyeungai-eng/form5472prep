## 12.1 Link Building 101, Directories, Directory Template, Launch Platforms

### Fundamentals (§1)
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Compact Keywords needs few backlinks; on-page targeting does most of the work. Backlinks matter most for the *competitive* BOFU keywords (SERP full of high Page Scores / high-volume phrases). ColorBliss ranks at DA 8; target ~DA 20 as "a great amount" to chase lots of BOFU keywords. (src: 12.1 §1, §6)
- [NEW] Link-value hierarchy he lists (top to bottom): relevance of the whole *site* > the *page* > the *area on the page* > the *text around* the link > the *link text itself*. Plan placements so the surrounding sentence is about your niche, not just the anchor. (src: 12.1 §2)
- [NEW] Absolute-best link = high-DA site + relevant page + exact-match anchor + gets clicks + dofollow. You can't control all of that; do not optimise for one factor only. (src: 12.1 §8)
- [NEW] .edu and .gov links are specially valuable; go out of your way to get one if possible. A natural profile = mix of high-DA and low-DA sites. (src: 12.1 §1)
- [NEW] Nofollow still carries SEO value (studies; rankings rose for sites that got nofollow links) and engagement/clicks on a nofollow link is also valuable. "Don't stress" about dofollow vs nofollow; a natural profile has both. (src: 12.1 §1, §8)
- [NEW] Tool tip: Moz Bar "Highlight links" shows nofollow in red, followed in green — use it to verify each directory/press placement. (src: 12.1 §7; also 12.2 [00:08:00], 12.4 [00:17:00])

### Anchor text / safety (§2–§3)
- [NEW] Exact-match anchor text gives more value but over-use triggers a (often non-manual) ranking drop on the page or the whole site. Mix exact, partial, brand and naked-URL anchors; don't build only one anchor type or only one site type — looks spammy. (src: 12.1 §2)
- [NEW] White-hat only: paying directly for links violates Google's ToS (that is why paid links get nofollow); he teaches no black-hat tactics. Philosophy: brand-building beats risky links — strong brands attract links, "more links beget more links". (src: 12.1 §2–§3)
- [NEW] Keep new products/services on the SAME domain (rising tide lifts all BOFU pages). If you want a branded domain, buy it and 301 it to a path on the main site (e.g. compactkeywords.com -> edwardsturm.com/compact-keywords). Only separate domains if the brands are truly unrelated. (src: 12.1 §3; 12.4 [00:18:00])

### Directories (§4–§7)
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Directories = best effort-to-result; Google `<niche> directories` and submit to every free one. Worked example: newsletter -> googled "newsletter directories", submitted to free ones, page ranks #1 for "growth hacking newsletter". (src: 12.1 §4–§5)
- [NEW] Submit to BOTH niche-specific directories AND broad general business directories; every listing = a backlink. Types he names: marketing-agency (local/digital), EdTech, restaurants, SaaS, new-company directories. (src: 12.1 §4)
- [NEW] Directory descriptions are SEO copy: write long, descriptive text; sometimes use exact keywords in description/tagline/topics, especially *umbrella* niche terms (e.g. "digital marketing newsletter" covers growth hacking, media buying, SEO). Keyword research tells you which umbrella language to use. (src: 12.1 §5)
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Keep a record of every submission (email, login, blurb) so you can log back in and rewrite as your keyword targeting evolves. Template fields = see "On-site assets / directory template" below. (src: 12.1 §5)
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] boostbenchmark.com: $67, categorised list of directories "designed to get DA to 20/100", many dofollow, ~2 hours of submitting yourself; paying more = they submit for you. (src: 12.1 §6)
- [NEW] **Semrush Local / Listing Management**: connect a fully-filled Google Business Profile (pictures, media, reviews, descriptions, links, consistent NAP) -> one click pushes the business to dozens of directories. Key trick: the listing's website URL = his **services HUB page**, so link juice flows to the nested BOFU pages. Some links dofollow, some nofollow. (src: 12.1 §7)
- [NEW] Measured result: quick "keyword arbitration" BOFU page under the hub sat ~#8 for ~12 weeks, then after the Semrush directory push went #6 -> #1. Evidence that pointing directory links at the hub lifts child pages. (src: 12.1 §7)
- [NEW] Caveat for us: the Semrush Local route needs a Google Business Profile / NAP; form5472prep is an online service — check eligibility before paying. (derived from 12.1 §7)

### Launch platforms (§8)
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] ProductHunt.com (free) and BetaList.com (he says not free) are the two biggest tech launch platforms; link list already marks BetaList as paid/skip. (src: 12.1 §8)
- [NEW] **Fill out EVERY field** before launching. Under-filled listings land in Product Hunt's low-traffic "new" tab; completed ones reach the "featured" tab. If you land in "new", message PH support, say it is a real product and ask to be moved to featured — he says support is responsive and helped repeatedly. (src: 12.1 §8)
- [NEW] Launch effect = link + engagement signals; he shows a visible rankings jump in Moz rank tracking in the PH launch window, repeated across launches. Blogs also write up good launches. (src: 12.1 §8)
- [NEW] Cadence: re-launch every 3–6 months; easier to justify if there is a product update. (src: 12.1 §8)
- [NEW] Find more platforms: Google "Product Hunt alternatives"; ChatGPT prompt he used: "general business launch platforms like Product Hunt, but for all businesses, not just tech". Launch platforms exist per niche; many are free. (src: 12.1 §8)
- [NEW] His guide: edwardsturm.com/articles/product-hunt-results (traffic/users/backlinks from five launches). (src: 12.1 §8; 12.4 [00:15:00])

## 12.2 Expert Quotes, Press Releases, Press Kits

### Expert quote platforms
- [NEW] Why it works: journalists need credible quotes + stories; you give a quote, publication links your company; wording around the link also teaches Google what you are. Link may be dofollow, nofollow or brand mention only — mostly dofollow in his experience. (src: 12.2 [00:00:00]–[00:03:00])
- [IN-LINK-LIST] HARO/Connectively: he says Connectively shut down; he keeps the lesson because it shows how all the tools work. Journalist posts a query, many experts reply, journalist picks best (credible / best-written / agrees with their angle). (src: 12.2 [00:01:00]–[00:02:00])
- [NEW] Search the platform by YOUR keyword (he searched "SEO") rather than browsing all queries. (src: 12.2 [00:02:00])
- [NEW] **Answer template (his winning reply)**: (1) one-line credibility opener with LINKS to proof (clients, follower counts; journalists can verify); (2) a hook ("X is the best Y for a reason all marketers are sleeping on"); (3) short punchy sentences, 1–3-sentence paragraphs, confident tone; (4) concrete specifics/example; (5) requested rating/score if asked, with a small caveat for honesty; (6) link to your own article that deepens the point (his: "Pareto SEO" article, which links to the product + newsletter). (src: 12.2 [00:03:00]–[00:05:00])
- [NEW] Credibility opener matters most on topics where you must prove authority (AI/editing answer in Entrepreneur); less on casual ones (productivity tips). Keep LinkedIn recommendations/testimonials visible so verification works. (src: 12.2 [00:23:00]–[00:24:00])
- [NEW] **Title wording is SEO**: he signs as "SEO and marketing expert" (earlier "fractional CMO" — "CMO" signalled a broad marketing topic range). Pick the byline/job-title text deliberately; the link anchor + title tells Google the site's topic. Likewise he names his podcast "digital marketing podcast", "daily growth hacking TikTok" inside quotes to seed those keywords near the link. (src: 12.2 [00:10:00]–[00:11:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Featured.com: formerly Turkle; $99/mo -> $39.80/mo as free competitors appeared; free tier also got him accepted. (link list notes current pricing differs.) (src: 12.2 [00:06:00]–[00:08:00])
- [NEW] Featured.com stats: early screenshot 37 answers -> 27 published/scheduled, 7 not selected, 3 in review = 79% acceptance on a paid tier. He suspects paying users get favourable treatment but still got accepted on the free tier. Published in 31+ outlets: Entrepreneur.com (DA 92, dofollow), Startup Nation (DA 59, dofollow), Inc, Marketer Interview, etc. (src: 12.2 [00:07:00]–[00:10:00])
- [NEW] Show the coverage: announce each placement on social (clout/authority) and put "as seen in" logos on the website. (src: 12.2 [00:18:00]–[00:19:00])
- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Source of Sources (sourceofsources.com, free, Peter Shankman, HARO founder): email digest up to 3x/day with query + journalist contact; big publishers listed. (src: 12.2 [00:13:00]–[00:17:00])
- [NEW] **SOS rules / trap**: reply ONLY if truly on-topic; one off-topic pitch reported back = permanently removed, no appeal. Self-test: if you need >3 seconds to decide whether it helps the journalist, don't send. Anonymous requests: email Shankman who forwards. (src: 12.2 [00:15:00]–[00:16:00])
- [NEW] Quoted (probably Qwoted): basic plan limited, Pro $99/mo; he still got links. Why higher-priced tools can be better: less competition per query, and the vendor works harder to get you accepted to keep your subscription. Try one month, cancel if useless. (src: 12.2 [00:17:00]–[00:19:00])
- [IN-LINK-LIST] Help a B2B Writer (free; now MentionMatch per the link list): register as a source; you receive writer queries by email. (src: 12.2 [00:19:00]–[00:20:00])
- [NEW] Example B2B-Writer brief (HubSpot writer): wants first-hand experience, specific problem solved, tangible results, relevant statistics, ONE tip (listicles rejected), AI-generated answers detected and penalised, include name + surname + job title + website URL. Replicate those elements in every reply. (src: 12.2 [00:20:00]–[00:21:00])
- [NEW] Ops tip: create a Gmail folder/filter that routes all journalist-query emails (SOS, B2B writer, etc.) into one place; answer in batches. (src: 12.2 [00:19:00]–[00:20:00])
- [NEW] Trap: don't fabricate credentials or write BS that just confirms the journalist's pre-written angle. He notes some SEOs do (journalists often have the story pre-written, want a confirming "icing on the cake" quote; retention-driven SEOs invent accomplishments) — he does not recommend it; credible, honest answers win. For a tax service this is also a legal/trust risk. (src: 12.2 [00:21:00]–[00:24:00])
- [IN-LINK-LIST] Only pitch free tiers first (link list already restricts to free). Course adds: answers that are accepted can be reused as site content proof. (src: 12.2 [00:06:00]–[00:09:00])

### Press releases

## 12.3 Event Link Building

- [IN-PLAYBOOK] Taking over a dormant meetup, rebranding it and handing outreach to a VA (playbook mentions in one line); course adds everything below. Labelled by Sturm as experimental/optional; directories + expert quotes are the easy wins. (src: 12.3 [00:00:00]–[00:01:00])
- [NEW] Downsides: lots of waiting rather than work; works best in a big city; experimental. Upsides: can be done from anywhere (target a big city in your target country), builds top-of-mind awareness, grows your email list and your own group, boosts influence for outreach ("organiser of the biggest X meetup"), and puts your blurb + links on many event pages; fully outsourceable to a VA. (src: 12.3 [00:00:00]–[00:03:00], [00:14:00]–[00:16:00])
- [NEW] Organiser incentives (why it works): they want quantity of attendees and relevant attendees; an empty event is the worst outcome. (src: 12.3 [00:03:00]–[00:05:00])
- [NEW] **Step 1 — find abandoned groups**: organisers step down to avoid Meetup fees (his plan ~$174/yr "standard"; Pro $55/mo shows member emails and is worth one month). Join many groups in your target city and niche; search your mailbox for "needs organizer"; click "Become the organizer". Example: Disrupt HR NYC, founded 2015, 1,130 members, "48 hours left" notice. Standard plan is enough. (src: 12.3 [00:05:00]–[00:08:00])
- [NEW] **Step 2 — rebrand**: rename to `<City> <Niche>` (New York SEO, Chicago Generative AI, Miami Supplements, LA Video Game Development) — inclusive of all niche keywords; write a keyword-rich description; add links to the pages you want to rank (his services hub + a BOFU page that ranks #1) plus your socials; keep old photos for credibility. (src: 12.3 [00:08:00]–[00:10:00])
- [NEW] **Step 3 — attach to other people's events**: find same-city niche events on Gary's Guide (NYC/SF tech-business-marketing calendar), Eventbrite, Luma (lu.ma) etc.; contact organisers (or VA does, with a script): "I have a group in your niche, want to co-host and send my members." - [NEW] Blurb pattern (different from the Meetup description): "Co-hosted by the <City Niche> meetup group [Meetup link]. Founded by <yourdomain.com>. <Brand> offers <niche services from X to Y>." Alternate between naked URL and brand name; add a few more target keywords. Links are a mix of nofollow/dofollow. (src: 12.3 [00:12:00]–[00:14:00])
- [NEW] Tools named: Meetup.com, Eventbrite, Luma, Gary's Guide. His own track record: meetups (took over / founded / co-founded), group of 10,000 members that ranked #1 on meetup.com/topics/blockchain. (src: 12.3 [00:02:00]–[00:04:00])

## Top 15 NEW items most applicable to a low-DA niche B2B tax-filing service site

1. Submit the services hub (/services), not the homepage, to directories — it sends link equity down to all BOFU child pages; he saw #8 -> #1 after one push (12.1 §7).
2. Expert-quote answer template: credibility opener with verifiable links, punchy confident answer, link to a deep page; no AI text, no invented credentials (12.2 [00:03:00]–[00:05:00]) — fits the tax niche's trust requirement and our "no tax advice" copy rules.
3. Search quote platforms by your own keyword (e.g. "foreign-owned LLC", "Form 5472", "non-resident founder") and reply only to on-topic queries (SOS bans for one off-topic pitch) (12.2 [00:02:00], [00:15:00]).
4. Choose the byline/title wording deliberately (it is SEO text next to the link) — e.g. "foreign-owned US LLC compliance" rather than generic "tax" (12.2 [00:10:00]).
5. Podcast guesting via matchmaker.fm FREE tier (10 messages/month) targeting founder/nomad/e-residency/cross-border podcasts; give hosts a ready description with keyword anchors (12.5 [00:12:00]–[00:16:00]) — free, links to specific pages.
6. Give each host a DIFFERENT target page (penalty calculator, 5472 guide, pricing) rather than the homepage to build page-level authority without keyword stuffing (12.5 [00:08:00], [00:19:00]).
7. Statistics/data article (e.g. foreign-owned LLC filing numbers, penalty counts/amounts, deadlines by year, sourced to IRS) with "Editors' pick" top stats — cite-able, bloggers link to the source (12.4 [00:07:00]–[00:10:00]).
