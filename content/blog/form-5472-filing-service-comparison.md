## Do you need a CPA to file Form 5472?

Not as a requirement of the form. The IRS states it plainly: "Any tax professional with an IRS preparer tax identification number (PTIN) is authorized to prepare federal tax returns," while adding that "tax professionals have differing levels of skills, education and expertise" ([IRS: understanding tax return preparer credentials](https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/understanding-tax-return-preparer-credentials-and-qualifications)).

Three facts follow, narrow on purpose:

1. Form 5472 is an **information return** about transactions between the LLC and its foreign owner. It reports what moved; it does not compute tax or take a position on the owner's tax liability.
2. The IRS requires a **PTIN** of anyone who prepares a federal return for compensation. It does not require a CPA licence for this form.
3. So a CPA licence is not something Form 5472 itself requires of a paid preparer. That is a statement about the form's requirements, not about what any preparer is worth: a PTIN permits paid preparation, it does not by itself establish expertise.

That is the whole argument, and it stops there. A CPA, an enrolled agent or an attorney can do things a PTIN-only preparer cannot, and those come up on other work: a treaty position, a Form 1040-NR or 1120-F, transfer pricing between related entities, an IRS examination, or anything where you need someone to speak for you. On that last point the IRS is unambiguous — enrolled agents, CPAs and attorneys "have unlimited representation rights before the IRS", while a PTIN-only preparer has no authority to represent clients before the IRS. If a notice or an examination is already in play, that difference matters more than the price gap discussed here.

Nothing here says you do not need a professional. It sets out what Form 5472 requires of a paid preparer, which is a narrower question than what you want looking at your records before they go to the IRS. If you file Form 5472 for other people, [filing Form 5472 for clients without being a CPA](/blog/file-form-5472-for-clients-without-being-a-cpa) covers the same rules from the preparer's side.

## What does Form 5472 actually ask for?

This is the fastest way to judge what a $547 filing buys. Per the [IRS instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472), the package is a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached, and on that Form 1120 the *only* information required is the entity's name and address and items B and E on the first page, with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top. Four items: the cover page is clerical.

The judgement lives in Form 5472 itself:

- **Part I** identifies the reporting corporation — the LLC. Factual.
- **Part II** reports the 25% foreign shareholder; for a foreign-owned disregarded entity, the owner. Factual.
- **Part III** identifies the related party in the reportable transactions. Factual.
- **Part IV** reports monetary transactions with a foreign related party, line by line. This is where a wrongly classified owner payment lands in the wrong row.
- **Part V** is the box a foreign-owned disregarded entity checks for any other transaction not already in Part IV: the instructions name amounts connected with formation, dissolution, acquisition and disposition, including contributions and distributions, described on an attached statement.
- **Part VI** covers nonmonetary or less-than-full-consideration transactions with a foreign related party, with a schedule describing each.

So the form is mostly identification, with the difficulty concentrated in Parts IV to VI: deciding what each movement of money was. That is a classification task on your own records, not an audit or a tax opinion — which is the work a reviewing tax accountant does on our Standard tier, and the work an automated check does not do. For the line-by-line route, see [Form 5472: DIY vs hiring a preparer](/blog/form-5472-diy-vs-preparer); for costs generally, [Form 5472 filing cost](/blog/form-5472-cost).

Two constraints apply whoever prepares it. A foreign-owned disregarded entity cannot file Form 5472 electronically: the package goes by fax at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737, or by mail to the IRS PIN Unit in Ogden, Utah. And a fax transmission receipt is evidence that the package was sent, not confirmation that the IRS accepted it.

## The bottom line

One straightforward year and organised records: take the $79 AI-reviewed filing. A missed year, an owner loan you cannot confidently describe, a close deadline, or a preference for a person's judgement on the classification: the {{price.standard}} human review is the better value, and [you can start it here](/start). The CPA-signed service its page describes is priced at $448 to $547, but the form does not require the credential — and if what you need is representation before the IRS, buy that from an enrolled agent, CPA or attorney, not from a filing fee.

The $25,000 penalty is why any of this is worth comparing. The IRS assesses it on a reporting corporation that fails to file Form 5472 when due and in the manner prescribed; a substantially incomplete form counts as a failure to file; and if the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notification, a further $25,000 applies per related party per 30-day period. Against that, the gap between $79 and $547 is small.
