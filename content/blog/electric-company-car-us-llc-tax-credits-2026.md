If you acquire an electric company car in 2026, do not budget for the former federal clean-vehicle purchase credits. The IRS says the new, previously owned and qualified commercial clean-vehicle credits under sections **30D, 25E and 45W are unavailable for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025**. A possible business expense deduction is a separate question.

The dates matter more than an old “up to $7,500” headline. This guide addresses US federal rules for a non-US individual with a single-member disregarded US LLC. It does not determine local EV subsidies or VAT recovery.

## The acquisition cutoff comes before the delivery question

The IRS's [clean-energy credit change FAQs](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/faqs-for-modification-of-sections-25c-25d-25e-30c-30d-45l-45w-and-179d-under-public-law-119-21-139-stat-72-july-4-2025-commonly-known-as-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-obbb) distinguish acquisition from placing the vehicle in service. For these credits, acquisition requires a binding written contract and a payment by the cutoff. A nominal down payment or trade-in can count as payment.

A qualifying pre-cutoff acquisition delivered later therefore needs different review from a new 2026 purchase. Preserve the actual contract and payment evidence. Earlier eligibility is not established by a dealer's advertisement, an informal reservation or a model appearing on an old eligible-vehicle list.

The [commercial clean-vehicle page](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/commercial-clean-vehicle-credit) also requires the vehicle to be primarily used in the United States. An old qualifying acquisition does not automatically support a credit for an LLC owner's car kept and used abroad.

## A deduction, a credit and a discount are different amounts

| Item in a sales proposal | What it actually changes | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Dealer discount | Agreed purchase price | Written invoice and conditions |
| Operating-cost estimate | Forecast future spending | Mileage, electricity tariff, charging mix and assumptions |
| Business deduction | Eligible taxable income | Taxpayer, business use, method and limits |
| Tax credit | Tax liability under a specific credit | Acquisition date and every remaining eligibility condition |
| Owner contribution | Funding available to the LLC | Transfer evidence and information-return reporting |

The table is a review framework, not a claim that each item is available. Ask the seller to identify which line they mean whenever they describe a “tax saving.”

## Start with the business reason and cash cost

Here is a hypothetical three-year, US-dollar comparison. It is not a survey of vehicle prices or a forecast of resale values.

| Illustrative input | Electric car | Petrol car |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $40,000 | $32,000 |
| Resale proceeds after three years | $18,000 | $14,000 |
| Energy and routine maintenance over three years | $5,000 | $11,000 |
| Cost before financing, insurance and taxes | $27,000 | $29,000 |

The formula is **purchase price − resale proceeds + energy and routine maintenance**. For the EV: **$40,000 − $18,000 + $5,000 = $27,000**. For the petrol car: **$32,000 − $14,000 + $11,000 = $29,000**.

The EV is $2,000 cheaper on those assumed inputs, while requiring $8,000 more purchase cash. Financing, insurance, registration, charging installation, downtime and tax are excluded from both columns. A worse resale outcome or more expensive public charging could reverse the result. This cash comparison is not a depreciation schedule.

For a vehicle acquired after the cutoff, do not insert an unavailable federal 30D, 25E or 45W credit into the comparison. Only add a different incentive after verifying its current terms and your eligibility with its administering authority.

## Electric does not mean fully deductible

For a foreign owner, the LLC's US address does not establish a US deduction. [Publication 519](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519) connects nonresident business deductions to effectively connected income. Mixed business and personal driving still needs allocation, and a vehicle purchase requires the applicable cost-recovery analysis.

IRS vehicle-expense guidance allows mileage or actual costs when the taxpayer and vehicle qualify; it does not promise a full purchase-price write-off for an electric car. See [IRS Topic 510](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc510). Compare the methods using our [2026 mileage example](/blog/business-car-mileage-vs-actual-expenses-2026), including the midyear rate change.

## Make charging records usable

For a charging-cost review, retain the invoice, charging date, vehicle, location, kWh and amount. At home, distinguish the car's measured charging from the rest of the property's electricity bill. Record the allocation method and any employer or owner reimbursement.

This is a suggested evidence structure, not a universal metering requirement. Its purpose is to let an adviser trace a business-cost claim and detect duplicate payments. A card payment alone cannot show which vehicle was charged or which journeys were business-related.

If UK VAT is involved, read the separate [company-car VAT guide](/blog/uk-company-car-vat-buy-lease-electric). A charging reimbursement and the right to recover its VAT are different questions.

## Record owner funding even when there is no vehicle credit

The IRS [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) include contributions and distributions in the foreign-owned disregarded entity's reporting framework. Track money the owner contributes for the purchase, any owner-paid costs and repayments as actual dated events. The end of a purchase credit does not remove information-return obligations.

No. Section 45W is unavailable for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025. A documented acquisition by the cutoff with later delivery is a different fact pattern that needs eligibility review.
