**An Italy tax resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 after a reportable owner or related-party transaction. The individual’s codice fiscale is normally the FTIN, while contributions, withdrawals, loans, and owner-paid expenses must be separated from ordinary customer activity.**

Form 5472 discloses cross-border related-party transactions. It does not determine how Italy classifies the LLC or whether the owner owes US income tax.

If the LLC moved money to or from its Italian owner, [start a reviewed filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-italy-residents-us-llc) before the information-return deadline.

## What triggers Form 5472 for an Italian owner?

Three facts normally create the filing path: the LLC is a US disregarded single-member entity, the sole owner is foreign for US tax purposes, and a reportable transaction occurred with the owner or another related party.

The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) expressly include contributions, distributions, formation and dissolution payments, and other related-party transactions. The filing attaches to a limited pro forma Form 1120 and carries a current **$25,000 failure-to-file penalty per form, per year**.

| Event | Report on Form 5472? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Italian owner funds the US bank account | Yes | Capital contribution |
| LLC repays an owner loan | Yes | Related-party loan movement |
| Owner pays software personally | Yes | Amount paid on behalf of the LLC |
| Unrelated customer pays a sales invoice | Usually no | Third-party business revenue |
| LLC pays an Italian company owned by the same person | Yes | Separate foreign related-party transaction |

One Form 5472 is generally prepared for each transacting related party. A direct individual owner and that owner’s Italian company are not collapsed into a single form when both had transactions with the LLC.

## Which Italian identifier belongs in Part II?

An Italian individual generally enters the codice fiscale on Part II line 4b(3). The [Agenzia delle Entrate verification service](https://telematici.agenziaentrate.gov.it/VerificaCF/Scegli.jsp) identifies the codice fiscale as the identifier checked against Italy’s tax registry.

| Field | Entry for an Italian individual owner |
|---|---|
| 4a | Legal name and actual Italian residential address |
| 4b(1) | Existing SSN or ITIN, only if the owner has one |
| 4b(2) | Consistent LLC-assigned reference ID when 4b(1) is blank |
| 4b(3) | Codice fiscale |
| 4d | Italy as country of citizenship when factually correct |
| 4e | Italy when the owner files income tax there as a resident |

Do not substitute the LLC’s EIN for the owner’s identifier. The EIN identifies the reporting LLC; the codice fiscale identifies the Italian owner. An ITIN is not required solely to submit Form 5472.

## Does the US-Italy treaty change the filing?

The treaty can change income-tax treatment but does not waive Form 5472. The IRS lists Italy on its current [US income-tax treaty index](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z).

For a consultant or agency owner working entirely from Italy, service income is generally sourced where the services are performed. US workdays, a US office, US employees, inventory, or a dependent agent can create a different US trade-or-business analysis. Any treaty position must be supported and claimed where required; it is not automatic.

Italy may classify and tax the US LLC differently from the United States. An Italian adviser should determine the local entity classification, worldwide-income reporting, value-added-tax implications, and foreign-asset disclosures. The US Form 5472 package cannot resolve those Italian questions.

## How should EUR owner transactions be organized?

Use a transaction-level workpaper rather than an annual net number.

| Workpaper column | Example |
|---|---|
| Date | 2026-03-05 |
| Direction | Owner to LLC |
| Type | Capital contribution |
| Original amount | EUR 4,000 |
| Rate source and rate | Documented transaction-date rate |
| USD amount | Calculated and reported gross |
| Evidence | Bank transfer and capital memo |

The contribution and any later distribution should appear as distinct lines. ## How is an Italy-owned LLC filing completed?

1. **Confirm one-owner disregarded status.** A multi-member LLC generally follows the Form 1065 partnership path instead.
2. **Identify every related party.** List the individual owner and any related Italian entity that transacted with the LLC.
3. **Reconcile gross movements.** Trace contributions, distributions, loans, repayments, reimbursements, and related-party payments to source records.
4. **Prepare the US package.** Complete the pro forma Form 1120, one Form 5472 per related party, and the Part V or other required statements.
5. **File by fax or mail.** A foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot e-file the package.
6. **Retain the signed copy and transmission evidence.** Keep the final pages, source ledger, exchange-rate support, and delivery record together.

For a calendar-year entity, the package is generally due April 15 following the tax year, or October 15 after a timely Form 7004 extension. [Our deadline guide](/blog/form-5472-deadline-2026) explains the year-to-filing-year calendar.

## Frequently asked questions

### What FTIN does an Italian owner use on Form 5472?

An Italian individual normally uses the codice fiscale. An Italian entity uses its own applicable entity tax identifier; do not put the individual controller’s codice fiscale in the company’s FTIN field.

### Does an Italian owner need an ITIN?

Not solely for Form 5472. An ITIN can be needed for a separate individual US income-tax return, but Form 5472 permits a reference ID and FTIN when no US identifying number exists.

### Is Italian customer income reported on Form 5472?

Not merely because it is revenue. Form 5472 reports related-party transactions. An invoice paid by an unrelated Italian customer belongs in the business books, not automatically on the owner’s Form 5472.

### Can the owner net contributions against withdrawals?

No. Form 5472 uses gross related-party amounts. Record the contribution and withdrawal separately, even if they offset economically during the year.

### Does a tax treaty eliminate the $25,000 filing risk?

No. Treaty benefits concern income taxation; the section 6038A information return remains separate. A late or substantially incomplete Form 5472 can still attract the filing penalty.
