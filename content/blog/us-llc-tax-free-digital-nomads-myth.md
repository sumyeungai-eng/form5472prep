**Often there is no US income tax for a nonresident owner whose LLC has no US trade or business. But "tax-free" is not "filing-free": the LLC still files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 each year it has a reportable transaction with you, the penalty for missing it is $25,000, and your residence country may tax the profit.**

The pitch is familiar: form a Wyoming or New Mexico LLC, connect Stripe, and pay 0% tax while you work from Lisbon or Bangkok. Part of it is true. A single-member LLC pays no US tax of its own, and many nonresident owners who work entirely outside the United States owe no US income tax.

The pitch leaves out the paperwork. Owing no US tax and owing no US filing are separate questions, and for a foreign-owned LLC the answers are usually "no" and "yes". What your residence country charges is a third question no US structure answers.

## Is a US LLC tax-free for a nonresident owner?

Not automatically. The LLC itself pays no US federal income tax, but whether *you* owe US tax depends on facts, not on the entity.

For federal income tax purposes, the IRS treats a single-member LLC as disregarded as separate from its owner unless it elects to be taxed as a corporation. The LLC has no separate tax bill. Its income is treated as yours.

As a nonresident alien, you are then taxed by the US on two kinds of income. The IRS page on [taxation of nonresident aliens](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/taxation-of-nonresident-aliens) sets them out: effectively connected income (ECI), taxed at graduated rates on Form 1040-NR, and certain US-source fixed or periodical income (FDAP), taxed at a flat 30 percent or a lower treaty rate with no deductions.

Income that is neither generally carries no US federal income tax. That is the true core of the claim, and it is conditional: whether you have a US trade or business is a facts-and-circumstances question. We walk through the factors in [does a foreign-owned US LLC pay US tax?](/blog/does-foreign-owned-llc-pay-us-tax) rather than repeat them here. If your facts are anywhere near the line, the answer needs a qualified adviser, not a formation company's landing page.

## Which "tax-free LLC" claims hold up, and which do not?

Most claims pair a true sentence with an omitted one.

| Common claim | What is true | What the claim leaves out |
|---|---|---|
| "A US LLC pays 0% US tax" | A single-member LLC is disregarded and pays no entity-level federal income tax | You, the owner, can owe US tax on ECI or US-source FDAP income |
| "No income means nothing to file" | A zero-income LLC may owe no income tax return | A foreign-owned LLC files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 whenever a reportable transaction occurred, income or not |
| "You never need a US tax return" | Many nonresident owners with no US trade or business file no Form 1040-NR | A nonresident engaged in a US trade or business must file Form 1040-NR even with no income from it |
| "Work from anywhere, including the US" | Services performed outside the US generally produce foreign-source income | Personal services performed in the US can create a US trade or business, and US days count toward the substantial presence test |
| "0% tax, full stop" | US non-taxation can be a correct US answer | Your residence country applies its own rules and may tax the LLC's profit to you |
| "No tax residence anywhere, no tax anywhere" | A US LLC's filing does not depend on where you live | Being a nomad does not settle which country can tax you; that is a question of each country's law |
| "The IRS ignores small LLCs" | Form 5472 carries no tax | The instructions set a $25,000 penalty for failing to file, and a substantially incomplete form counts as a failure to file |

## Why does an LLC that owes no tax still file Form 5472?

Because Form 5472 is an information return about money moving between the LLC and its foreign owner, not a tax return.

The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that a foreign-owned US disregarded entity is treated as a corporation separate from its owner for the limited purposes of section 6038A reporting. Treas. Reg. § 1.6038A-1 applies that rule for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2017 and ending on or after 13 December 2017. The same instructions tell a foreign-owned US DE to file a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached, completing only the entity's name and address and items B and E on page 1, with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top.

The trigger is a **reportable transaction** between the LLC and you, or another foreign related party, during the year. The instructions include contributions to and distributions from the entity. In practice that means:

- the money you wired in to open or fund the account
- transfers from the LLC to your personal account
- loans in either direction
- the LLC paying your personal costs, or you paying the LLC's

**Customer revenue is not a reportable transaction.** A Stripe or Wise payout from a client stays off the form. Moving that balance to your personal account goes on it.

The instructions set a **$25,000** penalty for failing to file when due and in the prescribed manner, and add a further $25,000 for each 30-day period, or part of one, that the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notification. It is not automatic for every error, but it is a fixed amount with no connection to profit. An LLC that made $3,000 faces the same figure as one that made $3 million.

## When does a nomad's US LLC stop being US-tax-free?

When work is performed in the United States, or when you spend enough days there. Two separate tests matter.

**Working while you are in the US.** The IRS page on [effectively connected income](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/effectively-connected-income-eci) explains when a foreign person is engaged in a US trade or business, and [IRS Publication 519](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519) puts the personal-services rule bluntly: "If you perform personal services in the United States at any time during the tax year, you are usually considered engaged in a trade or business in the United States." A narrow exception requires all three of: working for a foreign person or company that is not itself in a US trade or business, no more than 90 days in the US during the year, and no more than $3,000 of pay for that work. Pub 519 also sources personal-services income to where the work is performed. Client work done during a US visit is therefore the point where "tax-free" most often stops being true; take advice before the trip, not after.

**If you are engaged in a US trade or business, the filing duty changes too.** The [Instructions for Form 1040-NR](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040nr) say a nonresident alien engaged in a US trade or business must file even with no income from that trade or business, no US-source income, or income exempt under a treaty. "No tax due" and "no return due" are separate questions here as well.

**Spending enough days in the US.** Enough days can make you a US tax resident under the substantial presence test, changing everything, including whether the LLC is foreign-owned. We cover the formula in the [digital nomad Form 5472 guide](/blog/form-5472-digital-nomad-us-llc).

## Does "no US tax" mean no tax in your home country?

No. The US answer binds the US only. Your residence country decides, under its own law, whether you are its tax resident and what it taxes.

In outline:

- Many countries tax residents on worldwide income, and may look through a US disregarded entity to tax its profit to you. Some classify a US LLC differently, which can create mismatches.
- Other systems tax foreign income only when remitted, or under special regimes. The rules differ widely and change often.
- Having no settled home does not place you outside every tax system. Residency tests can weigh days, homes and economic ties, and leaving a country does not always end residence there.

We do not advise on any country's personal tax. The question for your revenue authority or a local adviser is: *am I tax resident here, and how do you treat income from a US single-member LLC?* Whatever the answer, the US filing below is unchanged.

## How do you file the Form 5472 the "tax-free" pitch leaves out?

On paper, once a year. A foreign-owned US DE cannot file Form 5472 electronically.

1. **Prepare a pro forma Form 1120** with **"Foreign-owned U.S. DE"** across the top of page 1, completing only the LLC's name and address and items B and E.
2. **Prepare Form 5472**: Part I for the LLC, Part II for you as the foreign owner, including your foreign tax identification number (FTIN) or a reference ID, and the transaction parts listing contributions, distributions and other related-party amounts.
3. **Total each category in US dollars**, converting any other currency at a consistent, stated rate.
4. **Sign, then fax to 855-887-7737** (an IRS line), or mail to Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201.
5. **Keep the timestamped fax receipt** and the bank statements behind every figure.

For a calendar-year LLC the due date is generally **15 April**, and Form 7004 extends it. If you bought the "tax-free" story and never filed, file every outstanding year promptly; our [late filing guide](/blog/form-5472-filed-late-never-filed) covers the reasonable cause statement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Wyoming LLC tax-free for a nonresident?

The LLC pays no entity-level federal income tax as a disregarded entity, and you may owe no US income tax if you have no ECI or US-source FDAP income. Form 5472 is still due in any year with a reportable transaction, and state fees may apply.

### Do I pay US tax if all my clients are American?

Not merely because the clients are American. What matters is whether you are engaged in a US trade or business and have effectively connected income, which turns largely on where the work is performed. Get an adviser's view if you work from inside the US.

### My LLC made no profit. Do I still file?

Probably yes. Form 5472 is triggered by a reportable transaction with you, such as funding the account or taking money out, not by profit. Only a year with no reportable transaction at all needs no filing.

### Does a tax treaty make the LLC tax-free?

A treaty can affect income tax on business profits, and claiming it may require a US return. It does not remove Form 5472, which is an information return under section 6038A.

### If I am not tax resident anywhere, is the income tax-free?

Not necessarily. Each country applies its own residency tests, and a nomad can remain resident somewhere without intending to. Ask the relevant revenue authority or a local adviser. The US filing is due either way.

### Can I work from the US on a visit?

Personal services performed in the US can create a US trade or business, and US days count toward the substantial presence test. Both can change your US tax position, so take advice before working from the US.

### What happens if I never filed Form 5472?

The instructions set a $25,000 penalty for failing to file, and more if the failure continues after IRS notice. File every missed year promptly, with a reasonable cause statement, before the IRS contacts you.
