**Use actual Form 5472 transaction amounts when they can be determined. The IRS permits a reasonable estimate only when the actual amount cannot be determined, and the estimate generally must fall between 75% and 125% of the actual amount. A transaction or series totaling $50,000 or less may use the “$50,000 or less” reporting convention, but that is not an exemption from filing.**

These rules are relief for reporting uncertainty—not permission to ignore records, omit a category, or round every transfer.

If incomplete books are delaying a filing, [start a reviewed Form 5472 package](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-reasonable-estimates-small-amounts) with the bank records, owner ledger, and explanation of each gap.

## Three different concepts

The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) distinguish actual amounts, reasonable estimates, and the limited small-amount reporting convention.

| Concept | When it applies | What the filer should retain |
|---|---|---|
| Actual amount | The amount is determinable from reliable records | Ledger, invoices, bank proof, exchange-rate support |
| Reasonable estimate | The actual amount cannot be determined | Method, inputs, assumptions, missing-record explanation |
| “$50,000 or less” | A reportable transaction or series in a category does not exceed $50,000 | Evidence the total stayed within the threshold |
| Noncash value | Property, rights, obligations, or services are exchanged | Part VI description and reasonable fair market value support |

The $50,000 convention does not mean that LLCs with fewer than $50,000 of owner transactions can skip Form 5472. Filing is driven by foreign ownership and reportable transactions, not a general minimum-dollar exemption.

## The 75%-to-125% range

The IRS instructions treat an estimate as reasonable if it is at least 75% and not more than 125% of the actual amount. If the actual total later proves to be $40,000, that numerical range would be $30,000 to $50,000.

That range is a compliance test, not a target for casual rounding. A filer should still use the best information available and explain the method. Repeated estimates that always minimize reported payments can undermine the claim that the method was reasonable.

Mark estimated amounts as estimates on the form as instructed. Keep the calculation with the filing records so a reviewer can reproduce it.

## A defensible estimate workpaper

Suppose twelve months of related-party management fees were paid, but two monthly statements are unavailable. The LLC has ten verified monthly invoices, a fixed-rate contract, and bank evidence that the arrangement continued unchanged.

A defensible workpaper would:

1. total the ten verified invoices;
2. identify the two missing periods;
3. apply the contract rate only if the facts support continuity;
4. document currency conversion for each period;
5. label the estimated portion; and
6. preserve attempts to obtain the missing records.

An unsupported guess based only on the year-end cash balance would be weaker because it does not trace the direction, category, or timing of the related-party activity.

## Do not net away the threshold

If the owner contributes $35,000 and receives a $25,000 distribution, the transactions should not be netted to a $10,000 flow for Form 5472 analysis. Contributions and distributions are different gross directions and categories.

Likewise, do not combine unrelated categories merely to use one vague “$50,000 or less” entry. Maintain a ledger by related party, direction, and transaction type. The [reportable-transactions guide](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples) provides a classification model.

## Foreign currency needs its own support

An amount can be exact in euros, pounds, or yen but still require a US-dollar conversion method. Keep the original currency amount, transaction date, exchange-rate source, rate used, and resulting dollars together.

Do not treat exchange-rate movement as a reason to estimate the underlying transaction. It is a conversion issue. Use a consistently applied, reasonable method that fits the current instructions and the facts. See the [currency-conversion guide](/blog/form-5472-currency-conversion-exchange-rates).

## Correcting an estimate later

If better records arrive after filing, compare the filed amount with the supported actual amount and ask a qualified preparer whether an amended filing is appropriate. Materiality, category changes, timeliness, and whether the original return was substantially complete all matter.

The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that failure to file a timely and substantially complete return can trigger a $25,000 penalty. The estimate provisions should be used carefully and documented contemporaneously.
