**If your single-member US LLC collects paid newsletter or membership money, it files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 for every year it had a reportable transaction with you, its foreign owner. Subscriber payments, deferred annual plans, platform-held balances, refunds and chargebacks are not reported. What you withdraw or put in is.**

A subscription business has a money problem that a one-off sale does not: at any moment some of the cash has been collected but not earned, some has been earned but not paid out, and some may yet be clawed back. An annual plan charged in January funds a year of issues. A platform sits on a balance between payout runs. A supporter disputes a charge in month three, another cancels a pledge before it ever clears.

None of that is a related-party transaction. All of it is customer revenue in motion between unrelated parties — subscribers, card networks, platforms, your own accounts. The single moment Form 5472 cares about is when that money crosses between the LLC and you. [We prepare and fax the complete package from $149](/start).

## Does a paid newsletter or membership LLC have to file Form 5472?

Yes, in almost every year it operates. A foreign-owned US single-member LLC files when three things are true: the LLC is a disregarded entity for US tax purposes, its owner is a foreign person, and during the tax year the LLC had at least one reportable transaction with that owner or another foreign related party.

Under **Treas. Reg. § 1.6038A-1**, for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2017 and ending on or after 13 December 2017, a foreign-owned US disregarded entity is treated as a corporation separate from its owner **solely** for the § 6038A reporting rules. That is the whole reason a newsletter LLC that owes no US corporate tax still files a corporate-style information return.

The third condition is met the first time you move subscription money out to your personal account, or fund the LLC's hosting bill from your own card. The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that "a penalty of $25,000 will be assessed on any reporting corporation that fails to file Form 5472 when due and in the manner prescribed," and that "filing a substantially incomplete Form 5472 constitutes a failure to file Form 5472."

## Which newsletter and membership money flows are reportable?

Sort every flow by who stands on the other side. Subscribers, supporters, platforms, processors and software vendors are unrelated counterparties. You are a related party, and so is any company you own or control.

| Money flow in a subscription business | Other side of the flow | Reportable? | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly subscriber charge | Unrelated subscriber | No | Nowhere on the form; revenue in the books |
| Annual plan charged in full up front | Unrelated subscriber | No | Nowhere on the form, in any month of the year |
| Platform or processor fee deducted before payout | Unrelated platform | No | Nowhere on the form; operating expense |
| Balance a platform holds between payout runs | The LLC's own money in transit | No | Nowhere on the form; an LLC asset, not a transaction with you |
| Payout from the platform to the LLC's bank account | The LLC paying itself | No | Nowhere on the form; both accounts belong to the LLC |
| Refund issued to a subscriber | Unrelated subscriber | No | Nowhere on the form; reduces revenue |
| Chargeback or dispute lost | Unrelated subscriber, card network | No | Nowhere on the form; a business loss |
| Pledge cancelled or declined before it cleared | Unrelated supporter | No | Nowhere on the form; no money moved |
| Hosting, email delivery and design tools paid by the LLC | Unrelated vendors | No | Nowhere on the form; operating expense |
| The same tools paid on your personal card | You, the foreign owner | **Yes** | Part V statement — contribution, loan or reimbursable amount |
| Transfer from the LLC's bank account to your personal account | You, the foreign owner | **Yes** | Part V statement — distribution |
| Personal savings sent in to cover a thin month | You, the foreign owner | **Yes** | Part V statement — contribution or loan |
| A documented loan to or from you, and interest on it | You, the foreign owner | **Yes** | Part IV if it fits a listed monetary line; otherwise the Part V statement |
| LLC pays you a fee under a written services agreement | You, the foreign owner | **Yes** | Part IV if it fits a listed services or compensation line; otherwise Part V |
| LLC pays a company you own abroad for editing or illustration | Foreign related party | **Yes** | Part IV; that party may need its own Form 5472 |
| A platform payout routed to your personal account, then forwarded in | Routed through you | **Yes** | Part V statement — the pass-through is an owner movement |

Part IV covers "Monetary Transactions Between Reporting Corporations and Foreign Related Party" — the listed categories such as sales, rents, royalties, commissions and interest. Part V covers, in the instructions' words, transactions "not already entered in Part IV," which "include amounts paid or received in connection with the formation, dissolution, acquisition, and disposition of the entity, including contributions to, and distributions from, the entity." Check the Part IV lines first and use Part V for what does not fit: see [the Part V statement example](/blog/form-5472-part-v-statement-example) for the attachment's layout and [reportable transactions examples](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples) for the same categories outside a subscription business.

## Is an annual subscription collected up front reportable before it is earned?

No. The timing of when subscription revenue is earned has no bearing on Form 5472, because the subscriber is not a related party in January, in July, or at any point in the term. A reader who pays twelve months up front has bought a service from the LLC; nothing about that charge belongs on the form.

Deferred revenue still matters to the LLC's own books and to whatever accounting basis it uses, and it can matter to the balance-sheet-style figures elsewhere in a filing package. It simply never becomes an owner transaction by sitting there unearned.

Where up-front annual money does create a Form 5472 question is in what it tempts you to do. January cash that funds a year of work is easy to withdraw in one movement, and that single transfer is reportable in full, in the year you make it, not spread across the months the subscriptions cover.

## What happens on Form 5472 when subscribers refund, dispute or cancel?

Nothing happens on Form 5472. A refund, a lost dispute and a pledge that never cleared are all events between the LLC and unrelated parties, so none of the three is a reportable transaction, and none is netted against the amounts you report in the owner column.

Three practical consequences follow:

- **Reversals change the revenue figure, not the owner figure.** Report what actually moved between the LLC and you. A refund paid out of a platform balance in March does not shrink a distribution you took in February.
- **A clawback you cover personally is a separate reportable movement.** If the platform's balance cannot absorb a chargeback and you top up the LLC from your own money, that top-up is an owner contribution or loan, and it is reported as such.
- **Money you return out of your own pocket is not a refund by the LLC.** Refund subscribers from the LLC's accounts, or the transaction turns into a reportable owner movement with a refund attached to it.

- **Buy Me a Coffee**, checked 3 October 2026. The public [FAQ](https://buymeacoffee.com/faq) states that "you get paid directly to your bank account." A payout schedule, a minimum balance and whether funds are held before disbursement are **not stated on that FAQ**, so treat your own payout history in the dashboard as the record.
- **Substack and Patreon**, checked 3 October 2026. Both companies' help centres returned an access error to us on that date, so no mechanic is quoted from either. Read the platform's own payout and refund articles while signed in, and take the figures from your own payout and transaction exports rather than from anything stated here.

Generically, every one of these platforms does the same thing that matters: it collects from unrelated subscribers, may hold a balance for a period, deducts its own charges, and settles the remainder into an account you nominate. Not one step of that is a related-party transaction. ## How do you reconcile a platform balance to the owner-movement total at year end?

Run this once, after the year closes, and the filing writes itself.

1. Fix the window: the LLC's tax year, with the exact first and last day written at the top of the workpaper.
2. Export the platform's own payout report and its gross transaction report for that window, plus the same from any separate processor.
3. Export the LLC's bank and business card statements for the identical window.
4. Record the platform's opening and closing balances. Both are LLC assets held by a third party, not transactions with you.
5. Tie each payout on the platform report to a deposit line on the bank statement, and list every payout or deposit that fails to match.
6. Total refunds, lost disputes and declined or cancelled subscriptions separately. They reduce revenue and never enter the owner column.
7. List every movement between the LLC and you — date, direction, amount, currency and reason — from the bank and card statements, not from memory.
8. Label each owner movement a contribution, distribution, loan or reimbursement, and total the two directions separately. Do not net money in against money out.
9. Convert any non-USD amount to US dollars and write down the rate and its source next to the amount.
10. Confirm that every owner movement in the list has a document behind it, then carry only those totals to Form 5472.

The $25,000 penalty also reaches a failure to maintain required records, and the instructions require a reporting corporation to keep records "sufficient to establish the correctness of the reporting corporation's federal income tax return, including information or records that might be relevant to determine the correct treatment of transactions with related parties." A fuller list is in the [Form 5472 recordkeeping checklist](/blog/form-5472-recordkeeping-checklist).

## When and how is a newsletter LLC's Form 5472 package filed?

For a calendar-year LLC the package is generally due **15 April**, and a timely Form 7004 filed under the special disregarded-entity instructions extends it. The filing is Form 5472 attached to a **pro forma Form 1120** carrying **"Foreign-owned U.S. DE"** across the top of page 1, where only the entity's name and address and **items B and E** need completing.

1. Build Form 5472 and the Part V statement from the owner-movement schedule, not from the platform's revenue report.
2. Prepare the pro forma Form 1120 with the header, then sign and date it.
3. Send the package by fax to the IRS at **855-887-7737**, or by mail to Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201. It cannot be e-filed.
4. Keep the fax receipt. A transmission receipt is evidence of transmission, not IRS acceptance of the return.

The [About Form 5472 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472) links the current form and instructions.

## How does a subscription business differ from a coaching or course business here?

The filing mechanics are identical; only the money's shape differs. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do paid subscriptions go on Form 5472?

No. Subscriber and supporter payments are business revenue from unrelated customers. Form 5472 reports transactions between the LLC and its foreign owner or other foreign related parties, not sales.

### Is an annual plan charged in January reportable across the year?

No. The charge is never reportable, in January or later, because the subscriber is unrelated. Only your own withdrawals of that money are reportable, in the year each withdrawal happens.

### Is a balance the platform is holding a reportable transaction?

No. A held balance is the LLC's own money sitting with a third party. It belongs in the LLC's records as an asset, and it appears nowhere on Form 5472.

### Do refunds and chargebacks reduce what I report?

No. Refunds and chargebacks reduce revenue, not the owner column. Report the amounts that actually moved between the LLC and you, without netting reversals against them.

### A supporter's pledge was declined. Does anything change?

No. A declined or cancelled pledge means no money moved, so there is nothing to report and nothing to reverse. Keep the platform export that shows it as evidence.

### I topped up the LLC from my own card after a chargeback. Reportable?

Generally yes. Funding the LLC from personal money is a movement between owner and LLC. Record it as a contribution, loan or reimbursable amount, and keep the receipt.

### My newsletter made nothing this year. Do I still file?

Possibly. Paying the LLC's hosting bill personally or taking any money out is a reportable transaction even with no subscribers; a year with no owner movements at all may not require the form.
