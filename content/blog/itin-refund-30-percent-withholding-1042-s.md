## Why was 30% withheld from the payment?

Thirty per cent is the general chapter 3 withholding rate for certain US-source income paid to a foreign person when the payer cannot associate the payment with documentation supporting a lower rate or exemption. Interest, dividends, rents, royalties, and some compensation can fall within the fixed or determinable annual or periodical category.

Three explanations cover many cases:

1. **No valid Form W-8BEN was on file.** The withholding agent could not document the recipient's foreign status or a lower treaty rate.
2. **No treaty relief applied.** The recipient's country had no applicable treaty, the income article did not reduce the rate, or the recipient did not meet the treaty conditions.
3. **The payment was reported incorrectly.** The payer may have used the wrong amount, income code, recipient, source classification, or rate. A refund claim needs proof, not mere disagreement.

Platform payments can have different contractual character and source rules. Read the Form 1042-S and payer agreement before calling the entire 30% refundable.

## Does Form 1042-S prove that a refund is due?

No. Form 1042-S proves what the withholding agent reported and withheld. A refund exists only when that amount exceeds the US tax correctly due after applying the income's character, source, treaty position, exemptions, and any corrected payer information.

| Form 1042-S item | What to check | Why it matters to the refund |
|---|---|---|
| Income code | Does it match royalties, dividends, compensation, or another payment? | Different categories can have different source and treaty rules |
| Box 2 gross income | Does it reconcile to payer statements and the contract? | The return starts from the reported gross amount unless support shows a correction |
| Box 3b tax rate | Is 30.00 shown, or another chapter 3 rate? | The displayed rate shows the payer's withholding treatment |
| Box 7a federal tax withheld | Does it match the amount actually withheld? | The supported amount becomes the payment claimed on the return |
| Box 10 total withholding credit | Does it reconcile to the recipient copy? | The return claim must match the withholding evidence |
| Recipient name and TIN | Do they match the Form 1040-NR and ITIN record? | Identity mismatches can stop matching and delay the claim |

Ask the payer for a corrected Form 1042-S when the statement is wrong. Do not replace its figures on Form 1040-NR without explanation and evidence.

## How do you claim a Form 1042-S refund with an ITIN?

Claim the refund on Form 1040-NR and submit the ITIN application with that return when no personal US taxpayer number exists. The [Form W-7 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7) put a nonresident filing only for a refund in reason box **b** and require the W-7 application package to include the federal return unless an exception applies.

1. **Collect every recipient Form 1042-S.** Keep payer statements, contracts, payment reports, prior W-8BEN forms, residence evidence, and any corrected statement.
2. **Reconcile the income and withholding.** Match gross income and tax withheld to the payer records. Separate different income codes rather than blending them.
3. **Determine the correct US tax treatment.** Check source, income character, effectively connected income, treaty eligibility, and the correct rate. Use a qualified adviser when the answer is uncertain.
4. **Prepare Form 1040-NR.** Report the income in the proper part of the return, show supported withholding as a payment, and calculate the overpayment.
5. **Attach Form 1042-S.** The IRS instructions require acceptable proof of chapter 3 or chapter 4 withholding and direct the filer to attach the recipient statement to page 1.
6. **Prepare Form W-7 if no SSN or ITIN exists.** Check reason box b, provide the complete foreign address, and include current identity and foreign-status documentation.
7. **Submit one combined package and retain a copy.** The IRS assigns the ITIN, associates it with the return, and then processes the return. Keep the signed forms and all evidence.

Do not file Form W-7 alone for a refund that must be claimed on a return. The W-7 and Form 1040-NR travel together unless a separate W-7 exception genuinely applies.

## How does the $12,000 royalty example work?

The arithmetic shows why “30% withheld” and “30% refundable” are different statements. The figures below are illustrative, not a promised outcome or a treaty-rate claim.

**Illustrative baseline: no treaty relief or other exemption.** A foreign creator receives **$12,000** of US-source royalties. The payer withholds **$3,600**, calculated as $12,000 × 30%. If the full amount is correctly classified and taxable at 30%, the Form 1040-NR liability is $3,600 and the withholding refund is **$0**.

**Illustrative documented payer error.** Assume the same Form 1042-S incorrectly duplicated **$4,000** of royalties and the payer issues a corrected statement showing **$8,000**. At the same 30% rate, the supported tax is **$2,400**: $8,000 × 30%. With $3,600 actually withheld, the potential overpayment is **$1,200**: $3,600 − $2,400.

The second result depends on a real correction and consistent evidence. A taxpayer cannot create a refundable difference by relabelling valid royalties. A treaty-country resident may reach another result only after verifying the named treaty, residence, income article, eligibility conditions, and rate. No universal “treaty-country rate” exists.

## How long do you have to claim the refund?

The general limit is the later of **3 years from the date the federal income tax return was filed or 2 years from the date the tax was paid**. The [IRS refund-claim time-limit page](https://www.irs.gov/filing/time-you-can-claim-a-credit-or-refund) states that exact later-of rule and notes that exceptions can allow more time.

The rule is not “three years after Form 1042-S.” The return filing and tax payment dates matter, and the recoverable amount can be limited by the payment lookback. A claimant near the limit needs individual advice.

A refund statute is separate from the ordinary Form 1040-NR due date. Filing promptly also makes payer corrections and supporting records easier to obtain.

Four scenarios show why the same entry can produce no refund, an overpayment, or a claim needing treaty or source analysis.

**1. YouTube creator in a country with no applicable treaty relief.** The creator receives a Form 1042-S for correctly reported US-source royalties, and the full amount is properly subject to 30%. Form 1040-NR may reconcile the withholding, but no refund arises merely because the creator lives abroad. **2. KDP author resident in a treaty country, but no valid claim reached the payer.** The payer withheld 30%, while the author believes a royalty article reduces the tax. The author must verify treaty residence, the named treaty and article, limitation rules, and the rate. Form 1040-NR can claim supported relief; the article does not assume a country or percentage. **3. Brokerage recipient with a corrected duplicate payment.** The original recipient statement counted part of the distribution twice, and the broker issues a corrected Form 1042-S. The return uses the corrected income and supported withholding. The difference between tax withheld and correct tax may be refundable.

**4. Freelancer whose services were performed outside the United States.** A Form 1042-S describes compensation that the freelancer believes was sourced incorrectly. Service location, contract, income code, and payer correction matter. The freelancer should not assume that foreign performance automatically fixes the return without documentation or professional source analysis.

Form 1040-NR is the claim mechanism; the records determine the amount.

## How can you prevent 30% withholding next year?

Give the withholding agent an accurate Form W-8BEN before the next payment and update it when the certified facts change. The form documents foreign status and, when appropriate, claims a treaty rate for a named country and income article.

The [IRS Form W-8BEN instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben) provide that a treaty claimant supplies either a US TIN (an SSN or ITIN) on line 5 or a foreign tax identifying number on line 6a. An ITIN is generally needed only where a treaty claimant does not give a tax identifying number issued by the jurisdiction of tax residence on line 6a, and the instructions set out securities-related categories that need no ITIN at all.

Practical prevention means:

- use the legal name and permanent residence address that match the recipient account;
- provide the foreign TIN on line 6a when it is required, or check line 6b only when legally entitled;
- claim only a treaty country and article that apply to the payment;
- give the form to the payer, not the IRS;
- confirm the payer accepted the form before another payment; and
- review the next Form 1042-S promptly for income, rate, withholding, and TIN mismatches.

A valid W-8BEN changes future withholding. It does not itself refund tax already sent to the IRS; Form 1040-NR handles the prior-year claim.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is all 30% withholding on Form 1042-S refundable?

No. Only withholding that exceeds the correct US tax is refundable. If the income and 30% rate were correct and no treaty or exemption applies, the withholding can equal the final liability and produce no refund.

### Which return claims a Form 1042-S refund?

A nonresident individual generally claims the refund on Form 1040-NR. Attach the Form 1042-S showing income and US tax withheld, report the income correctly, and claim the supported withholding as a payment.

### Do I need an ITIN to receive the refund?

You need an SSN or ITIN as the identifying number on Form 1040-NR. If you are ineligible for an SSN and have no ITIN, submit Form W-7 reason box b with the return and supporting documents.

### Can I submit Form W-7 without Form 1040-NR?

Not for an ordinary return-based refund claim. The W-7 is submitted with Form 1040-NR. A no-return application works only when a specific W-7 exception applies and the required exception evidence is attached.

### Does a treaty automatically create a refund?

No. The claimant must qualify as a resident under a named treaty, identify the applicable income article, meet its conditions, and support the rate. The refundable amount is the excess of withholding over the tax correctly due.

### How long do I have to claim over-withheld tax?

Generally, the deadline is the later of three years after filing the federal return or two years after paying the tax. Exceptions and payment-lookback limits can change the result, so a claim near the deadline needs individual review.

### Will a CAA mail my original passport to the IRS?

No. A Certifying Acceptance Agent verifies eligible original identity documents and returns them immediately after review. The CAA submits the certification with the Form W-7 package, avoiding original-passport mailing.
