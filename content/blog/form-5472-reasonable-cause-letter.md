For Form 5472, the [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that a **$25,000** penalty is assessed when a reporting corporation fails to file when due and in the prescribed manner. A reasonable cause letter is therefore not a formality. It is the factual record that explains why the penalty should not apply.

## What is reasonable cause for a late Form 5472?

Reasonable cause is a facts-and-circumstances standard. It is not automatic forgiveness, and it is not created by copying persuasive wording from a template.

The IRS reasonable-cause guidance asks what happened, when it happened, how the event prevented compliance, and what attempts the taxpayer made to comply. For businesses, the IRS says the reasons apply to the person with authority to submit the return, deposit or tax. That point matters for an LLC owned by a foreign individual or foreign company: identify the actual responsible person, not only the entity name.

For Form 5472, the strongest letters usually show five things:

1. The owner had a real compliance process, adviser, formation provider or document review in place.
2. A specific fact caused the Form 5472 requirement to be missed.
3. The owner discovered the filing requirement on a specific date or through a specific event.
4. The owner corrected the missing filing promptly.
5. The owner changed the process so the same failure should not repeat.

Unsupported claims are weak. "I did not know" is not enough by itself because the IRS page says lack of knowledge generally does not qualify for failure-to-file relief. A stronger statement explains why the owner reasonably did not know despite ordinary care, what advice or documents the owner relied on, and how quickly the owner acted after discovering the issue.

## What should the reasonable cause letter contain?

The letter should contain a dated chronology, a cause explanation, evidence, corrective action and a prevention plan. Each paragraph should do a job.

| Section | Required substance | Evidence to attach |
|---|---|---|
| Taxpayer and year | Entity name, EIN, tax year, form, penalty or late filing being addressed | IRS notice, EIN letter, prior return record |
| Filing obligation | Why Form 5472 and pro forma Form 1120 were required | Formation documents, ownership chart, transaction ledger |
| Chronology | Exact dates for formation, advice, discovery, preparation and submission | Emails, engagement letters, bank records, fax receipt |
| Cause | The specific event or misunderstanding that prevented timely filing | Adviser correspondence, illness records, unavailable-record proof |
| Ordinary care | What the owner did before the failure to comply | Service agreements, tax calendar, advice request |
| Correction and prevention | What was filed, when it was filed and what changed | Completed package, receipt, new annual checklist |

Write in the first person for an individual owner or in the company's voice for an entity. Use exact dates. Name documents. Avoid adjectives that do no evidentiary work, such as "honest", "small", "accidental" or "unfair", unless the facts behind those words are also attached.

## Which facts can support reasonable cause?

Facts can support reasonable cause when they show ordinary business care and prudence despite the failure. The IRS page lists examples such as fires, natural disasters, civil disturbances, inability to get records, death, serious illness, unavoidable absence and certain system issues for failure-to-file or failure-to-pay penalties.

For a Form 5472 case, potentially useful facts include:

1. Documented reliance on a qualified tax adviser who received the relevant ownership and transaction facts.
2. Formation documents or service agreements showing that the provider did not offer U.S. tax filing advice.
3. Records showing that the owner had no prior U.S. filing experience but promptly sought help after learning of the requirement.
4. Medical, court, disaster or bank records that line up with the filing period.
5. A filed delinquent return and fax receipt showing prompt correction.

Weak facts include lack of funds by itself, general busyness, reliance on an unqualified formation seller as if it were a tax adviser, and a copied statement with no records. Adviser reliance is strongest when the taxpayer can show the question asked, complete facts supplied, advice received and prompt action after discovering the error.

## What evidence should be attached?

Attach evidence that proves the chronology and cause. More pages are not always better; irrelevant attachments make the real facts harder to see.

Use a labelled exhibit list:

1. Formation certificate and operating agreement.
2. EIN confirmation letter.
3. Ownership chart and foreign owner identifier records.
4. Bank statements showing the reportable transactions.
5. Engagement letters and adviser emails.
6. Formation-provider service description, if relevant.
7. Illness, disaster, court or bank-access records, if relevant.
8. Completed Form 5472 package and fax or mailing proof.
9. New filing calendar, checklist or engagement confirming future compliance.

Redact irrelevant personal information. Keep a full unredacted copy for your own records if your adviser recommends it. The IRS should be able to match each exhibit to a sentence in the letter.

## How should the reasonable cause statement be organised?

The statement should be organised so an IRS reviewer can decide whether the legal standard is met without reconstructing the facts. Chronology first, argument second.

Original element: use this annotated skeleton instead of a fake sample letter with invented facts:

1. **Opening request.** State the taxpayer name, EIN, tax year, form, notice number if any, and the relief requested. Do not claim guaranteed entitlement.
2. **Filing background.** Explain the LLC's formation, foreign ownership and reportable transaction facts for the year. Identify the missing Form 5472 package.
3. **Dated chronology.** List the exact dates when the LLC was formed, records were gathered, advice was sought, the requirement was discovered, the return was prepared and the package was sent.
4. **Cause of failure.** Describe the specific event, advice gap, record problem or circumstance that prevented timely filing. Connect the cause to the due date.
5. **Ordinary care before the failure.** Describe the steps the owner took to understand obligations before the deadline, including advisers, formation materials, calendars or document review.
6. **Prompt correction.** State what was filed, when it was filed, how it was sent and what receipt or delivery proof is attached.
7. **Future prevention.** Explain the concrete control now in place: annual reminder, preparer engagement, owner transaction ledger, document retention folder or responsible person.
8. **Exhibit list and signature.** List each attachment by label and sign under penalties of perjury when the submission requires or warrants that format.

The skeleton is deliberately factual. A real letter must use the taxpayer's real dates, real documents and real reason. Do not insert names, dates, amounts or adviser statements that are not true.

## Will the IRS read the letter before assessing a penalty?

Not necessarily. The IRS [delinquent international information return procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/delinquent-international-information-return-submission-procedures) state: "During the processing of the delinquent information return, penalties may be assessed without considering the attached reasonable cause statement." The same page says taxpayers may need to respond to IRS correspondence and submit or resubmit reasonable cause information.

That is why you keep the signed statement, exhibits and filing proof together. The statement attached to a delinquent return may preserve the argument, but a CP215 or other penalty notice can require a separate response by the date on the notice.

If a notice has already arrived, read the [penalty-notice response guide](/blog/form-5472-penalty-notice-what-to-do) before sending the same letter to the wrong address. Notice correspondence usually goes to the address shown on the notice, not automatically to the Ogden PIN Unit used for the delinquent return.

## How do you file the late Form 5472 package with the letter?

The late package should correct the missing return and preserve the reasonable cause record. A letter without the actual Form 5472 package does not fix the filing failure.

1. Identify every delinquent year.
2. Prepare the pro forma Form 1120, Form 5472 and required Part V or other attachments for each year.
3. Prepare a reasonable cause statement tied to the specific years and facts.
4. Sign the return package and statement where required.
5. Fax or mail the package using the dedicated IRS instructions for foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities.
6. Save the exact PDF or copy sent, timestamped fax receipt or mailing proof, and exhibit set.
7. Calendar the next annual filing so the same failure does not repeat.

For a 2025 calendar-year foreign-owned U.S. DE package, the normal due date is 15 April 2026 under the [IRS Form 1120 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120). Filing late after the applicable due date should be treated as a correction project, not as ordinary annual compliance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there an official Form 5472 reasonable cause template?

No universal IRS template establishes reasonable cause. A useful letter is tailored to the entity, year, cause, chronology, evidence and corrective action.

### Does being a first-time filer guarantee relief?

No. First-time status can be a mitigating fact, but reasonable cause remains a case-by-case determination based on responsible conduct and the cause of failure.

### Should the statement be sent without the delinquent return?

No. Correct the filing failure promptly. The statement supports the completed catch-up package; it does not replace Form 5472 or the pro forma Form 1120.

### Can the IRS assess the penalty before reviewing the letter?

Yes. Current IRS delinquent-information-return procedures state that penalties may be assessed without first considering the attached statement, so a later notice response may be required.

### Should a late filer wait for an IRS notice?

Usually no. The delinquent procedures address taxpayers who file before examination or IRS contact. Delay weakens prompt correction and prolongs exposure.

### Can lack of knowledge be reasonable cause?

Not by itself. The IRS says lack of knowledge generally does not qualify. The letter must explain why the misunderstanding was reasonable despite ordinary care.

### Does a reasonable cause letter guarantee penalty abatement?

No. The IRS decides from the facts, law and evidence. A clear letter improves the record, but it does not guarantee relief.

Reasonable cause is proved by a coherent record, not persuasive adjectives.
