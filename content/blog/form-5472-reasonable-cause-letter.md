**A Form 5472 reasonable cause letter should give a dated, fact-specific account showing that the taxpayer exercised ordinary business care and prudence, explain exactly what prevented timely compliance, document steps taken before and after the failure, and confirm prompt correction. A template alone cannot establish reasonable cause, because the IRS evaluates all facts and circumstances.**

## What is reasonable cause for a late Form 5472?

Reasonable cause is a facts-and-circumstances standard, not automatic forgiveness. The IRS [penalty-relief guidance](https://www.irs.gov/payments/penalty-relief-for-reasonable-cause) says the taxpayer generally must show ordinary care and prudence and explain what happened, when it happened, how it prevented compliance and what attempts were made to comply.

For Form 5472, the statutory initial penalty is $25,000 per required form and year. The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) also warn of continuation penalties if the failure continues after IRS notice.

## What should the letter contain?

Our five-part evidence framework keeps the statement specific:

| Part | Required substance |
|---|---|
| 1. Obligation | Entity, EIN, tax year, required Form 5472 and due date |
| 2. Chronology | Dates of formation, advice sought, discovery, corrective work and submission |
| 3. Cause | The concrete event or misunderstanding that prevented timely compliance |
| 4. Ordinary care | Steps taken to identify, prevent and correct the problem |
| 5. Evidence and prevention | Documents attached and procedures adopted for future years |

Write in the first person for an individual owner or in the company's voice for an entity. Use exact dates and name the people or documents involved. Avoid claims such as “I did not know” without explaining why that misunderstanding was reasonable and what diligence preceded it.

## Which facts can support reasonable cause?

The IRS considers evidence rather than labels. Potentially relevant facts include serious illness, unavailable records, documented reliance on a competent adviser who received all necessary facts, an IRS action, or a first-time-filer circumstance combined with responsible conduct.

Weak facts include lack of funds by itself, general busyness, reliance on an unqualified formation seller, or a copied statement with no records. Adviser reliance is strongest when the taxpayer can show the question asked, complete facts supplied, advice received and prompt action after discovering the error.

## What evidence should be attached?

Attach only documents that prove the chronology and cause:

- formation and EIN records;
- emails showing when professional advice was requested or received;
- hospital, court or disaster records when relevant;
- bank statements showing the transactions reported;
- prior filings and compliance history;
- the completed delinquent return; and
- a calendar or engagement confirming the new prevention procedure.

Redact irrelevant personal information. Keep a complete copy of everything sent.

## How should a reasonable cause statement be organised?

1. Identify the taxpayer, form, year and penalty provision.
2. State the relief requested without claiming entitlement.
3. Present a chronological narrative with exact dates.
4. Connect the cause to the filing failure.
5. Describe ordinary care before the failure and prompt correction afterward.
6. List and label supporting exhibits.
7. Explain the controls now in place.
8. Sign under penalties of perjury when appropriate to the submission.

Do not invent an IRS quotation or cite a regulation that does not match the penalty. A tax professional should review material penalty disputes.

## Will the IRS read the letter before assessing a penalty?

Not necessarily. The IRS [delinquent international information return procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/delinquent-international-information-return-submission-procedures), updated in April 2026, say penalties may be assessed during processing without considering an attached reasonable cause statement. The page warns that the taxpayer may need to resubmit the information in response to IRS correspondence.

That makes recordkeeping essential. An attached statement can document the position, but the owner should be ready to answer a notice by its deadline.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there an official Form 5472 reasonable cause template?

No universal IRS template establishes reasonable cause. A useful letter is tailored to the entity, year, cause, chronology, evidence and corrective action.

### Does being a first-time filer guarantee relief?

No. First-time status can be a mitigating fact, but reasonable cause remains a case-by-case determination based on responsible conduct and the cause of failure.

### Should the statement be sent without the delinquent return?

No. Correct the filing failure promptly. The statement supports the completed catch-up package; it does not replace Form 5472 or the pro forma Form 1120.

### Can the IRS assess the penalty before reviewing the letter?

Yes. Current delinquent-information-return procedures state that penalties may be assessed without first considering the attached statement, so a later notice response may be required.

### Should a late filer wait for an IRS notice?

Usually no. The delinquent procedures address taxpayers who file before examination or IRS contact. Delay weakens the claim of prompt correction and prolongs exposure.
