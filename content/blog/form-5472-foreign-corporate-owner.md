**A foreign company may wholly own a US single-member LLC, and the US LLC can remain a disregarded entity for federal tax purposes. When reportable transactions occur, Form 5472 identifies the foreign corporate owner, any ultimate indirect 25% foreign shareholder, and each related party. A separate Form 5472 is generally required for each related party with reportable transactions.**

Corporate ownership adds an ownership-chain exercise to the ordinary Form 5472 filing. The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) define a reporting corporation as a 25% foreign-owned U.S. corporation, including a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, or a foreign corporation engaged in a U.S. trade or business; the same instructions state that the initial penalty for failing to file Form 5472 when due is **$25,000**. ## Can a foreign company own a U.S. disregarded LLC?

Yes. A foreign company can be the sole owner of a domestic LLC, and the domestic LLC can still be disregarded for federal income tax purposes unless it elects corporate treatment. The [IRS single-member LLC page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/single-member-limited-liability-companies) says a single-member LLC is disregarded unless Form 8832 is filed to elect corporate treatment, and that a single-member LLC owned by a corporation should be reflected on the owner's federal tax return as a division of the corporation.

Form 5472 adds a limited reporting rule on top of that default classification. For tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2017, and ending on or after December 13, 2017, the IRS instructions treat a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity as separate from its owner and as a corporation only for section 6038A reporting. That special rule is why a disregarded LLC with a foreign corporate parent can still need a pro forma Form 1120 and Form 5472.

The U.S. LLC needs its own EIN for the Form 5472 package. The foreign parent does not simply write its home-country registration number where the LLC EIN belongs.

## Who is the direct owner and who is the ultimate owner?

The direct owner is the legal person that owns the U.S. LLC directly. The ultimate owner is the 25% foreign shareholder at the top of the indirect ownership chain, when ownership is attributed through one or more entities.

| Layer | Question | Form 5472 use |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. reporting entity | Which U.S. LLC is filing? | Part I name, address and EIN |
| Direct foreign owner | Which legal person directly owns the LLC? | Part II direct 25% foreign shareholder |
| Ultimate indirect owner | Which 25% foreign shareholder sits above the chain? | Part II ultimate indirect shareholder and attribution explanation |
| Transacting related party | Which related party had money, property or services move with the LLC? | Separate Form 5472 analysis |

If a foreign disregarded entity directly owns the U.S. LLC, the IRS instructions say to report the foreign disregarded entity as the direct owner. The ownership chain may still require ultimate-owner information and an attached explanation of attribution. Do not skip the intermediate company merely because the ultimate parent is more recognisable.

## Which identifiers does a foreign corporate owner provide?

A foreign corporate owner should be identified by legal name, jurisdiction, tax residence, address and taxpayer identifier. The Form 5472 identifier fields are not the place for a trade name, brand name or informal group label.

Collect these records before preparation:

1. Legal name in the country of organisation.
2. Registered address and principal business address.
3. Country of organisation and country of tax residence.
4. Foreign taxpayer identification number, if any.
5. U.S. EIN, if the parent already has one.
6. Consistent Form 5472 reference ID when required.
7. Ownership percentages through each entity in the chain.
8. Name and identifier data for every related party with reportable transactions.

The IRS instructions require a reference ID when certain foreign persons do not have a U.S. identifying number, and they require consistent use of that ID across Forms 5472 and future filings. A company-registration number can be used only if it is the correct identifier for the field or if it is deliberately incorporated into a reference ID format.

## Which corporate-group transactions are reportable?

Corporate ownership makes intercompany activity the core risk area. Review every transfer between the U.S. LLC and the foreign parent, plus every transfer with another related company in the group.

Reportable categories commonly include:

1. Capital contributions from the foreign parent.
2. Distributions from the LLC to the foreign parent.
3. Intercompany loans, repayments, interest and guarantees.
4. Management, engineering, technical or administrative services.
5. Rent, royalties, licences and intellectual-property transfers.
6. Inventory, equipment or asset sales.
7. Formation, acquisition, disposition and dissolution transactions.
8. Nonmonetary or less-than-full-consideration transfers.

Part IV contains monetary transaction categories for foreign related parties. Part V captures other transactions of a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, including formation and ownership events. Part VI can require a schedule for nonmonetary or below-market transactions with a foreign related party. Preserve intercompany agreements, invoices, loan schedules and exchange-rate support before finalising the form.

## How many Forms 5472 are required for a corporate group?

A separate Form 5472 is generally required for each related party with which the reporting corporation had reportable transactions. One foreign parent and two sister companies can therefore produce three Forms 5472 if all three had reportable transactions with the U.S. LLC during the year.

Original element: use a "party-by-party" matrix before drafting:

| Related party | Relationship to U.S. LLC | Transaction category | Separate Form 5472? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foreign parent | Direct 100% owner | Contribution, distribution, loan | Yes, if transactions occurred |
| Foreign sister company | Common control | Services, cost sharing, royalties | Yes, if transactions occurred |
| Ultimate holding company | Indirect owner | No direct transaction | Owner disclosure may apply; transaction form depends on activity |
| U.S. vendor | Third-party supplier | Ordinary vendor payments | Not a related-party Form 5472 transaction |

This matrix separates ownership disclosure from transaction reporting. The ultimate holding company may belong in Part II even if no money moved directly with the LLC. A sister company may need a separate Form 5472 because money or services moved, even if it owns no LLC interest.

## What changes if the parent elects corporate treatment for the LLC?

If the LLC files Form 8832 and elects to be treated as a corporation, the filing posture changes. The [IRS Form 8832 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8832) says an eligible entity uses Form 8832 to elect classification as a corporation, partnership or entity disregarded as separate from its owner.

A corporate election can move the LLC from a foreign-owned U.S. DE pro forma package to a real Form 1120 regime. Form 5472 may still apply to a 25% foreign-owned U.S. corporation with related-party transactions, but the return is no longer the same minimal pro forma 1120 package used by a disregarded entity.

The effective date matters. A midyear election can create one period as a disregarded entity and another period as a corporation. Do not treat Form 8832 as a clean-up tool for a year that was already subject to the foreign-owned U.S. DE rules.

For a calendar-year 2025 package, the ordinary due date is 15 April 2026 under the [IRS Form 1120 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120). Corporate elections and ownership charts should be resolved before the filing package is prepared.

## How is the corporate-owner package prepared?

The corporate-owner package is built from the entity chart and the intercompany ledger. Start with ownership, then prepare one transaction file for each related party.

1. Draw the legal ownership chain at the start and end of the year.
2. Identify direct, ultimate and transacting related parties.
3. Confirm whether the U.S. LLC stayed disregarded or made a classification election.
4. Reconcile intercompany accounts and convert foreign currency to U.S. dollars.
5. Prepare one Form 5472 per related party with reportable transactions.
6. Attach the forms to the U.S. LLC's pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC is a foreign-owned U.S. DE.
7. Include ownership-attribution, Part V and exchange-rate statements where needed.
8. Fax or mail the package to the dedicated IRS Ogden PIN Unit and retain evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the foreign parent need a U.S. EIN?

Not always solely because it owns the LLC. The U.S. LLC needs its own EIN. The foreign parent's U.S.-ID field is completed only if the parent has an applicable U.S. number.

### Is the parent company the related party?

Yes. A direct or indirect 25% foreign shareholder is a related party, and other companies in the ownership group may also meet the related-party rules.

### Does one Form 5472 cover the whole group?

Generally no. File a separate Form 5472 for each related party that had reportable transactions with the reporting entity.

### Must the ultimate owner be disclosed?

The form requests direct and ultimate indirect 25% foreign shareholders. Indirect ownership can require an attached explanation of attribution.

### Can the foreign parent consolidate the U.S. LLC's filing abroad?

No. Foreign consolidation does not replace the U.S. LLC's Form 5472 obligation. Local parent-country reporting is a separate question.

### Does a foreign corporate owner change the penalty?

No. The Form 5472 penalty is tied to the reporting corporation's failure to file, not to whether the foreign owner is an individual or company.

### Is a sister-company payment reportable?

It can be. If the sister company is a related party and had a reportable transaction with the U.S. LLC, a separate Form 5472 may be required.
