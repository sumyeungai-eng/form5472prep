**A foreign company may wholly own a US single-member LLC, and the US LLC can remain a disregarded entity for federal tax purposes. When reportable transactions occur, Form 5472 identifies the foreign corporate owner, any ultimate indirect 25% foreign shareholder, and each related party. A separate Form 5472 is generally required for each related party with reportable transactions.**

Corporate ownership adds an ownership-chain exercise to the ordinary transaction work. [Start the filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-foreign-corporate-owner) after gathering the foreign company's registration, tax number and shareholder chart.

## Can a foreign company own a US disregarded LLC?

Yes. A domestic eligible entity with one owner is generally disregarded unless it elects corporate treatment. The [Form 1120 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120) specifically state that a foreign person, including a foreign corporation, can wholly own a domestic disregarded entity covered by the limited section 6038A reporting rule.

The US LLC needs an EIN and files a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 when required. The foreign parent does not use the parent's foreign registration number as the LLC EIN.

## Who is the direct owner and who is the ultimate owner?

Map three layers:

| Layer | Question | Form 5472 significance |
|---|---|---|
| US reporting entity | Which US LLC is filing? | Part I name, address and EIN |
| Direct foreign owner | Which legal person directly owns the LLC? | Part II direct 25% foreign shareholder |
| Ultimate indirect owner | Who sits at the top of an indirect ownership chain? | Part II ultimate indirect shareholder and attribution explanation |

If a foreign disregarded entity directly owns the US LLC, the [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) say to report that foreign disregarded entity as the direct owner. The ownership chain may still require ultimate-owner information and an attached attribution explanation.

## Which identifiers does a foreign corporate owner provide?

Collect:

- legal name in the country of organisation;
- registered address;
- country of organisation and tax residence;
- foreign taxpayer identification number, if any;
- US EIN, if the parent already has one;
- consistent Form 5472 reference ID when required; and
- ownership percentages through each entity in the chain.

Do not substitute a trade name or company-register number without confirming it is the applicable foreign taxpayer identifier. If no FTIN exists, follow the “None” or “N/A” rule and reference-ID instructions.

## Which intercompany transactions are reportable?

Review every transfer between the US LLC and its parent or another related group member:

1. Capital contributions and distributions.
2. Intercompany loans, repayments, interest and guarantees.
3. Management, engineering, technical or administrative services.
4. Rent, royalties, licences and intellectual-property transfers.
5. Inventory or asset sales.
6. Formation, acquisition, disposition and dissolution transactions.

Part IV contains monetary transaction categories for foreign related parties. Part V captures other transactions of a foreign-owned US disregarded entity. Report gross categories and preserve the intercompany agreements and calculations.

## How many Forms 5472 are required?

The IRS instructions generally require a separate Form 5472 for each foreign or domestic related party with which the reporting corporation had reportable transactions. One parent and two transacting group companies can therefore produce three forms, even if the same ultimate owner controls all of them.

## How is the corporate-owner package prepared?

1. Draw the legal ownership chain at the start and end of the year.
2. Identify direct, ultimate and transacting related parties.
3. Reconcile intercompany accounts and convert foreign currency to USD.
4. Prepare one Form 5472 per related party.
5. Attach the forms to the US LLC's pro forma Form 1120.
6. Include ownership-attribution and Part V statements.
7. Fax or mail the package to the dedicated IRS Ogden PIN Unit and retain evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the foreign parent need a US EIN?

Not always solely because it owns the LLC. The US LLC needs its own EIN. The foreign parent's US-ID field is completed only if it has an applicable US number.

### Is the parent company the related party?

Yes. A direct or indirect 25% foreign shareholder is a related party, and other companies in the ownership group may also meet the related-party rules.

### Does one Form 5472 cover the whole group?

Generally no. File a separate Form 5472 for each related party that had reportable transactions with the US reporting entity.

### Must the ultimate owner be disclosed?

The form requests direct and ultimate indirect 25% foreign shareholders. Indirect ownership can require an attached explanation of attribution.

### Can the foreign parent consolidate the US LLC's filing abroad?

Foreign consolidation does not replace the US LLC's Form 5472 obligation. Local parent-country reporting is a separate question.
