**Affiliate commissions and ad-network payouts into your single-member US LLC are customer revenue and stay off Form 5472. What the form reports is money moving between the LLC and you: withdrawals, money you put in, site costs you paid personally, and a domain or site you handed the LLC. File for every year one of those happened.**

An affiliate or content business is unusually easy to run through a US LLC and unusually easy to mix up with yourself. The sites were often built before the LLC existed, the hosting renews on a card you have had for years, and a sponsor emails you rather than the company. None of that changes what Form 5472 asks for; all of it changes what your answer looks like.

The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that a **$25,000 penalty** will be assessed on a reporting corporation that fails to file when due and in the manner prescribed, and that a substantially incomplete form counts as a failure to file. ## Does an affiliate or content site's US LLC have to file Form 5472?

Yes, in almost every year it operated. A foreign-owned US single-member LLC files when three things are true: it is a disregarded entity for US tax purposes, its owner is a foreign person, and during the tax year it had at least one reportable transaction with that owner or another foreign related party.

Under **Treas. Reg. § 1.6038A-1**, for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2017 and ending on or after 13 December 2017, a foreign-owned US disregarded entity is treated as a corporation separate from its owner **solely** for the § 6038A reporting rules. A content LLC that owes no US corporate tax therefore still files a corporate-style information return.

The third condition is the one affiliates clear without noticing. Funding the bank account, letting a renewal hit your own card, or sending a month's commissions to your personal account each creates a reportable transaction, in a record year and in a year the sites earned nothing.

## Which affiliate and content-site money flows are reportable?

Sort every flow by who stands on the other side of it. Networks, sponsors, ad exchanges, hosts and tool vendors are unrelated counterparties. You are a related party, and so is any company you own or control.

| Money flow in an affiliate or content business | Counterparty | Reportable? | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affiliate network pays a month's commissions to the LLC's bank account | Unrelated network | No; business revenue | Nowhere on the form |
| Ad network or exchange pays display or video revenue to the LLC | Unrelated network | No; business revenue | Nowhere on the form |
| Sponsor pays the LLC a flat fee for a placement or newsletter slot | Unrelated sponsor | No; business revenue | Nowhere on the form |
| Network reverses or claws back a commission already paid | Unrelated network | No; revenue adjustment | Nowhere on the form |
| LLC pays hosting, CDN, email, rank tracking and writers from its own account | Unrelated vendors | No; operating expenses | Nowhere on the form |
| LLC sells one of its content sites to an unrelated buyer | Unrelated buyer | No; a sale to an outsider | Nowhere on the form |
| **You pay an LLC hosting or tool subscription on your personal card** | Foreign owner | **Yes** | Part V statement as a contribution or reimbursable amount, or Part IV "Amounts borrowed" if you book it as a loan |
| **You transfer a domain or a finished site to the LLC for nothing** | Foreign owner | **Yes** | Part V statement as a contribution |
| **LLC transfers money to your personal account** | Foreign owner | **Yes** | Part V statement as a distribution |
| **A sponsor pays you personally and you forward the money to the LLC** | Foreign owner, on the forwarding leg | **Yes** | Part V statement as a contribution or loan |
| **LLC lends you money, or you lend the LLC money** | Foreign owner | **Yes** | Part IV "Amounts loaned" or "Amounts borrowed" |
| **LLC pays interest to you on an owner loan** | Foreign owner | **Yes** | Part IV "Interest paid" |
| **LLC pays a content agency you own in your home country** | Foreign related party | **Yes** | Part IV as a payment for services or "Other amounts paid"; that party may need its own Form 5472 |

Part IV line numbers move between revisions of the form, so the table names categories instead. The Part V box, per the instructions, covers "any other transaction, as defined by Regulations section 1.482-1(i)(7) not already entered in Part IV," including "amounts paid or received in connection with the formation, dissolution, acquisition, and disposition of the entity, including contributions to, and distributions from, the entity," described on an attached statement. Our [Part V statement example](/blog/form-5472-part-v-statement-example) shows the format.

## Are affiliate commissions and ad revenue reportable because they are "commissions"?

No. Part IV has lines for commissions, and that wording catches people out, but every Part IV line reports an amount exchanged with a **foreign related party**. A commission an unrelated network pays your LLC for sending it buyers is customer revenue, and it never appears on the form however large it is.

- **Gross, net and banked are three numbers.** What the merchant booked, what the network kept and what hit the LLC's account all differ. None of them is a Form 5472 figure.
- **Whose account received it.** Keep the network account, the payout method and the bank account in the LLC's name. Revenue that lands on you first becomes an owner movement when you pass it on.
- **Holdbacks are not owner transactions.** A balance a network has not released is still unrelated-party money. Nothing is reportable because it sat in a dashboard over 31 December.

## Which card paid the subscription? The legacy-renewal problem

Whichever card paid it decides the answer, and in a content business the card is usually the wrong one. Hosting, a premium theme and a keyword tool were set up years before the LLC and still renew on a personal card. The bill is the LLC's cost, the money came from you, and that is a transaction between owner and LLC.

Those renewals are each reportable, and they are small enough that owners skip them and file a form that understates owner movements. Do three things instead:

1. Pull twelve months of statements for every card that touched a site expense, including cards you no longer use.
2. Mark each charge as the LLC's cost or genuinely personal, and keep the invoice naming the site or account.
3. Decide once, in writing, whether the total is a contribution, a loan or a reimbursable amount.

A reimbursement you pay yourself in a later year is a second reportable movement in that later year, not a cancellation of the first. [Owner loans, contributions and reimbursements](/blog/form-5472-owner-loans-contributions-reimbursements) sets out the categories.

## Is a domain or a site you contributed in kind reportable?

Yes. Handing the LLC a domain, a built-out site or an email list without being paid for it is a contribution to the entity, and the Part V statement covers contributions whether they arrive as cash or as property. Affiliates who registered domains personally and formed the LLC later do this routinely without recording it.

Two parts need care and neither is ours to decide. The value to attribute to the contribution is a valuation question for a qualified adviser, and your home-country treatment of giving an asset to a US entity is outside what we prepare. What we can say is that the movement belongs on the statement for the year the transfer happened, with the transfer agreement or registrar record kept alongside it.

The reverse direction is reportable too: a site the LLC owns that you move into your own name, or into another company of yours, is a distribution or a related-party transfer, not an internal reshuffle.

## What if a sponsor pays you personally instead of the LLC?

The sponsorship fee is still unrelated-party revenue, but your handling of it creates a reportable movement. A sponsor who pays your personal account because that is the invoice they had on file has not made a related-party payment; passing that money to the LLC afterwards is a contribution or a loan from you, and is reported as one.

The fix is upstream: invoice sponsorships in the LLC's name with the LLC's bank details, so the money never routes through you. Where it has already happened, record the date, amount and direction and keep both bank records with the sponsor's invoice.

Keeping a sponsorship fee personally and never passing it on creates no owner movement at all. That does not make the income disappear; it moves it out of the LLC's books and into a question for your own adviser.

## What happens on Form 5472 when you sell a content site?

Selling a site to an unrelated buyer is not a reportable transaction. The buyer is a third party, so the sale price, the escrow release and any earn-out stay off the form in the same way commissions do. What becomes reportable is the proceeds leaving: a transfer to your personal account is a distribution in the normal category.

- **Selling a site is not selling the LLC.** Selling membership interests changes the LLC's ownership, a different filing question handled in [Form 5472 and a change of ownership](/blog/form-5472-change-of-ownership).
- **Selling to yourself or your own company is not a third-party sale.** That is a related-party disposition and goes on the form.
- **A broker's or escrow fee is an unrelated expense**, a cost of the sale rather than an owner movement.

One commercial detail, not a tax one: Amazon's **Associates Program Operating Agreement** (updated October 15, 2025, checked 3 October 2026) states, "You may not assign this Agreement, by operation of law or otherwise, without our express prior written approval." A buyer therefore usually opens their own network accounts. That affects the deal, not the filing.

## What do the ad and affiliate platforms' own pages say about payouts?

We describe only what the companies' own pages state, checked 3 October 2026.

- **Google AdSense.** The [AdSense "Steps to getting paid" page](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1709858) says that "if your current balance reaches the payment threshold by the end of the month, and there are no other payment holds, you'll be issued a payment between the 21st and the 26th of the month." It does not state one universal payment threshold, pointing instead to a separate thresholds reference, so we state no threshold figure.
- **Amazon Associates.** The [Associates Program Operating Agreement](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement), updated October 15, 2025, refers to commission income "paid or payable to you" and sets out no payout mechanics on that page, so we describe none.

Neither page addresses Form 5472, and neither needs to: both describe a platform settling unrelated-advertiser money into an account you nominate. We state no commission rate or fee percentage for either company, because neither page we read gives one.

## When and how is the affiliate LLC's package filed?

For a calendar-year LLC, the 2025 package is generally due **15 April 2026**, and a timely Form 7004 filed under the special disregarded-entity instructions extends it. The package cannot be e-filed.

1. Build the owner-movement schedule: every transfer between the LLC and you, every site cost you paid personally, every in-kind contribution, each with date, direction, amount and category.
2. Convert non-USD amounts to US dollars and record the rate and its source.
3. Prepare Form 5472 with the Part V statement, and the **pro forma Form 1120** carrying "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" across the top of page 1, where only the entity's name and address and items B and E are required.
4. Sign and date the pro forma return.
5. Fax the package to the IRS on **855-887-7737**, or mail it to Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201.
6. Keep the fax receipt with the package. It is transmission evidence, not IRS acceptance of the return.

The [About Form 5472 page](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5472) links the current form and instructions. The $25,000 penalty also applies to a failure to maintain the records required by § 1.6038A-3, so the schedule behind the form matters as much as the form.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do affiliate commissions go on Form 5472?

No. A commission an unrelated network pays your LLC is customer revenue. Form 5472 reports transactions between the LLC and its foreign owner or other foreign related parties, not sales or commissions earned from outsiders.

### Is AdSense or ad-network revenue reportable?

No. Advertising revenue settled into the LLC's own account is third-party income. It becomes relevant to the form only once you move some of it between the LLC and yourself.

### I pay hosting on my personal card. Does that count?

Generally yes. Paying an LLC cost with personal money is a movement between owner and LLC. Record it as a contribution, loan or reimbursable amount, keep the invoice, and treat any later repayment separately.

### I gave the LLC a domain I already owned. Is that reportable?

Yes. A domain or site transferred to the LLC for no payment is a contribution to the entity and belongs on the Part V statement. The value to attribute is a question for a qualified adviser.

### A sponsor paid me personally. What do I report?

The sponsor's payment is not a related-party transaction. Forwarding the money to the LLC is, as a contribution or loan. Invoice future sponsorships in the LLC's name so the money reaches the company directly.

### I sold a site this year. Does the sale go on the form?

Not if the buyer is unrelated to you. The sale price stays off the form; withdrawing the proceeds to your personal account is the reportable distribution. A sale to your own company is a related-party disposition and is reported.

### My sites earned nothing this year. Do I still file?

Possibly. A renewal on your personal card, money you put in, or anything you took out is a reportable transaction in a zero-revenue year. A year with no owner movements at all may not require the form.
