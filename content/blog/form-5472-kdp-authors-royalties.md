**If a foreign-owned US single-member LLC receives your KDP and audiobook royalties, it files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 for every year it had a reportable transaction with you. The royalties themselves are revenue and stay off the form. What you move between yourself and the LLC is what gets reported.**

Self-publishing generates an unusual amount of tax paperwork for a one-person business: a tax interview in the publishing dashboard, a W-8 form, possible withholding, a year-end statement from each platform, and a separate annual filing for the LLC itself. The documents come from different senders and answer different questions.

The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state a **$25,000 penalty** on a reporting corporation that fails to file when due and in the manner prescribed, and that a substantially incomplete Form 5472 constitutes a failure to file. ## Does a self-published author's US LLC have to file Form 5472?

Yes, in most years. Three conditions trigger it: the LLC is a disregarded entity for US tax purposes, its owner is a foreign person, and during the tax year it had at least one reportable transaction with that owner or another foreign related party.

Under **Treas. Reg. § 1.6038A-1**, for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2017 and ending on or after 13 December 2017, a foreign-owned US disregarded entity is treated as a corporation separate from its owner **solely** for the § 6038A reporting rules. That is why an author's LLC owing no US corporate tax still files a corporate-style information return.

Authors clear the third condition almost immediately. A royalty payout you move to your personal account is a reportable transaction, as is money you put in to open the bank account or fund an advertising push. Whether the LLC owes US income tax is a separate question, covered in [does a foreign-owned LLC pay US tax](/blog/does-foreign-owned-llc-pay-us-tax).

## Are KDP and audiobook royalties reportable transactions?

No. A royalty paid to the LLC by a publishing platform or audiobook distributor is revenue from an unrelated counterparty, like any other customer payment. It is not a transaction between the LLC and its foreign owner, so none of it belongs on Form 5472 — not the gross figure, not the net figure, not the platform's deduction in between.

Moving a platform balance into the LLC's own bank account is not reportable either, because both accounts belong to the LLC. The reportable moment comes later: money leaving the LLC for you, or your money coming in for the LLC.

| Author money movement | Counterparty | Form 5472 treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Reader buys your ebook or paperback through a store | Unrelated reader | Not reportable; business revenue |
| Audiobook royalties credited to the LLC's distributor account | Unrelated distributor | Not reportable; business revenue |
| Tax withheld by a platform before payout | Unrelated withholding agent | Not reportable; it reduces cash, it is not an owner transaction |
| Platform payout lands in the LLC's bank account | The LLC's own funds | Not reportable; money stays inside the LLC |
| LLC pays an unrelated editor, cover designer or narrator | Unrelated vendor | Not reportable; operating expense |
| LLC transfers royalty money to your personal account | You, the foreign owner | **Reportable** (distribution, loan or payment) |
| You pay the LLC's editor or ad bill from personal funds | You, the foreign owner | **Reportable** (contribution, loan or reimbursable amount) |
| You send personal savings in to fund the LLC's ad budget | You, the foreign owner | **Reportable** (contribution or loan) |
| A royalty account in your own name pays you, and you forward it to the LLC | You, the foreign owner | **Reportable**; the forwarding transfer is an owner movement |
| LLC pays a publishing company you own abroad | Foreign related party | **Reportable**; may need its own Form 5472 |

Part V covers other transactions not entered in Part IV, including amounts paid or received in connection with the formation, dissolution, acquisition and disposition of the entity, described on an attached statement. For more worked categories, see [reportable transactions examples](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples).

## Which three obligations do authors keep confusing?

Three obligations sit behind one royalty stream, imposed by three different bodies. Mixing them up produces both panic and missed filings.

| Obligation | Who requires it | What it covers | What it does not cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| The platform's tax paperwork: the W-8BEN or W-8BEN-E behind the account, any withholding, and the year-end statement | The platform, as payer or withholding agent | Who beneficially owns the royalty, the status the platform documented, and the tax it withholds and reports on its own forms | The LLC's annual IRS filing. It reports no movement between you and the LLC, and it is not a return |
| The LLC's Form 5472 package: Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 | The IRS, under § 6038A and Treas. Reg. § 1.6038A-1 | Identifying information for the LLC and its foreign owner, plus money and property moving between them, in each year with a reportable transaction | Royalty revenue, platform fees, amounts withheld, or any computation of tax owed. It is an information return |
| Your own home-country return | The tax authority where you are resident | Whatever that authority asks of a resident earning business income abroad, which only a local adviser can tell you | Nothing in it satisfies the LLC's US filing, and that filing does not satisfy it |

We prepare the middle row. We take no position on the first, and we cannot tell you what your own country taxes — take that to an adviser qualified where you live.

## What do the IRS and the platforms say about royalty withholding?

Withholding is where authors get hurt, so here is only what the primary pages say. We checked each on **3 October 2026** and state nothing beyond it.

- **The IRS.** The [NRA withholding page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/nra-withholding) states that most types of US source income received by a foreign person are subject to US tax of 30%, and that a reduced rate, including exemption, may apply where a Code section provides a lower rate or a tax treaty exists between the foreign person's country of residence and the United States.
- **Amazon KDP.** KDP's own [Tax Withholding help page](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201274690), checked 3 October 2026, names the marketplaces whose royalty payments it treats as subject to **30% US tax withholding** — eBook sales on Amazon.com, and print book sales on Amazon.com, Amazon.co.jp, Amazon.com.au and Amazon.ca — and says you may be eligible for a reduced rate if your country of permanent residence has an income tax treaty with the United States. The page also addresses providing a tax identification number in the tax profile.
- **ACX.** ACX's [tax forms help article](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-tax-forms), checked 3 October 2026, states that non-US publishers have US source income reported annually on IRS Form 1042-S, issued on or before **March 15** each year, and that US publishers receive Form 1099-MISC on or before January 31. It states no withholding percentage, so we state none.

Two limits. We fetched no individual country's treaty rate, so any specific treaty percentage you have seen quoted elsewhere is **unverified** here; confirm it against the IRS treaty materials or with an adviser. And whether a particular marketplace's royalties are US-source income on your facts depends on circumstances we cannot assess for you.

What withholding never changes is the Form 5472 position. Tax deducted before a payout reduces the cash reaching the LLC; it is not a transaction between the LLC and its owner, and filing Form 5472 neither reduces nor recovers it.

## How does an author assemble the figures for the filing?

Build the file once a year, in this order.

1. Download the annual royalty or payout report from each platform and distributor, in the LLC's name.
2. Download each year-end tax statement the platforms issue, and file it with the reports — not with the Form 5472 package.
3. Export the LLC's bank and card statements for the same period, then reconcile gross royalties, platform deductions, withholding and net payouts to the deposits that arrived.
4. List every transfer between the LLC and your personal accounts, with date, direction and amount.
5. List every LLC cost you paid personally — a cover, a proofread, an ad budget, a subscription — with the invoice and payment proof.
6. Decide and write down whether each owner movement is a contribution, distribution, loan or reimbursement; recording the intention at the time is what makes the figure defensible later. See [paying yourself from a US LLC as a non-resident](/blog/pay-yourself-from-us-llc-non-resident).
7. Convert non-USD amounts to US dollars and record the rate and its source.
8. Keep the signed package, the Part V statement and the delivery receipt together.

The $25,000 penalty also reaches a failure to maintain the records required by **§ 1.6038A-3**, so step 8 is not optional housekeeping. A fuller list is in the [recordkeeping checklist](/blog/form-5472-recordkeeping-checklist).

## When and how is an author's LLC package filed?

For a calendar-year LLC the regular due date is generally **15 April**, and a timely Form 7004 under the special disregarded-entity instructions extends it.

The package is Form 5472 attached to a **pro forma Form 1120** with **"Foreign-owned U.S. DE"** across the top of page 1. Only the LLC's name and address and items **B and E** are required.

1. Prepare Form 5472 and the Part V statement from your owner-movement schedule.
2. Prepare the pro forma Form 1120 with the header, then sign and date it.
3. Fax the package to the IRS at **855-887-7737**, or mail it to Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201. A foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot file Form 5472 electronically.
4. Keep the fax confirmation: transmission evidence, not IRS acceptance of the return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do KDP royalties go on Form 5472?

No. Royalties paid to the LLC by a publishing platform are revenue from an unrelated counterparty. Form 5472 reports transactions between the LLC and its foreign owner or other foreign related parties, not sales or royalty income.

### Does the tax a platform withholds get reported on Form 5472?

No. Withholding reduces the cash the LLC receives and is reported on the platform's own year-end form. It is not a transaction between the LLC and its owner, so it is not a Form 5472 item.

### Will filing Form 5472 refund royalty withholding?

No. Form 5472 is an information return about related-party transactions. It computes no tax and claims no refund. Any question about recovering amounts withheld belongs with a qualified tax adviser.

### My royalty account is in my own name, not the LLC's. What then?

The royalties are then paid to you, and the transfer you make into the LLC is itself an owner movement to report. Fix the account names going forward, and record the forwarding transfers for the year already past.

### Do I file a separate Form 5472 for each platform?

No. The form is filed per foreign related party, not per platform. A sole foreign owner usually files one Form 5472 covering all ebook, print and audiobook income combined for the year.

### I earned almost nothing from my books this year. Do I still file?

Possibly. Funding the LLC, paying its bills personally or withdrawing money is a reportable transaction even with negligible royalties. A year with no owner movements at all may not require the form.

### Does my W-8BEN cover the LLC's annual filing?

No. A W-8 form goes to a payer or withholding agent and documents who owns the income. Form 5472 goes to the IRS and reports qualifying transactions between the LLC and foreign related parties.
