**Airbnb US LLC non-resident taxes and Form 5472 cover different things. Airbnb and Vrbo guest payouts are operating revenue, not Form 5472 transactions. But purchase and furnishing money you put into the LLC, cleaning costs you cover personally, and every draw back to yourself are generally reportable related-party transactions.**

A short-term rental creates booking payouts, cleaning charges, platform fees, furniture purchases, owner top-ups and draws. Form 5472 is not a duplicate profit-and-loss statement. It follows movements between a foreign owner and the US LLC.

The distinction has real consequences. The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) impose a **$25,000 penalty per form per year** for failure to file; a substantially incomplete form also counts as a failure. If the owner ledger is already reconciled, [start an accountant-reviewed Form 5472 filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-airbnb-short-term-rental-host) rather than treating platform revenue as the reporting total.

## What does Form 5472 report for a short-term rental LLC?

Form 5472 reports transactions between a foreign-owned US disregarded LLC and its foreign owner or another related party. It does not report every guest booking, platform fee, cleaner invoice or utility bill.

The counterparty is the first classification test. An unrelated guest pays for accommodation; Airbnb or Vrbo settles that revenue to the LLC. Those are operating transactions. The foreign owner who funds a deposit, buys beds personally, covers a cleaner before the next payout or withdraws cash is a related party.

The movement can be reportable even when it never touches the LLC bank. Paying an LLC furniture supplier or cleaner personally funds an LLC obligation. The books should classify the funding as a contribution, loan or reimbursable cost. Keep a later reimbursement as a separate gross movement.

Treasury Regulation §1.6038A-2 supplies the Part IV reportable-transaction framework. The Form 5472 instructions also direct a foreign-owned US disregarded entity to Part V for contributions, distributions and other entity transactions not entered in Part IV.

## Which Airbnb host cash flows belong on Form 5472?

Owner and related-party cash flows belong on Form 5472; ordinary dealings with guests, platforms and independent vendors generally do not. The payment rail does not change the counterparty.

| Short-term-rental movement | Counterparty | Usual Form 5472 treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Airbnb or Vrbo payout to LLC | Unrelated platform settling guest revenue | Not reportable merely because it is a payout |
| Guest-facing cleaning fee included in booking | Unrelated guest/platform | Revenue, not an owner transaction |
| LLC pays independent cleaner | Unrelated vendor | Ordinary operating cost, generally not reportable |
| Owner pays cleaner personally | Foreign owner funds LLC cost | Reportable owner-paid expense, contribution or loan |
| Owner wires purchase or furnishing cash | Foreign owner | Reportable contribution or loan |
| LLC transfers a draw to owner | Foreign owner | Reportable distribution or other payment |
| LLC pays owner's foreign management company | Foreign related company | Reportable on a separate Form 5472 for that party |

“Cleaning-fee float” needs careful wording. A guest cleaning charge is booking revenue. Cash the owner advances to pay the cleaner is an owner-to-LLC transaction.

For a deeper classification guide, see [owner loans, contributions, draws and reimbursements](/blog/form-5472-owner-loans-contributions-reimbursements).

## Are Airbnb and Vrbo payouts reported on Form 5472?

Airbnb and Vrbo payouts are not reported on Form 5472 when they settle bookings from unrelated guests. Gross booking revenue, platform deductions and the net deposit belong in the rental books and income-tax analysis, not the LLC's owner-transaction total.

Reconcile the platform report because guest charges can differ from bank deposits after adjustments, taxes, fees or refunds. A related-party issue arises only when, for example, the LLC pays its owner, sends a draw to the owner's account or pays a foreign company the owner controls.

## How is US short-term rental income taxed for a nonresident owner?

US real-property rental income of a nonresident alien is generally FDAP income taxed at **30% of gross income**, unless it is effectively connected with a US trade or business or the owner makes the section 871(d) election. The IRS explains the rule and election on its [nonresident real-property page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/nonresident-aliens-real-property-located-in-the-us).

A valid section 871(d) election treats income from US real property as effectively connected income. The owner files Form 1040-NR and may claim deductions attributable to the property, so tax is calculated on net rather than gross rental income. The IRS says the election continues for later years until revoked.

Form 1040-NR does not replace Form 5472. One reports the owner's income-tax position; the other reports the LLC's related-party transactions. Substantial guest services can require a more specific income-character analysis. Section 871(d), treaty, service-income and state-tax decisions need a qualified tax adviser.

## Can Airbnb withhold US tax when host information is missing?

Airbnb can withhold from payouts when required US taxpayer information is missing, and the platform says payouts can also be suspended. Airbnb's [US host tax-information guidance](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/122) distinguishes W-9 information from W-8 forms and tells hosts to provide the form appropriate to their status.

Do not submit a W-9 merely because the LLC was formed in the United States. A disregarded LLC and its foreign owner require classification-specific analysis. Withholding on a payout statement is not a Form 5472 transaction and does not satisfy the LLC's filing duty.

## Do occupancy and lodging taxes change Form 5472?

Occupancy and lodging taxes are separate city or state obligations and do not change which owner transactions belong on Form 5472. Airbnb says it [automatically collects and remits certain taxes](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1036) but hosts may need to handle others manually. Platform collection is listing-specific, so check the platform settings, listing address and relevant local rules, and keep platform-collected tax separate in the books.

## How does the worked example reach Part V, Part IV and line 1f?

The worked example produces a **$210,000 Part V total** and no Part IV amount on the stated facts. The example is illustrative, uses the figures specified for this guide and assumes one foreign individual owner, one LLC, a full calendar year and no repayment of the furnishing cost.

| Illustrative annual item | Arithmetic | Form 5472 result |
|---|---:|---|
| Purchase contribution | **$180,000** | Part V attached statement: owner contribution/acquisition funding |
| Furniture paid personally | **$12,000** | Part V: owner-paid LLC cost treated as a contribution |
| Airbnb/Vrbo payouts | **$2,500 × 12 = $30,000** | Not reportable; unrelated platform revenue |
| Monthly owner draws | **$1,500 × 12 = $18,000** | Part V: distributions from LLC to owner |
| Part V total | **$180,000 + $12,000 + $18,000 = $210,000** | Included in line 1f for that foreign related party |

Part IV is **zero in this example** because no listed monetary category was paid to or received from the foreign owner beyond the Part V owner movements. Line 1f is **$210,000**, not the **$30,000** of platform payouts and not a net number of contributions minus draws. If the LLC reimbursed the **$12,000** furniture purchase, that repayment would be another owner movement and the facts would need to be updated.

Line 1h would also be **$210,000** if this were the LLC's only Form 5472. A foreign management company related to the owner would require another form and change the all-forms total.

## How should an Airbnb host build the filing records?

Build the annual file from platform reports, LLC statements and the owner's personal payments. A payout export cannot identify personally funded costs or later draws.

1. **Confirm the entity.** Identify each single-member LLC, its owner, tax year and whether it elected corporate treatment.
2. **Export platform activity.** Reconcile bookings, guest charges, refunds, taxes, fees and net payouts to the LLC bank.
3. **Review owner funding.** List purchase wires, deposits, furnishing money, repair top-ups and loans by date and source currency.
4. **Search personal accounts.** Identify furniture, cleaning, utilities, repairs or supplies paid for the LLC by the owner.
5. **List every draw.** Capture transfers to personal bank accounts, owner cards or expenses paid for the owner's benefit.
6. **Identify other related parties.** Separate the owner from any foreign management, cleaning or holding company under common control.
7. **Classify without netting.** Record direction, description and US-dollar amount for each contribution, loan, reimbursement, service payment and distribution.
8. **Tie the totals to the form.** Map listed related-party categories to Part IV, other disregarded-entity movements to the Part V statement and the total to line 1f.

The [Part V statement example](/blog/form-5472-part-v-statement-example) shows the attachment format and why gross inflows and outflows should remain visible.

## What do four short-term-rental host scenarios look like?

Four scenarios, worked through, show why the operating model matters.

### Scenario 1: A nonresident runs one condo

The owner funds the purchase and furnishings, receives booking payouts and takes monthly draws. Purchase funding, furnishing costs and every draw are reportable owner transactions. Platform revenue stays off Form 5472. Form 1040-NR and section 871(d) remain separate.

### Scenario 2: One owner operates multiple properties

Several listings inside one LLC do not make each payout a Form 5472 item. Reconcile revenue by property, then aggregate the LLC's owner transactions. Separate foreign-owned LLCs need separate filing analyses and records.

### Scenario 3: The LLC co-hosts properties owned by others

Co-host fees from unrelated owners are likely services income rather than rent from property the host owns. Those receipts generally stay off Form 5472. Payment from the LLC to its foreign owner for co-hosting work is a related-party transaction; the income-tax character needs adviser review.

### Scenario 4: The LLC closes for winter

Winter inactivity does not erase transactions from the rest of the tax year. Summer payouts remain operating revenue, while earlier top-ups and autumn draws remain owner transactions. A truly inactive LLC with no reportable transaction for the entire year has a different filing analysis from a seasonal LLC with zero winter bookings.

## When and how does the LLC file the Form 5472 package?

A calendar-year foreign-owned US disregarded entity files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120. For the **2025 tax year**, the due date was **15 April 2026**, or **15 October 2026** when Form 7004 was filed by the regular due date.

Part II identifies the foreign owner. Enter the owner's FTIN on line 4b(3). If no FTIN exists, enter “None” or “N/A”; do not leave the field blank. When line 4b(1) has no US identifying number, line 4b(2) requires a reference ID, and the same reference ID should be used each year.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Airbnb payouts go on Form 5472?

No, not when they settle bookings from unrelated guests. Reconcile gross booking activity to net deposits, but keep platform revenue outside the owner-transaction total.

### Is a guest cleaning fee reportable on Form 5472?

The guest-facing cleaning fee is booking revenue, not an owner transaction. A cleaner's bill paid personally by the foreign owner is different and generally belongs in the related-party records.

### Are furniture purchases paid personally reportable?

Generally yes when the furniture is for the LLC's property. Record the payment as a contribution, loan or reimbursable owner-paid cost, consistent with the books.

### Does Airbnb withholding replace Form 5472?

No. Platform withholding concerns tax collected from a payout. Form 5472 separately reports transactions between the LLC and its foreign owner or another related party.

### Does Form 1040-NR replace Form 5472?

No. Form 1040-NR reports the nonresident owner's US income-tax position. Form 5472 reports the LLC's related-party transactions. A host may need both.

### Does a winter-dormant Airbnb LLC still file?

It can. Seasonal inactivity does not erase purchase funding, owner-paid costs or draws elsewhere in the year. Review the full tax year, not only months with bookings.

### Does each Airbnb property need a separate Form 5472?

The filing follows the legal entity, not the listing. Multiple listings in one LLC share that entity's analysis; properties in separate LLCs require separate entity-by-entity reviews.
