**A nonresident individual who owns a disregarded US single-member LLC generally gives Form W-8BEN, not Form W-9. If a foreign company owns the disregarded LLC, that owner generally gives Form W-8BEN-E. The owner’s classification governs, while Form 5472 remains a separate annual filing for qualifying related-party transactions.**

The confusion is understandable. The LLC was formed in a US state, has an EIN, and may have a US bank account. A platform then asks for “your US tax form” and presents W-9 as the obvious choice. None of those facts makes a nonresident alien a US person or turns a disregarded LLC into a separate beneficial owner for withholding documentation.

The [IRS Instructions for Form W-8BEN](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben) say foreign persons can face **30% withholding on specified US-source income** and say the single owner of a disregarded entity is treated as the beneficial owner of income the entity receives. ## Is W-8BEN or W-9 correct for a foreign-owned LLC?

The correct form usually follows the tax status of the disregarded LLC’s owner, not the LLC’s state of formation or EIN. Start by confirming three facts: whether the LLC has one owner, whether it elected corporate tax treatment, and whether the owner is an individual or an entity.

| Situation presented to the payer | Form generally supplied | Name that governs |
|---|---|---|
| Nonresident alien individually owns a disregarded single-member LLC | W-8BEN | Foreign individual owner |
| Foreign corporation or other foreign entity owns a disregarded single-member LLC | W-8BEN-E | Foreign entity owner |
| US citizen, resident alien, or other US person is the payee | W-9 | US person |
| LLC elected to be taxed as a corporation | Reassess; do not use the disregarded-entity shortcut | Entity classification after election |
| Income is effectively connected with a US trade or business | W-8ECI may be relevant | Beneficial owner and income type |
| Payer asks about an employee or independent personal services | Reassess the payment and form set | Facts may point to another withholding form |

The table is a routing tool, not permission to ignore the payer’s onboarding questions. A withholding agent may ask for supporting documents or a substitute form. The legal classification and the income type still control the answer.

## Why does the owner’s form govern a disregarded LLC?

A disregarded entity is ignored as separate from its owner for the relevant federal tax classification. The [W-8BEN instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben) expressly say the owner of a disregarded entity, rather than the entity itself, submits the appropriate documentation for Chapters 3 and 4. They also say a nonresident alien who is the single owner is considered the beneficial owner of income received by the entity.

That produces a simple two-layer test:

1. **Classification layer:** Is the LLC still disregarded for federal tax purposes? Confirm that no corporate election changed the default treatment.
2. **Owner layer:** Is the beneficial owner a foreign individual, a foreign entity, or a US person?
3. **Payment layer:** Is the payment ordinary foreign-owned business income, specified US-source income subject to withholding, or effectively connected income?
4. **Documentation layer:** Give the payer the form matching the owner and payment, then retain the accepted version and any treaty support.
5. **Information-return layer:** Separately review money and property moving between the LLC and its foreign owner for Form 5472.

An EIN does not resolve any of those layers. It identifies the LLC for federal administration. It is not a certificate that the beneficial owner is a US person.

## When does an individual owner use Form W-8BEN?

A nonresident alien individual generally uses W-8BEN to establish foreign status and beneficial ownership, and to claim an applicable treaty rate when the requirements are met. The form goes to the payer, platform, bank, or withholding agent that requested it. It is not sent to the IRS by the owner.

Use the individual’s legal name, country of citizenship, permanent residence address, mailing address if different, and foreign tax identifying number where the instructions require it. A treaty claim depends on the owner’s country of tax residence and the relevant income article; the LLC’s formation state does not create treaty eligibility.

Do not select W-8BEN merely because the form is shorter. It is the individual form. A foreign company, partnership, trust, or other entity generally belongs on the W-8BEN-E path, with classification questions that do not appear on W-8BEN.

## When does a foreign entity owner use Form W-8BEN-E?

A foreign entity that owns the disregarded LLC generally provides W-8BEN-E. The [IRS Instructions for Form W-8BEN-E](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8bene) state that a disregarded entity generally does not submit the form itself; its owner supplies the appropriate documentation, such as W-8BEN-E when the owner is a foreign entity.

W-8BEN-E is more involved because it documents both Chapter 3 status and Chapter 4, or FATCA, status. The answer can depend on whether the owner is an operating company, financial institution, passive entity, government, charity, or another category. Do not copy an “active NFFE” selection from another business without testing the actual facts.

The instructions also allow the disregarded LLC’s name to be noted for account-matching purposes. Depending on the circumstances, the LLC name may appear on line 3 or as referencing information. That does not make the LLC the beneficial owner; it helps the withholding agent associate the foreign owner’s certification with the account held in the LLC name.

## Why should a nonresident owner avoid Form W-9?

Form W-9 is for a US person. The [IRS Instructions for Form W-9](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw9) say a foreign person may not provide it and should be directed to the appropriate W-8 form. A valid W-9 includes certifications signed under penalties of perjury.

Giving W-9 because an online menu rejects W-8BEN is not a harmless workaround. The owner would be certifying US-person status, creating records inconsistent with nonresident status and potentially prompting US information reporting under the wrong name and TIN.

Pause and escalate the onboarding issue instead. Ask the payer whether it can document a foreign beneficial owner behind a US disregarded entity, whether it accepts a substitute W-8 workflow, and where the LLC’s legal name should be placed for account matching. Preserve the payer’s written response.

## How do withholding forms differ from Form 5472?

W-8BEN, W-8BEN-E, and W-9 document a payee for the person making or reporting a payment. Form 5472 reports certain transactions between a US reporting corporation, including a foreign-owned US disregarded entity, and foreign related parties. One form does not replace the other.

| Question | W-8BEN / W-8BEN-E / W-9 | Form 5472 package |
|---|---|---|
| Who receives it? | Payer, platform, bank, or withholding agent | IRS Ogden PIN Unit |
| What does it establish? | Payee identity, status, and sometimes treaty claim | Related-party transactions and identifying information |
| Does customer revenue appear? | The form can document the beneficial owner of income | Unrelated customer revenue is not itself reportable |
| Do owner contributions and draws appear? | Not transaction schedules on the W-form | Generally reviewed and reported for a foreign-owned DE |
| Is it annual? | Updated when requested, expired, or facts change | Filed for each tax year with a reportable transaction |

Under the [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472), failure to file when required carries a **$25,000 penalty per form per year**, and a substantially incomplete form counts as failure to file. After the IRS gives notice and the failure continues beyond 90 days, another $25,000 applies for each 30-day period or fraction under §6038A(d)(2).

For Part II, use the owner’s FTIN on line 4b(3). If none exists, enter “None” or “N/A”; do not leave it blank. A reference ID on line 4b(2) is required whenever line 4b(1) has no US identifying number, and the same reference ID must be used each year.

## What do four common W-form scenarios look like?

Four scenarios, worked through, show why “US LLC equals W-9” fails.

### Scenario 1: Nonresident consultant owns the LLC personally

A consultant lives and performs work outside the United States through a disregarded Wyoming LLC. A client asks for tax documentation. The individual owner generally supplies W-8BEN. Client revenue does not become a Form 5472 transaction, but funding, drawings, loans, and owner-paid costs remain part of the Form 5472 review.

### Scenario 2: Foreign company owns the LLC

A non-US corporation is the single owner of a disregarded Delaware LLC. A bank asks for the payee’s form. The foreign company generally supplies W-8BEN-E and can identify the disregarded LLC for account matching as the instructions permit. Transactions between the US LLC and the foreign parent are separately tested for Form 5472.

### Scenario 3: Platform insists that an EIN requires W-9

An ecommerce platform sees a US EIN and displays only W-9. The foreign individual should not certify US-person status. The owner asks for the platform’s foreign-owner or disregarded-entity process, provides W-8BEN through that channel, and retains the acceptance record.

### Scenario 4: LLC made a corporate election

A founder previously filed an entity-classification election. The disregarded-entity shortcut may no longer apply, so the owner checks the election’s effective date and the payer’s requirements before submitting any form. The Form 5472 obligation may also continue under the rules for a 25%-foreign-owned domestic corporation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a US LLC always give Form W-9?

No. A disregarded single-member LLC generally follows its owner. A nonresident individual owner generally gives W-8BEN, while a foreign entity owner generally gives W-8BEN-E.

### Can a nonresident alien sign Form W-9 for an LLC?

Not merely because the LLC has a US EIN. W-9 certifies US-person status under penalties of perjury. A foreign owner should use the appropriate W-8 process unless the facts establish US-person status.

### Does an individual owner use W-8BEN-E?

Generally no. W-8BEN is the individual form. W-8BEN-E is generally for foreign entities and includes additional Chapter 4 classification questions.

### Where does the disregarded LLC name go?

The beneficial owner’s name governs. W-8BEN-E instructions permit the disregarded entity’s name on line 3 or as referencing information in specified circumstances so the payer can match the form to the account.

### Does W-8BEN eliminate all US withholding?

No. It documents foreign status and can support a valid treaty claim. The actual rate depends on income type, source, treaty eligibility, and the completed form.

### Does a W-8 form replace Form 5472?

No. The W-8 goes to a payer or withholding agent. Form 5472 goes to the IRS and reports qualifying transactions between the LLC and foreign related parties.

### What if the LLC elected corporate tax treatment?

Reassess the payee classification before using the owner-form shortcut. Confirm the election’s effective date and obtain advice on the corporation’s withholding documentation and Form 5472 position.
