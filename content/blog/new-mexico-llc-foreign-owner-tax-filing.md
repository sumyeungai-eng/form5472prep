**A New Mexico LLC has no state annual report, but that does not remove its federal filing duties. A foreign-owned single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 after any owner transaction. It must also keep a New Mexico registered agent and assess state tax nexus.**

New Mexico is often marketed to non-residents as a private, low-maintenance formation state. The pitch has a sound core: the state does not require an LLC annual report, and the public formation record need not work like a detailed annual ownership return. But “no annual report” is easily misheard as “no annual requirements.”

The federal rule is separate. The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that failure to file can trigger a **$25,000 penalty per form per year**. If your New Mexico LLC received money from you, paid money to you, or dealt with another foreign related party, [start a reviewed filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=new-mexico-llc-foreign-owner-tax-filing) before treating the company as maintenance-free.

## What must a non-resident New Mexico LLC owner do annually?

A non-resident owner should separate four different compliance tracks: New Mexico entity maintenance, New Mexico tax nexus, federal Form 5472, and any separate federal income-tax return. One track does not replace another.

| Requirement | Typical foreign-owned single-member LLC | Trigger or timing |
|---|---|---|
| New Mexico LLC annual report | None | New Mexico does not prescribe an annual LLC report |
| New Mexico registered agent | Required continuously | Keep an agent and New Mexico street address on record |
| New Mexico gross receipts registration and returns | Facts determine the result | New Mexico physical or economic nexus and taxable receipts |
| Form 5472 with pro forma Form 1120 | Usually required after an owner or related-party transaction | Federal return due date |
| Owner's US income-tax return | Separate analysis | US-source or effectively connected income may create a filing duty |

The practical lesson is simple: a clean New Mexico entity record does not prove IRS compliance, and a filed Form 5472 does not settle state tax or the owner's income tax.

## Why does New Mexico have no LLC annual report?

New Mexico's LLC law does not create the periodic report imposed on business corporations. The [Secretary of State's official statute index](https://www.sos.nm.gov/business-services/statutes-governing-business-in-nm/) lists “Corporate Reports” and the “Limited Liability Companies” Act as separate chapters. An LLC therefore does not file the corporation report merely because both entities use the same online portal.

That makes New Mexico low maintenance at the entity level. It also explains the privacy marketing: there is no annual LLC report that republishes a fresh manager or member list each year. Privacy is not anonymity, however. Banks, payment processors, the IRS, courts, and tax agencies can still require ownership information. The LLC must also maintain a registered agent for service of process.

With no annual report, there is no annual-report fee to quote. You should still log into the Secretary of State portal periodically, confirm the company remains active, and update the registered agent or address when facts change. Agent charges are private contract prices, not state fees, so they vary by provider.

## Does a New Mexico LLC owe franchise or gross receipts tax?

A typical single-member LLC disregarded for federal income-tax purposes does not owe a separate New Mexico LLC franchise tax merely for existing. New Mexico's corporate income and franchise framework reaches an LLC when it is taxed as a corporation, so an entity that elected corporate treatment needs a different analysis.

Gross receipts tax turns on activity and nexus, not the words “New Mexico LLC” on a certificate. The [New Mexico Taxation and Revenue Department's nexus guidance](https://www.tax.newmexico.gov/businesses/determining-nexus/) identifies physical connections such as a business location, property, employees, agents, services, or equipment in New Mexico. It also explains that an out-of-state business can create economic nexus through New Mexico-sourced taxable receipts.

Do not assume that a registered agent creates the same tax result as an office, employee, inventory, or New Mexico customer base. Conversely, forming elsewhere does not protect a business that actually operates in New Mexico. Registration, sourcing, deductions, marketplace collection, and return frequency require a state-tax review based on the business's facts.

## How does New Mexico compare with Wyoming and Delaware?

New Mexico has the lightest recurring entity filing in this three-state comparison, but the formation state does not change Form 5472.

| State | Recurring LLC state filing or tax | Federal Form 5472 effect |
|---|---|---|
| New Mexico | No LLC annual report; annual-report cost **$0** | None |
| Wyoming | Annual report and license tax; **$60 minimum** | None |
| Delaware | No LLC annual report; **$400 annual LLC tax (from tax year 2026; $300 for 2025)** | None |

The Wyoming figure was checked against the Wyoming Secretary of State's current fee guidance. Delaware's Division of Corporations now confirms a **$400** annual LLC tax on its [LLC tax instructions](https://corp.delaware.gov/alt-entitytaxinstructions/). State amounts change; check the state page again when payment is due.

New Mexico can be attractive when the owner values a small recurring state workload. Wyoming may suit an owner who accepts an annual report. Delaware is commonly chosen for legal and investor reasons rather than minimum recurring cost. None is automatically “best” for tax: where people, property, inventory, and customers are located can matter more than the formation certificate.

## When does a foreign-owned New Mexico LLC file Form 5472?

A New Mexico single-member LLC wholly owned by a non-US person generally files Form 5472 when it has a reportable transaction with its foreign owner or another foreign related party. The filing is attached to a limited pro forma Form 1120 even though the LLC is disregarded for most federal income-tax purposes.

Common triggers include:

- the owner's initial or later capital contribution;
- an owner draw or distribution;
- a loan between the owner and LLC;
- an LLC bill paid from the owner's personal account;
- a reimbursement between the LLC and owner; or
- goods, services, rent, royalties, or other dealings with a foreign related company.

Customer revenue and payments to unrelated vendors are not included merely because they pass through the LLC bank account. The counterparty and relationship control the Form 5472 analysis. The formation year is easy to miss because opening deposits and formation expenses can create reportable transactions before the business makes a sale.

The **2025 tax-year** filing for a calendar-year LLC was due **15 April 2026**. A timely Form 7004 extends the filing deadline to **15 October 2026**. An extension moves the due date; it does not convert owner transfers into non-reportable activity.

## What identifiers and filing details does the IRS require?

The LLC needs an EIN, and the foreign owner needs complete Part II identification. On line 4b(3), enter the owner's foreign tax identifying number. If none exists, enter “None” or “N/A”; do not leave the field blank. If line 4b(1) has no US identifying number, line 4b(2) requires a reference ID. Keep that reference ID unchanged in later years.

The filing process is:

1. Reconcile every LLC account and identify owner or related-party movements.
2. Complete the pro forma Form 1120 and write **“Foreign-owned U.S. DE”** across the top.
3. Complete one Form 5472 for each reportable foreign related party, including Part V support for transactions not captured in Part IV.
4. Convert reportable amounts to US dollars using a consistent, documented method.
5. Fax the signed package at **300 DPI or higher** to **855-887-7737**, or mail it to Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201.
6. Keep the complete package, source records, and timestamped fax confirmation.

A foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot e-file this package. A substantially incomplete Form 5472 counts as a failure to file. After the IRS sends a notice, a continuing failure beyond the stated 90-day period can add **$25,000 for each 30-day period or fraction** under IRC §6038A(d)(2).

## What do four New Mexico LLC scenarios look like?

Four scenarios, worked through, show why “no annual report” is not a complete compliance answer.

### Scenario 1: A consultant operating entirely from abroad

Leila forms a New Mexico LLC, contributes **$2,500**, serves clients outside New Mexico, and withdraws **$18,000** during the year. No New Mexico annual report is due. The contribution and draw are owner transactions for Form 5472; client receipts are not related-party transactions. New Mexico gross receipts nexus still deserves a facts check, especially if customers or services connect to the state.

### Scenario 2: A dormant LLC with one opening deposit

Mateo contributes **$1,000** to open the bank account, pays one unrelated formation vendor, and earns no revenue. The LLC's dormant label does not erase the contribution. Form 5472 is generally due because money moved from the owner to the LLC. No New Mexico annual report is due.

### Scenario 3: An online seller with New Mexico inventory

Aya runs the store abroad but stores inventory in New Mexico. Property in the state can create physical nexus, so gross receipts registration and sourcing need attention. Form 5472 separately reports owner funding and distributions, not ordinary customer sales or unrelated fulfilment charges.

### Scenario 4: An LLC owned through a foreign company

A foreign holding company owns the New Mexico LLC and also provides management services. The holding company is both owner and related-party service provider. The LLC may need to report contributions, payments, and service transactions, with a separate Form 5472 for each distinct reportable related party.

## How does an original New Mexico compliance map work?

Use a two-axis map: **who received or sent the money**, and **where the activity occurred**. It prevents state and federal questions from being mixed together.

Suppose an owner contributes **$4,000**, the LLC receives **$42,000** from unrelated customers, pays **$9,000** to unrelated vendors, and distributes **$12,000** to the owner. Separately, a contractor performs work in New Mexico.

The Form 5472 working total is **$16,000** of owner movements: **$4,000 contribution + $12,000 distribution**. The **$42,000** customer revenue and **$9,000** unrelated vendor spend remain bookkeeping items, not owner transactions. The New Mexico contractor does not change those Form 5472 amounts, but the in-state activity creates a separate nexus question.

This map is useful because one bank statement can support two reviews without forcing every line onto one form.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a New Mexico LLC have to file an annual report?

No. New Mexico does not require an LLC annual report. The LLC must still maintain a registered agent, update changed information when necessary, and handle any state tax registrations created by its activity.

### Does a foreign-owned New Mexico LLC need Form 5472?

Usually, if a wholly foreign-owned disregarded LLC had a transaction with its owner or another foreign related party. Initial funding, owner draws, loans, reimbursements, and owner-paid costs are common triggers.

### Is there a New Mexico LLC franchise tax?

A typical federally disregarded single-member LLC does not owe a separate LLC franchise tax merely for existing. An LLC taxed as a corporation, or an LLC with New Mexico business activity, needs a classification-specific state review.

### Does a registered agent create New Mexico tax nexus?

A registered agent alone is not the same as an operating office. Employees, property, services, agents, and New Mexico-sourced taxable receipts can affect nexus. Review the business's real activity rather than relying on its formation address.

### Does a zero-income New Mexico LLC file Form 5472?

It can. Form 5472 reports related-party transactions, not profit. An opening deposit, formation expense paid personally, or later owner withdrawal can create a filing requirement even when revenue is zero.

### Can a New Mexico LLC file Form 5472 online?

No. A foreign-owned US disregarded entity submits Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 by fax or mail to the IRS Ogden PIN Unit. Keep proof of delivery.

### Is New Mexico better than Wyoming for a non-resident?

New Mexico has no LLC annual report, while Wyoming has an annual report and license tax. The better state depends on cost, operations, legal needs, and nexus. Both have the same federal Form 5472 rules.
