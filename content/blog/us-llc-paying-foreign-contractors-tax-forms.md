**A U.S. LLC does not put every payment to a foreign contractor on Form 5472. First identify the payee and whether that person is related to the LLC, then document where the services were physically performed. The W-8 or W-9, Form 1099, Form 1042-S, withholding, and Form 5472 questions are separate checks.**

An unrelated designer working entirely in Colombia is a different case from the owner's foreign development company or a consultant who works partly in New York. Payment route, currency, and bank location do not decide the source of service income.

## Start with the worker and the payment

Before choosing a tax form, record:

1. the contractor's legal name and whether the payee is an individual or entity;
2. the contractor's U.S. or foreign tax status;
3. whether the contractor is related to the LLC or its owner;
4. whether the relationship is genuinely contractor rather than employee;
5. what the payment buys—services, intellectual-property rights, goods, or a mixture; and
6. where the work was physically performed.

The IRS says worker classification depends on the full relationship, including behavioral control, financial control, and the parties' relationship—not merely the contract label ([IRS worker-classification guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee)). An employment relationship can create payroll obligations outside this guide.

If a software agreement includes both development work and a continuing license, separate the components instead of treating the whole invoice as contractor services. The [related-party royalties guide](/blog/form-5472-royalties-license-fees-intellectual-property) explains why an IP payment can have different sourcing and withholding rules.

## Which W-form belongs in the file?

[IRS Publication 515](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p515) says a Form W-9 generally documents a U.S. payee, while a foreign payee generally provides the appropriate form in the W-8 series. A foreign individual commonly uses Form W-8BEN; a foreign entity commonly uses Form W-8BEN-E. Other forms can apply—for example, Form W-8ECI for qualifying effectively connected income or Form 8233 for certain personal-service treaty claims.

The form must match the actual payee and payment. Do not ask a foreign individual to sign Form W-9 merely because the payer is a U.S. LLC. The contractor gives Form W-8BEN to the payer or withholding agent, not directly to the IRS ([IRS W-8BEN instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben)).

The site's [W-8BEN versus W-9 guide](/blog/w8ben-vs-w9-foreign-owned-llc) focuses on a foreign-owned LLC as payee. Here the LLC is the payer, but the same core rule applies: identify the beneficial owner rather than choosing a form from the bank account or invoice header alone.

## Where was the work actually done?

For personal services, the place of performance generally determines source. It does not matter where the contract was signed, where payment was sent, or where the payer resides. When services are performed partly inside and partly outside the United States, the payer needs an accurate allocation based on the facts; a time basis is often appropriate ([IRS Publication 515](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p515)).

Ask for a signed work-location statement covering the service period, supported by calendars, travel records, project logs, or another reasonable record. “Remote” is not a country. A contractor who normally lives abroad but spends part of the project working in the United States creates a mixed-location review.

## Decision matrix: four separate reporting tracks

| Hypothetical facts | Documentation starting point | Form 5472 | Other U.S. reporting check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unrelated foreign individual performs all design work in Colombia | W-8BEN plus work-location evidence | Generally no, solely for this vendor payment | Foreign-source services may fall outside NRA withholding and Form 1042-S, but confirm the facts |
| Unrelated U.S. person provides marketing services | W-9 | Generally no, solely for this vendor payment | Review current Form 1099-NEC rules and threshold |
| Foreign company controlled by the LLC owner provides development abroad | Appropriate entity W-8 documentation, ownership map, contract, invoices | Potentially yes as related-party service consideration | Perform a separate sourcing and withholding review |
| Foreign individual performs part of a consulting project in the United States | Appropriate W-8 documentation and location allocation | Depends on relationship, not location alone | U.S.-source personal services can create withholding and Forms 1042/1042-S issues; obtain specialist advice before payment |

The matrix is a triage tool, not a final return determination. A valid W-8 documents status; it does not establish work location, worker classification, treaty eligibility, or the character of every payment.

## When does Form 5472 enter the picture?

Form 5472 is a related-party information return. The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) define related parties through ownership and specified relationship rules and include qualifying service consideration among reportable transactions. Therefore:

- payment to an unrelated foreign freelancer generally stays outside Form 5472 solely because it crosses a border;
- payment to the foreign owner for services can be reportable;
- payment to a foreign company controlled by the owner can be reportable on a separate Form 5472; and
- reporting an amount on Form 5472 does not satisfy any withholding or information-return obligation.

Use the [reportable-transactions examples](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples) to separate ordinary vendor expenses from owner activity. For controlled providers, use the [related-party services guide](/blog/form-5472-related-party-services-management-fees).

## What about Form 1099-NEC, Form 1042-S, and withholding?

Do not apply a single “foreign contractor” answer. Publication 515 says foreign persons who provide a valid Form W-8 are exempt from Form 1099 reporting and backup withholding. Form 1099-NEC should instead be reviewed for a U.S. nonemployee or when claimed foreign status is undocumented or unreliable. Its threshold and exceptions are tax-year specific.

Forms 1042 and 1042-S are part of the foreign-payee track. Publication 515 states that compensation for U.S.-performed personal services by a nonresident alien is generally subject to withholding unless an exemption or graduated-rate rule applies. It also requires Form 1042-S for covered amounts even in some cases where no tax is withheld. Treaty claims, effectively connected income, mixed-location work, and missing documentation deserve specialist review.

## Keep one contractor evidence file

Retain the signed contract, accepted W-form, ownership check, worker-classification memo, invoices, payment proof, service dates, work-location evidence, payment-character analysis, and any withholding or information returns. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does every foreign contractor complete Form W-8BEN?

No. W-8BEN is generally for a foreign individual beneficial owner. A foreign entity may need W-8BEN-E, while effectively connected income, an intermediary, or a personal-service treaty claim may require a different form.

### Does paying a foreign contractor require Form 5472?

Not solely because the contractor is foreign. Form 5472 turns on whether the contractor is a related party and whether the payment is a reportable transaction.

### Is work performed abroad automatically free of every U.S. filing?

No. Work location is central to service-income sourcing, but status documentation, worker classification, payment character, mixed-location days, and other reporting rules still need review.
