**A Japan tax resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 after a reportable transaction with the owner or another related party. A Japanese individual normally uses the 12-digit My Number as the FTIN; a Japanese entity uses its corporate number.**

Form 5472 reports ownership-linked transactions, not the LLC’s ordinary profit. Japanese and US income-tax conclusions require a separate analysis of activity, source, entity classification, and treaty positions.

## What transactions trigger Form 5472 for a Japan owner?

The filing generally applies when a foreign-owned US disregarded entity has a transaction with its owner or another related party. The [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) include contributions, distributions, formation costs, dissolution costs, and other exchanges covered by Part V.

| Activity | Form 5472 result |
|---|---|
| Owner sends JPY-derived funds to open the US account | Reportable capital contribution |
| LLC sends a payout to the owner in Japan | Reportable distribution |
| Owner pays the LLC’s subscription from a personal card | Reportable owner-funded expense |
| Unrelated customer buys a service | Ordinary customer revenue, not automatically reportable |
| LLC purchases services from the owner’s Japanese company | Related-party transaction, generally on a separate form |

The current failure-to-file penalty is **$25,000 per form, per year**. A substantially incomplete form can be treated as not filed, so the owner ledger and identifiers matter as much as filing on time.

## What Japanese identifier belongs in Part II?

Japan’s National Tax Agency describes the [My Number system](https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/tetsuzuki/mynumberinfo/gaiyo.htm) as the individual numbering system used for tax and social-security administration and distinguishes it from the publicly available corporate number.

| Party on Form 5472 | FTIN entry | Reference-ID rule |
|---|---|---|
| Japan-resident individual owner | 12-digit My Number | Add an alphanumeric reference ID when no US number is entered |
| Japanese corporate owner | 13-digit corporate number | Use the company’s own identifier and address |
| Individual with existing ITIN/SSN | Enter the US number in 4b(1) | Continue using the FTIN consistently |
| Owner genuinely without an FTIN | “None” or “N/A” | Do not leave the required foreign-owned-DE field unexplained |

Protect My Number carefully. It belongs in the secured tax package, not in email subject lines, public notes, or an unprotected shared spreadsheet. Form5472 Prep collects tax identifiers through the filing portal.

## Does the US-Japan treaty eliminate Form 5472?

No. Japan is listed on the IRS [US income-tax treaty index](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z), but the treaty does not erase the section 6038A information return.

For personal services, the IRS generally sources income where the work is physically performed. A founder coding or consulting entirely from Japan often has foreign-source services income, but US workdays, a US office, employees, a dependent agent, or US inventory can change the result. Treaty relief must be claimed correctly when required.

Japan may classify the US LLC differently from US federal law. A Japanese adviser should assess local entity classification, worldwide income, consumption tax, and any foreign-company reporting. Form 5472 does not settle those issues.

## How should yen transactions be converted?

Use transaction-level conversion rather than an unexplained annual average when dates and amounts are available.

| Workpaper field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Date | Actual movement date |
| Type | Contribution, distribution, loan, reimbursement, or related-party charge |
| Original amount | JPY amount from the statement |
| Rate basis | Named, consistently applied exchange-rate source |
| USD value | Arithmetic used in the filing |
| Evidence | Bank statement, card receipt, invoice, or owner memo |

A contribution and later distribution remain separate gross transactions. The [Form 5472 transaction examples](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples) and [currency guide](/blog/form-5472-currency-conversion-exchange-rates) provide more classifications.

## How does a Japan-owned LLC complete the filing?

1. **Verify the entity path.** Confirm one foreign owner and no election that changes disregarded status.
2. **Identify each related party.** Distinguish the individual owner from any Japanese company that also dealt with the LLC.
3. **Reconcile and convert the ledger.** Preserve JPY records and calculate supported USD gross amounts.
4. **Prepare the filing package.** Complete the pro forma Form 1120, Form 5472, and Part V or other statements.
5. **Sign and deliver it to Ogden.** The package must be faxed or mailed; it cannot be e-filed for a foreign-owned US disregarded entity.
6. **Retain proof and source records.** Keep the exact signed package, transmission record, My Number support, and conversion schedule.

For a calendar-year LLC, the filing is generally due April 15 following the tax year, or October 15 after a timely Form 7004 extension.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is My Number the FTIN for Form 5472?

Yes, for a Japan-resident individual who has one. A Japanese entity uses its corporate number rather than the individual controller’s My Number.

### Does a Japanese owner need an ITIN?

Not solely for Form 5472. The owner can use a consistent reference ID and Japanese FTIN when no US identifying number exists.

### Are Japanese customer receipts reportable?

Not simply because they enter the LLC’s account. Form 5472 focuses on owner and related-party transactions; unrelated customer revenue belongs in the books but is not automatically a Part V transaction.

### Does working from Japan always mean no US tax?

No. Work location is important for services, but US workdays, personnel, inventory, agents, passive income, and other facts can create US tax consequences. Form 5472 remains a separate filing question.

### Can a Japan-owned LLC e-file the package?

No. A foreign-owned US disregarded entity must fax or mail Form 5472 with the pro forma Form 1120 to the dedicated IRS unit.
