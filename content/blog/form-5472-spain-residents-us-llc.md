**A Spain tax resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC has a reportable transaction with the owner or another related party. Use the owner’s Spanish NIF as the FTIN and report owner funding and withdrawals separately from customer revenue.**

The filing is an information return, not a calculation of Spanish or US income tax. A US customer, a US bank account, and a US LLC do not by themselves decide where the owner owes income tax.

If the LLC was funded, paid owner expenses, or returned money to Spain, [start a reviewed Form 5472 filing](/start) before assuming that zero profit means zero filing. Owners in other countries can compare their rules on our [Form 5472 by country](/form-5472-by-country) page.

## When does a Spain resident’s US LLC file Form 5472?

A Spain-owned LLC generally files when it is a domestic single-member LLC disregarded for federal income-tax purposes, its sole owner is foreign for US tax purposes, and it had at least one related-party transaction during the year.

The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) include contributions, distributions, formation payments, dissolution payments, and other transactions with the foreign owner. The IRS states that failure to file a timely and substantially complete form can trigger a **$25,000 penalty per form, per year**.

| LLC event | Form 5472 treatment | Typical record |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays the state formation fee | Owner-funded formation transaction | Receipt and formation invoice |
| Owner wires EUR to the LLC | Capital contribution | Bank transfer and exchange-rate record |
| LLC sends cash to the owner in Spain | Distribution | Bank statement and owner ledger |
| Spanish client pays an invoice | Third-party customer revenue, not an owner transaction | Invoice and sales ledger |
| LLC pays the owner’s Spanish company | Related-party payment | Contract, invoice, and separate Form 5472 analysis |

Gross related-party movements should not be netted. A USD 5,000 contribution and a USD 2,000 distribution remain two reportable movements even though the owner’s net funding is USD 3,000.

The test turns on US status, not nationality: an owner who is a US citizen or US tax resident is not a foreign owner for this purpose, even while living in Spain.

For a calendar-year LLC, the 2026 package is generally due April 15, 2027. A [Form 5472 extension (Form 7004)](/form-7004-extension) filed by that date, with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top, generally moves the deadline to October 15, 2027.

## What Spanish tax number goes on Form 5472?

Use the actual Spanish NIF belonging to the individual owner on Part II line 4b(3). Spain’s [Agencia Tributaria explains](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/ayuda/manuales-videos-folletos/manuales-practicos/guia-practica-cumplimentacion-modelo-censal-036/capitulo-01-cuestiones-generales/causas-presentacion-obligados-declarar/alta-modelo-036/casilla-110-solicitud-numero-identificacion-036/personas-fisicas.html) that an individual’s NIF is generally the DNI number for a Spanish national or the NIE for a foreign national.

| Form 5472 field | Spain-resident individual |
|---|---|
| 4b(1) US identifying number | Existing SSN or ITIN, if any; do not obtain an ITIN solely for Form 5472 |
| 4b(2) Reference ID | LLC-created alphanumeric ID when line 4b(1) is blank; reuse it every year |
| 4b(3) FTIN | Spanish NIF, commonly based on DNI or NIE |
| 4a owner address | The owner’s real Spanish address, not the LLC’s registered-agent address |

An NIF and a reference ID serve different fields. Providing the NIF does not eliminate the reference-ID requirement when no US identifying number appears on line 4b(1). Our [FTIN and reference-ID guide](/blog/form-5472-ftin-reference-id-foreign-address) explains the distinction.

## Does the US-Spain tax treaty remove Form 5472?

No. The US-Spain income tax treaty can affect whether particular US-source income is taxable, but it does not cancel the LLC’s section 6038A information reporting.

The IRS maintains the current [US-Spain treaty documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/spain-tax-treaty-documents): the convention signed in Madrid on 22 February 1990, a 2013 protocol amending it, and technical explanations. Treaty relief depends on the owner’s tax residence, income type, US activity, and whether the required position is claimed. Form 5472 remains a separate disclosure of related-party transactions.

For service income, the IRS generally sources personal services where the work is physically performed. A consultant working entirely from Spain often has foreign-source service income, while US workdays, a US office, inventory, employees, or a dependent agent can change the result. Read [whether a foreign-owned LLC pays US tax](/blog/does-foreign-owned-llc-pay-us-tax) for the two-track analysis.

Spanish tax treatment is also separate. A Spanish adviser should analyze whether Spain treats the US LLC as transparent or as a foreign entity, how worldwide income is reported, and whether information reporting applies locally.

## How does Spain view a resident who owns a US LLC?

Spain taxes its residents on worldwide income, so the US disregarded-entity label does not settle the Spanish answer. Whether the LLC’s profit is attributed to the owner as it arises or taxed only when distributed is a Spanish classification question for a Spanish adviser.

Three provisions of the Spanish personal income tax law, [Ley 35/2006 (LIRPF)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-20764#a9), frame that conversation:

- **Article 9** makes an individual resident in Spain if they spend more than 183 days in Spanish territory during the calendar year, or if the main centre or base of their activities or economic interests is in Spain. Residence is presumed, unless proven otherwise, when the non-separated spouse and dependent minor children habitually live in Spain.
- **Article 2** taxes a resident’s income regardless of where it arose or where the payer resides.
- **Article 87** treats foreign entities whose legal nature is identical or analogous to Spanish income-attribution entities (*entidades en régimen de atribución de rentas*) as attribution entities, whose income is allocated to their members.

Whether a particular single-member US LLC falls within article 87 depends on its documents and facts. Confirm the Spanish treatment with an *asesor fiscal* before filing your Spanish return.

## Does Modelo 720 apply to a Spanish owner’s US LLC?

It may. Modelo 720 is Spain’s informative return on assets and rights held abroad, and it is separate from Form 5472. Filing one never satisfies the other, so a Spanish resident with a US LLC should check both every year.

Under [Orden HAP/72/2013](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2013-954), the return is filed by Spanish residents between 1 January and 31 March of the year after the year reported. Its categories include:

| Modelo 720 category | Who reports | Why a US LLC owner should check |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts at foreign financial institutions | Holders, representatives, authorised persons, beneficiaries, people with power of disposal, and beneficial owners | The owner usually signs on the LLC’s US business account |
| Securities or rights representing a participation in any type of legal entity located abroad | Holders and beneficial owners | The LLC membership interest itself |
| Real estate located abroad | Holders and beneficial owners | Only if the LLC or owner holds US property |

The implementing regulation, [Real Decreto 1065/2007](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2007-15984) (articles 42 bis, 42 ter and 54 bis), exempts a category when the combined values do not exceed EUR 50,000. Whether the LLC interest or its account falls in scope, how it is valued, and whether an exemption applies are questions for a Spanish adviser. Modelo 720 is not filed with the IRS and is not part of our package.

## How are euro amounts converted to US dollars?

Report Form 5472 amounts in US dollars, converted with a reasonable, consistently applied method. Keep the transaction date, original EUR amount, rate source, rate, and USD result for every related-party movement, and attach the schedule to the Part V statement.

Worked example for a 2026 calendar year:

| Transaction | Original amount | Working | Form 5472 workpaper amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Owner pays formation and registered-agent invoices by card | USD 420 | Already in USD | USD 420 |
| Owner capital contribution from a Spanish bank | EUR 5,000 | EUR 5,000 x 1.0850 | USD 5,425 |
| Distribution to the owner in Spain | EUR 2,000 | EUR 2,000 x 1.1200 | USD 2,240 |
| Total related-party value reviewed |  | USD 420 + USD 5,425 + USD 2,240 | USD 8,085 |

The rates above are illustrative; use documented rates for the relevant dates or another published source applied consistently. For reference, the IRS published a 2025 yearly average of 0.886 euros per US dollar; our [IRS yearly average exchange rates](/irs-yearly-average-exchange-rates) tool lists the published figures by year.

## How do Spain owners prepare the filing package?

1. **Confirm the entity classification.** The streamlined package applies to a foreign-owned domestic disregarded entity, not a multi-member partnership or an LLC that elected corporate treatment.
2. **Reconcile owner and related-party activity.** Separate customer revenue and ordinary supplier costs from contributions, distributions, loans, reimbursements, and related-company payments.
3. **Convert euro amounts to US dollars.** Record the transaction date, original EUR amount, rate source, rate used, and resulting USD amount.
4. **Complete the pro forma Form 1120 and Form 5472.** Write “Foreign-owned U.S. DE” across the Form 1120 and attach the required Part V statement.
5. **Sign and file by the deadline.** A calendar-year 2026 package is generally due April 15, 2027, or October 15, 2027 after a timely Form 7004 extension.
6. **Keep the exact filed copy and delivery evidence.** Foreign-owned disregarded entities cannot e-file this package; the IRS instructions provide the dedicated fax and mailing routes.

## Four Spanish scenarios, worked through

**Madrid consultant, Wyoming LLC, all work performed in Spain.** The owner invoices EU clients through the LLC, has no US office, and funded the account with EUR 5,000. Whatever the US income-tax answer, the contribution and later withdrawals are related-party transactions, so Form 5472 is generally required.

**Valencia ecommerce seller, Delaware LLC, stock in a US warehouse.** US inventory raises a US income-tax question that needs advice, but the Form 5472 package does not wait for it. Owner loans, repayments and distributions still go on the ledger.

**Remote worker in Barcelona with a dormant LLC.** No sales, but the owner paid the registered agent and state fee personally. Those owner-paid costs can still be reportable. Our [Barcelona digital nomad guide](/blog/barcelona-digital-nomad-visa-us-llc-tax-election) covers the separate Spanish visa and tax-regime questions.

**Owner whose Spanish SL bills the LLC.** The SL is a related party in its own right. Its management fees need their own Form 5472 analysis, separate from the owner’s contributions and draws.

## What mistakes do Spain owners make?

Most errors come from netting movements, using the wrong identifier, or treating one country’s return as covering the other’s.

- Reporting only the year-end net owner balance instead of each gross contribution and distribution.
- Entering the LLC’s EIN or the owner’s passport number as the FTIN instead of the NIF.
- Assuming the treaty, a loss, or zero US income removes the filing.
- Assuming a filed Form 5472 covers Modelo 720, or that a filed Modelo 720 covers Form 5472.
- Missing April 15 without filing Form 7004 first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Spain resident need an ITIN for Form 5472?

No. A foreign owner does not need an ITIN solely to complete Form 5472. Use an existing US number if the owner already has one, otherwise provide a consistent reference ID and the Spanish FTIN.

### Is a Spanish NIF the FTIN on Form 5472?

Yes. For an individual, the Spanish NIF—generally based on the DNI or NIE—is the foreign tax identification number normally entered on Part II line 4b(3).

### Are payments from US customers reported on Form 5472?

Ordinary customer payments are not reportable merely because the customer is American. Form 5472 focuses on transactions between the LLC and its owner or another related party.

### Does no US income tax mean no Form 5472?

No. Income-tax liability and information reporting are separate. A Spain-based owner can owe no US federal income tax yet still file Form 5472 because of a contribution, distribution, loan, or owner-paid cost.

### Does filing Modelo 720 replace Form 5472?

No. Modelo 720 is a Spanish informative return filed with the Agencia Tributaria between 1 January and 31 March. Form 5472 is a US information return filed with the IRS. A Spanish resident may need both.

### Can a Spain-owned LLC e-file Form 5472?

Not when it is a foreign-owned US disregarded entity using a pro forma Form 1120. The IRS requires that package to be faxed or mailed to the dedicated Ogden PIN Unit.
