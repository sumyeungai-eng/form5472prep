**A foreign-owned Florida single-member LLC generally files a Sunbiz annual report each year to remain active and may also need federal Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120 when it has a reportable transaction with its owner. The annual report is due by May 1; a calendar-year federal package is generally due April 15.**

Florida compliance looks simple because the annual report is online and does not ask for a balance sheet. The trap is assuming that Sunbiz is the tax filing. Sunbiz maintains the Florida entity record. Form 5472 reports related-party transactions to the IRS.

The Florida Department of State states that a 2026 annual report for a limited liability company is due by 11:59 PM EST on Friday, May 1, 2026, before a $400 late fee is assessed, and the same page lists the LLC annual report fee as $138.75 ([Florida Sunbiz annual report page](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/manage-business/efile/annual-report/)). If the owner funded or withdrew money from the company, [start the federal filing here](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=florida-llc-foreign-owner-tax-filing) and keep the Sunbiz filing as a separate state task.

## What are the annual requirements for a Florida LLC?

A foreign-owned Florida LLC should usually check the Sunbiz annual report first, then separately check federal Form 5472. The two filings ask different questions.

| Filing | Agency | 2026 rule | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Florida annual report | Florida Department of State, Division of Corporations | File online by May 1 to avoid the late fee | The entity record stays active and current |
| Florida LLC annual-report fee | Florida Department of State | $138.75 for an LLC annual report | The state report was paid |
| Federal Form 5472 + pro forma 1120 | IRS | Generally April 15 for a calendar-year foreign-owned DE with a reportable transaction | Related-party transactions were disclosed |
| Form 7004 extension | IRS Ogden PIN Unit | File by the regular federal due date | Federal extension only, not a Sunbiz extension |

The IRS Form 5472 instructions say a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity generally files Form 5472 when it had a reportable transaction with a related party, and the form is attached to a pro forma Form 1120 ([IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)). The Florida annual report does not satisfy that IRS rule. A late, incomplete, or unfiled Form 5472 draws a $25,000 penalty per form per year under IRC §6038A(d), and $25,000 more for each 30-day period the failure continues past 90 days after an IRS notice ([IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)).

Separate Florida taxes can still apply if the LLC has employees, sales-taxable activity, a corporate tax election or other Florida tax facts.

## What does the Florida annual report do?

The Florida annual report updates or confirms the LLC's public record. It is not a financial report, income-tax return or Form 5472 substitute.

The Florida Department of State explains that the annual report updates or confirms the Division of Corporations' records, is not a financial statement, and must be filed each year to maintain active status ([Florida Sunbiz annual report page](https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/manage-business/efile/annual-report/)). The report can update information such as the principal office address, mailing address, registered agent, and names or addresses of managers or authorised persons.

The first report is generally due in the calendar year after formation. A Florida LLC formed in 2026 normally has its first annual report due in 2027, but that does not delay federal Form 5472 if the LLC had a formation, funding or other reportable transaction during 2026.

Florida's page also warns that a report not filed by the September administrative deadline can lead to administrative dissolution or revocation. That state status problem is separate from IRS penalties.

## When does Form 5472 apply to a Florida LLC?

Form 5472 applies when the Florida LLC is a wholly foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity and had a reportable transaction with the foreign owner or another related party during the tax year. The key fact is the related-party transaction, not the Sunbiz annual report.

The IRS instructions define a foreign-owned U.S. DE as a domestic disregarded entity wholly owned by a foreign person and state that it is treated as separate from its owner for limited section 6038A reporting purposes ([IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)). The instructions also say foreign-owned U.S. DEs cannot file Form 5472 electronically and must fax or mail the package to the dedicated Ogden PIN Unit procedure.

Common Florida-owner triggers include:

1. Owner paid Florida formation costs personally.
2. Owner paid the Sunbiz annual report fee personally.
3. Owner contributed capital into an LLC bank account.
4. LLC distributed cash back to the owner.
5. Owner lent money to the LLC, or the LLC repaid a loan.
6. LLC paid the owner's personal expense.
7. LLC was formed, acquired, disposed of or dissolved during the year.

An active Sunbiz status does not answer the federal question. A Florida LLC can be active and still have no Form 5472 obligation in a truly transaction-free year. A dormant Florida LLC can have a Form 5472 obligation because the owner paid one state fee personally.

## What is the best 2026 filing calendar?

A Florida foreign owner should use the one-month gap between the federal and state dates to finish the federal transaction work first, then confirm the Sunbiz record.

1. **January:** download bank statements, processor exports and owner payment records for the prior year.
2. **February:** identify owner contributions, distributions, loans, reimbursements and personally paid Florida costs.
3. **March:** prepare the Form 5472 transaction schedule and Part V statement.
4. **By 15 April 2026 for the 2025 tax year:** file Form 5472 with the pro forma Form 1120, or file Form 7004 for a federal extension.
5. **By May 1, 2026:** file the Florida annual report and pay the state fee.
6. **After filing:** save the Sunbiz confirmation and IRS fax receipt in separate folders.
7. **Before closing:** file the proper Florida dissolution or withdrawal and review any final Form 5472 year.

The federal extension does not extend the Florida annual report. The Florida late fee does not replace the IRS penalty. Keeping the two calendars separate prevents expensive category errors.

## What Florida records should a foreign owner keep?

A Florida foreign owner should keep the Sunbiz record, the federal filing package and the owner ledger together but labelled separately. The same invoice can matter for different reasons.

| Record | Florida purpose | Federal Form 5472 purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Articles of organisation | Shows Florida formation | Shows entity history |
| Sunbiz annual report confirmation | Shows active-status filing | Owner-paid fee may enter the ledger |
| Registered-agent records | Supports state record | Owner-paid invoices may be reportable |
| EIN letter | Often entered in Sunbiz updates | Required for Form 5472 package |
| Bank statements | Usually not part of annual report | Source for owner transactions |
| Owner ledger | Internal support | Core Form 5472 workpaper |
| IRS fax receipt | No Sunbiz purpose | Proof of federal delivery |

The owner ledger should identify the amount, date, direction, currency, counterparty and category of every owner-related transaction. If the Florida fee was paid from the owner's personal card, record that separately from payments made by the LLC itself.

## What do four Florida scenarios look like in practice?

Florida scenarios turn on timing and owner payments.

**1. Florida LLC formed in 2026, owner funded the bank account.** The first Florida annual report is generally due in 2027, because annual reports are filed in the calendar year after formation. The 2026 federal Form 5472 analysis starts immediately because formation and capital funding can be reportable transactions for the 2026 tax year.

**2. Existing Florida LLC, no customer revenue, owner paid Sunbiz personally.** The Sunbiz report keeps the entity active. The owner-paid annual report fee is a related-party movement that should be reviewed for Form 5472. No customer revenue does not make the federal filing disappear.

**3. Florida LLC misses the 2026 annual report deadline.** Arithmetic using the state figures: $138.75 annual report fee + $400 late fee = $538.75 before any reinstatement-related costs. That state cost does not include, replace or reduce the IRS Form 5472 penalty risk.

**4. Florida LLC closes after distributing remaining cash.** The Florida dissolution filing can end future Sunbiz annual reports when done properly. The final distribution to the foreign owner is a related-party transaction, so the final federal Form 5472 year still needs review.

## Does Florida charge an LLC franchise tax?

Florida does not charge the Delaware-style flat annual LLC franchise tax covered in many state-selection comparisons. For a default Florida LLC, the recurring state entity-maintenance item in this guide is the Sunbiz annual report and its fee.

That narrow answer should not be stretched. Florida can have other tax obligations depending on activity. Sales-tax collection, reemployment tax, corporate income tax after an entity election, and local licensing are separate issues. A foreign-owned LLC with U.S. employees, Florida inventory or taxable Florida sales should get state-specific advice.

For many non-U.S. owners using a Florida LLC only as a payment wrapper, the practical annual pair is simpler: Sunbiz annual report for the state, and Form 5472 plus pro forma 1120 for the IRS when owner transactions occurred.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the 2026 Florida LLC annual report due?

The Florida Department of State says the 2026 annual report is due by 11:59 PM EST on Friday, May 1, 2026, before the late fee applies.

### What is the Florida LLC annual report fee?

The Florida Department of State lists the annual report fee for a limited liability company as $138.75.

### Does the annual report replace Form 5472?

No. The Florida annual report maintains the public entity record. Form 5472 reports transactions between a foreign-owned LLC and related parties to the IRS.

### Does a no-income Florida LLC file Form 5472?

It may. Owner funding, personally paid annual-report fees, withdrawals or reimbursements can be reportable even with no customer revenue.

### Does Florida require an annual-report balance sheet?

No. Sunbiz says the annual report is not a financial statement. It updates entity and management details.

### What if the LLC was formed in 2026?

The first Florida annual report is generally due in 2027, but the first federal Form 5472 may be due for 2026 if formation or funding transactions occurred.

### Can a Florida LLC owe no franchise tax but still file Form 5472?

Yes. Florida's lack of a flat LLC franchise tax does not change the IRS reporting rule for foreign-owned disregarded entities with owner transactions.
