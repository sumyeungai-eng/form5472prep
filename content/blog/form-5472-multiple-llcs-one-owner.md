**A foreign person who owns multiple US single-member LLCs should review each LLC separately for Form 5472. Common ownership does not normally turn three legal entities into one filing package. Each qualifying foreign-owned US disregarded entity needs its own EIN, tax-year ledger, pro forma Form 1120, and Form 5472 for each related party with reportable transactions.**

The number of owners and the number of entities are different counts. One owner can create several reporting entities, and each entity can have more than one related party.

If two or more LLCs had owner funding, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements, or intercompany activity, [start with a separate intake for each LLC](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-multiple-llcs-one-owner).

## The entity-by-party rule

Treasury Regulation [section 1.6038A-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6038A-2) requires each reporting corporation to make a separate annual Form 5472 for each related party with which it had a reportable transaction. The [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) apply the reporting-corporation rule to a foreign-owned US disregarded entity.

That creates two dimensions:

| Dimension | Count | Filing effect |
|---|---:|---|
| US LLCs treated as separate reporting entities | One count per LLC | Separate pro forma Form 1120 package per LLC |
| Related parties transacting with an LLC | One count per related party | Separate Form 5472 within that LLC's package |

For example, three LLCs owned directly by one foreign individual can produce three pro forma Form 1120 packages. If LLC 2 also paid a foreign company controlled by the owner, LLC 2 may need a second Form 5472 for that related company.

## A worked filing matrix

Assume one nonresident owner has three wholly owned disregarded LLCs:

| Entity | Activity during the year | Related parties | Likely package |
|---|---|---:|---|
| LLC A | Owner contributed $5,000 | Owner | One pro forma 1120 + one Form 5472 |
| LLC B | Owner contributed $2,000; LLC paid owner's foreign company $8,000 for services | Owner and foreign company | One pro forma 1120 + two Forms 5472 |
| LLC C | No money, property, costs, funding, or transfers | None identified | Document the zero-transaction conclusion; filing may not be required |

The result is not “one owner, one Form 5472.” It is an entity-by-party analysis.

## Do transfers between two commonly owned LLCs count?

They can. A transfer between sister LLCs should not be labeled an ordinary bank transfer and ignored. Determine the legal payer, legal recipient, purpose, ownership chain, and whether either party is related under the Form 5472 rules.

Classify the transfer as a loan, service payment, purchase, contribution, distribution through the owner, reimbursement, or another defensible category. Keep separate books so the same movement is not accidentally recorded as revenue in one LLC and owner capital in another.

## Can the owner consolidate all LLCs on one return?

Not merely because one person owns them. The consolidated Form 5472 rule in the IRS instructions concerns eligible members of an affiliated corporate group filing a consolidated federal income-tax return. It is not a shortcut for unrelated domestic disregarded LLC packages under common individual ownership.

Series LLCs, tiered disregarded structures, and a parent LLC owning subsidiary LLCs require a more careful classification review. State-law “series,” federal entity treatment, and the actual path of transactions can produce results that differ from three directly owned standalone LLCs. Do not assume every cell or subsidiary uses the same answer.

## Keep one control sheet across the group

Create a master schedule without merging the books:

| Control field | LLC A | LLC B | LLC C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal name and EIN | Complete | Complete | Complete |
| Formation and closure dates | Complete | Complete | Complete |
| Direct and indirect owner | Owner X | Owner X | Owner X |
| Owner contributions | $5,000 | $2,000 | $0 |
| Owner distributions | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Other related parties | None | Foreign Co Y | None |
| Intercompany transfers | None | Review | None |
| Filing decision and evidence | File | File two Forms | Retain zero test |

The master sheet is a control, not a combined ledger. Each LLC should retain its own bank records, invoices, contracts, exchange-rate support, and signed filing copy.

## Common failure points

- Reusing one LLC's EIN on another package.
- Reporting all owner transfers on the LLC with the main bank account.
- Netting sister-company payments against owner funding.
- Filing one Form 5472 for the owner and ignoring an additional related company.
- Treating a dormant LLC as transaction-free without checking who paid its annual state and registered-agent costs.
- Closing one LLC but omitting its final distributions and dissolution costs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can one foreign owner file one Form 5472 for all LLCs?

No. Common ownership does not normally combine separate US disregarded entities into one package. Each LLC is reviewed as its own reporting entity, and each qualifying LLC uses its own EIN, pro forma Form 1120, and Form 5472 set.

### Does each LLC need a separate EIN?

Yes. The filing package identifies the reporting LLC, so each separate LLC should use its own EIN. Reusing one entity's EIN on another package is one of the common failure points this post warns against.

### Do transfers between sister LLCs matter?

They can. A sister-company transfer should be classified by legal payer, legal recipient, purpose, ownership chain, and relationship status. It may be a loan, service payment, purchase, contribution, distribution through the owner, reimbursement, or another supported category.

### How should I organize records for multiple LLCs?

Keep a master control sheet, but do not merge the books. Each LLC should retain its own bank records, invoices, contracts, exchange-rate support, signed filing copy, and delivery proof tied to that entity's legal name and EIN.
