**Form 5472 cannot be prepared from a bank statement alone. The preparer needs the LLC's EIN and formation details, the owner's identification and country, a 2026 transaction ledger labelling every owner-LLC movement, and the document behind each one — invoice, loan note, receipt, or valuation — plus the year-end total assets figure.**

There is a gap between "I have my bank statements" and "the form can be filled in," and almost every delayed filing sits in that gap. A statement shows that $40,000 moved. The form asks what the $40,000 was. Only a document answers the second question.

This is the gather list: what to put in front of a preparer, in what form, before any line of the form gets touched. [You can start the filing and upload documents as they arrive](/start). ## What does the regulation actually require you to hold?

Permanent books, not a folder of receipts. The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that "a reporting corporation must keep the permanent books of account or records as required by section 6001," and that "these books must be sufficient to establish the correctness of the reporting corporation's federal income tax return," including records relevant to the correct treatment of related-party transactions.

[Treasury Regulation § 1.6038A-3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6038A-3) names the categories: original entry books and transaction records, profit and loss statements, pricing documents, foreign country and third party filings, ownership and capital structure records, and records of loans, services, and other non-sales transactions. A reporting corporation must keep those that are relevant to its own business and its related-party transactions — a one-owner LLC with no intercompany pricing does not need pricing documents it never generated.

## What does the preparer need before touching the form?

Five things, in this order of difficulty.

1. **Entity identity.** The LLC's EIN notice, its formation state and date, its registered address, and its current standing.
2. **Owner identity.** Your legal name as it appears on identification, your residence address, your country of citizenship and of tax residence, and a foreign taxpayer identifying number if you have one.
3. **Related-party identity.** The same details for every entity you control that transacted with the LLC — your offshore company, your other LLC, a family holding entity.
4. **A labelled transaction ledger for 2026.** One row per movement between the LLC and you or a related party: date, amount, currency, direction, and a one-sentence description of what it was.
5. **The document behind each ledger row.** Invoice, loan note, receipt, resolution, valuation — whichever applies.

Items 1 to 3 take an afternoon. Items 4 and 5 are the whole job.

## Which document proves which entry on the form?

| Money movement in 2026 | Where the Form 5472 instructions put it | Document that establishes it |
|---|---|---|
| You wire cash in, with no repayment expected | Part V, as a contribution | Owner memo or resolution stating it is capital, plus the bank record |
| You lend cash to the LLC | Part IV, as a loan and any interest | Signed loan note with rate and term, plus an interest schedule |
| The LLC repays you | Part IV | The same note, a repayment schedule, and the bank record |
| The LLC pays cash out to you | Part V, as a distribution | Owner memo recording the distribution, plus the bank record |
| You pay an LLC expense from your personal account | Part IV or Part V, depending on treatment | Vendor receipt in the LLC's name, plus a reimbursement report |
| Your foreign company invoices the LLC for services | Part IV | Invoice, the service contract, and the bank record |
| You contribute equipment, software, or intellectual property | Part VI, as a nonmonetary transaction | Transfer document and support for the fair market value |
| The LLC was formed, dissolved, or restructured in 2026 | Part V | State formation or dissolution filings |
| Total assets at the end of the tax year | Part I | A balance sheet as at the last day of the tax year |

Two notes on the table. The Form 5472 instructions restrict Part IV to transactions with a *foreign* related party where money "was the sole consideration," and say not to complete it for domestic related parties. And a noncash capital contribution touches two parts — the instructions name "contributions and distributions" among the other transactions reported in Part V, while Part VI covers "nonmonetary and less-than-full consideration transactions" — which is exactly why a valuation, not just a description, is what the preparer needs for it.

## What goes in Part V, and why does it need a written description?

The box on its own is not the filing. The Form 5472 instructions say a foreign-owned US disregarded entity checks the Part V box if it had "any other transaction, as defined in Regulations section 1.482-1(i)(7)," that is not already reported in Part IV, and that such transactions "include formation, dissolution, acquisition, and disposition amounts, as well as contributions and distributions." The instructions then require those transactions to be described on an attached statement.

That attached statement is written from your ledger. If the ledger says "15 March 2026, $25,000 in, capital contribution to fund the Stripe reserve," the statement writes itself. If the ledger says "15 March 2026, $25,000 in," there is nothing to write, and the filing stalls while someone emails you to ask.

## How do you reconstruct records that were never kept?

You rebuild forward from the money, you write down what you actually know, and you never backdate a document.

1. **Pull every statement for the year.** Every LLC bank account, every payment platform, every account you closed during 2026. Download rather than screenshot; closed accounts stop being downloadable.
2. **Pull your personal statements for the same period**, so that transfers show both sides.
3. **Build the ledger from the statements**, one row per movement involving you or a related party, with nothing netted off.
4. **Classify each row by what happened**, using email, contracts, and invoices as evidence of intent at the time.
5. **Where no document exists, write a dated memo now** that states the facts you can establish, the basis for the classification, and that it was prepared in 2026 or 2027 from bank records. A contemporaneous note of a reconstruction is a record. A document dated to look original is a different problem entirely.
6. **Test loans against reality.** If there is no note, no rate, no term, and no repayment, calling a series of transfers a loan is a conclusion the records do not support — and a mislabelled loan distorts the form.
7. **Rebuild the year-end balance sheet** so the Part I total assets figure is supported by something.
8. **Keep the reconstruction file with the filing**, including the memos, so a later reviewer sees how the numbers were derived.

The honest version of a reconstruction, clearly labelled, is far stronger than a confident set of numbers with nothing behind them.

## How long do these records have to survive the filing?

Longer than you think. Section 1.6038A-3 requires records to be kept "as long as they may be relevant or material" to determining the correct tax treatment of related-party transactions, and says that in no case may they be kept for less than the applicable statute of limitations for the relevant taxable year. The IRS's general guidance on [how long to keep records](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records) makes the same point from the other side: "the length of time you should keep a document depends on the action, expense, or event which the document records."

There is also a location rule most owners miss. Section 1.6038A-3 requires records to be maintained within the United States unless its conditions are met, and where they are held abroad it allows delivery of the originals or duplicates to the Service "within 60 days of the request," with 120 days substituted for material profit and loss statements that have to be created.

## What happens if the records simply are not there?

The exposure is the reason to act rather than wait. The Form 5472 instructions set a penalty of "$25,000" on a reporting corporation that "fails to file Form 5472 when due and in the manner prescribed," with "an additional penalty of $25,000" where the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notification, applied "with respect to each related party for which a failure occurs for each 30-day period."

A filing built from a reconstructed, documented ledger is a filing. A year left unfiled because the records felt too thin is the thing the penalty is written for.

## The bottom line

Gather four things before anything else: the EIN notice, your own identification details, a labelled 2026 ledger of every owner-LLC movement, and the document behind each row. ## Frequently asked questions

### Are bank statements enough to prepare Form 5472?

No. A statement establishes that money moved and when. It does not establish whether the movement was a contribution, a loan, a repayment, a distribution, or a payment for services — and that classification is what decides where the amount goes on the form.

### What do I send if I never wrote a loan note?

Send the transfers and say so. A dated memo setting out the amounts, the understanding at the time, and any email evidence is a record. Inventing a note dated years ago is not an option worth considering.

### Does Form 5472 require me to attach the underlying documents?

The instructions require an attached statement describing Part V transactions and a schedule for Part VI, not the invoices themselves. The underlying documents are what § 1.6038A-3 makes you keep and produce on request.

### Can the LLC keep its records outside the United States?

Section 1.6038A-3 requires records to be maintained within the United States unless its conditions are met, and where they sit abroad it sets a 60-day production window after a request, extended to 120 days for material profit and loss statements that must be created.

### How many years of records do I need if several years are unfiled?

One complete set per unfiled year, because the form is filed per year and the penalty is assessed per year and per related party. Count the open years first, then gather, so the oldest year is not the one you run out of time on.

### Do I need records for ordinary customer sales?

Section 6001 requires books sufficient to establish the correctness of the return, so keep them. But ordinary sales to unrelated customers are not what Parts IV, V, and VI report — those parts report transactions with related parties.

### What is the one document owners most often cannot produce?

Support for the year-end total assets figure in Part I. Owners keep the transaction trail and skip the balance sheet, then discover that no statement in the file shows what the LLC owned on the last day of the year.
