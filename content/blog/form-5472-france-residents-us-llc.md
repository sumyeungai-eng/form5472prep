**A French resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the company has a reportable transaction with its owner or another related party. Formation funding, contributions, withdrawals, loans and owner-paid expenses may trigger the return even when the LLC earned no income.**

The US filing does not replace French reporting or determine how France classifies the LLC. It records related-party transactions for the IRS. A French owner can [start the Form 5472 process online](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-france) after gathering the LLC's EIN, ownership details and transaction ledger.

## Who must file for a French-owned US LLC?

Under the [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472), a US disregarded entity wholly owned by a foreign person is treated as a reporting corporation for section 6038A. Form 5472 is generally required when the LLC had a reportable transaction with a related party during the tax year.

The owner is a related party. That means these common first-year events matter:

- paying the formation company from a personal card;
- contributing money to open a US bank account;
- lending money to the LLC;
- taking an owner withdrawal; or
- transferring property, software rights or services between owner and LLC.

A company with no sales can therefore have a filing obligation. Read the [dormant-LLC guide](/blog/form-5472-dormant-llc-no-income) if activity was limited.

## Which French number is the FTIN?

For an individual with a French tax obligation, the relevant number is generally the numéro fiscal de référence, also called the numéro SPI. The [OECD's France TIN profile](https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/France-TIN.pdf), supplied from jurisdiction information, says the French administration issues this unique number when an individual is registered as having a tax obligation. A French entity commonly uses its SIREN as the relevant identifier.

| Filing item | What to collect |
|---|---|
| LLC identification | Legal name, US address and EIN |
| Individual owner | Legal name, French address, numéro fiscal/SPI if issued |
| Entity owner | Legal name, country of organisation and SIREN or relevant FTIN |
| No US owner number | Use a consistent reference ID when required |
| No French FTIN | Enter “None” or “N/A” where the IRS instructions permit |

Do not enter the LLC's EIN as the owner's foreign number. The return distinguishes the reporting entity from its owner.

## Which French-owner transactions are reportable?

Create a two-way ledger before classifying anything:

| Direction | Examples to review |
|---|---|
| Owner to LLC | capital, loans, personally paid formation or operating costs, transferred assets |
| LLC to owner | withdrawals, loan repayment, reimbursements, personal bills paid by the LLC |
| Non-cash | services, licences, equipment, formation, acquisition, sale or dissolution |

Ordinary sales to unrelated customers are not Part V transactions merely because the owner later receives the profit. The customer receipt and the later owner withdrawal are separate events.

## How are euro amounts converted?

Form 5472 uses US dollars. The IRS [yearly average exchange-rate guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates) says the spot rate generally applies when an item is received, paid or accrued, and a published exchange rate should be used consistently.

Keep a conversion schedule with the date, EUR amount, source rate and USD amount. Consistent documentation matters more than selecting the rate that gives the most favourable total.

## How does a French owner file Form 5472?

1. Confirm that the LLC remained a single-member disregarded entity.
2. Reconcile all owner and related-party activity for the tax year.
3. Complete the pro forma Form 1120 and Form 5472.
4. Attach a Part V statement describing contributions, distributions and other lifecycle transactions.
5. Fax the package at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737, or mail it to the dedicated Ogden PIN Unit address.
6. Keep the submission and delivery receipt permanently.

For filings due in 2026, the 2025 calendar-year deadline is ordinarily April 15, 2026, or October 15, 2026 with a timely Form 7004 extension.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a French owner need an ITIN for Form 5472?

Usually no. The LLC needs an EIN. The owner may use the applicable French FTIN and a consistent reference ID when no US identifying number exists.

### What if a French resident has not received a numéro fiscal?

The IRS instructions permit “None” or “N/A” in the FTIN block when no FTIN exists. Do not invent a French identifier; use the proper US reference-ID process.

### Does a no-income LLC still file?

It can. Formation payments, capital contributions, withdrawals, loans and reimbursements are reportable related-party transactions even without customer revenue.

### Does Form 5472 settle French tax treatment?

No. Form 5472 is US information reporting. French income-tax, corporate-tax, social-charge and VAT treatment depends on separate facts and French law.

### What happens if Form 5472 is late?

The initial statutory penalty is $25,000 per required form and year. A late filer should complete the package promptly and document any reasonable-cause facts.
