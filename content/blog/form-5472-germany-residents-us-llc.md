**A German resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally must file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC has a reportable transaction with the owner or another related party. The obligation can arise from capital contributions, withdrawals, loans or owner-paid expenses even if the LLC has no income.**

Form 5472 is a US related-party information return. It is separate from any German income, trade-tax or VAT analysis. German owners can [begin the US filing here](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-germany); the LLC needs an EIN, but the owner does not need to obtain an ITIN solely for this form.

## When does a German-owned LLC file Form 5472?

The [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) apply when a domestic disregarded entity is wholly owned by a foreign person and has a reportable related-party transaction. “Disregarded” describes the federal tax classification, not an exemption from reporting.

Use this three-part test:

| Question | Filing signal |
|---|---|
| Was the LLC wholly owned by a German individual or entity? | Yes |
| Did it remain a disregarded entity for US federal tax purposes? | Yes |
| Was there any owner or related-party transfer during the year? | Yes means Form 5472 is generally required |

Formation funding commonly makes the first year reportable. An owner who pays the LLC's state fee or software bill personally has also moved value into the company.

## Which German tax number belongs on Form 5472?

For a German individual, use the personal tax identification number (Identifikationsnummer or IdNr) as the FTIN when applicable. Germany's [Federal Central Tax Office](https://online.portal.bzst.de/SharedDocs/Leistungsbeschreibung/EN/erneute_mitteilung_der_ID-Nr.html) explains that the IdNr identifies a taxpayer, is assigned automatically and remains valid for life.

Do not confuse the owner's German number with the US LLC's EIN. The identifiers belong in different fields:

- **LLC EIN:** the US reporting entity's number.
- **Owner FTIN:** the German owner's IdNr or the relevant entity identifier.
- **Owner US ID:** an SSN, ITIN or EIN only if the owner has one.
- **Reference ID:** a filer-created alphanumeric identifier used consistently when required.

If no FTIN exists, the IRS instructions permit a foreign-owned US disregarded entity to enter “None” or “N/A.”

## What transactions should a German owner report?

Form 5472 focuses on transactions between the LLC and related parties, not every business receipt or expense. Build a complete owner ledger covering:

1. Cash contributed to form or fund the LLC.
2. Cash or property distributed back to the owner.
3. Loans, repayments and interest between owner and LLC.
4. Expenses paid personally for the LLC and reimbursements paid later.
5. Services, rent, intellectual property or equipment supplied between related parties.
6. Formation, acquisition, disposition and dissolution events described in Part V.

For a fuller classification table, use the [owner loans and reimbursements guide](/blog/form-5472-owner-loans-contributions-reimbursements).

## How are euro transactions translated into US dollars?

All amounts on Form 5472 are reported in US dollars, with an attached schedule showing exchange rates used. The IRS [foreign-currency guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates) says the spot rate generally applies when an item is received, paid or accrued and that a consistently used published rate is generally accepted.

Our practical rule is:

- Use transaction-date rates for occasional capital transfers or large irregular payments.
- Use one supportable, consistently applied convention for recurring transactions.
- Preserve the rate source, original EUR amount, USD result and transaction date.

## How does a German owner complete the filing?

1. Confirm the LLC's owner, classification and calendar or fiscal year.
2. Reconcile every related-party transfer and convert it to USD.
3. Prepare the pro forma Form 1120 with the required foreign-owned-DE notation.
4. Prepare Form 5472 and a Part V attachment describing formation, contributions and distributions.
5. Fax at 300 DPI or higher to the Ogden PIN Unit or use its dedicated mailing address.
6. Retain the filed return and delivery evidence with the underlying ledger.

The usual calendar-year due date is April 15, extended to October 15 by a timely Form 7004.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a German Steuer-ID the same as the LLC's EIN?

No. The German IdNr identifies the individual owner. The EIN identifies the US LLC. Each number belongs in its own field on the filing package.

### Does a German owner need an ITIN?

Not solely for Form 5472. The owner can use the applicable German FTIN and a reference ID where required. The US LLC must have an EIN.

### Does a dormant German-owned LLC file Form 5472?

It may. Owner funding, withdrawals, personally paid bills, reimbursements or closure transactions can create a filing obligation despite zero sales.

### Does Form 5472 decide how Germany taxes the LLC?

No. US information reporting and German tax classification are separate analyses. A German adviser should address German residence, income, trade-tax and VAT consequences.

### Can a German owner e-file Form 5472?

No. A foreign-owned US disregarded entity must fax or mail its Form 5472 and pro forma 1120 package to the dedicated IRS Ogden PIN Unit.
