**A Netherlands resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC has a reportable transaction with its owner or another related party. Capital contributions, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements and formation expenses can create the obligation even if the company earned no revenue.**

The filing gives the IRS related-party information. It does not decide Dutch tax classification or replace a Netherlands return. A Dutch owner can [start a guided filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-netherlands) using the LLC's EIN, owner information and a complete transaction ledger.

## Does a Dutch-owned US LLC have to file Form 5472?

The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) cover a domestic disregarded entity wholly owned by a foreign person. The LLC generally files when it had a reportable transaction with the foreign owner or another related party during the year.

Use a four-step decision check:

- **One owner:** the LLC had a single tax owner.
- **Foreign owner:** the owner was a Dutch individual or non-US entity.
- **Default treatment:** no effective election changed the LLC's disregarded status.
- **Related transaction:** money, property, services or another item of value moved between related parties.

Formation and capitalisation often make the first year reportable. Zero customer revenue is not a filing exception.

## Does the owner use a BSN or RSIN as the FTIN?

For a Dutch individual, the relevant identifier is generally the Burgerservicenummer (BSN). For a legal entity, review the Rechtspersonen en Samenwerkingsverbanden Informatienummer (RSIN). The Netherlands [Tax Administration's BSN guidance](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/en/individuals/content/what-is-a-citizen-service-number) explains that a BSN is a unique personal number and that a business receives either a BSN or an RSIN depending on its legal form.

| Owner | Form 5472 identification approach |
|---|---|
| Dutch individual | BSN as the FTIN when applicable |
| Dutch legal entity | RSIN or other applicable Dutch tax identifier |
| Owner with a US tax number | Enter it in the separate US-ID field |
| Owner with no FTIN | Use “None” or “N/A” where allowed and assign a reference ID when required |

Never place the US LLC's EIN in the owner's FTIN field.

## What related-party transactions need review?

The owner ledger should include both directions:

1. Owner contributions and startup funding.
2. Owner loans, repayments and interest.
3. LLC withdrawals or distributions to the owner.
4. Personally paid LLC expenses and later reimbursements.
5. Related-party rent, services, licences and property transfers.
6. Formation, acquisition, disposition and dissolution transactions.

The IRS form requires a Part V attachment for other transactions of a foreign-owned US disregarded entity. Our [Part V transaction examples](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples) show how common transfers are described.

## How are euro amounts converted to dollars?

The filing uses US dollars. IRS [foreign-currency guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates) says the prevailing spot rate generally applies when the item is received, paid or accrued and that a consistently used published rate is generally accepted.

Maintain a schedule showing the original EUR amount, transaction date, source rate and USD result. Large one-off contributions are clearest at the transaction-date rate; a consistent convention may be supportable for recurring items.

## How does a Dutch owner file the return?

1. Confirm the LLC's legal owner, classification and tax year.
2. Reconcile all owner and related-party transactions.
3. Convert foreign-currency items and save the rate schedule.
4. Prepare Form 5472, the Part V statement and the pro forma Form 1120.
5. Fax at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737 or mail to the dedicated IRS Ogden PIN Unit address.
6. Keep the signed package, workpapers and delivery evidence.

A calendar-year filing is normally due April 15 of the following year, or October 15 after a timely Form 7004 extension.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Dutch owner need an ITIN?

Usually no. The LLC needs an EIN. The owner can use the applicable BSN or RSIN as an FTIN and a consistent reference ID where required.

### Is the KvK number always the FTIN?

Do not assume so. The Netherlands Tax Administration distinguishes personal BSNs and entity RSINs. Match the identifier to the legal owner and its tax records.

### Does a Dutch-owned LLC with no income file?

It can. Funding, personally paid costs, withdrawals, loans and reimbursements can be reportable even when the LLC had no sales.

### Does Form 5472 determine Dutch tax?

No. It is a US information return. Dutch treatment depends on separate residence, classification, income and activity facts that may require Dutch advice.

### What is the penalty for a late Form 5472?

The initial penalty is $25,000 per required form and tax year. Substantially incomplete forms can be treated as failures to file.
