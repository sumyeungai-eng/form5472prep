**A Hong Kong resident who owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 whenever the LLC has a reportable transaction with its owner or another related party. Use the owner's HKID number as the FTIN, and do not assume the US-China treaty covers Hong Kong.**

That answer applies whether the LLC runs an ecommerce store, invoices consulting clients, holds an investment, or earned no revenue. Form 5472 records transactions between the LLC and related parties; it does not calculate the owner's Hong Kong or US income tax.

The distinction matters because an initial bank deposit, an owner-paid bill, or a withdrawal can create a filing obligation even when customers paid nothing. ## When must a Hong Kong resident file Form 5472?

A Hong Kong owner must generally file when all three conditions are present:

1. The business is a US single-member LLC that is disregarded for federal income tax purposes and has not elected corporate treatment.
2. The sole owner is a foreign person for US tax purposes.
3. The LLC had a reportable transaction with the owner or another related party during the tax year.

The filing package is Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120. An LLC can meet the third condition before it opens for business: money transferred from a Hong Kong account to fund formation costs or the US bank account is normally a capital contribution.

The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that the failure-to-file penalty is **$25,000 per form, per year** under IRC §6038A(d). A substantially incomplete form is treated as a failure to file. After an IRS notice, another $25,000 applies for each 30-day period, or fraction, continuing beyond the 90-day response period (IRC §6038A(d)(2)).

## Which Hong Kong transactions are reportable?

Money, property, or services moving between the LLC and a foreign related party are the focus. Third-party business activity is not automatically reportable merely because it passed through the LLC's account.

| Event in the LLC ledger | Form 5472 treatment | Where the analysis normally starts |
|---|---|---|
| Owner wires HKD to open the US account | Reportable capital contribution | Part V statement |
| LLC transfers funds to the owner in Hong Kong | Reportable distribution | Part V statement |
| Owner lends cash to the LLC | Reportable borrowing | Part IV loan category |
| Owner pays an LLC software bill personally | Reportable owner-funded amount | Part V statement |
| Customer pays a genuine sales invoice | Not an owner transaction | Business books only |
| LLC pays an unrelated supplier or Amazon fee | Not related-party activity | Business books only |
| LLC pays the owner's Hong Kong company | Reportable with that company | Separate Form 5472 for that related party |

Form 5472 uses gross transaction amounts rather than a net owner balance. A contribution and a later distribution remain two movements; they should not disappear because one offsets the other economically. Keep bank statements, platform statements, invoices, and a separate owner ledger so the filing can be traced back to records.

## What goes in Part II for a Hong Kong owner?

Enter the Hong Kong identifier on line 4b(3), and add a reference ID on line 4b(2) whenever line 4b(1) has no US identifying number.

The [OECD TIN portal](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/sub-issues/tax-identification-numbers.html) identifies the HKID number as the TIN equivalent for a Hong Kong individual. A Hong Kong entity instead uses its Business Registration number. Use the identifier belonging to the actual direct or ultimate owner shown on the relevant Part II line; do not substitute the LLC's EIN or registered-agent address.

| Part II line | Entry for a Hong Kong individual | Required handling |
|---|---|---|
| 4b(1) | SSN or ITIN, only if the owner already has one | Do not invent or borrow a US number |
| 4b(2) | LLC-assigned alphanumeric reference ID | Required if 4b(1) is blank; reuse the same ID every year |
| 4b(3) | HKID number | If the owner truly has no FTIN, enter "None" or "N/A"; never leave it blank |
| 4a | Owner's name and actual Hong Kong address | Do not use the US registered-agent address as the owner's address |

The reference ID and FTIN serve different purposes. Having an HKID does not remove the line 4b(2) requirement when the owner lacks a US TIN on line 4b(1). An owner does not need an ITIN solely to complete Form 5472.

## Does the US treaty or Hong Kong tax system remove the filing?

No. Hong Kong has no US income tax treaty, and the US-China income tax treaty does not extend to the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. The [IRS treaty A-to-Z list](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z) lists China but not Hong Kong as a treaty jurisdiction.

The absence of a treaty does not itself mean that every LLC profit is US-taxable. US income tax depends on the activity, source, people, inventory, and other facts. Amazon inventory held in US warehouses is a reason to obtain specific US advice rather than infer the answer from Form 5472.

Hong Kong's system is also separate. The [Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department explains](https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/tax/bus_pft.htm) that profits arising outside Hong Kong are generally not taxed there, while source is determined from the facts. Foreign-sourced-income rules can add complexity for some entities. A Hong Kong adviser should determine how the LLC and its profits are treated locally; Form 5472 does not answer that question.

## How should HKD transactions be converted to US dollars?

Convert each reportable amount to US dollars using a reasonable, consistently applied rate and attach a schedule showing the exchange rates used. A transaction-date rate is usually easier to defend than converting an unexplained year-end net balance.

The following is an **illustrative workpaper**, not a market-rate claim. Assume the owner's records show HKD 7.80 per USD on each example transaction date:

| Illustrative transaction | HKD amount | Arithmetic | USD amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Capital contribution | HKD 15,600 | 15,600 ÷ 7.80 | USD 2,000 |
| Distribution | HKD 3,900 | 3,900 ÷ 7.80 | USD 500 |
| Owner-paid expense | HKD 780 | 780 ÷ 7.80 | USD 100 |
| Gross related-party value |  | 2,000 + 500 + 100 | USD 2,600 |

The Part V attachment would describe the contribution, distribution, and owner-paid expense separately, with dates and conversion basis. Line 1f would reflect the total value reported for that related party, not merely the owner's ending receivable or the net cash retained by the LLC. ## How do four common Hong Kong scenarios work?

Four scenarios, worked through, show why revenue and owner transactions must be separated.

**1. A Hong Kong trader keeps Amazon inventory in the United States.** Customer receipts and ordinary Amazon charges are third-party activity, not owner transactions. Capital sent by the owner and cash drawn back are reportable. US inventory may create a separate income-tax or state-tax issue, so the trader needs advice beyond Form 5472.

**2. A consultant performs every service from Hong Kong.** Client payments into the LLC are revenue. A transfer from the LLC to the consultant's personal Hong Kong account is a reportable distribution. Where services were performed can matter for income-tax sourcing, but it does not reclassify the owner transfer as customer revenue.

**3. A Hong Kong holding company owns the LLC.** The company is the direct foreign owner, so its Business Registration number belongs in the company FTIN field. If the LLC also pays the individual controller, both the company and individual are foreign related parties: prepare a separate Form 5472 for each party with reportable transactions.

**4. A dormant LLC had no sales.** No revenue does not mean no transaction. A formation payment, bank-account deposit, registered-agent bill paid personally, or closing distribution can require the filing. A genuinely inactive LLC with no reportable transaction may fall outside the annual requirement, but verify the full ledger before concluding that nothing happened.

## How is the filing package prepared and delivered?

The package must be complete, signed, and sent to the dedicated IRS Ogden PIN Unit; a foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot e-file Form 5472.

1. **Reconcile the related-party ledger.** Separate customer and supplier activity from contributions, distributions, loans, owner-paid costs, and payments involving other related parties.
2. **Prepare the pro forma Form 1120.** Complete the limited identifying fields required for the disregarded entity and write **"Foreign-owned U.S. DE"** across the top.
3. **Prepare one Form 5472 per transacting related party.** Complete Part II with the correct HKID or entity identifier, plus the required reference ID when no US TIN appears on line 4b(1).
4. **Attach the Part V and exchange-rate schedules.** State the transaction type, direction, date, original currency, rate basis, and US-dollar amount.
5. **Fax at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737, or mail it.** The mailing address is Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201.
6. **Keep proof and the final package.** Retain the timestamped fax confirmation or mailing evidence with the exact signed copy and supporting records.

For a calendar-year LLC, the 2025 package was due **15 April 2026**. A Form 7004 sent to the same PIN Unit by that regular due date extended the filing to **15 October 2026**. The [Form 5472 deadline guide](/blog/form-5472-deadline-2026) explains the calendar.

US-formed LLCs are currently exempt from BOI reporting under FinCEN's interim final rule effective 26 March 2025. That exemption does not change the IRS filing. State annual reports, income-tax returns, sales tax, and Hong Kong filings also remain separate questions.

## What should a Hong Kong owner do after a missed deadline?

File the missing package promptly and assess whether a reasonable-cause statement is supportable under Treasury Regulation §1.6038A-4(b). Reasonable cause is fact-specific and relief is not automatic; a statement should describe the actual events, compliance steps, and supporting evidence rather than rely on a template excuse.

The $25,000 exposure applies separately to each missing form and year. If the IRS has already sent a notice, the 90-day continuation timetable makes a quick, accurate response especially important. Our [late Form 5472 guide](/blog/form-5472-filed-late-never-filed) explains the catch-up sequence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Hong Kong resident need Form 5472 for a US LLC?

Yes, when a foreign-owned US single-member LLC has a reportable transaction with its owner or another related party. Contributions, distributions, loans, and owner-paid costs commonly trigger the filing even when the LLC has no revenue.

### What FTIN does a Hong Kong individual use on Form 5472?

Use the individual's HKID number on Part II line 4b(3). If the owner has no FTIN, enter "None" or "N/A" rather than leaving the box blank.

### Is a reference ID required when I provide my HKID?

Yes, if line 4b(1) has no US identifying number. Put an LLC-assigned reference ID on line 4b(2) and reuse the same identifier every filing year. The reference ID is additional to the HKID FTIN.

### Does the US-China tax treaty apply to Hong Kong residents?

No. The US-China income tax treaty does not extend to the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. Form 5472 remains required based on the LLC's ownership and related-party transactions.

### Are Amazon sales reported on Form 5472?

Ordinary customer sales are not reported merely because Amazon collected them. Owner funding, owner withdrawals, and transactions with a related Hong Kong company are reportable. US-held inventory can create separate tax questions.

### Does a dormant Hong Kong-owned LLC still file?

Often, because formation funding, bank deposits, owner-paid fees, or closing transfers are reportable. An LLC with no revenue can still file. Review every movement before treating the entity as having no reportable transactions.

### Can a foreign-owned US LLC e-file Form 5472?

No. A foreign-owned US disregarded entity must fax or mail Form 5472 with its pro forma Form 1120 to the dedicated IRS Ogden PIN Unit.
