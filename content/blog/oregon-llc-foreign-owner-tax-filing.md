**A foreign-owned Oregon LLC should separate entity maintenance, Oregon tax obligations, and federal reporting. The LLC generally has an Oregon annual report; commercial activity can require Oregon Corporate Activity Tax registration or filing; and related-party transactions can trigger federal Form 5472. Oregon's lack of a general sales tax does not remove the other layers.**

The word “foreign” also has two meanings here. Oregon may call an LLC foreign because it was formed in another state, while Form 5472 focuses on ownership by a non-US person.

If a non-US owner funded, withdrew from, lent to, or paid expenses for the LLC, [start the federal Form 5472 package](/start) while resolving Oregon compliance separately.

## What state and federal filings can an Oregon LLC with a foreign owner have?

Separate six layers: the Oregon annual report, Corporate Activity Tax, income or excise tax, state and local taxes, the federal Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, and the owner's federal return. No single Oregon tax return settles every layer, and Form5472 Prep covers only the federal Form 5472 package for the standard profile.

| Layer | Core question | Filing authority | Form5472 Prep scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entity registration and annual report | Is the Oregon registration active and current? | Oregon Secretary of State | Not included |
| Corporate Activity Tax | How much commercial activity is sourced to Oregon? | Oregon Department of Revenue | Not included |
| Income or excise tax | Does the entity or owner have an Oregon tax filing position? | Oregon Department of Revenue | Not included |
| Specialized state and local taxes | Does the business have payroll, lodging, transit, property, or local exposure? | State and local agencies | Not included |
| Form 5472 + pro forma Form 1120 | Did the foreign-owned US disregarded entity have a related-party transaction? | IRS | Included for the standard profile |
| Owner federal return | Does the owner have US-source or effectively connected income? | IRS | Not included |

No single “Oregon LLC tax return” settles every row.

## What does foreign mean for an Oregon LLC with a non-US owner?

Foreign has two meanings. Under Oregon entity law, a foreign LLC is formed outside Oregon and registered to do business there. For Form 5472, the question is whether a non-US person owns the entity. The two definitions are independent of each other, so verify the entity's exact registration category in the official system.

The Oregon Secretary of State warns that an Oregon corporation or LLC must file an annual report and currently lists a $100 fee in its [official annual-report guidance](https://sos.oregon.gov/business/pages/dont-be-misled.aspx).

An LLC formed outside Oregon that registers to do business in Oregon is a foreign LLC under state entity law. Oregon's official [Business Xpress license directory](https://apps.oregon.gov/SOS/LicenseDirectory/LicenseDetail/6) currently lists a $275 registration fee and a $275 renewal fee for a foreign non-Oregon LLC.

Those definitions are independent of the owner's citizenship or tax residence:

| Entity and owner | Oregon state-law status | Federal Form 5472 ownership screen |
|---|---|---|
| Oregon LLC owned by a [French resident](/blog/form-5472-france-residents-us-llc) | Domestic Oregon LLC | Foreign-owned US entity |
| Delaware LLC owned by a US citizen and registered in Oregon | Foreign non-Oregon LLC | Not foreign-owned for Form 5472 |
| Delaware LLC owned by a French resident and registered in Oregon | Foreign non-Oregon LLC | Foreign-owned US entity |

Verify current fees and the entity's exact registration category in the official system before paying.

## What are Oregon's Corporate Activity Tax thresholds?

Oregon guidance currently describes three levels of Oregon commercial activity: $750,000 or less is generally excluded from CAT requirements, more than $750,000 means registering for CAT, and more than $1 million means filing a CAT return and paying CAT. Sourcing and exclusions can change the result, so near a threshold consult an Oregon tax professional.

Oregon's [Corporate Activity Tax guidance](https://www.oregon.gov/DOR/programs/businesses/Pages/corporate-activity-tax.aspx) currently uses three important commercial-activity levels:

| Oregon commercial activity | Current CAT consequence |
|---|---|
| $750,000 or less | Excluded from CAT requirements under the general threshold summary |
| More than $750,000 | Register for CAT |
| More than $1 million | File a CAT return and pay CAT |

The Department currently states that CAT is $250 plus 0.57% of taxable commercial activity over $1 million after the statutory subtraction. Registration and filing thresholds are based on Oregon commercial activity, not simply the LLC's bank balance or worldwide revenue.

Sourcing, exclusions, unitary groups, agency relationships, and the subtraction calculation can materially change the result. Businesses approaching either threshold should use an Oregon tax professional.

## No general sales tax is not no state tax

The Oregon Department of Revenue's [sales-tax page](https://www.oregon.gov/dor/programs/businesses/pages/sales-tax.aspx) confirms that Oregon does not have a general sales, use, or transaction tax. That does not cancel CAT, income or excise tax, payroll withholding, statewide transit tax, property tax, lodging taxes, or local obligations.

An Oregon e-commerce business can therefore have no general sales-tax collection in Oregon and still need annual-report, CAT, payroll, or federal filings. Sales into other states can also create those states' sales-tax duties.

## What triggers federal Form 5472 for an Oregon LLC?

A foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when it has a reportable transaction, such as owner contributions and distributions, loans, owner-paid expenses, or related-party services, rents, royalties, or property. It does not depend on reaching Oregon's CAT thresholds, so an LLC with no customer revenue can still have reporting.

Under the [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472), a foreign-owned US disregarded entity files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when it has a reportable transaction. Common transactions include:

- owner contributions and distributions;
- loans, advances, repayments, and interest;
- owner-paid LLC expenses and reimbursements;
- related-party services, rents, royalties, or property; and
- certain formation, acquisition, disposition, and dissolution activity.

High Oregon customer revenue is not itself a Form 5472 transaction when the customers are unrelated.

## A clean compliance file

Keep four folders:

1. Oregon formation, registration, registered-agent, and annual-report records;
2. Oregon-sourced receipts and CAT calculations;
3. federal income-tax and owner-level analysis; and
4. the Form 5472 related-party ledger with contributions, distributions, loans, reimbursements, services, and property transfers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an Oregon LLC have an annual report?

Yes. The post cites Oregon Secretary of State guidance saying an Oregon corporation or LLC must file an annual report, with the current fee verified in the official system when filing.

### Does Oregon's lack of sales tax remove state tax duties?

No. Oregon has no general sales, use, or transaction tax, but CAT, income or excise tax, payroll withholding, transit tax, property tax, lodging taxes, and local obligations can still matter.

### Do Oregon CAT thresholds affect Form 5472?

No. The federal Form 5472 trigger is separate from Oregon commercial-activity thresholds. An LLC with no customer revenue can still have Form 5472 reporting if the owner funded it.

### What records should an Oregon foreign owner keep?

Keep Oregon entity records, Oregon-sourced receipts and CAT calculations, federal income-tax analysis, and a separate Form 5472 related-party ledger for contributions, distributions, loans, reimbursements, services, and property transfers.
