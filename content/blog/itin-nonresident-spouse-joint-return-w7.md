**A nonresident spouse who cannot obtain an SSN may apply for an ITIN with Form W-7 when a joint federal return is appropriate. The package must align the W-7, joint return, identity evidence, and any required election statement; it does not decide whether the election is beneficial.**

The initial nonresident-spouse choice requires a joint return and generally brings both spouses' worldwide income into U.S. reporting while it is in effect. It is a personal cross-border tax decision, not an ITIN shortcut. [IRS nonresident-spouse guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/nonresident-spouse).

If a qualified adviser has already confirmed the joint-return approach, this guide helps assemble the ITIN side. For the broader eligibility question, start with [when a nonresident actually needs an ITIN](/blog/when-nonresident-actually-needs-itin).

## Confirm the decision before preparing Form W-7

Form W-7 reason **e** covers a resident or nonresident alien spouse who is ineligible for an SSN and is electing to file a joint U.S. return with a U.S.-citizen or resident-alien spouse. The form instructions require the U.S. spouse's full name and SSN or ITIN in the designated space. [Instructions for Form W-7](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7).

The election requires a statement signed by both spouses and attached to the first joint return. Treaty effects, prior returns, residency facts, and the election's continuation or ending rules can matter. [IRS nonresident-spouse guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/nonresident-spouse).

| Situation | ITIN-package implication | Decision that belongs elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| A qualified adviser confirms a first-year joint-return election | Use the return-based W-7 package and include the required election statement with the joint return | Whether the election is advantageous or valid for the couple |
| The spouse is eligible for an SSN | Do not substitute Form W-7 for the SSN process | Immigration and employment eligibility questions |
| The spouse needs an ITIN for a different IRS exception | Use the exception's evidence rules, not this joint-return checklist | Whether the exception applies to the income or treaty claim |
| The couple has not decided whether to elect joint treatment | Pause the W-7 package tied to a joint return | Worldwide-income, treaty, state, and future-year consequences |

## Put the package together in the right order

For a first-time applicant, the IRS generally requires Form W-7, the federal return for which the ITIN is needed, and identity/foreign-status evidence. Attach each W-7 to the front of the return and leave the ITIN applicant's SSN area blank. [IRS ITIN application guidance](https://www.irs.gov/tin/itin/how-to-apply-for-an-itin).

For the spouse described here, the working package normally has four parts:

1. **Joint federal return.** Prepare it only after the election decision; leave the ITIN applicant's SSN field blank.
2. **Form W-7.** Use reason e only when it accurately describes the spouse's basis, and enter the U.S. spouse's requested identifying information.
3. **Identity and foreign-status evidence.** A current passport is usually the only stand-alone document. Without it, the instructions generally require two accepted documents, including a photo for most applicants. [Instructions for Form W-7](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7).
4. **Election statement and return attachments.** The first-year choice needs both spouses' signed statement with the required declaration and identifying information.

File the complete package by the return due date. An ITIN is not needed just to submit an extension or estimated-tax payment. [Instructions for Form W-7](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7).

## Select a document-authentication route without assuming one is guaranteed

The IRS lists designated Taxpayer Assistance Centers, eligible VITA sites, and Certifying Acceptance Agents (CAAs) as options that can authenticate documents in some situations. CAA authority has limits: for dependents, it covers only passports and birth certificates; foreign military ID cards are excluded. [IRS application-route comparison](https://www.irs.gov/tin/itin/how-to-apply-for-an-itin).

| Route | What the IRS says it can do | Important limit |
|---|---|---|
| Direct mail | Accepts originals or issuing-agency-certified copies with the package | The IRS says mailed supporting documents are returned to the W-7 mailing address within 60 days |
| ITIN-service TAC | Authenticates most documents and returns them at the appointment | Appointment required; designated U.S. locations only |
| CAA | Authenticates most permitted documents and may help prepare/review W-7 | Document limits apply; fees and appointment process vary |

## Run a final consistency check

Before sending, compare the spouse's legal name, date of birth, citizenship, passport information, foreign address, and prior ITIN history across the package. Supporting documents must be consistent with the W-7 fields. A marriage certificate can explain a name change but does not replace identity evidence. [Instructions for Form W-7](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7).

Keep copies of the W-7, return, election statement, document list, and delivery evidence. The IRS says to allow 7 weeks for a status notice, or 9–11 weeks in January 15–April 30 or from overseas. CP566 requests information; CP567 requires resubmission. [IRS ITIN application guidance](https://www.irs.gov/tin/itin/how-to-apply-for-an-itin).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a nonresident spouse submit Form W-7 by itself before filing jointly?

Usually no. A return-based W-7 is generally attached to the return for which the ITIN is needed, unless a specific exception applies. Review the joint-return election first.

### Does Form W-7 reason e itself make the couple eligible to file jointly?

No. Reason e identifies the ITIN basis; it does not make the section 6013 choice, resolve eligibility, or determine worldwide-income consequences.

### Can a CAA always authenticate a nonresident spouse's passport remotely?

Do not assume that. The IRS does not establish one universal remote workflow; confirm the document-review process and limits with the CAA first.
