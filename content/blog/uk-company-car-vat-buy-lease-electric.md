In the UK, buying an electric company car does not automatically let a business recover the purchase VAT. **Car purchase VAT is generally blocked**, with exceptions such as qualifying business-only use without private availability. **A business lease normally has a 50% VAT block** covering private use, with the remaining recovery subject to the normal rules.

This guide concerns a UK VAT-registered business under the normal VAT rules. It does not determine whether a US LLC is the correct VAT claimant, must register in the UK, or is treated as a company for another UK tax. Confirm those facts before using the examples. These are UK rules, not an EU-wide car VAT policy.

## Start with the invoice and the claimant

[HMRC's business-expense VAT guidance](https://www.gov.uk/charge-reclaim-record-vat/reclaim-vat-business-expenses) requires valid VAT invoices and supporting records. Partial exemption can reduce recovery, and the Flat Rate Scheme has different rules. An invoice described as “business” does not by itself establish recoverable input tax.

For an international founder, write down the contracting purchaser, registered VAT person, vehicle owner or lessee and payer. If those are different, resolve why. A US EIN is not evidence of UK VAT registration or input-tax entitlement.

Before applying any percentage, check whether the invoice actually shows VAT. There is no purchase VAT to reclaim from a price merely assumed to be VAT-inclusive when the transaction does not supply deductible VAT.

## Purchase and lease recovery use different tests

HMRC's [VAT Notice 700/64, section 3](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-on-motoring-expenses-notice-70064) describes the purchase block and exceptions. For the business-only exception, the car must not be available for anyone's private use. Choosing not to drive privately is different from preventing private availability. Electric propulsion does not create a separate purchase-VAT exception in this guidance.

The normal lease restriction is explained in [HMRC manual VIT53300](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-input-tax/vit53300). The 50% block applies to the relevant leasing charge; the rest remains subject to business-use and partial-exemption rules. The manual also explains invoices that identify leasing and maintenance charges separately.

For a contract with bundled servicing, ask the supplier for the actual supply and invoice breakdown before applying the rental restriction to every line.

## An invoice example that avoids a false refund promise

These hypothetical figures assume a fully taxable UK VAT-registered business, ordinary passenger cars available for private use, valid invoices and the normal rules. The arithmetic assumes 20% VAT shown on the supplied invoices. It does not compare the lifetime economics of buying and leasing.

| Invoice | Net charge | VAT shown | Recovery assumed in this example | Cost after that recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Car purchase with private availability | £30,000 | £6,000 | £0 under the purchase block | £36,000 purchase outlay |
| One month's qualifying car rental | £500 | £100 | £50 after the normal 50% lease block | £550 for that month |

The rental calculation is **£500 + £100 − (£100 × 50%) = £550**. It does not mean the company recovers half the £600 invoice, or that leasing beats buying. The purchase row and one rental month cover different periods and commitments.

A real comparison needs the deposit, all rentals, maintenance terms, excess mileage, financing, disposal value and the business's actual VAT recovery. Keep the VAT decision separate from income-tax deductions, capital allowances and any employee benefit calculation.

## EV charging depends on who receives the supply

Under [VAT Notice 700/64, section 8](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-on-motoring-expenses-notice-70064), business-use charging at work or public charging points can support recovery subject to the normal rules. HMRC distinguishes an employee's home electricity supply: the employer cannot reclaim that VAT under the guidance reviewed. Sole proprietors and partners have a separate business-use home-charging route.

Record who received the electricity supply, where charging occurred and the business-use allocation. Do not treat an employee reimbursement as proof that the employer received a VAT-bearing supply.

## Assemble a car VAT review file

The following is a suggested adviser handoff, rather than a list of extra statutory forms:

| Record | What it helps resolve |
|---|---|
| Contract and VAT invoice | Purchaser, supply, VAT shown and itemized charges |
| Registration and legal-entity details | Whether the claimant matches the business in the transaction |
| Private-use policy and actual arrangements | Whether a purchase exception is supportable |
| Lease schedule and maintenance breakdown | Which charges need the lease restriction reviewed |
| Dated mileage and charging records | Business use and the recipient of electricity supplies |
| Owner payments and repayments | Funding events to reconcile separately for US reporting |

An adviser can then explain the accepted treatment next to each invoice instead of leaving a single “car VAT” percentage in the ledger. If use changes later, revisit the treatment rather than carrying the original assumption forward indefinitely.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an electric car's purchase VAT automatically recoverable?

No. Apply the car purchase block and its actual exceptions. An EV's treatment for another tax does not establish VAT recovery.

### Can I claim half the VAT on every car-related invoice?

No. The normal 50% restriction concerns relevant car leasing charges. Purchase, maintenance and electricity supplies have their own treatment, and the business's VAT position can impose further limits.
