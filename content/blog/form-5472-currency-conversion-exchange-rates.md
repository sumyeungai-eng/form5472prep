## What does Form 5472 require for foreign currency?

The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that Part IV and Part VI amounts are reported in US dollars and that a schedule showing the exchange rates used must be attached. Part V descriptions should also present reported transaction amounts consistently in USD.

The source ledger should preserve both values:

| Date | Original amount and currency | Rate source and convention | USD amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2026-02-10 | EUR 10,000 | Published spot rate on transfer date | Calculated result |
| 2026-07-18 | GBP 2,500 | Published spot rate on reimbursement date | Calculated result |

Never overwrite the original amount. The conversion should be reproducible from the source record.

## Should you use a spot rate or yearly average?

The IRS [foreign-currency page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates) says the prevailing spot rate generally applies when an item is received, paid or accrued. It also says the IRS has no single official exchange rate and generally accepts any posted rate used consistently.

Use this practical decision rule:

- **Large or irregular owner transfers:** transaction-date spot rate gives the clearest match.
- **Recurring, similar payments:** a documented consistent convention may be supportable.
- **Loan balances:** track each advance and repayment rather than converting only the closing balance.
- **Multiple-rate countries:** use the rate that applies to the actual facts and payment channel.

A yearly average published by the IRS can be a useful reference, but it should not automatically replace transaction-date treatment for discrete capital transfers.

## How do you perform the conversion?

The direction depends on how the quoted rate is expressed. On the IRS yearly-average table, rates show units of foreign currency per US dollar. Under that convention:

**foreign-currency amount ÷ listed rate = US-dollar amount**

Example framework: if a table showed 1.50 units of foreign currency per USD, a 15,000-unit transaction would convert to $10,000. That number is illustrative; use the actual rate for the currency, date and tax year.

For a source quoting USD per foreign-currency unit, the arithmetic reverses. Label the convention to prevent a plausible-looking but inverted result.

## What should the exchange-rate attachment show?

Create one attachment per Form 5472 or a clearly cross-referenced entity schedule containing:

1. Related-party name and reference ID.
2. Transaction date or covered period.
3. Original amount and currency.
4. Rate, source URL or publication and quote convention.
5. Calculation and USD result.
6. Form 5472 line or Part V category.

Our proprietary four-column workpaper—date, original value, rate evidence, USD value—keeps every total auditable. Add the form line as a fifth column when there are many categories.

## How do exchange rates affect contributions and withdrawals?

Convert each contribution or withdrawal when it occurs. Do not net all cash moving in and out before translation; Form 5472 reporting may need the gross flow and transaction description.

If the owner contributed EUR 20,000 and later withdrew EUR 5,000, record two events with their own dates and rates. The [owner-loans and contributions guide](/blog/form-5472-owner-loans-contributions-reimbursements) explains the classification step before conversion.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the IRS publish an official exchange rate?

The IRS says it has no single official rate and generally accepts a published rate used consistently. Its yearly-average table is a reference, not a universal mandate.

### Must Form 5472 amounts be in US dollars?

Yes. Report the form amounts in US dollars and attach a schedule showing the exchange rates used for foreign-currency transactions.

### Can every transaction use the annual average rate?

Not automatically. Discrete owner transfers are often better matched to transaction-date spot rates. Choose a supportable method based on the facts and apply it consistently.

### What happens if the conversion was inverted?

The USD total may be materially wrong. Recalculate using the quote convention and consider an amended filing if a submitted Form 5472 is materially incorrect.

### Should exchange-rate records be retained?

Yes. Keep the original statement, rate source, calculation and filed attachment as long as they may be relevant to tax administration.
