**Report Form 5472 amounts in US dollars and attach a schedule showing the exchange rates used. The IRS generally points to the spot rate when an item is received, paid or accrued and accepts a consistently used published rate. The method should match the transaction pattern and be documented, not selected for a preferred result.**

Currency conversion is part of the filing workpapers, not an afterthought. The [IRS yearly average currency exchange rates page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates) says the IRS has no official exchange rate, generally accepts any posted exchange rate used consistently, and currently lists 2025 yearly average rates including Euro Zone euro 0.886, United Kingdom pound 0.759, Canada dollar 1.398 and India rupee 87.133 per U.S. dollar. ## What does Form 5472 require for foreign currency?

Form 5472 requires reported amounts to be stated in U.S. dollars when the form requests transaction totals. The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) say line 1f and line 1h use the total value in U.S. dollars of foreign related-party transactions reported in Parts IV and VI, and, for a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, Part V.

The return should preserve two records: the source transaction in original currency and the U.S.-dollar amount reported on the form or attachment. Never overwrite the original bank-statement amount. The IRS or a later preparer should be able to reproduce the conversion without trusting a typed total.

| Date | Original amount and currency | Rate source and convention | U.S.-dollar amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2025-02-10 | EUR 10,000 | IRS 2025 yearly average, 0.886 EUR per USD | EUR 10,000 / 0.886 = USD 11,286.68 |
| 2025-07-18 | GBP 2,500 | IRS 2025 yearly average, 0.759 GBP per USD | GBP 2,500 / 0.759 = USD 3,293.81 |

Those examples use the IRS yearly-average convention only to show the arithmetic. The facts decide whether a yearly average, spot rate or other published rate is the better support for a specific transaction.

## Should Form 5472 use a spot rate or a yearly average?

Form 5472 exchange-rate method should fit the transaction pattern and be applied consistently. The IRS currency page says the prevailing spot rate generally applies when an item is received, paid or accrued, but also says the IRS has no single official rate and generally accepts a posted rate used consistently.

Use this decision framework:

| Transaction pattern | Better starting point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One large capital contribution | Spot rate on transfer date | The date and amount are discrete and easy to document |
| One owner distribution | Spot rate on payment date | The transaction is a specific cash movement |
| Monthly recurring owner reimbursements | Consistent published monthly or yearly method may be supportable | The pattern is repetitive and similar |
| Loan advances and repayments | Transaction-date schedule by advance and repayment | Netting can hide principal, repayment and interest facts |
| Multiple-rate country | Rate matching the actual payment channel | IRS says use the rate that applies to the facts and circumstances |

A yearly average is convenient, but convenience is not the legal standard. A method is stronger when the rate source, quote convention and reason for using it are written into the workpapers before the return is assembled.

## How do you convert foreign currency into U.S. dollars?

The conversion depends on how the rate is quoted. On the IRS yearly-average table, rates are shown as units of foreign currency per U.S. dollar. Under that convention, the arithmetic is:

1. Start with the foreign-currency amount.
2. Find the applicable yearly average rate for the currency and year.
3. Divide the foreign-currency amount by the listed rate.
4. Round consistently for the workpaper and reported total.
5. Label the rate as foreign currency per U.S. dollar.

Original element: the "direction test" prevents inverted exchange rates. Ask which result is economically plausible before filing. If EUR 10,000 converts near USD 11,287 using a 0.886 EUR-per-USD rate, multiplying instead would produce USD 8,860, which is directionally wrong under the IRS table convention because one euro was worth more than one U.S. dollar on that yearly-average basis.

For sources quoted as U.S. dollars per foreign-currency unit, the arithmetic reverses. A rate source that says 1 GBP = 1.318 USD would use multiplication. The rate source and convention matter as much as the number.

## What should the exchange-rate attachment show?

The exchange-rate attachment should let a reader trace each reported total from bank statement to Form 5472 line. A bare note saying "converted to USD" is not enough.

Use one attachment per Form 5472 or one cross-referenced entity schedule containing:

1. Related-party name and reference ID.
2. Transaction date or covered period.
3. Original amount and currency.
4. Rate, source URL or publication, and quote convention.
5. Calculation and U.S.-dollar result.
6. Form 5472 line or Part V category.
7. Notes explaining why that rate method fits the transaction pattern.

For the 2025 tax year, a calendar-year foreign-owned U.S. DE package is generally due 15 April 2026 under the [IRS Form 1120 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120), or 15 October 2026 if [Form 7004](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-7004) was timely filed. Build the exchange-rate schedule before signature, not after the fax receipt arrives.

## How do exchange rates affect contributions, withdrawals and loans?

Each contribution, withdrawal and loan movement should be converted as its own event unless a documented recurring-method convention is supportable. Do not net all money moving in and out before translation. Form 5472 reporting may need gross flows and transaction descriptions, especially for Part V.

Example: an owner contributes EUR 20,000 and later withdraws EUR 5,000. The workpaper should show two rows, two dates, two rates and two U.S.-dollar results. Netting to EUR 15,000 hides the distribution and can make the attachment inconsistent with the legal transaction history.

Loans need even more care. The Form 5472 instructions provide line-specific rules for amounts borrowed and amounts loaned, including outstanding balance or monthly average treatment for amounts loaned. Keep principal advances, repayments and interest separate before converting the totals. The [owner-loans and contributions guide](/blog/form-5472-owner-loans-contributions-reimbursements) explains the classification step before the exchange-rate step.

## Which exchange-rate mistakes create amendment risk?

Exchange-rate mistakes create amendment risk when they materially change the reported U.S.-dollar amount or obscure the transaction category. A small rounding difference is different from using the wrong direction, the wrong currency, or a rate that cannot be tied to any source.

High-risk errors include:

1. Multiplying when the published table requires division.
2. Using a 2026 rate for a 2025 tax-year transaction.
3. Converting a net annual amount instead of each contribution and distribution.
4. Using an unexplained bank rate for one transaction and a published average for another similar transaction.
5. Omitting the rate schedule from the signed package.
6. Reporting original-currency amounts on lines that require U.S. dollars.

If a filed Form 5472 contains a material conversion error, review the [amended Form 5472 guide](/blog/amended-form-5472-correcting-errors) before assuming the mistake can wait until next year.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the IRS publish an official exchange rate?

No. The IRS says it has no official exchange rate and generally accepts any posted exchange rate used consistently. Its yearly-average table is a reference, not a universal mandate.

### Must Form 5472 amounts be in U.S. dollars?

Yes. Form totals should be in U.S. dollars when the form asks for dollar amounts. Keep original-currency records and attach a rate schedule.

### Can every transaction use the annual average rate?

Not automatically. Discrete owner transfers are often better matched to transaction-date spot rates. Choose a supportable method based on the facts and apply it consistently.

### How do I use the IRS yearly-average table?

For the IRS table, divide the foreign-currency amount by the listed rate to convert to U.S. dollars. Label the year, currency and source.

### What happens if the conversion was inverted?

The U.S.-dollar total may be materially wrong. Recalculate using the quote convention and consider an amended filing if a submitted Form 5472 is materially incorrect.

### Should exchange-rate records be retained?

Yes. Keep the original statement, rate source, calculation and filed attachment as long as they may be relevant to tax administration.

### Does customer revenue need currency conversion for Form 5472?

Usually not merely because customers paid the LLC. Form 5472 focuses on related-party transactions. Owner contributions, distributions, loans and reimbursements are the usual conversion focus.
