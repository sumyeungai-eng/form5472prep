## What is the Form 5472 statute of limitations rule?

The practical rule is that the assessment clock does not properly close while required Form 5472 information remains unfiled. IRC section 6501(c)(8) is the statute that creates the problem.

Cornell's published text of [26 U.S.C. section 6501](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6501) states that when information required under section 6038A has not been furnished, the time for assessment of tax for the return, event or period to which the information relates does not expire before three years after the required information is furnished. The same subsection limits the broad rule to related items if the failure was due to reasonable cause and not willful neglect.

Translated: filing the missing Form 5472 is what starts the three-year post-filing period. Leaving the year untouched does not make the old year cleaner. It leaves the IRS with an argument that the assessment period remains open.

## Can the IRS penalize a missing Form 5472 from years ago?

Yes. The Form 5472 penalty is tied to the failure to file the required information return for that year. There is no practical "just skip the old years" option for a foreign-owned US disregarded LLC that had reportable transactions.

The penalty under section 6038A(d) applies per form, per year. If the IRS sends a notice and the failure continues more than 90 days after the notice, the instructions state that an additional **$25,000** applies for each 30-day period, or fraction of one, that the failure continues. Those are legal penalty amounts, not estimates of what the IRS will do in a specific case.

The IRS does not publish a clean, reliable public statistic showing how often it assesses Form 5472 penalties for every delinquent foreign-owned LLC fact pattern. Do not build a filing strategy around internet anecdotes about enforcement frequency. Build the strategy around the statute, the instructions, and the records you can prove.

## What happens to an owner with five unfiled years?

An owner with five unfiled years should assume each year needs its own filing package, its own Form 5472, and its own supporting statement. Filing the newest year only does not repair the earlier missing information.

If the owner files all five packages in 2026, the practical position changes. The missing information has been furnished, the owner has created proof of submission, and the three-year period described in section 6501(c)(8) begins to run from the furnishing date for the information. That filing does not guarantee penalty relief. It changes the posture from unfiled to filed.

## How does filing start the clock?

Filing starts the clock by furnishing the missing information. For a foreign-owned US disregarded entity, that means a signed pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached, sent under the method required by the instructions.

The IRS instructions say the foreign-owned US disregarded entity has no income tax return filing requirement of its own, but must file a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached. The instructions also say "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" should be written across the top, and that the package is filed by fax at 855-887-7737 or by mail to the Ogden PIN Unit.

Proof matters because the IRS may not send a conventional acceptance notice. A timestamped fax receipt, mailing receipt, and a copy of the exact package are the records that show when the information was furnished.

## What records should a foreign-owned LLC keep?

A foreign-owned LLC should keep enough records to prove the related-party transactions reported on Form 5472 and to support any reasonable-cause statement for late years. The Form 5472 instructions identify record maintenance requirements under Regulations section 1.6038A-3 as part of the compliance regime.

For a small LLC, the practical file usually includes:

- Formation documents and EIN confirmation.
- Bank statements for the LLC and owner transfers.
- Owner contribution and distribution ledger.
- Loan agreements or written notes for owner loans.
- Part V statement workpapers.
- Copies of every Form 5472 package filed.
- Fax or mailing proof for each year.
- Adviser or formation-agent correspondence if reasonable cause depends on reliance or lack of notice.

Keep the records by tax year. A catch-up package with five years in one folder is harder to defend than five year-specific files with clean transaction support.

## How should you catch up missing Form 5472 years?

The safest practical sequence is to identify every unfiled year first, then file complete packages consistently.

1. **List every year the LLC existed.** Start with the formation year, even if the LLC had no revenue.
2. **Identify reportable transactions by year.** Owner capital, owner draws, loans, owner-paid expenses and related-party service payments are the usual items.
3. **Prepare each year's Form 5472 package.** Use a pro forma Form 1120, Form 5472, and a Part V statement for that year.
4. **Use consistent owner identifiers.** If the foreign owner has no US identifying number, use a reference ID consistently from year to year as described in the instructions.
5. **Attach a reasonable-cause statement when appropriate.** The IRS [delinquent international information return procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/delinquent-international-information-return-submission-procedures) say penalties may still be assessed without considering the statement during processing, so keep a copy for later correspondence.
6. **Fax or mail and keep proof.** Retain the confirmation for each package.
7. **Respond separately to any IRS notice.** A late filing package and a penalty notice response are related, but they are not the same document.

The point of the catch-up is not pretending the late years were timely. The point is furnishing the missing information, stopping the unfiled status from continuing, and preserving the facts needed for penalty abatement.

## Should you skip old Form 5472 years because no tax was due?

No. "No US income tax" and "no Form 5472" are different conclusions. A foreign-owned US disregarded LLC can owe no US federal income tax and still have a Form 5472 obligation because money moved between the LLC and the foreign owner.

The oldest year is often the formation year. That year commonly has an owner capital contribution, EIN setup, bank funding, or owner-paid startup cost. Those transactions can be enough to require a Form 5472 even if the LLC had no customers and no profit.

Skipping old years also weakens the reasonable-cause story. A filing history that says "we discovered the issue and filed every missing year" is cleaner than one that says "we discovered the issue and chose which missing years to disclose."

## Frequently asked questions

### How far back can the IRS go for missing Form 5472?

There is no safe fixed cutoff. IRC section 6501(c)(8) can keep the assessment period open until three years after the required section 6038A information is furnished.

### Does filing late start the statute of limitations?

Filing late can start the three-year period described in section 6501(c)(8) because the missing information has been furnished. It does not make the filing timely or guarantee penalty relief.

### Can I file only the most recent missing year?

That is risky. Each year with a reportable transaction has its own Form 5472 obligation. Filing one year does not furnish the missing information for earlier years.

### Does no income mean the old year is safe?

No. Form 5472 reports related-party transactions, not income. A capital contribution, owner draw, owner loan or owner-paid expense can create the obligation even when the LLC had no revenue.

### Will a reasonable-cause letter stop the penalty?

Not necessarily. The IRS delinquent procedures say penalties may be assessed without considering the attached reasonable-cause statement during processing. The statement may need to be resubmitted in response to IRS correspondence.

### Should I wait until the IRS contacts me?

Usually no. Waiting leaves the years unfiled and weakens a voluntary-correction narrative. If you have missing years, gather records and file complete packages as soon as practicable.

### Is there a public statistic for Form 5472 enforcement rates?

No reliable IRS statistic gives an enforcement percentage for every foreign-owned LLC fact pattern. Treat enforcement anecdotes as anecdotes, not a filing strategy.
