**When a payer asks a foreign-owned US LLC for Form W-8BEN-E, the LLC's foreign owner completes it, not the LLC. The owner's legal name goes on line 1. The instructions signpost line 10, the reference line, for the LLC's own name, and allow line 3 only conditionally or for information.**

Deciding *which* withholding certificate to hand over is one question. Completing it is a different one, and it is where the mistakes happen: the LLC's name typed on line 1, the LLC's EIN entered as a foreign tax number, line 3 misread. If you have not settled which form applies, read [W-8BEN or W-9 for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/w8ben-vs-w9-foreign-owned-llc) first. If the annual federal return is what you need prepared, [start the Form 5472 filing](/start).

## Who completes Form W-8BEN-E when a foreign-owned US LLC is asked for it?

The foreign entity that owns the LLC completes the form. The [IRS Instructions for Form W-8BEN-E](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8bene) state that "Generally, a disregarded entity does not submit this Form W-8BEN-E to a withholding agent." The owner supplies the documentation instead.

So the LLC is not the filer. A payer may have requested the form in the LLC's name, but the signature block is signed for the owner.

If the single owner is a foreign *individual* rather than a foreign company, Form W-8BEN-E is the wrong form. The individual uses Form W-8BEN, and the [Instructions for Form W-8BEN](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben) confirm that a disregarded entity's single owner "is considered the beneficial owner of income received by the disregarded entity."

## Whose name goes on line 1 of Form W-8BEN-E?

The owner's legal name goes on line 1. The W-8BEN-E instructions are explicit: "If you are a disregarded entity or branch, do not enter your business name." The legal name of the owner is entered instead, looking through multiple disregarded entities where more than one sits in the chain.

Typing the LLC's name on line 1 is the most common error on this form. It names the wrong party as beneficial owner of the income — the fact the form exists to certify.

## When does the LLC's name belong on line 3 of Form W-8BEN-E?

Two different routes lead to a name on line 3, and conflating them is where this line goes wrong.

**Route one, the conditional entry.** A disregarded entity receiving a withholdable payment enters its name on line 3 if it has "been assigned a GIIN associated with the legal name of the disregarded entity," is a "reporting Model 1 FFI or reporting Model 2 FFI," and is "not a hybrid entity using this form to claim treaty benefits."

A GIIN is a Global Intermediary Identification Number, issued to a registered foreign financial institution. An ordinary trading, consulting or e-commerce LLC has none, so route one is unavailable to it.

**Route two, the informational entry.** The same instructions then permit an entry with none of those conditions: where you report the LLC's name "for only information purposes (that is, the disregarded entity is not reported on line 1 or in Part II of this form), you may enter the disregarded entity's name on line 3."

So an ordinary foreign-owned LLC *may* name itself on line 3 for information, provided it is not reported on line 1 or in Part II. What it must not do is treat that entry as a statement that the LLC is the beneficial owner or the chapter 4 payee.

Hybrids are a separate case. Where a disregarded entity is a hybrid making a treaty claim, "you are treated as the beneficial owner and should be identified on line 1," and such an entity "is required to complete Form W-8BEN-E." A hybrid claim is a specialist position; confirm it with a tax adviser before signing.

## Which line do the instructions signpost for the LLC's name?

Line 10, the reference line. Where the LLC's legal name is not required on line 3, the instructions say "you may want to notify the withholding agent that you are a disregarded entity receiving a payment or maintaining an account by indicating the name of the disregarded entity on line 10."

The general line 10 instruction fits that job: it "may be used by you or by the withholding agent or FFI to include any referencing information that is useful to the withholding agent to document the beneficial owner," and adds that you "may also want to use line 10 to include the number of the account."

On Form W-8BEN, the individual-owner equivalent, line 7 "may be used by the filer of Form W-8BEN or by the withholding agent," and the line 1 instructions contemplate "including the name and account number of the disregarded entity on line 7."

Either way, the reference line ties a certificate in the owner's name to an account in the LLC's name without misstating the beneficial owner.

## Which lines differ between W-8BEN-E and W-8BEN?

| Item | W-8BEN-E (foreign entity owner) | W-8BEN (foreign individual owner) |
|---|---|---|
| Who signs | The foreign entity that owns the LLC | The foreign individual who owns the LLC |
| Name field | Line 1, owner's legal name; not the LLC's | Line 1, the individual's own name |
| Disregarded entity name | Line 3, conditionally with a GIIN and reporting Model 1 or 2 FFI status, or for information only | Not a separate name line |
| Reference / account matching | Line 10 reference line | Line 7, may carry the LLC name and account number |
| Entity classification | Line 4 | Not applicable to an individual |
| FATCA (chapter 4) status | Line 5 | Not applicable to an individual |
| Foreign tax identifying number | Line 9b | Line 6a, with a line 6b box where none is legally required |
| Treaty claim | Part III, lines 14a to 14c | Part II, lines 9 and 10 |

Line 9b asks for the foreign tax identifying number of the person named on line 1 — the foreign owner. The instructions tie the requirement to a situation: where the form documents the filer as holding a financial account at a US office of a financial institution and the filer receives US-source income reportable on Form 1042-S, the filer "must provide on line 9b the FTIN issued to you by the jurisdiction in which you are a tax resident identified on line 6," subject to listed exceptions. Line 9c is the box for a filer not legally required to obtain an FTIN.

The LLC's US EIN is not a foreign tax identifying number and does not belong on line 9b. One warning: the sentence "Do not enter the tax identifying number of your owner(s)" sits in the special instructions for a **hybrid entity making a treaty claim**, where the entity itself is the line 1 beneficial owner and "your owner(s)" means that entity's owners. It does not tell an ordinary owner-filer to omit their own FTIN.

## When is Form W-9 the correct form instead?

Form W-9 is for a US person. The [Instructions for Form W-9](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw9) state that a foreign person "may not provide a Form W-9," and its certifications, made under penalties of perjury, include that "The payee is a U.S. person."

Two conditions genuinely change the answer. If the single owner is a US person, the W-9 comes from "the U.S. owner of the disregarded entity and not the disregarded entity." If the LLC has made an entity-classification election it is no longer disregarded, and the W-9 instructions give line 3a a box and space for an LLC to note its classification as a corporation, S corporation or partnership. Confirm any election and its effective date before choosing a form.

Note that the W-9 instructions contain no passage on a disregarded entity whose owner is a *foreign* person; that rule lives in the W-8BEN and W-8BEN-E instructions. The full routing analysis is in [W-8BEN or W-9 for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/w8ben-vs-w9-foreign-owned-llc).

## How long does a completed W-8BEN-E stay valid?

Both forms generally run from the date of signature to the last day of the third succeeding calendar year. The W-8BEN-E instructions state the form "will remain valid for purposes of both chapters 3 and 4" through that date unless a change in circumstances makes the information incorrect, and note that under certain conditions a form remains in effect indefinitely absent a change of circumstances.

A change in circumstances that makes any information incorrect ends validity early, and not every change qualifies. The W-8BEN instructions require the filer to notify the withholding agent, payer or FFI "within 30 days of the change in circumstances" and to file a new form.

Address changes are the case most often got wrong. Where Form W-8BEN certifies foreign status, "a change of address to an address in the United States is a change in circumstances," but "Generally, a change of address within the same foreign country or to another foreign country is not a change in circumstances." Where the form claims treaty benefits, a move to the United States or outside the treaty country is a change in circumstances. Moving between two foreign addresses does not, by itself, require a fresh form.

Without a valid form on file, the W-8BEN instructions warn that income may be subject to "withholding at the foreign-person withholding rate of 30%."

## Does a W-8 form replace Form 5472?

No. A Form W-8 is given to a payer, platform, bank or withholding agent. Form 5472 is filed with the IRS, with a pro forma Form 1120, to report transactions between a foreign-owned US disregarded entity and its foreign related parties. Supplying a W-8BEN-E does not discharge the Form 5472 obligation, and filing Form 5472 does not document a payee for withholding purposes.

They also run on different clocks. A Form W-8 is refreshed when it expires or the facts change; Form 5472 is a per-tax-year filing. Owner funding, withdrawals, loans and owner-paid costs are reviewed for Form 5472 whichever certificate the payer holds. See [FTIN and reference ID on Form 5472](/blog/form-5472-ftin-reference-id-foreign-address).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the LLC or the owner sign Form W-8BEN-E?

The owner. The instructions state that a disregarded entity generally does not submit the form, and the owner supplies the documentation. The signature is given by a person authorised to sign for the foreign owner.

### Can the LLC's name go on line 1?

No. The instructions say that a disregarded entity or branch must not enter its business name on line 1. The legal name of the owner goes there instead, because the owner is the beneficial owner being certified.

### Should line 3 be completed for a normal trading LLC?

It may be, for information only. The conditional entry needs a GIIN and reporting Model 1 or 2 FFI status, which an ordinary LLC lacks. The instructions separately permit entering the name for information where the LLC is not reported on line 1 or in Part II.

### Where does the LLC's EIN go on Form W-8BEN-E?

Not on line 9b. That line carries the foreign tax identifying number issued to the line 1 owner by the jurisdiction on line 6, and a US EIN is not one. Read the line 6 to 8 instructions directly for US TIN questions.

### How does a payer match a W-8BEN-E to an account in the LLC's name?

Through the reference line. The instructions say line 10 may carry referencing information useful to document the beneficial owner, including the account number, and the Form W-8BEN instructions contemplate the disregarded entity's name and account number on line 7.

### When does a W-8BEN-E expire?

Generally on the last day of the third succeeding calendar year after signature, and earlier if a change in circumstances makes the information incorrect. A move between two foreign addresses is generally not such a change; a move to a US address is.

### Does a treaty claim change which form is used?

It can. A hybrid entity claiming treaty benefits on its own behalf is required to complete Form W-8BEN-E and is identified on line 1. Treaty positions should be confirmed with a tax adviser before signing.
