**A timely Form 7004 extension is almost always cheaper, because it costs a filing fee and removes the $25,000 Form 5472 penalty exposure for that year. Filing late on reasonable cause costs the same preparation work plus a penalty no provider can promise the IRS will waive, and the continuation penalty can multiply it.**

The question comes up every April from foreign owners whose records are not ready: is it worth bothering with an extension, or is it simpler to file late and write a letter? Framed as a cost comparison, the two options are not close. The comparison below prices both routes for a single-member foreign-owned LLC using figures from the IRS instructions themselves. If the regular due date has not passed yet, [a Form 7004 extension](/form-7004-extension) is the cheap option.

This post covers a U.S.-formed single-member LLC wholly owned by a foreign person, treated as a disregarded entity, filing Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120.

## What are the two routes actually being compared?

**Route A — extend, then file.** File Form 7004 by the regular due date of the pro forma Form 1120, which moves the filing deadline out by the automatic extension period of generally six months, then file the complete Form 5472 package within that window. The [Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) confirm a foreign-owned U.S. DE "can request an extension of time to file by filing Form 7004."

**Route B — file late, request relief.** Miss the regular due date without a valid extension, then file the delinquent package with a reasonable cause statement and ask the IRS not to assess the penalty.

Route A is a deadline decision made before the due date. Route B is what is left afterwards. The two are alternatives only in the window before the regular due date passes.

## What does the on-time extension route cost?

Two line items, both known in advance:

1. **The Form 7004 filing itself** — one page, no signature required, faxed or mailed to the IRS Ogden PIN Unit. You can prepare it yourself at no cost beyond your time, or [order Form 7004 extension filing](/form-7004-extension) and see the fee on that page.
2. **The Form 5472 package**, prepared during the extended window. Our Standard preparation is {{price.standard}}, ready in 5-7 business days, with Express at {{price.express}} ready for you to check and sign within 24 hours, 7 days a week.

**Penalty exposure on Route A: nil for that year, provided the extension is valid and the package is filed inside the extended window.** The Form 5472 instructions assess the penalty on a reporting corporation that fails to file "when due" — and a valid extension moves when due.

The caveat is that "valid" is doing real work in that sentence. The [Form 7004 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7004) state that if the name on the form does not match the IRS database or the identifying number is incorrect, you will not have a valid extension, and that the IRS no longer sends notification that an extension has been approved. Our [line-by-line Form 7004 walkthrough](/blog/form-7004-line-by-line-foreign-owned-llc) covers the entries that invalidate one.

## What does the late-filing route cost?

The same preparation work, plus an open-ended penalty exposure.

The Form 5472 instructions state that "A penalty of $25,000 will be assessed on any reporting corporation that fails to file Form 5472" when due and in the required manner. Two features of that penalty drive the comparison:

- **It scales with related parties and years.** IRM 20.1.9.5.4(1) describes the initial penalty as asserted per related party per year.
- **It continues.** If the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notification, an additional $25,000 applies, and then applies per related party for each 30-day period or part of a period after the 90-day period ends. IRM 20.1.9.5.4(2) notes the continuation penalty is not subject to a maximum limit.

Against that sits a request for relief whose outcome is unknown at the time you choose the route.

The arithmetic on the exposure, using the instructions' own figures:

- **Route B, relief granted:** preparation fees + $0 penalty. Same as Route A, but only after a wait and an IRS decision.
- **Route B, relief refused:** preparation fees + **$25,000**.
- **Three unfiled years, one related party, relief refused:** 3 × $25,000 = **$75,000**, against +{{price.additionalYear}} per additional past year of preparation on our side.
- **A third case — the return still is not filed.** The continuation penalty runs on the failure itself, not on an unresolved abatement request, so it reaches a filer who has still not filed rather than one who filed late and is waiting on a decision. A failure still uncured 120 days past the 90-day notice window costs $25,000 initial + (4 × $25,000 continuation) = **$125,000** for one related party and one year. Filing stops that clock; arguing about it does not.

Route A's cost is a fee. Route B's cost is a fee plus a lottery ticket you did not want to buy.

## Can anyone promise the IRS will accept a reasonable cause statement?

No, and a provider who implies otherwise is selling something they do not control.

The IRS's own [delinquent international information return submission procedures](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/delinquent-international-information-return-submission-procedures) page (reviewed 19 April 2026) states that taxpayers "may attach a reasonable cause statement to each delinquent information return filed," and then warns that "penalties may be assessed without considering the attached reasonable cause statement." The page adds that "It may be necessary for taxpayers to respond to specific correspondence from the IRS." It carves out Forms 3520 and 3520-A as the forms whose reasonable cause statements are considered before a penalty is assessed. Form 5472 is not in that carve-out.

The [IRS reasonable cause page](https://www.irs.gov/payments/penalty-relief-for-reasonable-cause) states that reasonable cause "is determined on a case-by-case basis considering all the facts and circumstances of your situation." Case-by-case means no published rate, and no promise. There is no IRS-published acceptance rate for Form 5472 reasonable cause requests, which is precisely why the expected cost of Route B cannot be calculated honestly — and precisely why removing the variable is worth a small fee.

## Which route is cheaper once the due date has passed?

Neither, because the choice is gone. A Form 7004 filed after the regular due date does not retroactively extend it: the Form 5472 instructions require the DE to file Form 7004 "by the due date (excluding extensions) of the return."

After that date the only remaining question is how to file late well: complete years, a dated and evidenced statement, and prompt correction. Our [late or never-filed Form 5472 guide](/blog/form-5472-filed-late-never-filed) covers the package. Two shortcuts to be careful with: First Time Abate, which [reaches a Form 5472 penalty only in one narrow, conditional case](/blog/form-5472-first-time-penalty-abatement), and waiting for a notice before acting, since the continuation clock runs on an uncured failure from IRS notification.

## What does an extension not buy?

Three things, and assuming otherwise is how an extension turns into a late filing anyway:

- **It does not extend time to pay.** The Form 7004 instructions state plainly that "Form 7004 does not extend the time to pay tax." Owner-level tax, withholding and state obligations keep their own deadlines.
- **It does not remove an existing penalty.** A valid extension changes the due date for the year it covers. It does nothing for a prior year already assessed.
- **It does not file the return.** The extended date is a real deadline. A package that is not ready by the extended date lands in Route B with the extension fee already spent.

## The bottom line

Comparing a known fee against a $25,000 exposure that scales per related party, per year, and compounds every 30 days after a notice is not a close call. The extension is the cheap route whenever the regular due date is still ahead of you, and the only honest thing anyone can say about the late route is that the outcome is the IRS's decision, not the preparer's. If the due date has not passed, [file the Form 7004](/form-7004-extension); if it has, [start the catch-up package](/start) and make the statement as specific as the facts allow.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Form 7004 extension cheaper than filing Form 5472 late?

A timely extension removes the year's late-filing exposure entirely. Filing late costs the same preparation plus a $25,000 exposure that depends on an IRS reasonable cause decision, and no one can price that decision in advance.

### Does an extension prevent the $25,000 Form 5472 penalty?

A valid, timely extension moves the due date, and the penalty applies to a failure to file when due. It does not grant relief from a penalty for any year already late.

### How late can you file Form 7004?

It must be filed by the regular due date of the return, excluding extensions. The Form 5472 instructions require a foreign-owned U.S. DE to send Form 7004 by that date. A later Form 7004 does not extend anything.

### Will the IRS waive the penalty if I attach a reasonable cause statement?

Not necessarily. The IRS delinquent international information return page warns that penalties may be assessed without considering the attached reasonable cause statement, and says it may be necessary to respond to later IRS correspondence.

### How much is the Form 5472 penalty per year?

The Form 5472 instructions assess $25,000 for a failure to file when due and in the required manner, and IRM 20.1.9.5.4(1) describes the initial penalty as asserted per related party per year.

### Does the penalty keep growing after the first $25,000?

Yes, after notification. The Form 5472 instructions add $25,000 if the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notification, then per related party for each 30-day period or part of one afterwards.

### Can I file the extension myself and the return through you?

Yes. The Form 7004 is one page and requires no signature. Many owners file it themselves by the due date and hand over the Form 5472 package afterwards, which is reviewed by a tax accountant who holds an IRS PTIN.
