**A foreign-owned LLC can often open a US business account after it has an EIN, formation documents, a valid owner passport or photo ID, and verifiable business and residential addresses. Remote fintech applications may avoid a US visit, while branch banks often require the relevant members or managers to appear in person.**

Opening the account is an identity, entity, and risk-review process—not a benefit automatically delivered with the LLC. The provider decides which countries, activities, addresses, owners, and documents it will accept. Approval can never be guaranteed by a registered agent or formation service.

The EIN normally comes first. The [IRS explanation of Notice CP575](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp575-notice) says the notice confirms an entity’s EIN and may be needed to open a bank account. If the LLC has no EIN confirmation yet, [the EIN service is $149](/ein) and is the practical first step. Once money starts moving, [prepare the LLC’s Form 5472 filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=us-bank-account-foreign-owned-llc) from the same source records.

## What does a foreign-owned LLC need to open a US business account?

Most applications need evidence in four buckets: the entity exists, the entity has a tax identifier, the applicant is authorised, and the provider can identify the natural people behind the company. Exact requirements vary by provider and account type.

| Evidence bucket | Documents commonly requested | What to check before uploading |
|---|---|---|
| IRS identity | EIN and CP575 or other accepted EIN confirmation | LLC name and EIN match the application exactly |
| State formation | Articles of organisation, certificate of formation, or state registry record | Legal name, state, formation date, and status are current |
| Authority | Operating agreement, manager/member details, or authorising resolution when requested | Applicant has authority to act for the LLC |
| Personal identity | Valid passport or other accepted government photo ID | Name, date of birth, document number, and expiry are legible |
| Addresses | Registered, business/trading, and owner residential addresses | Each address is labelled correctly and can be supported |
| Business profile | Website, contracts, invoices, expected activity, customers, suppliers, and source of funds when requested | Description matches the LLC’s real activity |

This is not a universal checklist. Chase’s current [business account information page](https://www.chase.com/business/resources/business-bank-account-information) lists EIN, business-formation evidence, identification, ownership details, and people who need to be present for different entity types. Wise’s [US business verification guide](https://wise.com/help/articles/2953878/how-do-i-verify-my-us-business) asks for the EIN, registered address, owner/controller details, residential addresses, and photo ID for non-US beneficial owners.

Keep the original PDF or scan quality high. Names transliterated from another alphabet, an old passport, an amended LLC name, or a registered-agent address entered as the owner’s residence can create mismatches even when every document is genuine.

## Why does the EIN come before the bank application?

The EIN is the LLC’s federal tax identifier and a core matching field for most US business-account applications. A state filing creates the entity; it does not assign the federal number.

The original CP575 series notice, a current digital CP575 when available, or Letter 147C can serve as EIN confirmation. The IRS says the original CP575A-J notice cannot be duplicated or recreated, so store it permanently. If the confirmation is missing, follow the IRS recovery route rather than editing an SS-4 copy or producing a homemade letter.

Check three items before applying:

1. The LLC’s legal name on the EIN evidence matches the formation record.
2. The responsible-party and address history can be explained if the application uses newer information.
3. The applicant knows whether the provider accepts the particular EIN document available.

An EIN is necessary for this workflow but does not guarantee an account. Providers conduct their own identity, eligibility, compliance, and business-model review.

## Can a non-resident open the account without visiting the United States?

Yes, a remote application can be possible through an online provider, but eligibility depends on the owner’s country, business activity, addresses, and documents. Online options to compare include Mercury, Relay, and Wise Business; they are separate providers with different products and onboarding rules. Form5472 Prep has no partnership with them and does not recommend one as universally best.

A branch-based bank can follow a different process. Chase, for example, describes both online and in-person paths but states that the relevant people for a member-managed LLC or manager-managed LLC need to be present at account opening under its in-person requirements. That supports a general rule of thumb: traditional banks often require a branch visit for a foreign-owned LLC even though some entity and applicant combinations can apply online.

Remote does not mean “US address only.” A provider may distinguish the LLC’s registered address from its real operating address and the owner’s residential address. Give each address according to the field label. Do not claim that a registered-agent, mailbox, or virtual-office address is where the owner lives or where the business operates.

## How should a foreign owner apply for a US business account?

Apply in a sequence that prevents avoidable name, address, and authority mismatches.

1. **Finish the formation record.** Download the state-stamped formation document and confirm the legal name.
2. **Obtain and preserve the EIN evidence.** Save CP575 or another provider-accepted IRS confirmation as an original-quality file.
3. **Prepare the authority file.** Finalise the operating agreement and identify the member, manager, and person applying.
4. **Build the identity pack.** Use a valid passport or other accepted photo ID and current residential-address evidence if requested.
5. **Write a plain business description.** Explain the product or service, operating countries, customer type, suppliers, expected payment routes, and source of opening funds accurately.
6. **Compare eligibility before submitting.** Check supported owner countries, excluded activities, address rules, account features, and whether a US visit is required on the provider’s own current site.
7. **Submit one consistent application.** Use the same legal names, dates, ownership percentages, and addresses across fields and documents.
8. **Answer follow-up questions directly.** Supply contracts, invoices, a website, source-of-funds evidence, or an explanation only when requested and keep a copy.
9. **Record the first funding transfer.** Save the sending statement, incoming transaction, exchange calculation, and bookkeeping entry because the transfer may feed Form 5472 Part V.

Do not submit several altered versions after a rejection without understanding the reason. Inconsistent applications can make a genuine business harder to verify.

## How does the first bank deposit feed Form 5472 Part V?

The first owner-funded deposit is commonly the LLC’s first reportable transaction. The bank sees opening cash; Form 5472 sees a transaction between a foreign owner and a foreign-owned US disregarded entity.

The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) say Part V covers other transactions under Treasury Regulation §1.6038A-2, including amounts connected with formation and contributions to or distributions from the entity. The LLC checks Part V and describes those transactions on an attached statement.

Consider this illustrative funding trail, created for the guide:

| Date | Bank evidence | Book treatment to confirm | Part V workpaper treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 January | Owner wires **$8,000** from a personal foreign account | Owner capital contribution | Report **$8,000** contribution |
| 12 January | Bank deducts **$25** incoming or intermediary charge | Bank fee paid to an unrelated provider | Keep in books; not another owner contribution |
| 20 February | Owner pays **$1,250** LLC software bill personally | Contribution, loan, or amount due to owner | Report the owner-funded **$1,250** under the supported classification |
| 5 March | LLC repays owner **$250** | Reimbursement or partial repayment | Report the **$250** movement back to owner |

The owner-to-LLC value reviewed for Part V is **$9,250 = $8,000 + $1,250**. The separate LLC-to-owner value is **$250**. The **$25** third-party bank fee belongs in the books but is not owner funding. Do not net the flows into **$9,000**; preserve direction and description.

If the owner sent another currency, keep the original amount, transaction date, exchange-rate source, calculation, and resulting US-dollar amount. The Form 5472 instructions require US-dollar reporting and an exchange-rate schedule for foreign-currency related-party transactions.

## What bank records should the LLC keep?

Keep a record trail that connects provider onboarding, bookkeeping, and the federal information return. The account statement alone often does not identify whether a transfer was capital, debt, reimbursement, revenue, or a distribution.

Use this bank-to-return file:

- EIN confirmation and formation documents used in the application
- Operating agreement and current ownership record
- Complete bank and payment-account statements
- Deposit advices, wire confirmations, and sending-account evidence
- A related-party ledger showing date, counterparty, direction, currency, USD amount, and purpose
- Contracts, invoices, receipts, and approvals supporting owner-paid costs
- Exchange-rate workpapers
- A reconciliation from related-party ledger totals to Form 5472 Parts IV and V
- Filed return, Part V attachment, fax receipt, and IRS correspondence

Separate customer receipts from owner deposits as soon as the account opens. A wire from the owner labelled “transfer” is not self-explanatory. Record the classification when the event happens, while the source and purpose are still clear.

## Does having a US account change the LLC’s tax classification?

No. A US account does not turn a disregarded LLC into a C corporation, prove that no income tax is due, or replace any federal return. Tax classification comes from federal default rules and elections; tax exposure depends on the business facts.

A foreign-owned US disregarded LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when it had a reportable transaction. For the **2025 calendar tax year**, the filing was due **15 April 2026**, or **15 October 2026** after a timely Form 7004 extension.

Write “Foreign-owned U.S. DE” across the pro forma Form 1120. The package cannot be e-filed. Fax it at **300 DPI or higher** to **855-887-7737**, or mail it to the IRS Ogden PIN Unit address in the instructions. If the owner has no FTIN, enter “None” or “N/A”; when no US identifying number exists, use the required reference ID consistently each year.

The initial failure-to-file penalty is **$25,000 per form per year**, and a substantially incomplete return counts as failure. After 90 days from an IRS notice, IRC §6038A(d)(2) can add **$25,000 for each 30-day period or fraction** that the failure continues.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a foreign-owned LLC open a US bank account?

Often, yes. Approval depends on the provider’s supported countries, business activity, addresses, ownership, identification, and risk review. Formation alone does not guarantee acceptance.

### Can I open the account without visiting the US?

Yes, some online providers accept remote applications from eligible foreign owners. Traditional branch banks generally require an in-person visit for many foreign-owned LLC applications. Check the provider’s current rules before applying.

### Do I need an EIN before opening the account?

Usually. Business-account providers commonly request the LLC’s EIN and acceptable IRS confirmation. Obtain the EIN first, preserve CP575 or other accepted evidence, and make sure the legal name matches the formation record.

### Is CP575 the same as an EIN?

CP575 is an IRS notice confirming the EIN; it is not a second number. Providers may accept the original notice, a current digital CP575, Letter 147C, or another specified IRS document.

### Can I use my registered agent’s address?

Use it only where the application asks for the LLC’s registered address and the provider permits it. Do not present that address as the owner’s residence or the business’s operating location when it is not.

### Is the first deposit reportable on Form 5472?

Usually, when the foreign owner funds a disregarded LLC. Record it as a contribution, loan, or other supported owner transaction and describe it in the Part V attachment rather than treating it as customer revenue.

### Does a bank account mean the LLC owes US tax?

No. The account itself does not decide income tax. Business activities, income source, people, inventory, elections, and other facts matter. Form 5472 can still be required even when no federal income tax is due.
