**An Australian resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC has a reportable transaction with its owner or another related party. Owner funding, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements, formation costs and dissolution transactions can qualify even when the LLC earned no revenue.**

The filing is a US information return, not a calculation of Australian tax. An Australian owner can [start a guided Form 5472 filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-australia) without an SSN or ITIN; the US LLC needs its own EIN.

## Does an Australian-owned US LLC have to file Form 5472?

The filing test turns on US tax classification and related-party activity. The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) treat a domestic disregarded entity wholly owned by a foreign person as a reporting corporation for the limited purpose of section 6038A.

An Australian-owned LLC normally files when all three conditions are true:

1. The LLC was formed in a US state.
2. One Australian person or entity owned 100% of the LLC and no corporate election changed its default classification.
3. The LLC had at least one reportable transaction with the owner or another related party during the tax year.

The third condition is broader than revenue. The contribution used to open the bank account can create a filing obligation in the LLC's first year. A dormant company can also have reportable activity if the owner paid a registered-agent invoice or withdrew cash.

## Which Australian number goes in the FTIN field?

For an individual, the relevant foreign taxpayer identification number is normally the Australian Tax File Number. The [Australian Taxation Office](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-6faec75b-2772-4cf2-ac8d-ddb6f6d0d102) describes a TFN as a unique personal reference number, usually nine digits, that remains with the holder for life.

| Form 5472 field | Australian owner information |
|---|---|
| Foreign owner name | Full legal name, matching tax records |
| Country of citizenship or organisation | Australia, when applicable |
| Country of tax residence | Australia, when applicable |
| FTIN | Individual TFN; use the entity's tax identifier when the owner is a company |
| US identifying number | SSN, ITIN or EIN only if one exists |
| Reference ID | A consistent filer-created alphanumeric ID when required |

The IRS instructions say a foreign-owned US disregarded entity may enter “None” or “N/A” when the owner has no FTIN. Do not substitute the LLC's EIN for the owner's FTIN.

## Which transactions should an Australian owner collect?

Collect every transfer of value between the LLC and its owner before deciding where it belongs. A practical Australian-owner ledger has four buckets:

- **Owner to LLC:** formation funding, capital contributions, loans and bills paid personally.
- **LLC to owner:** withdrawals, loan repayments, reimbursements and personal expenses paid by the business.
- **Owner services or property:** management services, equipment, intellectual property or rent supplied by the owner.
- **Lifecycle events:** formation, acquisition of the interest, sale, transfer or dissolution.

Ordinary customer receipts from unrelated buyers are not reportable merely because Stripe or a bank processed them. The relationship between the parties—not the payment platform—controls. See the [reportable-transactions examples](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples) for classifications.

## How should Australian-dollar transactions be converted?

Form 5472 amounts are stated in US dollars. The IRS says to use the spot rate when an item is received, paid or accrued and generally accepts any published rate used consistently. Its [yearly average exchange-rate page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates) lists AUD rates and explains the conversion method.

Use one documented method for comparable transactions and keep the source with the workpapers. For irregular transfers, transaction-date rates usually preserve the clearest audit trail.

## How does an Australian owner file the package?

1. Confirm the LLC's classification and tax year.
2. Reconcile related-party transactions in US dollars.
3. Complete the pro forma Form 1120 and write “Foreign-owned U.S. DE” across the top.
4. Complete one Form 5472 for each related party and attach the Part V statement.
5. Fax the package at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737, or use the dedicated Ogden mailing address in the instructions.
6. Keep the complete package and timestamped transmission receipt.

A calendar-year return is generally due April 15 of the following year, or October 15 after a timely Form 7004 extension.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an Australian owner need an ITIN to file Form 5472?

Usually no. The LLC needs an EIN. An individual owner can provide a TFN and a consistent reference ID where required; an SSN or ITIN is entered only if one already exists.

### Does a no-income Australian-owned LLC still file?

It can. Capital contributions, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements, formation costs and owner-paid expenses can be reportable even when revenue was zero.

### Does Form 5472 replace an Australian tax return?

No. Form 5472 is a US information return. Australian reporting depends on the owner's residence, entity classification and facts, so local Australian advice may still be necessary.

### What is the late-filing penalty?

The initial penalty is $25,000 for each required Form 5472 that is late, incomplete or not maintained with required records. Additional penalties can follow after IRS notice.

### Can the package be e-filed?

No. The IRS instructions state that a foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot electronically file Form 5472; the package must be faxed or mailed to the dedicated Ogden PIN Unit.
