**The number in the upper right corner of the letter tells you what the IRS wants. CP215 charges a civil penalty, CP259 and CP518B say a return is missing, CP161 and CP162 are balance and electronic-filing notices, CP504 and LT11 are levy notices, and Notice 972CG proposes information-return penalties.**

You are holding an envelope with a two-letter prefix and three digits on it, and nothing on the first page explains in plain words what the IRS has decided. This guide does one job: it tells you which notice you are looking at, what that notice asks for, and what deadline the IRS's own page for it states. Every number below links to the page irs.gov publishes for it, all confirmed live on 3 October 2026.

Before you read anything else, find the number. The IRS's [Understanding your IRS notice or letter](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-irs-notice-or-letter) hub — reviewed 26 May 2026 — says it plainly: "You can find the CP or LTR number on the right corner of the letter." It is usually printed above the tax year and the amount.

## Which IRS notice numbers might a Form 5472 filer receive?

Nine numbers account for most of the mail a foreign-owned US LLC gets after a filing problem. Each row below is taken from the IRS page for that notice, with the wording the IRS uses. Where the deadline column says no day count is published, that is because the IRS page for that notice does not state one — the date printed on your copy is the operative date.

| Notice | What it means | What it asks for | Deadline the IRS page states | First thing to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [CP215](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp215-notice) | "We sent you this notice because we charged you a civil penalty." The page does not name any particular form or code section. | Payment of the penalty, or contact with the IRS if you disagree | No day count published; the page says the notice "explains your due date, amount due, and payment options" | Confirm the tax year shown is a year you genuinely did not file |
| [CP259](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp259-notice) | "We show that you are required to file a tax return for the tax periods indicated on your notice but haven't." | The missing business return, or the enclosed Response form explaining why none is due | No day count published; the page says to file "immediately" | Check whether that period's pro forma Form 1120 and Form 5472 were ever transmitted |
| [CP518B](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp518b-notice) | "This is a final reminder notice that we still have no record that you filed your prior tax returns." Business version. | A signed paper return with all schedules, plus the enclosed Response form | No day count published; the FAQ says no action is needed if you filed within the last eight weeks | Treat it as the last stage before the IRS determines the tax itself |
| [CP518](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp518-notice) | The individual counterpart: "This is your final reminder; we still don't have a record that you filed your prior year tax returns." | The delinquent return with Form 15103, or Form 15103 alone explaining why none is due | No day count published | Check whose name the notice is addressed to — the owner or the LLC |
| [CP161](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp161-notice) | "You received this notice because you have an unpaid balance due." | Payment of the balance by the payment due date on the notice | "Contact us within 10 days of the date of your notice if you think we made a mistake" | Compare the figure with your own records before paying |
| [CP162](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp162-notice) | A penalty charged because a return was not filed electronically as required. The page discusses partnerships and treats a REMIC return as a partnership return. | Full payment, or a call from an authorised partner or officer who meets the stated criteria | Payment "by the date shown on your letter" | Establish which return the penalty attaches to — this one is not about Form 5472 |
| [CP504](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp504-notice) | "This notice is your Notice of Intent to Levy (Internal Revenue Code section 6331(d))." | Immediate payment of the unpaid balance | No day count published; the page says to pay "immediately" | Stop and get help the same week — collection has begun |
| [LT11 / Letter 1058](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-lt11-notice-or-letter-1058) | "We haven't received your payment for overdue taxes. We intend to seize your property or rights to property." | Payment, or an appeal of the proposed levy | No day count published; the page points to Form 12153 and the collection due process FAQs | Read the appeal instructions on the letter before anything else |
| [Notice 972CG](https://www.irs.gov/payments/information-return-penalties) | A proposed penalty for information returns filed late, not filed electronically when required, or filed with missing or incorrect payee TINs. | A reasonable-cause response before the penalty is assessed | "Respond within 45 days (60 days if you're a foreign filer) before we assess the penalty" | Identify which information returns — 1099-type filings, not Form 5472 |

Two safety lines belong with that table, and they matter more than any row in it.

**The dates printed on your own notice govern, not this article.** None of the pages above replaces the letter in your hand. Where a notice shows a payment due date, a response-by date or an appeal deadline, that printed date is the one the IRS works from. The hub page states the consequence directly: "To guarantee your appeal rights, reply by the due date."

**A number missing from the table is not thereby fake.** The IRS sends many notices that have nothing to do with a foreign-owned LLC, and the table above is deliberately short. If your number is not here, search it on the IRS notices hub rather than assuming anything. The hub also gives the real test for a suspicious letter: "If the letter doesn't appear in your search or if it looks suspicious, call 800-829-1040."

## What does a CP215 notice actually say on its face?

CP215 says a civil penalty has been charged, and nothing more specific than that. The IRS page for it — reviewed 29 January 2026 — gives one sentence of explanation: "We sent you this notice because we charged you a civil penalty." It does not name Form 5472, does not cite a code section, and does not publish a response window in days.

That silence is why the notice is so disorienting. The entity name, the tax year and the amount on your copy are what identify the penalty, not the notice number. Read the year first and check it against the years your LLC existed and had owner transactions.

What to do about the charge is a separate subject with its own guide: see [our walkthrough of a Form 5472 penalty notice](/blog/form-5472-penalty-notice-what-to-do) for the response itself.

## Why would a foreign-owned LLC get a CP259 or a CP518B?

CP259 and CP518B both say the same thing in escalating tones: an IRS system expects a return under your EIN and has not matched one to it. CP259 opens that conversation. CP518B is labelled on its own page as a final reminder, and the page warns that if the IRS does not hear back, "We may determine your tax for you. Penalty and interest can continue to accrue."

For a foreign-owned single-member LLC, the return the system is usually looking for is the pro forma Form 1120 that carries Form 5472. A package that was faxed but never posted to the EIN looks identical, from the IRS side, to one that was never sent. Both notices give you a Response form precisely so you can say which happened.

Neither notice is a penalty. Each is a request for a return or an explanation, which makes both of them better news than a CP215 — and a reason to move fast rather than slowly. The catch-up mechanics for the years themselves are covered in [filing Form 5472 late or never filed](/blog/form-5472-filed-late-never-filed).

## Is Notice 972CG the same as a Form 5472 penalty?

No. Notice 972CG is a proposed penalty about information returns such as 1099-type filings — the IRS [information return penalties](https://www.irs.gov/payments/information-return-penalties) page, reviewed 11 May 2026, describes it as covering returns filed late, not filed electronically when required, or filed with missing or incorrect payee taxpayer identification numbers.

Two things about it are worth knowing even so. First, the IRS calls it **Notice 972CG**, not Letter 972CG, and the response window is published: "Respond within 45 days (60 days if you're a foreign filer) before we assess the penalty." That 60-day figure is one of the few places the IRS states a longer window for a filer outside the United States.

Second, a foreign-owned LLC can receive it. An LLC that paid US contractors and filed the resulting forms late is in scope for 972CG while also owing Form 5472 for the same year. The two obligations are unrelated, and a response to one does nothing for the other.

## What do CP504 and LT11 mean, and how do they differ?

CP504 and LT11 are both collection notices, and the difference between them is which rights they carry. CP504 identifies itself as the Notice of Intent to Levy under Internal Revenue Code section 6331(d) and lists what can be taken, including a state income tax refund. LT11 and Letter 1058 share one IRS page, titled "Understanding your LT11 notice or letter 1058," and that page states the intent to seize property while pointing to the appeal route: Form 12153, Publication 1660 and the collection due process FAQs.

Neither page publishes a day count. Both say to act immediately. An unpaid Form 5472 penalty that has reached either stage is past the point where a self-prepared letter is the right move — this is where a tax attorney or CPA earns the fee.

## What should you do in the first hour after opening the envelope?

1. **Write down the notice number, the tax year, the amount and every printed date.** Those five facts determine everything that follows.
2. **Check the entity.** Confirm the EIN and the name match your LLC and not a similarly named entity.
3. **Look up the number on the IRS notices hub** and read the page for your notice rather than a forum summary of it.
4. **Pull your filing evidence for that year** — fax confirmations, mailing receipts, the package itself.
5. **Decide which half of the problem you are solving.** A missing return is a filing job. An assessed penalty is a correspondence job. Many envelopes create both.
6. **Diarise the printed deadline, then work backwards.** If the date is close and your package is not ready, a written request for more time sent before the date beats silence.
7. **Keep the envelope and the postmark.** Dates of receipt occasionally matter later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the notice number printed?

On the right corner of the letter, according to the IRS notices hub. Look for a "CP" or "LTR" prefix followed by digits, usually above the tax year and the amount. Notice 972CG is labelled differently and appears as a notice name rather than a CP number.

### Does every Form 5472 penalty arrive as a CP215?

The IRS does not publish a mapping between Form 5472 and any notice number. The CP215 page says only that a civil penalty was charged. Identify the penalty from the entity name, tax year and amount on your own copy, not from the number alone.

### My notice gives no deadline in days. What is my deadline?

The date printed on your notice. Several IRS notice pages publish no response window at all, which means the printed date is the only one that counts. Where a notice asks you to respond, replying by that date also protects your appeal rights.

### Is CP162 a Form 5472 notice?

No. The IRS page describes CP162 as a penalty for not filing a return electronically as required, and discusses partnerships and REMIC returns. Form 5472 and its pro forma Form 1120 cannot be filed electronically at all, so establish which return the penalty attaches to.

### What is the 45-day figure people mention?

It belongs to Notice 972CG only. The IRS information return penalties page states: "Respond within 45 days (60 days if you're a foreign filer) before we assess the penalty." Do not apply that window to a CP notice, which carries its own printed date.

### My notice number is not on your table. Is it a scam?

Not necessarily, and assuming so is risky. The table covers a narrow set. Search your number on the IRS notices hub; if it does not appear there or the letter looks suspicious, the hub tells you to call 800-829-1040 and follow the representative's instructions.
