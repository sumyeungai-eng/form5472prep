A foreign-owned U.S. single-member LLC generally files a final Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 for the year it closes when reportable transactions occurred. Dissolution expenses, owner-paid bills, debt settlements, and final distributions can be reportable. Mark the pro forma Form 1120 as final, send the package by the applicable deadline, and retain proof of delivery.

[Start a final-year filing](/start) before closing the LLC’s records.

## Why can a dissolved LLC still owe Form 5472?

Dissolution itself can create reportable transactions. The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) include amounts paid or received in connection with the dissolution or disposition of a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, including distributions from the entity.

State dissolution and federal filing are separate steps. Cancelling the LLC with the state does not retroactively remove federal information-reporting obligations for the period the entity existed.

## What belongs in the final-year transaction review?

Use the closure ledger:

| Closure event | Possible Form 5472 category |
|---|---|
| Owner pays final registered-agent or state fee | Owner-paid LLC obligation or contribution |
| LLC repays owner loan | Related-party loan repayment |
| LLC pays interest to owner | Related-party interest |
| LLC transfers remaining cash to owner | Final distribution |
| LLC distributes equipment or property | Nonmonetary distribution |
| Owner assumes an LLC obligation | Potential noncash related-party transaction |
| Related foreign company waives a balance | Potential Part VI transaction |

Record the gross events separately. A bank account that ends at zero can still contain substantial reportable transfers during the final year.

## How do you mark the filing as final?

The pro forma Form 1120 includes a “Final return” checkbox in item E. Marking that box tells the IRS the filing covers the entity’s final return period. The Form 5472 remains attached as the information return.

The IRS says a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity cannot e-file Form 5472. The package must be faxed or mailed to the dedicated Ogden PIN Unit under the [current filing instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472).

## What else should happen when an LLC closes?

The IRS [closing-a-business guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/closing-a-business) says businesses should file final returns and related forms, address employees and contractors when applicable, pay taxes due, close the IRS business account, and keep records. State dissolution, annual-report obligations, licenses, payroll, sales tax, bank accounts, and contracts require their own closure steps.

## When is the final Form 5472 due?

The due date depends on the LLC’s final tax period and classification. A calendar-year foreign-owned disregarded entity that remains on a calendar year generally follows the Form 1120 due date in the following year, with a timely extension available.

Do not assume state dissolution makes the federal return immediately due or eliminates the regular due date. Confirm short-period or classification issues with a qualified tax professional.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I file after closing an LLC with no revenue?

Often yes. Dissolution expenses, owner payments, loan settlements, and final distributions can be reportable even when the LLC earned no revenue.

### Is state dissolution enough?

No. State dissolution ends the entity under state law; federal returns and information forms remain separate obligations.

### Should I close the bank account before preparing the return?

Reconcile and preserve the complete records first. The final bank transfer and account fee can affect the closure ledger.

### Can I cancel the EIN?

The IRS can close the business account after final obligations are handled. An EIN is not reassigned, and closing the account does not erase prior filing duties.
