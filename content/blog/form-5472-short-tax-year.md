**A foreign-owned US LLC formed late in the year can have a short first Form 5472 reporting period. A calendar-year LLC formed on November 10 generally reviews activity from November 10 through December 31 and files after that period closes. It does not wait until the first anniversary of formation.**

A six-week tax year can still contain formation costs, owner funding, a bank deposit, or a distribution. The short period changes the dates, not the definition of a reportable transaction.

If the LLC was formed before December 31 and the owner paid any setup cost, [start the short-year filing review](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-short-tax-year).

## What determines the short-year period?

The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) say a foreign-owned US disregarded entity uses the tax year of its owner for US filing requirements or, if the owner has no such US tax year, the calendar year. Many nonresident individual owners therefore use a December 31 year-end for this package.

The practical start point is the entity's legal formation date, not the date its EIN arrived, bank account opened, website launched, or first customer paid.

| Event | Does it normally start the entity's existence? |
|---|---|
| State accepts the LLC formation | Yes, use the legal effective date |
| IRS assigns an EIN | No |
| Bank opens the account | No |
| First sale occurs | No |
| Owner begins bookkeeping | No |

Confirm delayed-effective formation language and unusual state facts before choosing the start date.

## Example: LLC formed on November 10

Assume a foreign owner forms a Wyoming LLC on November 10, 2026. The owner pays a $150 organizer charge, contributes $1,500 to the LLC on November 20, and the LLC pays $300 back to the owner on December 28.

The first reporting period is November 10 through December 31, 2026. The workpaper should preserve the three gross movements:

1. owner-paid formation value: $150;
2. owner contribution: $1,500; and
3. LLC-to-owner payment: $300, classified according to its actual purpose.

Do not net the contribution and payment to $1,200. Do not annualize the six-week activity. The form reports what occurred during the actual short period.

For a calendar-year pro forma Form 1120, the package is generally due April 15, 2027, subject to weekends and holidays. A timely Form 7004 can extend the filing deadline, and the [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) specify using the Form 1120 code for the extension.

## Which transactions belong in the first short year?

Include transactions that occurred after legal formation and on or before the tax-year end:

- formation or organizer amounts paid for the LLC;
- registered-agent and address-service costs paid by the owner;
- cash or property contributed to the LLC;
- loans between the owner and LLC;
- reimbursements and distributions;
- related-party service or license payments; and
- acquisition or dissolution activity within the period.

Do not pull January transactions backward merely because they relate to December operations. Preserve the actual transaction date, accounting method, and supporting evidence.

## What if the LLC was formed on December 30?

Two days can still be a tax year. The filing analysis may be small, but formation itself often produces the owner-funded cost or contribution that creates reportable activity.

The better question is not “Was the LLC open long enough?” It is “Did a reportable transaction occur while the LLC existed during that tax year?” Our [first-year Form 5472 guide](/blog/first-year-form-5472-new-llc) shows how to trace setup costs paid before the company had a bank account.

## What if the owner has a fiscal year?

The Form 5472 instructions tie a foreign-owned disregarded entity to the owner's US tax year when the owner has one. A foreign corporate owner with a US filing obligation may therefore create a non-calendar answer.

Do not rely only on the “closing month” entered on Form SS-4. Entity classification, the owner's actual US filing year, and any required-year rules control the analysis. A foreign corporate parent or an election to corporate treatment can also change which return is being filed. Use the [Form 5472 versus 1120-F guide](/blog/form-5472-vs-1120-f) when a foreign company owns the LLC.

## Short-year preflight checklist

| Check | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Exact legal effective date | State-stamped formation document |
| Tax-year end | Owner's US filing year or calendar-year conclusion |
| EIN matches the LLC | CP 575 or 147C record |
| All post-formation owner activity captured | Bank, card, invoice, and ledger records |
| Pre-formation organizer costs classified | Contract, payer, and reimbursement trail |
| Gross flows kept separate | Contribution, distribution, loan, and repayment columns |
| Extension filed by regular due date if needed | Form 7004 confirmation |
