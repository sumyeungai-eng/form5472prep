## Who can get the EIN online for free?

The IRS online tool can issue an EIN immediately to eligible applicants. Current eligibility includes a domestic organization, a principal place of business in the United States or a U.S. territory, and the responsible party’s SSN or ITIN. The person using the tool must be the responsible party or an authorized representative.

The tool cannot be used when the principal place of business is outside the United States. Those applicants use phone, fax, or mail. A foreign-owned U.S. LLC can be domestically formed while still having its real principal place of business abroad, so state formation alone does not establish online eligibility.

## What work does a paid EIN service replace?

A managed application can replace several practical tasks:

- interpreting the SS-4 fields for a foreign-owned LLC;
- matching the legal name and formation information;
- identifying the actual responsible party;
- using “Foreign” or “N/A” on line 7b when permitted;
- selecting the appropriate phone or fax route;
- maintaining the completed form and submission record; and
- communicating the assigned EIN to the owner.

It does not buy faster treatment from the IRS, change the entity’s tax classification, guarantee a bank account, or remove future filing duties.

## When DIY is the better choice

Apply directly when the application is straightforward, you understand the entity classification, and you can use the IRS channel that fits your location. The direct process is appropriate for many owners and avoids a professional fee.

For an international fax filing, follow the current [Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4), use a return fax number that works, sign the form, and keep the complete submission. For an international phone application, the caller must be authorized and able to answer the SS-4 questions.

## When the $149 service can be worth it

Paid help is most relevant when:

- the foreign owner cannot use the online tool;
- U.S. business-hour calling is inconvenient;
- the owner is unsure how to describe the LLC on SS-4;
- a previous submission contained inconsistent information;
- the business needs a clean form record for its compliance file; or
- the owner would rather spend time on banking and operations.

The buying decision is an exchange of money for preparation and administrative handling, not a purchase of the EIN itself.

## Avoid misleading “EIN fees”

Before paying, confirm the total price, refund terms, exact deliverables, processing estimate, and whether the provider is applying for a new number or retrieving an existing one. A business should have only one EIN. Do not order a second application because the confirmation letter is missing.

If the LLC already has an EIN, locate the original notice, bank record, prior return, or request IRS verification instead of buying a new-number service. If the LLC has not been formed, complete state formation before applying.
