**A Swiss resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC has a reportable transaction with the owner or another related party. A Swiss individual normally uses the AHV insurance number as the FTIN; a Swiss entity normally uses its UID.**

The form reports gross related-party transactions. It does not decide whether Switzerland recognizes the LLC as transparent, whether treaty relief applies, or whether US income tax is due.

When owner funding or withdrawals occurred, [start the reviewed filing package](/start) before the deadline. Owners elsewhere can compare their rules on our [Form 5472 by country](/form-5472-by-country) page.

## When must a Swiss-owned LLC file Form 5472?

A foreign-owned domestic disregarded entity generally files after any transaction with its foreign owner or another related party. Common triggers include formation funding, capital contributions, distributions, loans, reimbursements, and payments involving a related Swiss company.

The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) require a pro forma Form 1120 and state a current **$25,000 penalty for a late, missing, or substantially incomplete form**.

| Swiss-US LLC event | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Owner deposits CHF-funded cash into the LLC | Reportable contribution |
| LLC transfers money to the owner | Reportable distribution |
| Owner pays the LLC’s registered-agent bill | Reportable owner-funded amount |
| Unrelated client pays an invoice | Not an owner transaction |
| LLC pays a Swiss company controlled by the owner | Related-party payment; separate-form analysis |

The reporting is gross. A CHF-funded contribution and a later withdrawal remain separate even if the LLC ends the year owing nothing to the owner.

The test looks at US status. A Swiss resident who is also a US citizen or US tax resident is not a foreign owner for this purpose, and the LLC does not file the foreign-owned package.

For a calendar-year LLC, the 2026 package is generally due April 15, 2027. A [Form 5472 extension (Form 7004)](/form-7004-extension) filed by that date, with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top, generally moves the deadline to October 15, 2027.

## Which Swiss tax identifier goes on the form?

The Swiss Federal Tax Administration identifies the [AHV insurance number as the TIN for resident individuals and the UID for resident entities](https://www.estv.admin.ch/de/steueridentifikationsnummer-aia). Use the identifier belonging to the party actually listed in Part II or Part III.

| Owner type | FTIN entry | Other identifier handling |
|---|---|---|
| Swiss-resident individual | AHV insurance number | Add a consistent reference ID if no US identifying number exists |
| Swiss corporation or other entity | UID | Use the entity’s name, address, and identifier—not the controller’s AHV number |
| Owner with an existing ITIN or SSN | Enter it on 4b(1) | Continue to enter the FTIN consistently |
| Owner with no FTIN | Enter “None” or “N/A” when factually correct | Never invent an identifier |

An individual does not need to obtain an ITIN solely for Form 5472. The [ITIN guide](/blog/itin-required-form-5472) explains when a separate US return can create an ITIN need.

## Does the US-Switzerland treaty cancel Form 5472?

No. Switzerland appears on the IRS [income-tax treaty index](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z), but treaty provisions address income taxation, not the LLC’s related-party information return.

The IRS publishes the [US-Switzerland treaty documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/switzerland-tax-treaty-documents): the convention signed in Washington on 2 October 1996, a protocol signed on 23 September 2009, and technical explanations. Read them with a Swiss adviser rather than assuming a particular article covers your LLC.

For services physically performed in Switzerland, US sourcing rules generally treat the service income as foreign-source. US workdays, an office, employees, inventory, or a dependent agent can change the analysis. A treaty position must also be properly claimed where required.

Swiss tax treatment remains a separate matter. A Swiss adviser should determine the LLC’s local classification, whether income belongs to the owner or entity, and what wealth-tax, value-added-tax, or foreign-entity reporting follows from the facts.

## How does Switzerland tax a resident who owns a US LLC?

A Swiss resident is generally taxed on worldwide income, and on worldwide wealth for cantonal purposes, with foreign businesses, permanent establishments and real estate carved out but usually counted for the rate. How the LLC fits those rules is a classification question for a Swiss adviser.

The Federal Tax Administration’s dossier on [subjective tax liability](https://www.estv2.admin.ch/stp/ds/e-subjektive-steuerpflicht-de.pdf) (legislation as at 1 January 2026) summarises the federal rules:

- A person is fully taxable in Switzerland with a tax domicile there, or with a stay of at least 30 days while working, or at least 90 days without working (article 3 of the Federal Direct Tax Act, DBG).
- Unlimited liability covers total income and, for cantonal taxes, total wealth (article 6(1) DBG), subject to double tax treaties.
- Businesses, permanent establishments and real estate abroad, and their income, are allocated to where they are located, but are regularly taken into account to set the rate.

Whether your US LLC is treated as a foreign business of yours, a foreign company you hold shares in, or something else decides which of those rules applies. That is for your Swiss *Treuhänder* or tax adviser, not Form 5472.

## Does the US LLC go on the Swiss wealth tax return?

Generally yes, in some form. Switzerland’s wealth tax is levied by cantons and communes on total net assets, and the taxpayer declares assets in Switzerland and abroad. Exactly how the LLC interest or its assets is declared and valued is a cantonal question for your adviser.

The ESTV dossier on the [wealth tax of individuals](https://www.estv2.admin.ch/stp/ds/d-vermoegenssteuer-np-de.pdf) states that no direct federal tax has been levied on individuals’ wealth since 1959. It says total net wealth is taxable under article 13(1) of the Tax Harmonisation Act (StHG), that the taxpayer must declare all assets in Switzerland and abroad, and it lists bank balances and securities, including shares and GmbH interests, among taxable assets.

For a US LLC owner, that means the Swiss return and the US return look at different things. The Swiss return asks what you own on the valuation date; Form 5472 asks which related-party transactions happened during the year. Keep the same CHF-to-USD workpaper available for both, but do not expect one to answer the other.

## What is a reliable CHF-to-USD workpaper?

Use a repeatable six-field record for every related-party movement:

1. transaction date;
2. related party;
3. transaction type and direction;
4. original CHF amount;
5. exchange-rate source and rate; and
6. resulting USD amount with source evidence.

For example, do not convert only the owner’s year-end net balance. A contribution on one date and a distribution on another may use different reasonable rates and must remain separate in the Part V description. Our [exchange-rate guide](/blog/form-5472-currency-conversion-exchange-rates) gives a fuller workpaper model.

Worked example for a 2026 calendar year:

| Transaction | Original amount | Working | Form 5472 workpaper amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Owner capital contribution from a Swiss account | CHF 8,000 | CHF 8,000 x 1.1300 | USD 9,040 |
| Owner pays the registered-agent invoice by card | USD 300 | Already in USD | USD 300 |
| Distribution to the owner in Switzerland | CHF 3,500 | CHF 3,500 x 1.1800 | USD 4,130 |
| Total related-party value reviewed |  | USD 9,040 + USD 300 + USD 4,130 | USD 13,470 |

The rates are illustrative. Replace them with documented rates for the relevant dates or another published source applied consistently. For reference, the IRS published a 2025 yearly average of 0.831 Swiss francs per US dollar; our [IRS yearly average exchange rates](/irs-yearly-average-exchange-rates) tool lists the published figures by year.

## How does a Swiss owner file the package?

1. **Confirm the LLC is a one-owner disregarded entity.** Different entity classifications require different returns.
2. **Map the owner and every related party.** Use the AHV number for the individual or UID for the Swiss entity as applicable.
3. **Reconcile gross related-party movements.** Do not mix client revenue with contributions, distributions, loans, or related-company charges.
4. **Prepare the pro forma Form 1120 and Form 5472.** Add the required supporting statement for Part V transactions.
5. **Sign and file by fax or mail.** The foreign-owned disregarded-entity package cannot be e-filed.
6. **Retain the package and delivery evidence.** Keep the exact signed pages, conversion workpaper, statements, and transmission record.

A calendar-year 2026 package is generally due April 15, 2027, or October 15, 2027 after a timely extension. Our guide on [how to file Form 5472](/file-form-5472) walks through each step, including the 300 DPI fax requirement and the mailing alternative.

## Four Swiss scenarios, worked through

**Zurich consultant, Wyoming LLC, work done in Switzerland.** The owner funded the LLC with CHF 8,000 and later withdrew cash. US income-tax exposure depends on the full facts, but those movements are related-party transactions, so Form 5472 is generally required.

**Swiss GmbH as the LLC’s owner.** The GmbH, not its shareholder, is the foreign owner. Use the GmbH’s name, Swiss address and UID, and keep the shareholder’s AHV number off the form.

**Geneva resident whose own GmbH invoices the LLC.** The GmbH is a related party separate from the individual owner. Its charges need their own Form 5472 analysis alongside the owner’s contributions and draws.

**Dormant LLC with owner-paid fees.** No sales, but the owner paid the state fee and registered agent personally. Those payments can still be reportable, and the Swiss wealth tax return may still need to show the LLC.

## What mistakes do Swiss owners make?

The common mistakes are mixing up identifiers, netting the ledger, and assuming the Swiss return covers the US one.

- Entering the LLC’s EIN, or a passport number, instead of the AHV number or UID.
- Using the shareholder’s AHV number when a Swiss company is the owner.
- Converting only the year-end net balance from CHF.
- Assuming the treaty or zero US income removes the filing.
- Assuming the wealth tax declaration in Switzerland satisfies US reporting.
- Missing April 15 without filing Form 7004 first.

## Frequently asked questions

### What FTIN does a Swiss individual use?

A Swiss-resident individual normally uses the AHV insurance number as the FTIN. A Swiss company normally uses its UID instead.

### Must a Swiss owner obtain an ITIN?

No, not solely for Form 5472. Use a consistent LLC-assigned reference ID when the owner lacks a US identifying number, and enter the applicable Swiss FTIN.

### Does a Swiss client payment go on Form 5472?

Not merely because the payment is foreign. Ordinary unrelated-customer revenue is different from a transaction with the owner or another related party.

### Does the tax treaty remove the filing obligation?

No. Treaty relief may affect US income tax on specific income, but it does not replace the Form 5472 disclosure required after reportable related-party transactions.

### Does declaring the LLC for Swiss wealth tax replace Form 5472?

No. Your Swiss tax return, filed with the cantonal tax authority, reports what you own for wealth tax. Form 5472 is filed with the IRS and reports related-party transactions. A Swiss resident may need both.

### Can Form 5472 amounts be reported in Swiss francs?

No. The form reports US-dollar values. Preserve the original CHF amount and rate support in the workpaper, then report the consistently converted USD amount.
