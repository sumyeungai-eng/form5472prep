**A pro forma Form 1120 is the abbreviated cover return that a wholly foreign-owned US disregarded entity uses to transmit Form 5472. It does not automatically turn the LLC into a corporation for all federal income-tax purposes. The filer completes the limited identifying items required by the instructions, writes “Foreign-owned U.S. DE” across the top, and attaches Form 5472.**

The pro forma return and Form 5472 form one filing package. Owners can [start the guided package](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=pro-forma-1120) when they want both documents prepared consistently.

## Why does a disregarded LLC use Form 1120?

The LLC is treated as separate from its foreign owner only for the limited reporting requirements under section 6038A. The [2025 Form 1120 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120) state that a covered foreign-owned domestic disregarded entity files a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached by the return due date.

The pro forma 1120 supplies the reporting entity's identifying shell. Form 5472 supplies the ownership and related-party transaction information.

## Is the pro forma Form 1120 a full corporate return?

No. Compare the two:

| Item | Pro forma 1120 for foreign-owned DE | Full Form 1120 corporate return |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Transmit Form 5472 under section 6038A | Report corporate income tax |
| Entity status | LLC remains disregarded except for limited reporting rule | Entity is taxed as a corporation |
| Completion | Limited identifying items required by instructions | Full applicable income, deduction, tax and schedule fields |
| Filing method here | Fax or mail with Form 5472 | Method follows ordinary corporate-return rules |

Do not fill random zeroes throughout every corporate line. Complete the items the current instructions require for the pro forma package and apply any separate return obligation based on the facts.

## What information goes on the pro forma 1120?

The IRS instructions identify limited fields for a foreign-owned disregarded entity. The package commonly needs:

- LLC legal name and address;
- employer identification number;
- date formed;
- total assets when required by the form instructions;
- the owner's country on the specified line;
- tax year; and
- “Foreign-owned U.S. DE” written across the top.

Use the LLC's legal information, not the owner's personal name in place of the entity. The LLC must obtain an EIN for this reporting purpose; the [Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4) specifically address “Foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity-Form 5472.”

## What gets attached to the pro forma return?

Attach one Form 5472 for each related party with reportable transactions. Include the Part V statement describing contributions, distributions, formation, acquisition, disposition or dissolution transactions of the disregarded entity.

Additional attachments may include an exchange-rate schedule, ownership-attribution explanation or reasonable cause statement. Label each with the LLC's name, EIN and tax year so a separated page can be matched back to the return.

## How is the package filed?

1. Confirm the LLC remained wholly foreign-owned and disregarded.
2. Complete the limited pro forma Form 1120 fields.
3. Write “Foreign-owned U.S. DE” across the top.
4. Complete Form 5472 and all required attachments.
5. Assemble one ordered PDF with Form 1120 first.
6. Fax at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737, or mail to the dedicated Ogden PIN Unit address in the [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472).
7. Keep the exact package and delivery proof.

The usual calendar-year deadline is April 15, with a six-month extension available through a timely Form 7004.

## What mistakes cause incomplete packages?

Common errors include sending Form 5472 without the pro forma 1120, omitting the foreign-owned-DE notation, using the owner's number as the LLC EIN, leaving Part V unsupported, filing one Form 5472 for multiple related parties, or mailing to the ordinary Form 1120 address.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does filing a pro forma 1120 make the LLC a C corporation?

No. The foreign-owned disregarded entity is treated as a corporation only for limited section 6038A reporting. A separate Form 8832 election can change classification.

### Does the pro forma Form 1120 report business profit?

Not as a full corporate income-tax return. Other US tax returns may be required depending on trade, income, elections, employees and owner facts.

### Can the LLC file Form 5472 without Form 1120?

No. A covered foreign-owned US disregarded entity attaches Form 5472 to the required pro forma Form 1120.

### Does the LLC need an EIN?

Yes. The US LLC uses its EIN on the filing package even when its foreign individual owner has no SSN or ITIN.

### Can the pro forma package be e-filed?

No. The current Form 5472 instructions require the foreign-owned disregarded entity to fax or mail the package to the Ogden PIN Unit.
