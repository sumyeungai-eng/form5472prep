**Form 5472, the FBAR and Form 8938 answer three different questions, go to two different agencies and run on separate deadlines. Form 5472 is your US LLC's information return about dealings with its owner. The FBAR reports foreign financial accounts to FinCEN. Form 8938 is a tax-return attachment about foreign assets.**

Owners of foreign-owned US LLCs meet these three names in the same forum threads and come away believing they are variations of one obligation. They are not. A nonresident owner of a US LLC can end up owing one of them, two of them, all three, or none — and the facts that decide each one barely overlap.

What we can say precisely is the first one. If your single-member US LLC had a reportable transaction with you or another related party, it files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120, and the [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state a **$25,000** penalty for a reporting corporation that fails to file "when due and in the manner prescribed." [We prepare and fax that package from $149](/start).

What we cannot say is whether you personally owe an FBAR or a Form 8938. Both turn on your own account holdings, asset holdings and US tax status, which we do not see and are not qualified to assess. This guide gives you each form's test and its authority so you can take the right question to the right professional.

## What question does each of the three forms answer?

Each form exists to answer one question for one audience, and the questions are not versions of each other.

**Form 5472 asks a question about an entity's dealings with its related parties.** The Instructions for Form 5472 say the form provides "information required under sections 6038A and 6038C when reportable transactions occur during the tax year of a reporting corporation with a foreign or domestic related party." The filer is the US entity. Customer revenue is outside the question entirely.

**The FBAR asks a question about where accounts are held.** It is a Bank Secrecy Act report, not a tax form. The [IRS FBAR page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/report-of-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts-fbar) frames it as a report by "a U.S. person, including a citizen, resident, corporation, partnership, limited liability company, trust and estate" of "a financial interest in or signature or other authority over at least one financial account located outside the United States." Income is irrelevant to it.

**Form 8938 asks a question about what foreign assets a taxpayer holds.** The [Instructions for Form 8938](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938) direct a filer to "Attach Form 8938 to your annual return and file by the due date (including extensions) for that return." It is a section 6038D disclosure riding along with an income tax return, so it presupposes a filer who files such a return.

Three different subjects: a transaction, an account location, an asset holding. Three different filers: an entity, a US person, a taxpayer.

## Who files Form 5472, and why is it the LLC's own return?

The LLC files it, not you — and that is the part worth internalising, because it is the only one of the three where the entity is the filer rather than a person.

A foreign-owned US single-member LLC is normally disregarded for income tax. Under **Treas. Reg. § 1.6038A-1**, for tax years beginning on or after 1 January 2017 and ending on or after 13 December 2017, such an entity is treated as a corporation separate from its owner **solely** for the § 6038A reporting rules. That narrow fiction is why an LLC with no US tax liability still files a corporate-style information return.

The mechanics follow from that. Form 5472 attaches to a **pro forma Form 1120** with "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top of page 1; only the entity's name and address and items B and E are required. It cannot be e-filed. It goes by fax to **855-887-7737** or by mail to Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201.

The trigger is a reportable transaction with a related party: owner contributions, distributions, loans in either direction, company costs you paid personally, and payments for services between you and the LLC. Money from customers and platforms is not a reportable transaction — [worked examples of reportable transactions](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples) walk through the sorting.

If you are also wondering about Form **5471**, that is the opposite-direction form for certain US persons connected to foreign corporations; the [Form 5472 vs Form 5471 comparison](/blog/form-5472-vs-form-5471) covers which way the ownership has to point.

## Who files an FBAR, and why is the LLC tested separately from you?

Two separate persons have to be tested, and the result for one does not decide the other.

The IRS FBAR page lists a "limited liability company" among the US persons that must file. A US-organised LLC is therefore a US person for this report in its own right, regardless of where its owner lives and regardless of being disregarded for income tax. Whether it actually has an FBAR obligation depends on whether it has a financial interest in, or signature or other authority over, foreign financial accounts, and on their aggregate value during the calendar year.

Your own position is a different question with a different answer. A nonresident owner is not automatically a US person for this purpose, and an account in your personal name is not automatically an LLC account. Both determinations rest on facts about account ownership, account location and balances that only you and your adviser can establish from the account agreements.

We are not able to run either test for you, and nothing here says you do or do not have an FBAR to file. The threshold wording, the account-location problem and the evidence to gather are set out in our dedicated piece on [whether a foreign-owned US LLC needs an FBAR](/blog/foreign-owned-us-llc-fbar). Take the conclusion to a professional who handles FBARs before you act on it.

## Who files Form 8938, and why can we not tell you whether you do?

Form 8938 is filed by "specified individuals and specified domestic entities" who hold specified foreign financial assets above a reporting threshold, and both halves of that sentence are determinations about you rather than about your LLC.

The Instructions for Form 8938 describe a specified individual as a US citizen or "a resident alien of the United States for any part of the tax year," among other categories. A specified domestic entity is defined separately and includes certain closely held domestic corporations and trusts by reference to their income and beneficiaries. Working out whether a particular LLC or a particular person falls inside either definition is a legal analysis of your facts.

**The thresholds vary**, and we deliberately do not reproduce them here. The instructions state that "your applicable reporting threshold depends upon whether you are married, file a joint federal income tax return, and live inside (or outside) the United States." Read the figures from the IRS directly rather than from a blog, because quoting the wrong row is how people conclude they are safe when they are not.

One structural point does carry across cleanly: Form 8938 attaches to an annual income tax return. A person who files no US income tax return has no return to attach it to — but whether you are required to file such a return is itself a question for an adviser, not an inference you should draw from owning a disregarded LLC.

## Can you owe one, some or none of the three?

Yes — all four outcomes are possible, and the combinations are not a package deal.

| Assumption we hear | Why it does not follow |
|---|---|
| "I filed Form 5472, so the IRS has what it needs." | Form 5472 reports related-party transactions to the IRS. The FBAR reports accounts to FinCEN under a different statute. Neither is evidence of the other. |
| "I'm not a US citizen, so none of this touches me." | Form 5472 is filed by the LLC, not by you. Your own status is a separate question from the entity's. |
| "My LLC is disregarded, so it has no obligations of its own." | Disregarded status is an income tax characterisation. It does not remove the § 6038A reporting fiction, and it does not stop a US-organised LLC being a US person for FBAR purposes. |
| "My accounts hold under $10,000 each, so there is nothing to report." | The FBAR test is stated in the aggregate across accounts, not per account. The figures and exceptions belong to your adviser's analysis. |
| "Form 8938 and the FBAR are the same thing filed twice." | Different filers, assets, thresholds, deadlines and agencies. The [IRS comparison of Form 8938 and FBAR requirements](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/comparison-of-form-8938-and-fbar-requirements) sets them out line by line. |

## How should you work through the three questions in order?

Take them one at a time, in the order that matches who the filer is.

1. **Settle the LLC's Form 5472 position first.** List every movement between the LLC and you or any entity you control, for each tax year the LLC has existed. One such movement in a year puts that year in scope.
2. **Separate customer money from owner money.** Platform payouts, client invoices and processor fees are unrelated-party revenue. What you withdrew, contributed, lent or paid personally is the reportable set.
3. **Ask the FBAR question about the LLC.** Identify every financial account the LLC holds or controls, where each one is actually maintained, and the highest value each reached during the calendar year. Keep the account agreements.
4. **Ask the FBAR and Form 8938 questions about yourself.** Your own citizenship, residence, filing status and asset holdings drive both, and they are independent of step 3.
5. **Hand steps 3 and 4 to a qualified adviser with the documents.** Give them the account agreements and statements rather than a summary. An adviser who handles FBAR and § 6038D work can give you an answer; a Form 5472 preparer cannot.

The order matters because the Form 5472 deadline arrives with the LLC's return, which is usually the nearest one. Our [Form 5472 deadline guide](/blog/form-5472-deadline-2026) sets out the dates and the extension route.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does filing Form 5472 cover my FBAR?

No. Form 5472 goes to the IRS under sections 6038A and 6038C. The FBAR goes to FinCEN through the BSA E-Filing System under the Bank Secrecy Act. The IRS FBAR page states you do not file the FBAR with your federal tax return.

### Is my US LLC a US person for FBAR purposes?

The IRS FBAR page lists a limited liability company among the US persons that must file. Whether your LLC actually has a reportable position depends on its foreign accounts and their values — facts for your adviser, not a conclusion we can reach.

### Can you tell me whether I have to file an FBAR or Form 8938?

No. Both depend on your accounts, assets, residence and US tax status, which we neither see nor are qualified to assess. We give you the test and the IRS or FinCEN page stating it, and you take those to a qualified adviser.

### Does Form 8938 replace the FBAR?

No. The IRS publishes a comparison of Form 8938 and FBAR requirements precisely because the filers, assets, thresholds, deadlines and receiving agencies differ. Filing one does not satisfy the other, and some assets can appear on both.

### I am not a US citizen or resident. Does my LLC still file Form 5472?

If it is a foreign-owned US disregarded entity that had a reportable transaction with a related party, yes — the LLC is the filer. Treas. Reg. § 1.6038A-1 treats it as a separate corporation solely for the section 6038A reporting rules.

### Which of the three has the largest headline penalty?

As stated on the pages cited here, Form 5472 carries $25,000 for failure to file when due and in the manner prescribed, and Form 8938 starts at $10,000. FBAR penalties are set in Title 31 and adjusted annually for inflation.
