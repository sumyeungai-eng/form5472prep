A foreign-owned US LLC paying for a car does not, by itself, make the car deductible. For a single-member LLC with no corporate tax election, first identify the owner's applicable income-tax rules, then establish the vehicle's business use. Keep any owner funding or personal benefit on a separate Form 5472 review schedule.

This guide is for an LLC wholly owned by a non-US individual and treated as a disregarded entity. A corporate election, another member, or the owner becoming a US tax resident changes the analysis.

## Which return would claim the deduction?

The IRS generally treats a single-member LLC without a corporate election as part of its owner for federal income-tax purposes. See the [IRS single-member LLC guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/single-member-limited-liability-companies).

For a nonresident individual, ordinary business deductions generally must relate to income effectively connected with a US trade or business. A US registration and American customers do not establish that connection. [Publication 519, chapter 5](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p519) explains this limit; our [foreign-owned LLC income-tax guide](/blog/does-foreign-owned-llc-pay-us-tax) explains the facts to check.

If no US income-tax deduction is available, a car payment does not become a US refund merely because it came from the LLC account. The owner's residence country may apply its own rules to the business and vehicle.

The LLC's **pro forma Form 1120 attached to Form 5472 is not a place to claim car deductions**. The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) require only limited identifying information on that cover return. An owner-level US income-tax filing, when required, is a separate engagement.

## Business purpose matters more than the payment card

Personal, living and family costs generally do not become business expenses when paid from a business account. Vehicle expenses used for both purposes must be split. The [IRS business-expense FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/small-business-self-employed-other-business/income-expenses/income-expenses-1) makes both points.

The useful question is therefore “what did this trip accomplish?” A receipt establishes that money was spent; it rarely establishes why the journey was business-related.

| Situation | Evidence to assemble | Decision still needed |
|---|---|---|
| LLC buys a vehicle from an unrelated dealer | Purchase agreement, title, payment, first business-use date | Asset treatment and applicable deduction rules |
| Owner buys a car personally and uses it for client visits | Ownership records, trip log, reimbursement agreement | Which costs belong to the business and which taxpayer claims them |
| LLC pays the owner's personal car bills | Invoice, purpose, owner-account entry | Personal benefit, repayment or another supported classification |
| Owner contributes a car to the LLC | Transfer documents, condition, valuation evidence, date | Property contribution, basis and reporting treatment |
| LLC pays rent to the owner for vehicle use | Actual agreement, payments, use records | Income-tax treatment and related-party reporting |

This table is an evidence map. It does not assign a deduction or a Form 5472 amount just from the transaction label.

## Commuting and private trips need their own category

Under the general US rules, travel between home and a main or regular workplace is personal commuting. A business call during the journey does not change it. Travel from a qualifying principal-place-of-business home office to another work location can have different treatment. See [Publication 463, transportation expenses](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463).

For a mobile founder, “I work everywhere” is not a substitute for identifying a tax home, regular workplace and actual trip purpose. Record those facts before classifying a drive to a coworking space or airport. A holiday itinerary and a client itinerary can share the same car without sharing the same deduction treatment.

## One purchase can create several records

Consider this hypothetical sequence, in US dollars:

1. The foreign owner transfers **$12,000** to the LLC.
2. The LLC buys a car for **$20,000**, using that funding plus existing cash.
3. During the year, the trip log records **5,000 business miles** and **10,000 total miles**.

The owner transfer, dealer payment and mileage allocation answer different questions. The business-use ratio is **5,000 ÷ 10,000 = 50%**. That ratio is not a conclusion that $10,000 of the purchase price can be deducted immediately. Vehicle cost recovery has its own limits and timing.

For Form 5472, review the owner funding separately from the purchase from an unrelated dealer. Contributions, distributions, related-party tangible-property transactions and nonmonetary exchanges sit within the reporting framework in [Treasury Regulation § 1.6038A-2(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6038A-2). A dealer payment should not be copied into an owner-transfer total merely because the owner funded the account first.

If the LLC provides the owner private use of a vehicle, preserve the arrangement and use records for the preparer to assess. The absence of a cash transfer to the owner does not settle a property-use transaction.

## Build the file before choosing a deduction method

Create one vehicle folder with the ownership or lease documents, purchase and disposal dates, annual odometer readings, dated trip purposes, running-cost receipts and an owner-payment reconciliation. Mark the country where the car is kept and used. Keep receipts in the original currency and retain the conversion method used in the US reporting workpapers.

Then compare the eligible [standard mileage and actual-expense methods](/blog/business-car-mileage-vs-actual-expenses-2026). If the car is electric, also read the [current EV deduction and credit guide](/blog/electric-company-car-us-llc-tax-credits-2026). Neither choice resolves foreign VAT or local vehicle-registration requirements.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does putting the LLC name on the title guarantee a deduction?

No. The relevant taxpayer, income-tax connection, business use and deduction rules still matter. Ownership documents are one part of the evidence.

### Is buying a car itself an owner distribution?

Not automatically. A genuine LLC purchase from an unrelated dealer differs from paying an owner's personal bill or transferring property to the owner. Keep the actual arrangement and funding trail for classification.
