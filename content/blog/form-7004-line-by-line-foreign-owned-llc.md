**A foreign-owned U.S. disregarded LLC enters form code 12 (Form 1120) on Form 7004, Part I, line 1, leaves the Part II checkboxes on lines 2, 3 and 4 blank, states its tax year on line 5a, and enters -0- on lines 6, 7 and 8 when no tax is due. Form 7004 requires no signature.**

Form 7004 is one page, which is what makes it dangerous: a foreign-owned LLC that gets one of the handful of entries wrong does not find out until the IRS treats the return as late. The walkthrough below goes entry by entry through the December 2025 revision, citing the instruction behind each one. If you would rather hand the form over, [we file Form 7004 for foreign-owned LLCs](/form-7004-extension).

This guide covers one filer: a U.S.-formed single-member LLC wholly owned by a foreign person and treated as a disregarded entity, which files Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120. An LLC that elected corporate treatment, a multi-member LLC, or a foreign corporation has a different analysis. For whether an extension is available at all and what it buys you, see our [Form 5472 extension guide](/blog/form-5472-extension); this post is the line-by-line fill-in.

## What does Form 7004 ask for above Part I?

The top block of Form 7004 (Rev. December 2025) asks for the filer name, identifying number and address, in these fields: **Name**, **Identifying number**, **Number and street** with room or suite number, **City or town**, **State or province**, **Country**, and **ZIP or foreign postal code**.

The **Name** is the LLC's legal name as registered with the IRS, not the owner's name and not a trading name. The **Identifying number** is the LLC's own EIN. The [Form 7004 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7004) are blunt about the consequence of getting either wrong: if the name entered on Form 7004 does not match the IRS database, or the identifying number is incorrect, you will not have a valid extension.

The **State or province** and **Country** fields exist because filers like this one have a foreign address: use whichever address — the LLC's U.S. registered agent or the owner's own — should receive IRS correspondence.

## Which form code goes on Form 7004, Part I, line 1?

**Code 12.** Form 7004, Part I, line 1 says "Enter the form code for the return listed below that this application is for," and the table printed on the form lists **Form 1120 — 12**.

The [Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) confirm this is the right code for a disregarded entity: "Because the Form 5472 of a DE must be attached to a pro forma Form 1120, the code for Form 1120 should be entered on Form 7004, Part I, line 1."

This is the entry filers most often leave blank, because the LLC is not a corporation and is not filing a real Form 1120. The IRS position is the opposite: the Form 5472 rides on a pro forma Form 1120, so the Form 1120 code is the code. Do not use 15 (Form 1120-F), 25 (Form 1120-S) or 09 (Form 1065).

## What should a foreign-owned LLC write across the top of Form 7004?

**"Foreign-owned U.S. DE."** The Form 5472 instructions state that "'Foreign-owned U.S. DE' should be written across the top of Form 7004" — the same notation that goes across the top of the pro forma Form 1120 itself.

The notation is how the IRS Ogden PIN Unit recognises what the submission is. Without it, the form arrives at a special-handling address with nothing identifying the return behind it.

## Which Part II lines apply to a foreign-owned disregarded LLC?

Part II is headed "All Filers Must Complete This Part," which filers read as "check something here." In the ordinary case, a foreign-owned U.S. DE checks none of the three boxes.

| Form 7004 line | What the line is for | Typical foreign-owned U.S. DE entry |
|---|---|---|
| **1** (Part I) | Form code for the return being extended | **12** (Form 1120) |
| **2** | "If the organization is a foreign corporation that does not have an office or place of business in the United States" | Blank — the filer is a **U.S.-formed** entity, not a foreign corporation |
| **3** | Common parent of a group filing a consolidated return | Blank — a single-member LLC has no consolidated group |
| **4** | Entity that "qualifies under Regulations section 1.6081-5" | Blank unless a tax professional confirms the regulation applies |
| **5a** | Calendar year, or the tax year's beginning and ending dates | The LLC's tax year (calendar year for most) |
| **5b** | Short tax year reason: initial return, final return, change in accounting period, consolidated return to be filed, other | Checked **only** if the tax year is under 12 months |
| **6** | "Tentative total tax" | **-0-** when no tax is expected |
| **7** | "Total payments and credits" | **-0-** when there are none |
| **8** | "Balance due. Subtract line 7 from line 6" | **-0-** |

**Line 2** is the one foreign owners reach for instinctively, because the word "foreign" appears in it. The box is for a *foreign corporation* — an entity organised outside the United States. A Delaware or Wyoming LLC owned by a foreign person is a domestic entity with a foreign owner, which is not the same thing.

**Line 4** deserves care rather than a guess. The Form 7004 instructions describe line 4 as being for entities "entitled to an automatic extension of time to file and pay under Regulations section 1.6081-5." [26 CFR 1.6081-5(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.6081-5) grants that extension to a closed list that includes "Domestic corporations which transact their business and keep their records and books of account outside the United States and Puerto Rico," and requires a statement attached to the return showing the filer falls within the list. Whether a disregarded entity filing a pro forma Form 1120 sits inside that list is addressed in neither set of instructions, and the Form 5472 extension route does not tell a foreign-owned U.S. DE to check line 4. Leave line 4 blank unless a tax professional confirms the regulation covers the entity.

## What goes on Form 7004 lines 6, 7 and 8 when no tax is due?

Enter **-0-** on each line. The Form 7004 instructions describe line 6 as the total tax, including any nonrefundable credits, that the entity expects to owe for the tax year, and direct filers to enter zero as -0- when none is expected. Line 7 is "Total payments and credits." Line 8 is "Balance due. Subtract line 7 from line 6."

A foreign-owned single-member LLC filing a pro forma Form 1120 is not reporting income on that form: the pro forma return exists to carry Form 5472. A zero on all three lines is the normal entry. Leaving the lines empty invites a processing question; writing -0- answers it.

One warning that applies even when lines 6 to 8 are zeros: the Form 7004 instructions state plainly that "Form 7004 does not extend the time to pay tax." If the LLC or its owner has a separate U.S. tax liability — owner-level income tax, withholding, employment tax, a state filing — the extension does nothing for the payment deadline on any of it.

## Does Form 7004 need a signature?

**No.** The Form 7004 instructions say, in four words, "No signature is required on this form." The December 2025 form carries no signature block: Part II ends at line 8, followed by the Privacy Act and Paperwork Reduction Act notice.

Filers hunt for a signature line anyway, then sign somewhere random or hold the form while chasing the owner's signature. Neither is necessary. The Form 5472 package itself does still need the owner's signature on the pro forma Form 1120.

## Where does a foreign-owned U.S. DE send Form 7004?

To the special Ogden address in the Form 5472 instructions, by the regular due date. The Form 5472 instructions tell the DE to "fax or mail the Form 7004 to the fax number or mailing address identified earlier, by the due date (excluding extensions) of the return," and add: "For these entities, do not use the regular filing address listed in the Instructions for Form 7004."

That fax number and address are:

1. **Fax** (at 300 DPI or higher) to **855-887-7737** — the IRS Ogden fax line for these filings, not a phone number you can call and not our number.
2. **Or mail** to Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201.

Two sets of IRS instructions pull in different directions on electronic filing, and the more specific one wins. The Form 7004 instructions say Form 7004 "can be filed electronically for most returns," excluding only Forms 8612, 8613, 8725, 8831, 8876 and 706-GS(D) — a list that does not mention Form 1120. The Form 5472 instructions override that general statement for this filer by sending the DE to the PIN Unit instead, and they are the instructions written for a foreign-owned U.S. DE. Separately, those instructions state: "If you are a foreign-owned U.S. DE, you cannot file Form 5472 electronically."

Expect no confirmation. The Form 7004 instructions state that the IRS will no longer send notification that an extension has been approved; filers are contacted only if the request is denied. A provider's fax transmission report is evidence that you sent something, not that the IRS accepted it.

## When is Form 7004 due for a foreign-owned LLC?

By the regular due date of the pro forma Form 1120, with no extensions. The [Instructions for Form 1120](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120) set the general rule: "Generally, a corporation must file its income tax return by the 15th day of the 4th month after the end of its tax year," and allow filing on the next business day when the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday. For a calendar-year LLC, that is 15 April of the following year, absent special relief. The Form 7004 automatic extension period "is generally 6 months," which moves a calendar-year filer to 15 October.

A late Form 7004 is not a short extension. It is nothing. Once the regular due date passes without a valid extension, the question changes to [filing late and arguing reasonable cause](/blog/form-5472-filed-late-never-filed), with the $25,000 penalty in the Form 5472 instructions in play. If the LLC still has no EIN, read [EIN pending at the Form 5472 deadline](/blog/form-5472-ein-pending-deadline) before assuming a placeholder will hold.

## The bottom line

Form 7004 for a foreign-owned disregarded LLC is six entries and a notation: code 12, the LLC's exact name and EIN, "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" across the top, the tax year on line 5a, -0- on lines 6 to 8, no signature, faxed or mailed to the Ogden PIN Unit by the regular due date. The errors that void an extension are mundane and all of them are avoidable before you send it. You can [order Form 7004 extension filing](/form-7004-extension) or file it yourself; either way it has to be in by the original due date.

## Frequently asked questions

### What form code does a foreign-owned LLC use on Form 7004?

Code **12**, the Form 1120 code. The Form 5472 instructions state that because a DE's Form 5472 attaches to a pro forma Form 1120, the Form 1120 code goes on Form 7004, Part I, line 1.

### Should a foreign-owned LLC check box 2 on Form 7004?

No. Line 2 is for a foreign corporation with no U.S. office or place of business. A U.S.-formed LLC owned by a foreign person is a domestic entity, so the box does not apply.

### Does Form 7004 require a signature?

No. The Form 7004 instructions state: "No signature is required on this form." The December 2025 revision has no signature block. The pro forma Form 1120 behind it does still need signing.

### What do you put on Form 7004 line 6 if the LLC owes no tax?

Enter **-0-**. The Form 7004 instructions direct filers to enter the total tax expected for the year and to show zero as -0-. Lines 7 and 8 are also -0- when there are no payments or credits.

### Can Form 7004 be e-filed for a foreign-owned disregarded entity?

The Form 5472 instructions direct a foreign-owned U.S. DE to fax or mail Form 7004 to the Ogden PIN Unit and not to use the regular Form 7004 filing address. Follow that instruction rather than the general e-file statement.

### Does the IRS confirm that Form 7004 was approved?

No. The Form 7004 instructions state that the IRS no longer sends notification of approval and contacts filers only if the request is denied.

### What happens if Form 7004 has the wrong name or EIN?

The Form 7004 instructions state that a name not matching the IRS database, or an incorrect identifying number, means you will not have a valid extension. Verify both against the LLC's EIN confirmation letter before sending.
