**US sales tax is not VAT. There is no federal US sales tax and no US VAT, no input credits to reclaim, and no single national registration. Each state sets its own rules, and in many states a marketplace such as Amazon or Etsy collects the tax instead of the seller.**

Most foreign owners of US LLCs arrive at this question with a VAT-shaped mental model, and almost every part of that model misfires. There is no registration number that works nationwide, no quarterly return that nets purchases against sales, and no single threshold above which you must register. If you want the nexus analysis in depth — where a state can require you to collect at all — read [sales tax nexus for a foreign-owned LLC](/blog/sales-tax-nexus-foreign-owned-llc). ## How is US sales tax different from VAT?

| | US sales tax | VAT (as most foreign owners know it) |
|---|---|---|
| Who imposes it | Individual states, and in many cases local jurisdictions within them | Typically a single national government |
| Is there a national version | No. There is no federal US sales tax | Usually one national system |
| Registration | Separately with each state where the seller has an obligation | Typically one registration covering the country |
| Threshold | Set by each state, and the tests differ between states | Typically one national threshold |
| Charged at which stage | Generally once, on the retail sale to the end customer | Generally at each stage of the supply chain |
| Reclaiming tax on your own purchases | No input-credit mechanism of the VAT kind | Input VAT on business purchases is generally recoverable |
| Who can collect instead of the seller | A marketplace facilitator, in many states | Varies by country |

The VAT column describes the general shape of a VAT system rather than any one country's rules, and no figures are given for it. Form5472 Prep does not advise on VAT; the owner's own country's VAT or GST position belongs with a local adviser.

The structural difference that matters most is the input credit. A VAT-registered business is used to recovering tax on its own inputs, so the tax it collects is only partly money it hands over. US sales tax has no equivalent: tax collected from a customer is held for the state and remitted to the state.

## Does a foreign-owned US LLC charge VAT on its US sales?

No. A US LLC selling to US customers does not charge VAT, because the United States does not operate a VAT. Where the LLC has a sales tax obligation, it charges that state's sales tax on taxable sales to customers in that state.

An invoice from a US LLC therefore carries no VAT line and no VAT number. Customers outside the United States who ask for one are applying their own country's expectations to a US entity. Whether the LLC has any obligation under a *non-US* VAT system is a separate question governed by that country's rules, not by US law, and is outside the scope of this guide.

## Can the LLC reclaim US sales tax it pays on its own purchases?

There is no VAT-style input credit in US sales tax. A foreign-owned LLC that pays sales tax on equipment, software or supplies does not file a return to recover it the way a VAT-registered business recovers input VAT.

The nearest equivalent is narrower and works differently: a resale exemption. A business buying goods to resell can generally give the seller a resale certificate so the tax is not charged on that purchase in the first place, rather than paying it and reclaiming it later. The Texas Comptroller's remote-seller guidance illustrates that resale sales are still part of the measurement, noting that total Texas revenue includes gross receipts from taxable and nontaxable sales, "including sales for resale and sales to exempt entities" ([Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/remote-sellers.php)).

Two consequences follow for a foreign owner's budgeting. Sales tax collected from customers is not revenue, and sales tax paid on the LLC's own costs is generally just a cost.

## Is there one US registration threshold, like a VAT threshold?

No. Each state sets its own threshold, its own measurement period and its own test. The general rule that a state can require an out-of-state seller to collect without the seller being physically present there dates from the 2018 Supreme Court decision in *South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc.*; California's tax agency presents its own remote-seller rule as flowing from that decision together with state legislation AB 147, applying "to taxable sales of tangible personal property to California consumers on and after April 1, 2019" ([CDTFA](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair.htm)).

Three states, each verified against that state's own revenue pages on 30 October 2026, show how little they have in common:

| State | Threshold | What is measured | Measurement period |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Dakota | $100,000 | Gross revenue from sales into South Dakota | Previous or current calendar year |
| California | $500,000 | Combined sales of tangible personal property for delivery into California by the retailer and all related persons | Preceding or current calendar year |
| Texas | $500,000 | Total Texas revenue, including taxable and nontaxable sales, sales for resale and sales to exempt entities | Preceding twelve calendar months |

Sources: the [South Dakota Department of Revenue](https://dor.sd.gov/businesses/taxes/sales-use-tax/) states the criterion as gross revenue from sales into South Dakota exceeding $100,000 in the previous or current calendar year, and lists no separate transaction-count test on that page; the [CDTFA](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair.htm) sets the California figure at $500,000 of combined sales by the retailer and all persons related to the retailer; the [Texas Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/remote-sellers.php) states that remote sellers with total Texas revenue of less than $500,000 in the preceding twelve calendar months are not required to obtain a permit or collect use tax.

Note what differs beyond the headline number. Texas measures a rolling twelve calendar months while South Dakota and California measure calendar years. California aggregates related persons. Texas counts resale and exempt sales in the measurement even though they are not themselves taxable. Three states, three different arithmetic exercises on the same sales data.

Registration timing also differs. Texas states that a seller crossing the threshold must begin collecting use tax "beginning no later than the first day of the fourth month after the month" in which it crossed. Thresholds and rules change, so confirm each state's current page before acting on any figure, including the figures above.

Rates are separate from thresholds and are also set per state. South Dakota states that "The state sales and use tax rate is 4.2%", and local rates can apply on top in many states.

## Who collects the tax when the sale goes through a marketplace?

In many states the marketplace does, not the seller. Marketplace facilitator rules shift the collection duty to the platform for sales made through it, so a seller on Amazon or Etsy is generally not separately collecting on those orders.

Texas states the point directly: a remote seller whose only sales into Texas are through a marketplace provider that collects and reports the tax on its behalf "is not required to hold a Texas tax permit." The same guidance keeps a record-keeping duty with the seller, requiring records "of all marketplace sales for at least four years."

Two qualifications matter for a foreign owner. A seller's own website is not a marketplace, so direct sales through a Shopify or WooCommerce store sit with the LLC. And marketplace collection does not answer the separate question of whether stored inventory creates a physical presence in a state.

## Does any of this affect the LLC's Form 5472 filing?

No. Sales tax and Form 5472 are unrelated obligations that happen to land on the same LLC.

Form 5472 is a federal information return, filed with a pro forma Form 1120, reporting transactions between a foreign-owned US disregarded entity and its foreign related parties — owner funding, withdrawals, loans and owner-paid costs. The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) set the penalty for failing to file when required at $25,000 per form per year. Sales tax registration is a state matter that never appears on that federal return, and an LLC with no sales tax obligation anywhere can still have a Form 5472 filing requirement. For the federal side in full, see [what Form 5472 is and who must file](/blog/what-is-form-5472).

## What should a foreign owner actually check?

1. Separate direct sales, made through the LLC's own website, from marketplace sales where a platform collects.
2. For direct sales, total the sales into each state and compare them against that state's own current threshold and measurement period.
3. Check whether inventory is physically stored in any state, which is a separate test from the sales threshold.
4. Confirm, platform by platform, which sales a marketplace is already collecting on.
5. Keep marketplace sales records; Texas, for example, requires them for at least four years.
6. Register only where a state's own test is actually met, and take a state tax adviser's view before registering anywhere.
7. Stop expecting a VAT-style return: there is no input credit and no single national filing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a US LLC charge VAT?

No. The United States does not operate a VAT, so a US LLC issues invoices with no VAT line and no VAT number. Where it has an obligation, it charges the relevant state's sales tax instead.

### Is there a federal US sales tax?

No. Sales tax in the United States is imposed by individual states, and in many cases by local jurisdictions within them. There is no national sales tax and no national registration covering every state.

### Can a foreign-owned LLC reclaim sales tax on its purchases?

Not through a VAT-style input credit, which US sales tax does not have. The narrower equivalent is a resale exemption, where a reseller gives a certificate so tax is not charged on that purchase at all.

### Is there a single threshold before a US LLC must register?

No. Each state sets its own. South Dakota uses $100,000 of gross revenue into the state in the previous or current calendar year; California uses $500,000 of combined sales; Texas uses $500,000 of total Texas revenue over the preceding twelve calendar months.

### Does Amazon or Etsy collecting the tax end the LLC's obligations?

Not entirely. Texas states that a seller selling only through a marketplace provider that collects on its behalf need not hold a Texas permit, but the seller must still keep records of marketplace sales for at least four years, and stored inventory remains a separate question.

### Is sales tax included in the advertised price?

Sales tax is calculated on the taxable sale and collected from the customer, and the amount depends on the state and local rates that apply to that sale. South Dakota states a state rate of 4.2%, with local rates possible on top in many states.

### Does owing sales tax mean the LLC owes US federal income tax?

No. Sales tax is a state transaction tax collected from customers and remitted to a state. Whether the foreign owner owes US federal income tax turns on different questions entirely and should be assessed with a tax adviser.
