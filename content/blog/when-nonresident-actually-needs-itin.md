**Many nonresidents, including foreign LLC owners, do not need an ITIN. You need one only when a specific US federal tax purpose requires your personal taxpayer number and you cannot get an SSN—for example, filing Form 1040-NR, making a qualifying treaty claim, or supporting one of the documented Form W-7 exceptions.**

The useful question is which return, withholding rule, or reporting requirement needs your personal US taxpayer number. An LLC, EIN, bank account, or Form 5472 filing does not automatically supply the answer.

The [IRS Form W-7 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7) define an ITIN as a number for someone who needs a US taxpayer identification number for federal tax purposes but cannot get an SSN. The same instructions say to allow about **7 weeks** for an application status notice, or **9–11 weeks** during 15 January through 30 April or when filing from overseas.

## Which situations actually require an ITIN?

An ITIN is generally needed when a nonresident who is ineligible for an SSN must put a personal US taxpayer number on a federal return, refund claim, or supported withholding application.

Common genuine triggers include:

- **Filing Form 1040-NR for effectively connected income.** A nonresident conducting a US trade or business may need the individual return and a personal identifying number.
- **Claiming back tax withheld at source.** A nonresident filing Form 1040-NR solely to recover over-withholding uses Form W-7 reason box **b** when no SSN or ITIN already exists.
- **Making a treaty claim that cannot be documented with a foreign TIN.** Form W-8BEN may require a US TIN in a narrower set of circumstances, explained below.
- **Being included as a qualifying spouse or dependent on a US return.** Eligibility depends on the tax benefit, relationship, return, and SSN rules; foreign family status alone is insufficient.
- **Meeting an exception to the return-attachment rule.** Mortgage reporting, a FIRPTA withholding certificate for US real-property disposition, specified income, and T.D. 9363 can support an application with the required evidence.

Form W-7 is not a general identity application. Wanting a US number for convenience, platform onboarding, or a future possibility does not establish eligibility without a present federal tax purpose.

## When does a nonresident not need an ITIN?

A nonresident does not need an ITIN merely to own a US LLC, obtain the LLC's EIN, open an ordinary business bank account, or file the LLC's Form 5472 package. Each task uses a business identifier or foreign-owner information instead.

**Form 5472.** The LLC uses its EIN. The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) say a foreign-owned US disregarded entity enters the owner's FTIN, if any, and “None” or “N/A” when none exists. A reference ID is required when no US identifying number is entered. Read [why Form 5472 does not itself require an ITIN](/blog/itin-required-form-5472) before applying for the wrong number.

**An EIN.** The responsible party can enter “foreign” or “N/A” on Form SS-4 line 7b when that person has no SSN or ITIN and is ineligible for either. The LLC—not its owner—receives the EIN. The [foreign-owner EIN guide](/blog/ein-for-foreign-owned-llc-without-ssn) explains the separate application.

**Business banking.** The [IRS EIN page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/employer-identification-number) lists opening a bank account as an EIN use. A bank can impose its own checks, but an account is not by itself a W-7 reason. Ask whether “tax ID” means the LLC's EIN, the owner's FTIN, or a personal US TIN.

**An ordinary Form W-8BEN.** A foreign individual commonly gives Form W-8BEN to document foreign status. Line 5 is for an SSN or ITIN, while line 6a is for a foreign tax identifying number. The [Form W-8BEN instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw8ben) state that a foreign TIN issued by the jurisdiction of tax residence may be given on line 6a for the purpose of claiming treaty benefits, rather than a US TIN on line 5 where one would otherwise be required. A valid foreign TIN can therefore remove the need for an ITIN in many treaty claims.

## When does Form 1040-NR create the ITIN need?

Form 1040-NR creates the most common clear ITIN trigger when a nonresident individual has no SSN. The return may report income effectively connected with a US trade or business, report other US-source income, or claim a refund of tax withheld at source.

Form W-7 reason box **b** expressly covers both a nonresident who must file a US federal return and a nonresident filing only to obtain a refund. The usual application package contains the completed W-7, the original federal return, and current identity and foreign-status documents. The W-7 is attached to the front of the return; the IRS assigns the ITIN before processing the return.

Owning a US LLC does not prove that Form 1040-NR is due. Income source, US business activity, withholding, and treaty facts determine the individual-return question. An LLC filing obligation can exist without a personal return or ITIN obligation.

## When does a treaty claim require a US TIN?

A treaty claim requires an ITIN only when the withholding documentation rules call for a US TIN and an acceptable FTIN does not satisfy them. The W-8BEN instructions say certain treaty benefits can be claimed with an SSN or ITIN on line 5 **or** an FTIN on line 6.

The instructions generally require an ITIN for a treaty claimant who does not provide a tax identifying number issued by the jurisdiction of tax residence on line 6. The instructions also identify limited categories of actively traded or publicly offered securities-related income for which an ITIN is not required. Do not turn “I live in a treaty country” into an automatic W-7 application.

A treaty-based W-7 without a return commonly uses boxes **a** and **h**, identifies Exception 1 or 2, states the treaty country and article, and includes the required evidence. Verify the actual treaty and payment.

## Which Form W-7 exceptions can replace a tax return?

The five W-7 exceptions replace the attached federal return only when the applicant submits the evidence required in the IRS exception tables. Selecting box h without proof is not enough.

- **Exception 1** covers certain passive income subject to third-party withholding or treaty benefits, including specified partnership, interest, annuity, rental, pension, and other passive-income cases.
- **Exception 2** covers specified other income, including certain treaty claims for compensation, scholarships, fellowships, grants, or gambling income, plus certain taxable scholarship or grant cases.
- **Exception 3** covers a US home mortgage subject to third-party mortgage-interest reporting.
- **Exception 4** covers specified dispositions of US real-property interests by foreign persons, including withholding-certificate documentation.
- **Exception 5** covers a non-US representative of a foreign corporation who needs an ITIN for the e-filing reporting obligation under T.D. 9363.

Exception applicants use box **h** and write the exception number, applicable subsection, and category on the dotted line. Treaty applicants may also need box **a** or **f**. The IRS evidence table determines whether the no-return route works.

## How should you decide before applying for an ITIN?

Decide from the federal tax document backwards. A five-step review prevents an unnecessary application and identifies the right support for a necessary one.

1. **Name the federal tax event.** Identify the Form 1040-NR filing, treaty claim, withholding refund, mortgage report, real-property disposition, dependent benefit, or other W-7 exception.
2. **Rule out SSN eligibility.** An ITIN is available only to someone who needs a US taxpayer number but cannot obtain an SSN.
3. **Check whether another number works.** Form 5472 may use an FTIN and reference ID; Form SS-4 may use the permitted foreign entry; Form W-8BEN may accept an FTIN.
4. **Match the W-7 category.** Use box b with an individual return, or the correct reason and numbered exception with the evidence the instructions require.
5. **Build one consistent package.** Names, dates, foreign address, return, withholding forms, treaty evidence, and identity documents must describe the same person and transaction.

Do not obtain an ITIN “just in case.” Applying only when the tax trigger is documented produces a cleaner W-7 and avoids confusing the LLC's EIN with the owner's personal number.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all nonresidents need an ITIN?

No. A nonresident needs an ITIN only for a current federal tax purpose that requires a personal US taxpayer number when the person cannot obtain an SSN. Foreign status, LLC ownership, or a US bank account alone is not enough.

### Do I need an ITIN to own a US LLC?

No. Ownership alone does not require an ITIN. The LLC may need an EIN, and a foreign-owned disregarded LLC may need Form 5472. A separate individual return, refund, treaty, or W-7 exception can create a personal ITIN need.

### Do I need an ITIN to file Form 5472?

Usually not. The LLC uses its EIN. The foreign owner can use an FTIN and, when no US identifying number exists, a consistent reference ID. If no FTIN exists, the Form 5472 instructions permit “None” or “N/A.”

### Can I get an EIN without an ITIN?

Yes. Form SS-4 permits “foreign” or “N/A” on line 7b when the responsible party has no SSN or ITIN and is ineligible for either. The EIN belongs to the entity, not the foreign owner personally.

### Does Form W-8BEN always require an ITIN?

No. An ordinary foreign-status certification does not always require line 5, and a foreign TIN on line 6a can satisfy many treaty claims. A US TIN is required only in the circumstances stated in the W-8BEN instructions.

### Which W-7 box applies to a withholding refund?

Box b applies to a nonresident filing a US federal return only to claim a refund. Submit Form W-7 with the Form 1040-NR and the required identity, foreign-status, and withholding evidence.

### Can a CAA keep me from mailing my passport?

Yes. A Certifying Acceptance Agent can verify eligible original identity documents and return them immediately after reviewing authenticity. The CAA submits the certification with the W-7 package, so the applicant does not mail the original passport to the IRS.
