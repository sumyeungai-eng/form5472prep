**An LLC is often the simpler choice for a bootstrapped non-resident founder who expects to take profits out. A C corporation is often the practical choice for a founder seeking US venture capital or retaining profits. Tax classification, dividend withholding, compliance, and the planned exit should decide—not formation speed alone.**

The legal wrapper and the federal tax classification are related but not identical. A single-member LLC is usually disregarded for federal income tax unless it elects corporate treatment. A C corporation is a separate taxpayer. That difference changes the return, the way profits reach the founder, and the work needed after formation.

The reporting risk exists on both paths. The [IRS Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) impose a **$25,000 penalty per form, per year** when a required Form 5472 is not filed on time and in the prescribed manner. If the entity choice is already made and the foreign-owned business had related-party transactions, [start the information-return filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=llc-vs-c-corp-non-resident-founders) while an adviser reviews the wider structure.

## What is the main LLC vs C corp difference for a non-resident founder?

The main difference is where federal income-tax reporting sits. A foreign-owned single-member LLC that remains disregarded generally does not calculate its operating income on the pro forma Form 1120 used with Form 5472. Its owner’s US income-tax position is analysed separately. A C corporation reports its own income, deductions, credits, and tax on a full Form 1120.

The [Form 1120 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120) say a domestic entity electing to be an association taxable as a corporation files Form 1120 and attaches a copy of Form 8832 for the election year. The same instructions distinguish that real return from the limited pro forma Form 1120 filed by a foreign-owned disregarded entity with Form 5472.

Neither structure creates a universal “no US tax” answer. The LLC owner’s US trade-or-business facts, source of income, employees, agents, inventory, and activities matter. A C corporation is a domestic taxpayer even when every shareholder lives abroad. Entity selection therefore needs modelling by a US international-tax adviser and, usually, an adviser in the founder’s home country.

## How do an LLC and a C corporation compare?

The table compares a US single-member LLC that keeps its default disregarded status with a US C corporation. It does not cover a multi-member LLC, partnership election, or an LLC electing corporate treatment.

| Decision point | Disregarded single-member LLC | C corporation |
|---|---|---|
| Federal taxpayer | Activity is generally reflected at owner level; US income-tax filing depends on the owner’s facts | Corporation is a separate federal taxpayer |
| Core annual package | Pro forma Form 1120 plus Form 5472 when the foreign-owned LLC had a reportable transaction | Full Form 1120; Form 5472 when at least 25% foreign-owned and it had a reportable transaction |
| Federal corporate tax | No corporate tax at LLC level merely because the pro forma 1120 is filed | IRC §11 imposes tax on corporate taxable income |
| Cash paid to founder | Classified under the owner/LLC facts, often as a draw, loan repayment, reimbursement, or other owner movement | A distribution may be a dividend, subject to corporate-law and tax rules |
| Dividend layer | No corporate dividend merely because a disregarded LLC transfers owner cash | Potential corporate tax followed by dividend tax or withholding when profits are distributed |
| US venture financing | Possible legally, but often not the structure selected for institutional equity rounds | Common structure for issuing stock and venture financing, especially a Delaware corporation |
| Form 5472 penalty | $25,000 when a required form is missed or substantially incomplete | The same $25,000 penalty applies when Form 5472 is required |
| Filing method | Foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot e-file Form 5472 | Corporation generally attaches Form 5472 to its income-tax return, including an e-filed return |

Stripe Atlas, for example, supports formation of either a Delaware C corporation or an LLC. Availability from a formation platform does not make both structures equally suitable. A founder should choose the tax and financing result first, then choose the formation route.

For a corporation, [IRC §11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/11) sets the federal tax at **21% of taxable income**. That is not 21% of revenue, and state or specialist federal rules can change the total burden. The LLC column cannot be reduced to one rate because the owner’s source and business facts control the income-tax analysis.

## Why can a C corporation create two tax layers?

A C corporation can face one tax layer on corporate taxable income and another when after-tax earnings are distributed. The second layer is why a founder who expects regular withdrawals should model cash extraction before incorporating.

For a nonresident alien individual, [IRC §871](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/871) starts with a **30% tax on specified US-source income**, including dividends, subject to statutory exceptions. An applicable income-tax treaty may reduce the dividend rate if its conditions and documentation are satisfied. The corporation normally has withholding and reporting responsibilities when it pays the dividend.

“Double taxation” does not mean every dollar is automatically taxed twice at the headline rates. A distribution must be characterised, corporate earnings and profits matter, treaty eligibility must be tested, and the founder’s country may tax the receipt or allow relief. The point is structural: a C corporation can place a separate taxpayer between the business and the founder.

A disregarded LLC avoids that corporate dividend mechanism, but it does not promise tax-free profit. The owner may need Form 1040-NR or another return if the activity produces effectively connected income or another US filing trigger. Home-country treatment can also differ from US treatment.

## Which five questions decide between an LLC and a C corp?

Use the five-question founder filter as a discussion sheet for advisers. No single answer controls, but the pattern usually exposes which structure deserves deeper modelling.

1. **Are you raising institutional US venture capital soon?** If a priced equity round or standard venture instruments are central to the plan, ask investors and counsel whether a Delaware C corporation is expected.
2. **Will the business reinvest most profits?** Retaining cash may make the corporate layer easier to tolerate than a plan built around frequent founder distributions.
3. **Do you need cash distributions now?** Model the corporation’s tax, dividend character, withholding, and home-country result against the LLC owner-level result.
4. **Does the founder’s country have a relevant US treaty?** Confirm treaty eligibility, limitation provisions, beneficial ownership, and the rate that would actually apply; residence alone is not enough.
5. **What is the intended exit?** A stock sale, asset sale, acquisition, founder buyout, or long-term cash-flow business can produce different results. Model the likely path rather than an abstract sale.

Score no points and average nothing. The filter is not a calculator. It forces five commercial facts into the tax conversation before a low-friction formation decision becomes expensive to reverse.

## What do four founder scenarios look like?

Four scenarios, worked through, show why the founder’s use of cash and capital matters more than a generic “best entity” label.

### Scenario 1: Bootstrapped consultant taking monthly cash

A non-US consultant performs services outside the United States, has no US team, and transfers available cash to a personal account each month. A disregarded LLC may keep the entity-level filing lighter and avoids turning every profit distribution into a corporate dividend. The owner still needs a proper US trade-or-business and home-country analysis.

### Scenario 2: SaaS team preparing a US venture round

Two foreign founders expect to issue equity, add employees, and approach US venture funds. A Delaware C corporation may fit the financing plan better. The corporation files a real Form 1120 and may need Form 5472 for transactions with 25% foreign shareholders or other foreign related parties.

### Scenario 3: Ecommerce founder reinvesting profits

A founder keeps most cash in the business for inventory and advertising. A C corporation may appear attractive because distributions are not immediate, but US inventory, state activity, payroll, and the future exit can dominate the analysis. The founder should compare a corporation with the actual owner-level LLC result before electing.

### Scenario 4: Profitable company paying a treaty-country founder

A corporation earns profit and plans a dividend to its foreign shareholder. The statutory dividend rule is only the starting point. Treaty residence, beneficial ownership, paperwork, home-country tax, and foreign tax relief must be checked. Form 5472 remains a separate related-party information-return question.

## Does Form 5472 apply to both structures?

Yes, Form 5472 can apply to both, but the filing package differs. A foreign-owned US disregarded LLC generally files when it had any Part IV, Part V, or Part VI reportable transaction with its foreign owner or another related party. Formation funding, contributions, distributions, loans, reimbursements, and specified related-party payments can count under Treasury Regulation §1.6038A-2.

A domestic C corporation generally tests Form 5472 when it is at least 25% foreign-owned and had a reportable transaction with a foreign related party. Ordinary unrelated customer revenue is not transformed into a related-party transaction merely because the shareholder is foreign.

For a calendar-year **2025 tax year**, the due date was **15 April 2026**, or **15 October 2026** with a timely Form 7004 extension. A disregarded LLC writes “Foreign-owned U.S. DE” across its pro forma Form 1120 and cannot e-file the package. It faxes at **300 DPI or higher** to **855-887-7737**, or mails the Ogden PIN Unit at the address in the Form 5472 instructions. A corporation follows its Form 1120 filing method.

If the foreign owner has no US identifying number, the required Part II reference ID must be used consistently each year. The FTIN field takes the owner’s foreign tax number or “None” or “N/A” when none exists. Leaving both identity paths unexplained can make the return incomplete.

A substantially incomplete Form 5472 counts as a failure to file. After the IRS sends notice and the failure continues beyond 90 days, IRC §6038A(d)(2) adds **$25,000 for each 30-day period or fraction** that the failure continues. The entity decision does not reduce that exposure.

## Can an LLC become taxed as a corporation later?

Yes, an eligible LLC can file Form 8832 to elect association status, but the change is a tax event and not a cosmetic setting. The election changes the LLC from a disregarded entity into a corporation for federal purposes, changes the annual return, and may create deemed transactions.

Read the [Form 8832 election guide](/blog/form-8832-election-foreign-owned-llc) before discussing timing with an adviser. If there is more than one member, start with the [multi-member LLC filing guide](/blog/multi-member-llc-form-5472-or-1065), because the default baseline is different.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an LLC or C corp better for a non-US founder?

An LLC is often simpler for a bootstrapped founder taking profits out. A C corporation often fits institutional US fundraising. The correct choice depends on business location, distributions, treaty position, home-country tax, investors, and exit plans.

### Can a non-resident own a US C corporation?

Yes. Foreign ownership does not by itself prevent C corporation ownership. The company is a separate US taxpayer, and foreign ownership can create Form 5472, withholding, and related-party compliance obligations.

### Does a foreign-owned C corp file Form 5472?

It generally files Form 5472 when it is at least 25% foreign-owned and had a reportable transaction with a foreign related party. Ownership alone is not the complete transaction test.

### Does a foreign-owned LLC pay 21% corporate tax?

Not if it remains disregarded merely because it files a pro forma Form 1120. The 21% federal corporate rate applies to corporate taxable income. An LLC electing corporate classification moves into that system.

### Can a treaty eliminate Form 5472?

No. A treaty may affect income tax or dividend withholding, but Form 5472 is a separate information-reporting obligation for qualifying entities and transactions.

### Can I convert my LLC to a C corp before fundraising?

Possibly, through a legal conversion, contribution, merger, or tax-classification election depending on the facts. Each route can have tax and corporate-law consequences, so obtain advice before signing financing documents.

### Is Delaware required for a C corporation?

No. A corporation can be formed under another state’s law. Delaware is common in venture-backed structures, but investor expectations, legal costs, state filings, operating location, and governance needs should drive the choice.
