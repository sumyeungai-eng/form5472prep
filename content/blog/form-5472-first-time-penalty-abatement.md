**The general First Time Abate waiver does not reach a Form 5472 penalty: IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1 lists Form 5472 as an event-based filing it does not cover. One conditional exception exists in IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3), where the penalty was systemically assessed because Form 5472 was attached to a late-filed Form 1120. Otherwise the route is reasonable cause.**

First Time Abate — usually written FTA — is the one piece of IRS penalty relief that works like a coupon. You ask, the criteria are mechanical, and a clean three-year record gets the penalty removed without arguing about why you were late. It is the first thing anyone finds when they search for help with a penalty, and for a foreign-owned LLC holding a Form 5472 penalty notice it is almost always the wrong door — with one conditional exception that depends on how the penalty was assessed.

Knowing that early matters, because the months spent pursuing FTA are months the continuation penalty clock can be running. If you have unfiled years behind the penalised one, [filing the missing years](/start) is usually the first step in any relief request, whichever route you take.

## Which penalties does First Time Abate actually cover?

Three, and they are all tied to an income tax return or a deposit. The [IRS administrative penalty relief page](https://www.irs.gov/payments/administrative-penalty-relief) (reviewed 22 October 2026) lists FTA as available for:

- **Failure to file** — IRC 6651(a)(1) for tax returns, 6698(a)(1) for partnership returns, 6699(a)(1) for S corporation returns
- **Failure to pay** — IRC 6651(a)(2) and 6651(a)(3)
- **Failure to deposit** — IRC 6656

The Form 5472 penalty is not on that list, and neither is any other international information return penalty. The page does not mention Form 5472, Form 5471, IRC 6038 or IRC 6038A at all.

## Does First Time Abate apply to a Form 5472 penalty?

As a general waiver, no — and the Internal Revenue Manual says so by name rather than by omission. One conditional exception, set out further down, runs through the related Form 1120 rather than through the Form 5472.

[IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part20/irm_20-001-001r) — the FTA subsection — sets out at paragraph 7 that "penalty relief under the FTA waiver does not apply to the following," and includes "Returns with an event-based filing requirement, generally returns filed once or infrequently" and "Information reporting that is dependent on another filing, such as various forms that are attached." Paragraph 8 then gives examples of returns where FTA is not applicable, and the list includes **"Form 5472, Information Return of a 25% Foreign-Owned U.S. Corporation."**

[IRM 20.1.9](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part20/irm_20-001-009), the international penalties section, repeats the point at IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3)(b): "The first time abatement (FTA) penalty relief provisions do not apply to event-based filing requirements."

## Why is Form 5472 outside First Time Abate?

Because of how the form is filed, not because of any judgment about the filer.

FTA's core eligibility test is a comparison: the IRS looks at whether **the same return type** was timely filed for the prior three years. That test needs an annual return with a filing history to compare against. A Form 5472 is filed when a reportable transaction with a related party occurs — an event — and it is attached to a pro forma Form 1120 rather than standing on its own. Both of the exclusions in IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1(7) describe that situation exactly: an event-based filing requirement, and information reporting dependent on another filing.

So "this is my first time" is a factual statement that the FTA waiver has no mechanism to act on for Form 5472 standing on its own. It is not that the IRS rejected your clean record; there is no general FTA box for this penalty to go in.

## Is there any case where First Time Abate reaches a Form 5472 penalty?

Yes — one, and it is narrow, conditional and keyed to how the penalty was assessed. It applies where the IRS asserted the penalty automatically because the Form 5472 arrived attached to a late-filed Form 1120.

IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3) carries a passage headed "Systemically Assessed Initial Penalty With PRN 711," which opens: "When this penalty is systemically asserted because Form 5472 is attached to a late filed Form 1120," relief may be considered in conjunction with the failure-to-file penalty relief granted on the related Form 1120. The passage repeats that FTA does not apply to event-based filing requirements such as Form 5472 — and then gives an exception.

The exception has two alternative triggers, and only one of them has to be true. The penalty assessed with PRN 711 may be abated with penalty reason code 018 where the failure-to-file penalty on the related Form 1120 **was** abated under the FTA provisions using PRC 018 — **or** where that Form 1120 "would have been eligible for FTA abatement" but no failure-to-file penalty was assessed at all "because there was **$0 tax due or it was a fully paid return**."

The second trigger is the one a foreign-owned LLC should read twice. A pro forma Form 1120 exists to carry Form 5472 and reports no tax, so there is normally no failure-to-file penalty on it to abate under FTA — which is precisely the situation the IRM writes relief for. For this filer it is not an edge case; it is the ordinary case.

Two further conditions apply, and both look backwards rather than at the facts of the failure:

1. The taxpayer had **no similar penalties — penalty reference numbers 625 or 711 — in the three prior periods**.
2. The **related Form 1120 was not filed late in the three prior periods**.

Three limits are worth stating plainly. The route turns on the penalty having been systemically asserted with PRN 711 off a late-filed Form 1120, so a penalty asserted some other way is outside the passage. The relief is an abatement the IRS applies to the account, not a waiver claimable by ticking a list. And the first thing to establish is which penalty reference number the notice actually assesses, because the PRC 018 route is keyed to PRN 711. IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3) also gives a parallel route: where the related Form 1120's failure-to-file penalty was abated under a reason code other than PRC 018 and there were no similar penalties in the three prior periods, the PRN 711 penalty may be abated under that same reason code.

## What relief does exist for a $25,000 Form 5472 penalty?

Reasonable cause, which is a regulatory standard rather than an administrative waiver.

The [Instructions for Form 5472](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that "A penalty of $25,000 will be assessed on any reporting corporation that fails to file Form 5472" when due and in the required manner, and that the filing requirement sits under sections 6038A and 6038C. IRM 20.1.9.5.5(1) identifies the reasonable cause provision for IRC 6038A penalties as **Treas. Reg. 1.6038A-4(b)**, and states that the corporation must make a written showing of the facts in a statement that includes "a written statement containing a declaration that it was made under the penalties of perjury."

| Relief route | Reaches a Form 5472 penalty? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| First Time Abate as a general waiver | **No** — Form 5472 is listed as not applicable | IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1(7)–(8); IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3) |
| PRC 018 abatement of a penalty systemically assessed with PRN 711 | **Possible** — where the related Form 1120 was abated under FTA, or had $0 tax due or was fully paid, plus both three-prior-period conditions | IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3) |
| Reasonable cause under Treas. Reg. 1.6038A-4(b) | **Yes** — the available route, decided on facts | IRM 20.1.9.5.5(1); IRS reasonable cause page |
| Reasonable cause against the continuation penalty | **Generally not** beyond 90 days after the notice date | IRM 20.1.9.5.5(2), citing Treas. Reg. 1.6038A-4(b)(1) |
| Clean three-year compliance history on its own | **No** — a mitigating factor inside a reasonable cause case, not a standalone basis | IRS reasonable cause page |
| Liberal reasonable cause for small corporations | Possible factor; gross receipts and prompt compliance matter | Treas. Reg. 1.6038A-4(b)(2)(ii), per IRM 20.1.9.5.5(1) |

The difference between the two routes is not paperwork. FTA is mechanical and reasonable cause is evidentiary: the [IRS penalty relief page](https://www.irs.gov/payments/penalty-relief-for-reasonable-cause) (reviewed 21 June 2026) states that "Reasonable cause is determined on a case-by-case basis considering all the facts and circumstances of your situation."

## Does being a first-time filer help at all?

Yes — as a factor inside a reasonable cause argument, not as a waiver on its own.

The IRS reasonable cause page treats information return penalties differently from failure-to-file penalties. For information return penalties, it says the taxpayer must have acted responsibly before and after the failure, and must show significant mitigating factors or events beyond their control — and it names **being a first-time filer or having a good compliance history** among those mitigating factors.

That is a real use for a clean record. It belongs in the narrative, next to the dated facts about what happened, alongside evidence that the filing was corrected promptly. What it cannot do is replace the narrative. The same page warns that "Lack of knowledge" does not generally count as reasonable cause, and that reliance on a tax professional does not transfer your responsibility.

## Does reasonable cause reach the Form 5472 continuation penalty?

Generally not past the 90-day mark. The Form 5472 instructions set out that if the failure continues for more than 90 days after IRS notification, an additional $25,000 penalty applies, and then applies per related party for each 30-day period or part of one after the 90-day period ends.

IRM 20.1.9.5.5(2) states that reasonable cause can generally extend only to 90 days after the notice date, citing Treas. Reg. 1.6038A-4(b)(1), and concludes that there is therefore no reasonable cause exception for the continuation penalty. The practical consequence is a deadline inside a deadline: once a notice letter has been issued, the 90 days that follow are the window in which compliance stops the stacking, regardless of how strong the reasonable cause story is.

## The bottom line

First Time Abate is a genuine remedy for the wrong penalty, with one exception worth checking: if the penalty was systemically assessed with PRN 711 because the Form 5472 rode on a late-filed Form 1120, IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3) allows a PRC 018 abatement even where no failure-to-file penalty was assessed because there was $0 tax due — the normal state of a pro forma Form 1120. Outside that route the remedy is a documented reasonable cause showing under Treas. Reg. 1.6038A-4(b), signed under penalties of perjury, with a clean compliance history serving as one supporting fact rather than the argument. Nobody can promise the IRS will accept it. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use First Time Abate on a Form 5472 penalty?

Not as a general waiver — IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1 lists Form 5472 as not applicable. One exception: a penalty systemically assessed with PRN 711 off a late-filed Form 1120 may be abated with PRC 018 under IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3).

### What penalties does First Time Abate cover?

Failure to file under IRC 6651(a)(1), 6698(a)(1) and 6699(a)(1); failure to pay under IRC 6651(a)(2) and (a)(3); and failure to deposit under IRC 6656. International information return penalties are not on the IRS list.

### Does a pro forma Form 1120 with no tax due block FTA-linked relief?

No — IRM 20.1.9.5.5(3) writes for it. Relief with PRC 018 is available where the related Form 1120 would have been eligible for FTA abatement but no failure-to-file penalty was assessed because there was $0 tax due.

### Does a clean three-year filing record remove a Form 5472 penalty?

Not by itself. The IRS reasonable cause page treats a good compliance history or first-time filer status as a mitigating factor for information return penalties, to be shown alongside having acted responsibly before and after the failure.

### Is the $25,000 Form 5472 penalty per year or per form?

The Form 5472 instructions assess $25,000 on a reporting corporation that fails to file when due and in the required manner, and IRM 20.1.9.5.4(1) describes the initial penalty as asserted per related party per year.

### Can reasonable cause stop the continuation penalty?

Generally no. IRM 20.1.9.5.5(2) states that reasonable cause can extend only to 90 days after the notice date, so filing within that 90-day window — not arguing afterwards — is what stops the stacking.
