**A New Zealand resident who wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 after a reportable owner or related-party transaction. Use the individual’s IRD number as the FTIN, report funding and withdrawals gross, and analyze New Zealand’s classification of the US LLC separately.**

The US information return does not calculate New Zealand tax or determine whether the owner has US effectively connected income. The same LLC can be disregarded in the United States but treated differently in New Zealand.

## When does a New Zealand owner file Form 5472?

A filing generally follows when the LLC is a one-owner US disregarded entity, the sole owner is foreign for US tax purposes, and the LLC had a transaction with the owner or another related party.

The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) include contributions, distributions, formation payments, dissolution payments, and other related-party exchanges. The current penalty for a late, missing, or substantially incomplete form is **$25,000 per form, per year**.

| LLC activity | Form 5472 classification |
|---|---|
| Owner transfers NZD-funded cash into the LLC | Capital contribution |
| LLC sends money to the owner’s New Zealand account | Distribution |
| Owner pays the registered agent personally | Owner-funded formation or operating cost |
| Unrelated client pays an invoice | Customer revenue, not automatically a related-party transaction |
| LLC pays a New Zealand company controlled by the owner | Related-party payment; separate-form analysis |

Form 5472 uses gross amounts. Do not net a contribution against a later payout or combine two related parties in one total.

## What New Zealand tax number goes on Form 5472?

Inland Revenue states that an [IRD number is a tax identification number](https://www.ird.govt.nz/international/individuals/tax-for-new-zealand-tax-residents). An individual owner normally enters the personal IRD number as the FTIN. A New Zealand entity uses its own IRD number.

| Form field | New Zealand owner entry |
|---|---|
| 4a | Owner’s legal name and actual New Zealand address |
| 4b(1) | Existing SSN or ITIN, if any |
| 4b(2) | Consistent LLC-assigned reference ID when no US number exists |
| 4b(3) | Personal IRD number |
| 4d/4e | Citizenship and country of income-tax residence based on the facts |

The owner’s IRD number and the LLC’s EIN are not interchangeable. The owner also does not need an ITIN solely to complete Form 5472.

## How can New Zealand classify a US LLC differently?

New Zealand’s Inland Revenue has published detailed guidance on [New Zealand tax consequences of investing in a US LLC](https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/rulings/public/2023/pr-pub-23-09---23-13). The rulings illustrate that New Zealand can treat a US LLC as a foreign company even where the United States taxes it transparently.

The cited rulings address particular fact patterns and should not be generalized to every wholly owned disregarded LLC. The key point is that US “disregarded” status does not automatically control New Zealand treatment. A New Zealand adviser should assess worldwide income, foreign-company or FIF/CFC implications, distributions, and foreign tax credits.

The US-New Zealand treaty can affect income taxation but does not waive Form 5472. The information return remains tied to foreign ownership and related-party transactions.

## Does remote work from New Zealand create US income tax?

Personal services are generally sourced where the work is physically performed. A consultant serving US customers entirely from New Zealand often has foreign-source service income and no US trade or business, while US workdays, employees, an office, inventory, or a dependent agent can change the result.

“No US income tax” still does not mean “no US filing.” The owner may need the Form 5472 package because of funding or withdrawals even when all services were performed in New Zealand. ## How should NZD transactions be prepared?

1. **Export every owner and related-party movement.** Include owner-paid expenses that never touched the LLC account.
2. **Assign a transaction type.** Use contribution, distribution, loan, repayment, reimbursement, or related-party service/payment.
3. **Record the original NZD amount and date.** Preserve the bank or card source.
4. **Apply a named, consistent exchange-rate method.** Calculate the US-dollar amount and retain the arithmetic.
5. **Reconcile totals to the Part V statement and line 1f.** Gross inflows and outflows remain separate.

The [owner-loan and contribution guide](/blog/form-5472-owner-loans-contributions-reimbursements) provides classification examples.

## How is the filing delivered?

The pro forma Form 1120, Form 5472, and supporting statements are signed and submitted together. A foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot e-file the package; it must use the dedicated IRS fax or mailing route shown in the current instructions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an IRD number the FTIN on Form 5472?

Yes. A New Zealand individual normally enters the personal IRD number as the foreign tax identification number. A New Zealand company uses its own IRD number.

### Does a New Zealand owner need an ITIN?

Not solely for Form 5472. A consistent reference ID and the owner’s IRD number can be used when no US identifying number exists.

### Is all LLC revenue reported on Form 5472?

No. Form 5472 focuses on transactions with the owner and other related parties. Ordinary unrelated-customer revenue is not automatically a reportable owner transaction.

### Does New Zealand treat a US LLC as disregarded?

Not necessarily. New Zealand can classify a US LLC as a foreign company even when the United States disregards it. Local treatment requires a New Zealand-specific review.

### Can a New Zealand owner file Form 5472 online?

Not for a foreign-owned US disregarded entity. The pro forma Form 1120 and Form 5472 package must be faxed or mailed to the IRS’s dedicated unit.
