**No US state requires you to file a single-member LLC's operating agreement with the Secretary of State — the document that gets filed is the articles of organization or certificate of formation. The operating agreement is the owner's own internal record, and banks, payment processors and marketplaces frequently ask a foreign owner to produce one anyway.** Whether your formation state's LLC act requires you to *have* a written agreement even without filing it varies, so check that state's own statute rather than assuming a rule that applies elsewhere.

That gap between "not filed" and "not needed" is where most confusion sits. Delaware's LLC Act is explicit that a limited liability company agreement can be written, oral or implied, and is not subject to a statute of frauds — meaning Delaware does not require one to exist in writing at all ([6 Del. C. § 18-101(9)](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc01/index.html)). Other states' acts are worded differently, so the safest habit for a foreign owner is to [check your formation state's LLC act](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c018/sc01/index.html) directly rather than relying on general guidance like this post. If your gap is the federal filing rather than the agreement, [Form5472 Prep prepares and faxes Form 5472](/start) from the same contribution and distribution records an operating agreement documents.

## Does my formation state require an operating agreement?

It depends on the state, and this is the one question in this post you should not answer from a blog. Some state LLC acts require every LLC to adopt an operating agreement (written or otherwise) as a matter of internal governance even though it is never filed anywhere; others are silent or explicitly permissive.

Delaware's approach is verified: under 6 Del. C. § 18-101(9), a "limited liability company agreement" may be written, oral or implied, and a company is not required to execute one in writing for it to exist. That tells you Delaware imposes no formal, signed-document requirement — it does not tell you what New Mexico, Nevada or any other state requires, because each state's LLC act is separate legislation. Read your own formation state's LLC act, or its Secretary of State FAQ page, before concluding either way. Generic advice that "your state doesn't require one" is exactly the kind of unverified claim that causes real problems if it turns out to be wrong for your state.

## What does a single-member LLC operating agreement typically contain?

As a matter of general practice — not a template we're supplying — a single-member operating agreement usually documents: the member's identity and 100% ownership; whether the LLC is member-managed or manager-managed; how capital contributions were or will be made; how distributions are taken; recordkeeping and banking authority; and what happens on dissolution or a change of member. None of that is legal advice about what your document should say — it is a description of what this kind of document commonly addresses so you know what to look for or ask a local attorney to draft.

## Why would a non-resident owner want one if it's optional?

Because the people asking for it are not the state — they are your bank, payment processor or marketplace, and their reasons are practical rather than legal. A [US bank account application](/blog/us-bank-account-foreign-owned-llc) commonly asks for authority evidence — an operating agreement, manager/member details, or an authorizing resolution — alongside the EIN and formation documents, specifically so the provider can confirm who is allowed to act for the LLC.

For a foreign-owned single-member LLC, the document does three jobs at once: it satisfies a bank or processor's know-your-customer file; it documents, in the owner's own words, that the LLC is a single-member disregarded entity wholly owned by one non-US person; and it evidences the capital contribution that funded the business, which is often the LLC's very first reportable event for federal purposes.

## How does an operating agreement connect to Form 5472?

It connects through the transactions it describes, not through the form itself — no line on Form 5472 asks whether an operating agreement exists. The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) require a foreign-owned disregarded entity to report transactions with its foreign owner, including capital contributions and distributions, on Part IV and the Part V attached statement. An operating agreement that states the contribution amount, the date, and how distributions are to be handled gives you a paper trail that matches what you put on that statement — the same contribution a bank asked about when opening the account is the one your preparer needs when it comes to filing.

| Clause | Why a bank or processor asks | What it evidences for Form 5472 |
|---|---|---|
| Member identity and ownership | Confirms who controls the account and signs for the LLC | Confirms the related party named in Part III |
| Capital contribution | Shows how the business was funded and by whom | Supports the contribution amount reported in Part V |
| Management structure | Confirms who can authorize transfers | Confirms who can create a reportable transaction |
| Distribution terms | Shows how money is meant to move to the owner | Helps classify a transfer as a distribution rather than a loan |
| Dissolution terms | Part of standard risk review | Relevant to a final-year Form 5472 if the LLC winds up |

## Does an operating agreement change how my LLC is taxed?

No. An operating agreement is a governance document; it has no effect on federal tax classification. A single-member LLC is, by default, a disregarded entity for federal tax purposes unless the owner files [Form 8832 to elect corporate treatment](/blog/form-8832-election-foreign-owned-llc). Writing "this LLC elects to be taxed as a corporation" into an operating agreement does nothing on its own — the election is made with the IRS, not with the LLC's internal paperwork. Keep the two documents aligned, but don't confuse having one with having made the other.

## Four scenarios, worked through

**A UK freelancer forming a Wyoming single-member LLC.** Her bank's online application lists "operating agreement or authorizing document" among the items it wants before opening the account. She has one drafted by a local attorney familiar with Wyoming's LLC act, naming herself as sole member and manager, and uploads it alongside her EIN confirmation.

**A Delaware LLC wholly owned by a foreign holding company.** The "member" here is a company, not an individual, so the operating agreement records the holding company as 100% member and states how it funds the LLC. That funding flow — parent company to LLC — is exactly what shows up as a capital contribution on the LLC's Form 5472 Part V.

**An owner who has run the LLC for three years with nothing in writing.** Nothing has gone wrong yet, but the first time a payment processor's compliance review asks for authority documentation, there is nothing to send. Putting a basic agreement in place after the fact, dated when it's actually signed, is better than continuing without one — but it cannot retroactively describe transactions from years the owner can no longer fully reconstruct.

**An owner who just filed Form 8832 to elect corporate tax treatment.** The election changes how the LLC is taxed; it does not rewrite the operating agreement. The owner updates the agreement's references to tax treatment so the two documents describe the same entity consistently, and separately confirms with a tax adviser what the election means for future filings.

## Putting a basic agreement in place

If your formation state's LLC act, or your bank, tells you one is needed, this is roughly the sequence owners follow — it is not legal advice, and a local attorney can adapt it to your state and situation:

1. **Confirm what your formation state's LLC act says** about single-member operating agreements before drafting anything.
2. **Identify the member** exactly as named on the formation document and EIN.
3. **State the management structure** — member-managed or manager-managed — consistently with what you tell your bank.
4. **Record the capital contribution**: amount, date, and source, matching your actual bank records.
5. **Describe distribution and dissolution terms** in plain language.
6. **Sign and date it**, even though Delaware and some other states don't require a signature for the agreement to be valid.
7. **Store it with your formation documents and EIN confirmation**, and keep the version you actually operate under.
8. **Update it, and re-sign, after any material change** — a new member, a management change, or a tax election.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all US states require an LLC operating agreement?

No single answer covers every state. Some LLC acts require one even though it's never filed; others treat it as optional. Check your specific formation state's LLC act rather than assuming another state's rule applies to you.

### Does Delaware require a written operating agreement?

No. Delaware's LLC Act treats the agreement as optional and allows it to be written, oral or implied, with no statute-of-frauds requirement (6 Del. C. § 18-101(9)).

### Can I write my own single-member operating agreement?

Many owners do, especially for simple single-member structures. Whether a self-drafted document is adequate depends on your bank's requirements and your state's law — a local attorney can confirm it fits your situation.

### Does Form5472 Prep draft operating agreements?

No. We prepare and fax Form 5472 and the pro forma Form 1120 from your existing records. We do not draft governance documents, form LLCs, or give legal advice.

### Does an operating agreement replace Form 8832?

No. An operating agreement is internal governance; Form 8832 is the actual IRS election that changes federal tax classification. They should stay consistent with each other but are separate documents.

### What happens if my LLC never had a written operating agreement?

Usually nothing changes with the state, since it isn't filed there. It can slow down a bank or processor's KYC review if they specifically ask for one and you have nothing to send.

### Where should I keep the operating agreement once it's signed?

With your other core LLC records — formation documents, EIN confirmation, and bank paperwork — so it's on hand whenever a provider's onboarding asks for authority evidence.
