**A Singapore resident or company that wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC has a reportable transaction with its owner or another related party. Contributions, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements and formation costs can trigger filing even when the LLC has no sales.**

Form 5472 reports US related-party information; it does not replace Singapore tax analysis. The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) state that a required Form 5472 filed late, filed substantially incomplete, or not supported by required records can trigger a **$25,000** penalty. Owners ready to prepare the package can [start the guided filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-singapore-residents-us-llc).

## When must a Singapore-owned LLC file?

A Singapore-owned LLC generally files Form 5472 when it is a domestic disregarded entity wholly owned by a foreign person and it had a reportable transaction with the owner or another related party during the tax year. The IRS instructions treat foreign-owned US disregarded entities as reporting corporations for section 6038A reporting purposes.

Use this trigger test:

1. A US LLC had one owner for US federal tax purposes.
2. The owner was a foreign person, including a Singapore individual, Singapore company or other non-US person.
3. The LLC did not elect a different federal tax classification.
4. Value moved between the LLC and its owner or another related party during the year.

The initial deposit used to fund the LLC often satisfies item four. So can owner loans, withdrawals, reimbursements, owner-paid formation costs and closure-year cash transfers. Customer revenue from unrelated buyers is a different category and is not automatically a related-party transaction.

For a calendar-year LLC, the 2025 Form 5472 package was generally due 15 April 2026 under the [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472). A timely Form 7004 extension generally moved the deadline to 15 October 2026. The IRS instructions say a foreign-owned US disregarded entity requests the extension by filing Form 7004 by the regular due date.

## Which Singapore identifier goes on Form 5472?

The correct FTIN depends on the legal owner. IRAS digital-service pages identify an individual's tax reference number as NRIC or FIN and refer to a business tax reference number as UEN in the information needed for Singapore tax services on [iras.gov.sg](https://www.iras.gov.sg/digital-services/self-employed).

Use the owner type to select the identifier:

| Owner type | Identifier to review for the FTIN field |
|---|---|
| Singapore citizen or permanent resident | NRIC |
| Foreign individual with Singapore immigration status | FIN |
| Singapore company or local business | UEN |
| Owner with an existing US number | SSN, ITIN or EIN in the separate US-ID field |
| Owner with no applicable FTIN | "None" or "N/A" only where the IRS instructions permit it, plus a reference ID where required |

The US LLC's EIN never substitutes for the owner's NRIC, FIN or UEN. Each number identifies a different person or entity. The LLC needs an EIN for the US filing package; the owner provides the applicable Singapore identifier or reference ID.

## What does having no US-Singapore income tax treaty change?

Singapore owners cannot rely on a US-Singapore income tax treaty for US income-tax relief because the IRS treaty A-to-Z page does not list Singapore among countries with US income tax treaty documents. The [IRS treaty A-to-Z page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z) lists treaty countries alphabetically and skips Singapore under S.

That treaty fact affects income-tax analysis, not the Form 5472 trigger. A Singapore owner with no US trade or business may still have no US income tax under domestic rules. A Singapore owner with US inventory, US people, US real estate or US services may need US tax advice. In both cases, related-party transactions can still require Form 5472.

| Question | Singapore owner answer |
|---|---|
| Is there a US-Singapore income tax treaty? | No treaty is listed on the IRS A-to-Z page |
| Does no treaty automatically create US income tax? | No; domestic US rules still matter |
| Does no treaty remove Form 5472? | No |
| Does no US income tax remove Form 5472? | No, if a reportable transaction occurred |
| Does Singapore tax treatment remove Form 5472? | No |

## What transactions should the Singapore owner collect?

A Singapore owner should collect every owner-related movement before preparing Form 5472. The filing is based on related-party transactions, not simply on whether the LLC made a profit.

| Category | Singapore-owner examples | Why to collect it |
|---|---|---|
| Contributions | SGD or USD transfer to fund the LLC | Owner-to-LLC movement |
| Withdrawals | Distributions, draws, transfers to a Singapore account | LLC-to-owner movement |
| Loans | Owner advances money, LLC repays principal or interest | Related-party debt |
| Reimbursements | Personal card paid LLC expenses, LLC later repays | Related-party payment trail |
| Services and IP | Owner provides services, software, licence rights or equipment | Non-cash or service transaction review |
| Lifecycle events | Formation, acquisition, transfer or dissolution | Part V facts for a foreign-owned US DE |

Payment processors do not determine reportability. A customer payment from an unrelated buyer differs from a later transfer from the LLC to its owner. Wise, Stripe, PayPal and bank transfers should be classified by who paid whom and why.

For more examples, use the [Form 5472 reportable transactions guide](/blog/form-5472-reportable-transactions-examples).

## How should SGD amounts be translated?

SGD amounts should be translated into a US-dollar workpaper that supports the Form 5472 totals. The IRS instructions ask for US-dollar values for related-party transaction fields, so the workpaper should keep the original SGD amount, date, exchange-rate source, USD result and classification beside the bank record.

Worked example:

| Transaction | Original amount | Working | USD workpaper amount |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Owner contribution | SGD 4,000 | SGD 4,000 x 0.7400 | USD 2,960 |
| Owner reimbursement | SGD 600 | SGD 600 x 0.7350 | USD 441 |
| Owner withdrawal | USD 1,200 | Already in USD | USD 1,200 |
| Total related-party value reviewed |  | USD 2,960 + USD 441 + USD 1,200 | USD 4,601 |

The exchange rates are illustrative, not official. Replace them with documented transaction-date rates or another consistently used published source accepted by your preparer. The filed amount should be traceable from the original SGD or USD record without recreating the work months later.

## How does a Singapore owner file?

The Singapore owner files through the US LLC by attaching Form 5472 to a pro forma Form 1120. The IRS instructions say "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" should be written across the top of the pro forma Form 1120 and that foreign-owned US disregarded entities file by fax at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737, or by mail to the dedicated Ogden PIN Unit address.

Follow this procedure:

1. Confirm the LLC's owner, classification and tax year.
2. Collect the LLC EIN and the owner's NRIC, FIN, UEN or reference ID.
3. Reconcile all related-party activity and translate non-USD amounts.
4. Prepare Form 5472 and the Part V statement.
5. Attach Form 5472 to a pro forma Form 1120 marked "Foreign-owned U.S. DE."
6. Review names, addresses, EIN, FTIN, reference ID and transaction totals.
7. Fax or mail the signed package to the dedicated IRS Ogden PIN Unit address in the instructions.
8. Retain the complete package and timestamped proof of transmission.

Foreign-owned US disregarded entities cannot e-file Form 5472 under the IRS instructions. Keeping the timestamped receipt is part of the filing process, not an optional administrative extra.

## How should a Singapore owner think about Singapore tax?

Singapore tax treatment is separate from Form 5472, and a Singapore resident or company should get Singapore advice before assuming the US LLC is invisible locally. IRAS states that overseas income received in Singapore is generally not taxable, but also lists categories of overseas income that can be taxable in its [income-received-from-overseas guidance](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/what-is-taxable-what-is-not/income-received-from-overseas).

That Singapore point is intentionally narrow because local tax depends on facts such as source, remittance, partnership involvement, management and business activity. Form 5472 does not decide those questions. Form 5472 only reports US related-party information for the foreign-owned US disregarded entity.

## Four scenarios, worked through

**Singapore consultant, Wyoming LLC, all services performed from Singapore.** The owner has no US office, no US employees and no US workdays. US income-tax treatment depends on domestic US rules because no US-Singapore income tax treaty is listed by the IRS. The owner contributed funds and later took withdrawals, so Form 5472 is generally required.

**Singapore ecommerce seller, Delaware LLC, US warehouse inventory.** US inventory can create a serious US income-tax question. The absence of a US-Singapore treaty makes professional US advice more important, not less. Form 5472 remains separate, and owner loans, reimbursements and distributions should be reconciled.

**Singapore SaaS founder, New Mexico LLC, no sales in 2025.** The owner paid formation costs personally and later reimbursed the cost from the LLC account. No customer revenue does not remove the reimbursement. The 2025 package was generally due 15 April 2026, or 15 October 2026 with a timely extension.

**Singapore company owns a US disregarded LLC.** The Singapore company is the foreign owner and uses its entity details, not a director's personal NRIC, unless the director is the legal owner. Transactions between the Singapore company and the US LLC should be reviewed for Form 5472.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Singapore owner need an ITIN for Form 5472?

Usually no. The LLC needs an EIN. A Singapore individual owner generally uses the applicable NRIC or FIN as the FTIN and a reference ID where required.

### Which number does a Singapore company owner use?

A Singapore company generally uses its UEN as the relevant foreign identifier. Match the number to the legal owner, not to a director unless the director personally owns the LLC.

### Does a dormant Singapore-owned LLC have to file?

It may. Owner funding, personally paid fees, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements or closure activity can create a filing obligation despite zero revenue.

### Is there a US-Singapore income tax treaty?

No US-Singapore income tax treaty is listed on the IRS treaty A-to-Z page. Singapore owners rely on domestic US tax rules for US income-tax analysis.

### Can Form 5472 be filed online?

No. The IRS instructions say a foreign-owned US disregarded entity cannot file Form 5472 electronically. The package is faxed or mailed to the Ogden PIN Unit.

### Does Form 5472 replace Singapore tax filings?

No. Form 5472 is US information reporting. Singapore tax treatment depends on separate source, remittance, management and business-activity facts.
