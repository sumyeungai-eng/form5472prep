**A Singapore resident or company that wholly owns a US single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when the LLC has a reportable transaction with its owner or another related party. Contributions, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements and formation costs can trigger filing even when the LLC has no sales.**

Form 5472 reports US related-party information; it does not replace a Singapore tax analysis. The LLC needs an EIN, while the owner provides the applicable foreign identifier. Owners ready to prepare the package can [start the guided filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-singapore).

## When must a Singapore-owned LLC file?

The [IRS instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) treat a foreign-owned US disregarded entity as a reporting corporation for section 6038A. A filing is generally required when the entity has any reportable transaction with its foreign owner or another related party.

The practical trigger test is:

1. A US LLC had one owner for federal tax purposes.
2. That owner was a foreign person, including a Singapore individual or entity.
3. The LLC did not elect a different federal classification.
4. Value moved between the LLC and a related party during the year.

The initial deposit used to fund the LLC often satisfies item four.

## Which Singapore identifier goes on Form 5472?

The correct FTIN depends on the owner type. The [OECD Singapore TIN profile](https://www.oecd.org/tax/automatic-exchange/crs-implementation-and-assistance/tax-identification-numbers/Singapore-TIN.pdf) explains that an individual's TIN is generally the NRIC or FIN shown on the relevant identity or immigration document, while local businesses and companies use a UEN.

| Owner type | Identifier to review for the FTIN field |
|---|---|
| Singapore citizen or permanent resident | NRIC |
| Foreign individual working or residing in Singapore | FIN on the work or immigration pass |
| Singapore company or local business | UEN |
| Owner with no applicable FTIN | “None” or “N/A” where permitted, plus a reference ID when required |

The US LLC's EIN never substitutes for the owner's NRIC, FIN or UEN. Each identifies a different person.

## What transactions should the Singapore owner collect?

Review every owner-related movement, including:

- formation and registered-agent costs paid by the owner;
- SGD or USD capital contributions;
- owner loans and repayments;
- withdrawals and distributions;
- business costs charged to a personal card;
- reimbursements to the owner;
- services, IP or equipment transferred between related parties; and
- acquisition, disposition or dissolution of the entity.

Payment processors do not determine reportability. A customer payment from an unrelated buyer differs from a later transfer from the LLC to its owner. See [Stripe, PayPal and Wise activity](/blog/stripe-paypal-wise-form-5472) for examples.

## How should SGD amounts be translated?

US returns report amounts in dollars. The IRS [exchange-rate guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates) says to use the prevailing spot rate when an item is received, paid or accrued in general, and it accepts consistently used published rates.

Our evidence-first method records four items for every non-USD transaction: original SGD amount, date, rate source and converted USD total. Keep the calculation with the bank or card record.

## How does a Singapore owner file?

1. Confirm ownership and federal classification.
2. Reconcile all related-party activity and translate non-USD amounts.
3. Complete Form 5472 and its Part V statement.
4. Attach the form to a pro forma Form 1120 marked “Foreign-owned U.S. DE.”
5. Fax at 300 DPI or higher to the IRS Ogden PIN Unit, or mail to the dedicated address in the instructions.
6. Retain the complete package and timestamped proof of transmission.

For a calendar-year LLC, the usual due date is April 15 of the next year, extended to October 15 after a timely Form 7004.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Singapore owner need an ITIN?

Not solely to file Form 5472. The owner can provide the applicable NRIC or FIN as an FTIN and a reference ID where required. The LLC must have an EIN.

### Which number does a Singapore company owner use?

A Singapore entity generally uses its UEN as the relevant foreign identifier. Confirm the legal owner and match the number to its incorporation records.

### Does a dormant Singapore-owned LLC have to file?

It may. Owner funding, personally paid fees, withdrawals or closure activity can create a filing obligation despite zero revenue.

### Does Form 5472 replace Singapore tax filings?

No. Form 5472 is US information reporting. Singapore corporate or individual tax treatment depends on separate residence, management and income facts.

### Can Form 5472 be filed online?

No. A foreign-owned US disregarded entity must fax or mail the package to the IRS Ogden PIN Unit under the current instructions.
