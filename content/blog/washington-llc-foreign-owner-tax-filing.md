**A foreign-owned Washington LLC should check three separate compliance layers: the Washington annual report, Washington Department of Revenue obligations such as business and occupation tax or sales tax, and the federal Form 5472 information return. Completing one layer does not complete the others.**

Washington has no individual income tax on wages, but that slogan does not remove LLC reporting, gross-receipts tax, retail sales tax, or federal information filing.

If the LLC had funding, withdrawals, loans, reimbursements, or other transactions with its foreign owner, [start the federal Form 5472 package](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=washington-llc-foreign-owner-tax-filing) while resolving the Washington filings separately.

## The Washington federal-state matrix

| Compliance layer | Trigger | Agency | Form5472 Prep scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual report | Maintaining the Washington entity | Secretary of State | Not included |
| Business license and tax registration | Washington business activity or statutory registration trigger | Department of Revenue | Not included |
| B&O tax return | Taxable Washington gross receipts under the applicable classification | Department of Revenue | Not included |
| Retail sales tax | Taxable retail sales with Washington nexus | Department of Revenue | Not included |
| Form 5472 + pro forma Form 1120 | Foreign-owned US disregarded entity with a reportable related-party transaction | IRS | Included for the standard profile |
| Owner income-tax return | Owner has a separate US federal filing obligation | IRS | Not included |

This matrix prevents a common mistake: treating a Secretary of State renewal as a tax return, or treating a federal filing as proof that Washington accounts are current.

## Washington annual report

The Washington Secretary of State's [annual-report guidance](https://www.sos.wa.gov/vi/node/30116) currently lists a $70 fee for a profit corporation or LLC. The report is due by the last day of the month in which the entity was first formed or registered. The state also lists a $25 delinquency fee and requires a registered agent with a physical Washington address.

Fees and procedures can change, so verify the amount in the official filing system when submitting. Calendar the deadline using the entity's original Washington registration month, not the federal tax-return due date.

## Business and occupation tax is based on gross receipts

Washington's [business and occupation tax](https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/business-occupation-tax) is a gross-receipts tax. The tax calculation depends on the business classification, and the Department of Revenue states that businesses generally do not deduct labor, materials, taxes, or other costs from gross income when computing B&O tax.

That makes profit an unreliable screening shortcut. An LLC can have little or no net profit and still have Washington gross-receipts reporting.

Key facts to collect include:

- where employees and contractors perform work;
- where customers receive the benefit of services;
- where inventory is stored and delivered;
- Washington sales by tax classification;
- marketplace-facilitator sales; and
- the LLC's business-license and Department of Revenue account status.

## Remote sales can create registration duties

Washington's [remote-seller guidance](https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/retail-sales-tax/marketplace-fairness-leveling-playing-field/remote-sellers) currently states that remote businesses exceeding $100,000 of combined Washington gross receipts in the current or prior year generally must register and report. A business organized or commercially domiciled in Washington can have a registration obligation without waiting to exceed that economic-nexus threshold.

Marketplace sales need separate handling. A marketplace facilitator may collect retail sales tax, while the seller can still have reporting and B&O consequences. Do not exclude marketplace revenue from the nexus review merely because the platform collected tax.

## Three example profiles

| Profile | Washington screening | Federal screening |
|---|---|---|
| Washington LLC, foreign consultant works entirely abroad, no Washington customers | Annual report; confirm whether state tax registration is required | Owner funding and withdrawals may trigger Form 5472 |
| Washington LLC stores goods in Seattle and sells online | Annual report, registration, B&O, sales tax, and nexus review | Form 5472 plus separate federal income-tax analysis |
| Delaware LLC exceeds the Washington remote-sales threshold | Washington registration and tax review even though formed elsewhere | Form 5472 depends on foreign ownership and related-party transactions |

State-law “foreign LLC” usually means an LLC formed in another US state. It is different from a Washington LLC whose owner lives outside the United States. A business can be foreign under one definition, both, or neither.

## Federal Form 5472 remains separate

The [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) require a foreign-owned US disregarded entity to file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 when it has a reportable transaction. Contributions, distributions, loans, repayments, reimbursements, related-party services, and formation or dissolution activity may qualify.

Customer sales to unrelated parties do not become Form 5472 transactions merely because the customers are in Washington. Build a separate related-party ledger and use the state books only as a reconciliation source.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Washington LLC annual report replace Form 5472?

No. The annual report keeps the Washington entity record current. Form 5472 is a separate federal related-party information return filed with a pro forma Form 1120.

### Is Washington B&O tax based on profit?

No. The post explains that Washington B&O tax is a gross-receipts tax, and the Department of Revenue generally does not allow deductions for labor, materials, taxes, or other costs from gross income.

### Can remote sales create Washington registration duties?

Yes. The post notes that remote businesses exceeding Washington's current gross-receipts threshold generally must register and report, while a Washington-organized or commercially domiciled business may have duties without waiting for that threshold.
