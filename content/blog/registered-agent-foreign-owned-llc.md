**Every US LLC must continuously maintain a registered agent with a physical street address in its formation state. A non-resident owner living abroad generally cannot serve as their own registered agent, because most states require the agent to have an in-state address — Wyoming's Secretary of State, for example, requires an agent to "reside in Wyoming" and hold "a physical Wyoming address." Registered agents receive legal and state mail; they do not file your federal Form 5472.**

If you formed a US LLC from outside the country, you almost certainly used — and still need — a commercial registered agent service. This guide covers what the role actually does, why it does not substitute for federal tax compliance, what happens if the appointment lapses, and how to switch agents when you need to. If your registered agent renewal reminder is what brought you here, note that renewing it does not touch your [federal Form 5472 obligation](/do-i-need-to-file-form-5472), which we prepare and fax starting at $149 through [Form5472 Prep](/start).

## What does a registered agent actually do?

A registered agent's job is narrow: maintain a physical address in the LLC's formation state and be available during business hours to accept **service of process** — lawsuits, subpoenas, and official state correspondence — on the LLC's behalf, then forward it to the owner promptly.

[Wyoming's Secretary of State](https://sos.wyo.gov/FAQS.aspx?root=RAO) describes the requirement directly: a registered office "shall be located at a street address in Wyoming which shall be a physical location where an individual can accept service of process." A PO box does not satisfy this. [Delaware's Division of Corporations](https://corp.delaware.gov/agents/) states its agents must "maintain a street address and office located in Delaware and be open during normal business hours for the purpose of accepting service of process." Every state sets its own registered-agent rules; check your formation state's Secretary of State site for the exact requirement.

## Can a non-resident owner be their own registered agent?

Generally, no — because the address requirement is tied to physical presence in the state, not to who owns the LLC. [Wyoming's Secretary of State FAQ](https://sos.wyo.gov/FAQS.aspx?root=RAO) states an individual acting as a registered agent "must be at least eighteen (18) years old, reside in Wyoming, have a physical Wyoming address and valid email address." A non-resident living in another country has no Wyoming address to offer, so the requirement rules out self-representation by residency alone — this is not a nationality restriction, it is a physical-presence one, and it would equally rule out a US citizen who lives in a different state.

This is exactly why commercial registered agent services exist: they hold a qualifying in-state address so an owner does not need one.

## Registered agent vs mailing address vs virtual office — what each does

| Service | What it provides | Does it satisfy the registered-agent requirement? |
|---|---|---|
| Registered agent | In-state street address, accepts service of process, forwards it to you | Yes — this is the legally required role |
| Mailing address / virtual mailbox | An address to receive general business mail | No — most are not staffed to accept service of process during business hours or configured for the state's registered-agent filing |
| Virtual office | Meeting space, sometimes a business address, occasional mail handling | No, unless the provider is separately registered as your agent of record |
| Your home address (if you have one in-state) | An address you control | Only if it is a physical street address in the formation state and you are willing to have it appear on the public entity record |

Using a mailing address or virtual office as your registered-agent address without confirming the provider is actually appointed as your agent of record is a common way owners discover — usually via a default judgment or a lapsed-status notice — that service of process was never received.

## What a registered agent does NOT do

This is the assumption that causes real compliance gaps for foreign-owned LLCs: **a registered agent is not a tax preparer and does not file Form 5472.** The agent's job ends at forwarding legal and state mail. It does not:

- File or review your federal Form 5472 or pro forma Form 1120;
- Determine whether a transaction between you and the LLC is reportable;
- File your state annual report or franchise tax on its own, unless you separately pay it for that service;
- Give you tax or legal advice.

Owners who pay a registered-agent renewal often assume the LLC's annual compliance is handled. It is not — the registered-agent fee covers exactly the narrow role above. ## What happens if your registered agent resigns or the address lapses?

If a registered agent resigns without a successor being appointed, or the agent's address becomes invalid (the office closes, the service is no longer paid), the LLC no longer has a valid registered agent on file. States generally treat this as a compliance failure that can lead to the entity falling out of **good standing** and, if uncorrected, administrative dissolution — the specific consequence and timeline is set by each state, so check your formation state's Secretary of State page for what applies to your entity. Wyoming, for example, provides a specific "Statement of Resignation" or "Statement of Resignation with Successor Appointed" form an outgoing agent files, at a $5.00 filing fee per entity.

## How do you change a registered agent?

1. **Choose a new registered agent** that maintains a qualifying physical address in your formation state and offers the service commercially, or confirm eligibility if you have a qualifying in-state address yourself.
2. **Confirm the new agent's consent.** Most states require the incoming agent to accept the appointment as part of the filing.
3. **File the state's change-of-agent form.** Wyoming, for instance, requires an "Appointment of New Registered Agent and Office" form with a $5.00 filing fee; other states use their own form and fee — check your formation state's Secretary of State site.
4. **Confirm the change is reflected** on the state's public entity search before canceling service with your prior agent.
5. **Update your own records** — the registered agent's address is separate from your LLC's business or mailing address, and from the address you use on your IRS filings.

## Four scenarios

1. **A UK resident who formed a Wyoming LLC and tried to list their UK address as the registered office.** Not permitted — Wyoming requires a physical Wyoming street address. They need a commercial registered agent with a Wyoming address.
2. **An owner whose registered agent renewal lapsed because a card expired.** The LLC likely fell out of good standing once the state had no valid agent on file; the owner needs to reinstate the agent (and possibly the entity) through the state's process, separately from any federal filing.
3. **An owner who assumed their registered-agent company "handles everything," including taxes.** The agent maintained the address and forwarded state mail only; the LLC still had an unfiled Form 5472 for a year with a reportable owner contribution.
4. **An owner switching from one commercial registered agent to a cheaper one.** They filed the formation state's change-of-agent form, confirmed the new agent's acceptance, and verified the update on the state's entity search — a purely state-level process, unrelated to any IRS filing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use my home country address as my LLC's registered agent address?

No. States require a physical street address inside the formation state itself. An address outside that state — including a foreign address — does not meet the requirement, which is why most non-resident owners use a commercial registered agent.

### Does my registered agent file my Form 5472?

No. A registered agent's role is limited to maintaining a state address and forwarding legal and state mail. Form 5472 is a separate federal filing you (or a filing service) must handle on its own schedule.

### What happens if I don't have a registered agent?

Your LLC can fall out of good standing with the state and, if uncorrected, face administrative dissolution — the exact process and timeline vary by state, so check your formation state's Secretary of State site.

### Is a virtual mailbox the same as a registered agent?

Not necessarily. A virtual mailbox handles general mail; a registered agent must be available during business hours to accept service of process and is a specific legal appointment on file with the state. Confirm the provider is registered as your agent of record.

### How much does a registered agent cost?

It varies by provider and state, and we have not verified current commercial pricing here — get a quote from a licensed registered-agent service in your formation state. Separately, some states charge a small state filing fee to change agents, such as Wyoming's $5.00 form fee.

### Can I be my own registered agent if I live in a different US state than my LLC?

Generally no, for the same reason a non-resident abroad cannot: the agent needs a physical address within the LLC's specific formation state, not just anywhere in the United States.
