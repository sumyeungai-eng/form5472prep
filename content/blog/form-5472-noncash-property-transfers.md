**A foreign-owned US LLC can have a Form 5472 reportable transaction even when no money changes hands. Property, rights, obligations, and services transferred between the LLC and a foreign related party may require a Part VI description and a reasonable estimate of fair market value. A contribution or distribution may also need the appropriate Part V classification.**

That makes a bank-only review incomplete. A laptop supplied by the owner, software licensed without an invoice, debt assumed for the LLC, or services exchanged below market can all require attention.

If the owner transferred noncash value to or from the LLC, [start a reviewed Form 5472 package](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-noncash-property-transfers) with the transfer evidence and valuation support.

## The noncash transfer matrix

The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) require a Part VI description when a reportable transaction involves nonmonetary consideration or less than full consideration. The implementing rule, [Treasury Regulation section 1.6038A-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6038A-2), covers exchanges of property, rights, obligations, and services.

| Event | Likely Form 5472 review | Records to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Owner contributes a computer to the LLC | Contribution classification plus Part VI review | Purchase record, condition, transfer date, ownership evidence, value support |
| LLC distributes equipment to the owner | Distribution classification plus Part VI review | Asset register, approval, transfer date, value support |
| Owner lets the LLC use software or a trademark at no charge | Rights or services transferred for less than full consideration | License terms, ownership, period of use, valuation method |
| Foreign affiliate assumes an LLC obligation | Transfer of an obligation | Original agreement, assumption document, balance, parties |
| LLC pays market price to an unrelated supplier | Usually outside Form 5472 | Invoice, payment proof, relationship check |

The correct location on the form depends on the transaction's substance. Do not force a noncash owner contribution into only one disclosure bucket without checking both the ownership transaction and the nonmonetary exchange.

## Fair market value is not the same as tax basis

Part VI asks for a reasonable estimate of fair market value when possible. That reporting value is not automatically the LLC's tax basis, a depreciation conclusion, or an appraisal for another tax purpose.

Suppose the foreign owner transfers a used laptop to the LLC. The owner originally paid $4,000, but comparable used units sell for approximately $2,500 on the transfer date. The workpaper might use $2,500 as the supported fair market value estimate while separately preserving the original cost and tax-basis analysis. The form should not silently treat the two numbers as interchangeable.

Useful valuation evidence can include:

- contemporaneous third-party listings for similar property;
- a recent independent appraisal for a material asset;
- an arm's-length license or service quote;
- a documented discounted-cash-flow or royalty method prepared by a specialist; and
- an explanation of age, condition, restrictions, and territory.

Material intellectual property, real estate, or business transfers usually justify professional valuation and international tax advice.

## Build a transfer record that survives review

For each noncash item, record:

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Parties | Legal owner before and after the transfer |
| Relationship | Direct owner, indirect owner, or other related party |
| Transfer date | Date control, use, or the obligation changed |
| Property or service | Specific description, not “miscellaneous asset” |
| Consideration | Cash, property, services, assumed debt, or none |
| Fair market value | Amount, currency, method, and evidence date |
| Cost or tax basis | Preserved separately from fair market value |
| Business purpose | Why the LLC received or transferred the item |
| Form treatment | Part V, Part VI, or another current-form category |

The records should show the gross exchange. If the owner contributes equipment and the LLC later reimburses shipping, retain the contribution and reimbursement as separate events rather than netting them.

## Noncash services also matter

A related party can transfer value through work rather than property. An owner may build the LLC's website, provide management support, or license software without issuing an invoice. Routine acts performed only as an owner do not automatically create a service transaction, but a structured exchange of valuable services for no or below-market consideration needs review.

Document the actual arrangement before estimating a number. Identify who performed the work, when and where it was performed, the deliverables, what the parties agreed, and whether the LLC owed anything in return. Our [related-party services guide](/blog/form-5472-related-party-services-management-fees) covers the monetary and nonmonetary distinction in more detail.

## Separate disclosure from other tax conclusions

Reporting a noncash transfer does not determine:

- whether the transfer is taxable;
- the recipient's tax basis or holding period;
- whether depreciation or amortization is available;
- whether section 482 requires an arm's-length adjustment; or
- whether withholding, sales tax, customs, or state tax applies.
