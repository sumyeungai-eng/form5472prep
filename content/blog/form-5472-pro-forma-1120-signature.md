For a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, do not assume that signing a PDF electronically makes its Form 5472 package ready for IRS submission. **A conservative approach is to sign the completed pro forma Form 1120 in ink, then scan that signed page for the fax package, or mail the signed paper package.** This is a practical recommendation, not a claim that every other method automatically invalidates a filing.

Two separate questions matter: how a document is signed, and how it reaches the IRS. The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) authorize the special fax or mail route for foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entities and expressly exclude their Form 5472 from electronic filing. A PDF sent through an online fax provider is still a fax submission, not IRS e-file.

## Which form actually has the signature block?

The current [Form 5472, revised December 2023](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f5472.pdf), has no taxpayer signature block. Do not invent one on its last page or assume every page needs signing.

The relevant block appears near the bottom of page 1 of [Form 1120](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1120.pdf). It contains the officer signature, date and title fields, followed by a separate paid-preparer section. The declaration covers the return and accompanying schedules and statements. Read the complete package before signing, not just the cover page.

There is an important qualification. The special Form 5472 instruction limits required pro forma Form 1120 information to the entity's name, address, and items B and E; it does not separately address signing. The general [Form 1120 instructions, “Who Must Sign”](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120), require a signed, dated return by an authorized officer. We recommend signing the pro forma cover rather than treating the special instruction's silence as a reason to omit it.

For an LLC, establish who is authorized to act for the entity and use that person's actual capacity. If an accountant or another representative will sign for it, ask what authority permits that signature. Preparing a return and being authorized to sign for the taxpayer are different roles; the separate preparer block does not erase that distinction.

## What do the IRS electronic-signature rules establish?

[IRM 10.10.1](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part10/irm_10-010-001), effective August 12, 2024, recognizes electronic signatures but makes permission document-specific. Its limited handwritten-signature deviation, Exhibit 10.10.1-2, lists several specialized 1120 forms—not plain Form 1120, a pro forma 1120, or Form 5472. Exhibit 10.10.1-1 lists a PIN method for Form 1120; that is not blanket permission to paste a signature into a faxed PDF.

The same manual's section 10.10.1.6.1 concerns specified authenticated IRS interactions outside ordinary filing. It is not a general initial-return exception. Neither the deviation list nor those interaction rules establishes broad approval for this package's digital-only signatures.

That leaves a narrower, useful conclusion: **do not rely on a generic e-signature capability as proof that the particular tax document and delivery route are covered.** Ask a preparer recommending another method to identify the applicable IRS authority, including its date and scope.

## Signature method and submission route: compare them separately

This table is a preparation decision aid, not an IRS acceptance matrix. “Not established” does not mean a particular filing has been rejected.

| Method | Submission route | Evidence and practical limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Sign the completed form in ink; scan the signed page | Special DE fax route | Conservative recommendation here. Keep the ink original and exact transmitted package. |
| Sign the completed form in ink; send the paper | Special DE mail route | Keep a complete copy and delivery records. Use the dedicated address, not an ordinary corporate-return address. |
| Draw a signature with a mouse, finger or stylus | Fax or paper printout | A signature created on screen; approval for this exact filing is not established by the guidance above. |
| Paste a saved signature image into the PDF | Fax or paper printout | Different from scanning a page signed in ink after completion. Request the authority supporting its use. |
| Type a name or use an e-signature platform | Fax or paper printout | The platform's certificate alone does not establish the IRS rule for this package. |
| Use an IRS business e-file signature authorization process | Eligible corporate e-file return | A separate process; do not apply it automatically to a foreign-owned DE's pro forma package. |

Ordinary corporate e-file has specific signature procedures, including the practitioner PIN method described in [IRM 3.42.4.11.2.3](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part3/irm_03-042-004r). A corporation filing a full income tax return is not the same filing situation as an LLC using the special DE route.

## A practical preparation-and-signing checklist

Use this conservative workflow before sending a new package:

1. **Confirm classification.** Check that the filing is actually for a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, rather than an LLC taxed as a corporation.
2. **Finish the package first.** Reconcile identifiers, tax year, related parties and attachments. The [pro forma 1120 guide](/blog/pro-forma-form-1120-foreign-owned-llc) explains the cover's limited role.
3. **Resolve signing authority.** Identify the authorized person and the capacity in which they will sign. Ask the preparer to resolve uncertainty before submission.
4. **Sign the final cover in ink.** Enter the actual signing date and title. Do not backdate or insert somebody else's signature.
5. **Scan and inspect.** For fax, replace the unsigned cover with the signed scan, preserve page order, and check that the signature, EIN and small text remain legible. Do not change the return after signing without having the signer review the changes.
6. **Send by the appropriate route.** The IRS specifies fax at 300 DPI or higher to 855-887-7737, or mail to its dedicated Ogden PIN Unit address. Recheck the [current filing instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) immediately before sending.
7. **Keep one filing record.** Save the ink original, final transmitted file, destination, timestamp, page count and delivery evidence together. Our [fax guide](/blog/how-to-fax-form-5472-irs) covers transmission details.

## What if you already filed with an electronic signature?

Do not conclude from a forum post that a penalty is inevitable—or send another package reflexively. First obtain the exact file submitted. An on-screen acknowledgment in a preparer's portal may not be the signature ultimately placed on the IRS form.

Review who signed, where the signature appeared, how it was created, when the package was sent and by which route. Ask the preparer for the authority used and preserve any signature audit trail. If the concern remains unresolved, have a qualified adviser assess the actual filing and any appropriate correction. Follow any IRS notice's instructions and response deadline.

This article cannot determine whether the IRS accepted an individual filing. It also does not establish an automatic signature-related penalty or a universal requirement to refile.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use DocuSign or Adobe Acrobat to sign the pro forma 1120?

Do not assume the platform's availability answers the tax question. Ask for authority covering this document and submission route. For a new filing, signing the completed cover in ink is the conservative approach described here.

### Can I fax a scanned ink signature with Form 5472?

That is the conservative workflow recommended here: sign the completed pro forma Form 1120 on paper, scan that signed page and include it in the fax package. Keep the original. This differs from pasting a saved signature image into an unsigned form; the guidance reviewed does not separately guarantee acceptance of a particular pro forma signature method.

### My accountant says they can e-file it. What should I ask?

Ask whether they mean an online intake, an electronic signature, an online fax, or an actual IRS e-file transmission. Then confirm the entity's tax classification. Those terms are not interchangeable, and the special foreign-owned DE filing restriction remains relevant.
