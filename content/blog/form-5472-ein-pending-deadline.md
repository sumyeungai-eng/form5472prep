**A pending EIN does not, by itself, postpone a foreign-owned LLC's Form 5472 deadline. The IRS gives general return-filing guidance to enter “Applied For” with the application date when an EIN has not arrived. However, its special foreign-owned disregarded-entity instructions do not explain every pending-EIN detail for Form 5472 or Form 7004. Separate what the IRS actually says from a promise that any placeholder will secure a valid extension.**

This guide covers a U.S.-formed single-member LLC wholly owned by a foreign person and treated as a disregarded entity. An LLC taxed as a corporation, a partnership, or a foreign corporation needs a different filing analysis. If the deadline is close and the EIN is unresolved, [contact us about preparation scope](/contact) and obtain case-specific filing guidance from the IRS or a qualified tax adviser.

## Which EIN situation are you actually in?

“I don't have my EIN” can describe four different problems. Use this triage table before submitting another application or waiting for a letter.

| Situation | Next check |
|---|---|
| EIN assigned and number known, but the letter is missing | Verify the entity's name and number using existing records; investigate a replacement confirmation rather than applying again. |
| EIN may have been assigned, but number is unknown | Search formation-provider records, prior returns and banking documents; an authorized person can ask the IRS to locate the number. |
| SS-4 submitted, no assignment confirmed | Preserve the application and submission date; investigate the pending application and the filing deadline separately. |
| No EIN application submitted | Apply through an eligible route promptly. Do not describe an unsubmitted application as pending. |

The IRS explains how to locate an existing number on its [EIN page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/employer-identification-number). Its [SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4) tell applicants to use one application method per entity to avoid duplicate EINs. A missing confirmation letter is not, by itself, a reason to request a second number. See the [lost EIN and 147C letter guide](/blog/lost-ein-147c-letter-replacement) for that separate problem.

If the IRS returned the SS-4 or requested a correction, investigate that request instead of assuming the application is simply waiting in a queue. Follow the applicable response instructions and retain the correspondence.

## Does the filing obligation start when the EIN arrives?

No. The Form 5472 analysis starts with the entity's classification, ownership, tax year and reportable transactions—not the date an IRS letter reaches the owner. For a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity, reportable transactions can include formation-related payments, contributions and distributions. Having no sales does not settle the question. The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) describe these rules under Who Must File, Exceptions From Filing and Part V.

For example, an owner might fund an LLC in December and apply for its EIN in the following spring. The later application does not move the December contribution into the next tax year. Reconstruct the first year's activity before assuming there is nothing to file. Our [first-year filing guide](/blog/first-year-form-5472-new-llc) explains why formation and owner-paid costs deserve attention.

## What does the IRS say about “Applied For”?

Under “EIN applied for, but not received,” the [SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4) direct a filer whose return is due before the EIN arrives to put **“Applied For” and the application date** in the number space. They also say not to use an SSN as an EIN. This is real, general IRS guidance; a blanket statement that no return can ever be filed before assignment overlooks it.

The [2025 Form 1120 instructions, item B](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120), give the same pending-EIN direction for a corporation's return and separately require an EIN for ordinary electronic filing. That supports the general paper-return guidance; it is not a special ruling about every Form 5472 attachment field or Form 7004 extension.

There is an important limit. The [special Form 5472 instructions for foreign-owned U.S. DEs](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) require a pro forma Form 1120 with the entity's name, address and items B and E. Item B is the EIN field. Those instructions do not provide a separate, step-by-step pending-EIN procedure covering every identifier field in the attached package.

Consequently, this guide does **not** promise that writing those words anywhere in the package guarantees processing or a valid extension. Bring the general guidance, your actual SS-4 submission date and your specific forms to an authorized IRS contact or tax adviser. Do not invent a nine-digit number, use another entity's EIN, or substitute the owner's ITIN for the LLC's EIN.

## Can Form 7004 solve the timing problem?

A timely, properly completed Form 7004 can extend the filing deadline. It is not an automatic consequence of applying for an EIN. The [Form 7004 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7004) require the applicable identifying number and warn that a name or number mismatch with IRS records can invalidate the extension. They do not supply an explicit foreign-owned-DE pending-EIN workaround.

Where an EIN has already been assigned, the special DE extension route is clear in the [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472):

1. Prepare Form 7004 using the Form 1120 return code on Part I, line 1.
2. Mark the top “Foreign-owned U.S. DE.”
3. Send it by the regular return due date using the dedicated Form 5472 fax number or mailing address, not the ordinary Form 7004 mailing address.
4. Retain the submitted extension and transmission or mailing evidence.

That route does not establish that a numberless extension will be valid. If assignment remains unresolved, seek help before the deadline rather than relying on an online field accepting text. Software validation, successful fax transmission and IRS processing are different events.

For ordinary extension preparation, see [Form 5472 extensions using Form 7004](/blog/form-5472-extension). An extension of filing time does not extend payment time for any separate tax liability.

## How does the 2025 tax-year timeline work?

The following is a hypothetical calendar-year example, not an IRS determination about a particular company. Assume a foreign-owned disregarded LLC had a reportable contribution during 2025 and no special deadline relief applies.

| Date or event | Filing consequence |
|---|---|
| December 2025: LLC formed and owner contributes funds | Review that year's reportable activity; later EIN assignment does not move the contribution to another year. |
| March 2026: SS-4 submitted | Keep the signed application and submission evidence. The return deadline remains separate. |
| April 15, 2026: regular filing deadline | The return or a valid extension is due; an unresolved application alone does not create an extension. |
| September 11, 2026: records reviewed | Check whether a valid, timely extension actually exists. Do not assume one from the pending application. |
| October 15, 2026 | Extended filing deadline only if a valid, timely six-month extension applies. |

The dates follow the [Form 1120 due-date rule](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120) and the DE attachment rule. Other tax years, weekends, legal holidays or applicable relief can change the date. If April 15 passed without a valid extension, a new Form 7004 does not retroactively extend that deadline. Ask about [late or previously unfiled Form 5472 returns](/blog/form-5472-filed-late-never-filed); do not assume penalty relief is automatic.

## What should you prepare while the EIN is unresolved?

Build one evidence folder containing the formation record, signed SS-4, application method and date, submission evidence, IRS correspondence, tax-year dates, owner details and related-party transaction totals. Add any existing Form 7004 and its delivery records. This is a practical preparation checklist, not a list of attachments the IRS requires with every filing.

Preparing the transaction schedule now reduces work after the identifier issue is resolved. Keep IRS application timing separate from a preparer's turnaround estimate; [international EIN processing times](/blog/ein-processing-time-international-applicants) vary by route and workload. If no application exists, review our [EIN service](/ein). Once the identifier and filing scope are established, [start Form 5472 preparation](/start).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I write Applied For instead of an EIN?

The IRS SS-4 and Form 1120 item B instructions give that general return-filing direction, together with the application date. They do not spell out every foreign-owned-DE Form 5472 or Form 7004 scenario. Obtain case-specific guidance rather than treating it as a guarantee that an extension will be valid.

### Should I apply again if the EIN letter has not arrived?

Not simply because the letter is missing. First determine whether an EIN was already assigned. Search existing records and have an authorized person contact the IRS if necessary; duplicate applications can create avoidable confusion.

### Does an EIN application extend the Form 5472 deadline?

No. An EIN application is not Form 7004 and does not itself extend the filing deadline. Determine the actual due date and whether a valid, timely extension was filed.
