**Form 5472 reports transactions with related parties. Form 1120-F reports a foreign corporation's US income, deductions, and tax. When a foreign corporation owns a US single-member LLC, the LLC may file Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 while the foreign parent separately files Form 1120-F if its US activities require it.**

The word “1120” causes most of the confusion. A pro forma Form 1120 is only the cover for the LLC's Form 5472 package. Form 1120-F is a substantive income-tax return of a foreign corporation.

If the task is the LLC's pro forma package, [start the reviewed Form 5472 filing](/start?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=internal&utm_campaign=form-5472-vs-1120-f). If the foreign parent may have a US trade or business, it needs separate 1120-F advice.

## Which return belongs to which taxpayer?

| Filing | Taxpayer or reporting entity | Main purpose | Computes US income tax? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Form 5472 + pro forma Form 1120 | Foreign-owned domestic disregarded LLC | Disclose reportable transactions with the foreign owner and other related parties | No |
| Form 1120-F | Foreign corporation | Report US business activity, income, deductions, credits, and tax | Yes |
| Form 5472 attached to Form 1120-F | Foreign corporation engaged in a US trade or business, when the reporting rules and related transactions apply | Disclose the foreign corporation's related-party transactions | Not by itself |

The [IRS Form 1120-F filing-responsibilities page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/foreign-corporation-form-1120-f-filing-responsibilities) explains that a foreign corporation may need Form 1120-F when it is engaged in a US trade or business, has relevant US income, claims treaty protection, or takes another covered position. The [Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) identify both 25%-foreign-owned US corporations and foreign corporations engaged in a US trade or business as reporting corporations.

## Why does the US LLC use a pro forma Form 1120?

A foreign-owned domestic disregarded entity ordinarily has no separate federal income-tax return. For section 6038A reporting only, however, the entity is treated as a corporation and attaches Form 5472 to a limited pro forma Form 1120.

The IRS instructions say the pro forma requires the LLC's name and address and specified first-page items. It is not a full corporate return and should not be populated with a corporate profit-and-loss calculation merely to make it look complete. See the [pro forma Form 1120 guide](/blog/pro-forma-form-1120-foreign-owned-llc) for the limited-purpose package.

## When can the foreign parent also need Form 1120-F?

The foreign parent may need Form 1120-F if the disregarded LLC's activities are attributed to the parent and place the foreign corporation in a US trade or business, or if another 1120-F filing trigger applies.

The facts that matter include:

- employees or a fixed place of business in the United States;
- recurring US operations or a dependent agent;
- inventory and title-transfer facts;
- effectively connected income;
- US-source fixed, determinable, annual, or periodical income;
- a treaty-based return position; and
- a protective-return decision where the US trade-or-business status is uncertain.

The LLC's state of formation does not decide this federal income-tax result. A Delaware certificate and a US bank account are not substitutes for analyzing the business activity.

## A three-layer filing map

Use this map for a foreign corporation that wholly owns a US single-member LLC:

| Layer | Question | Possible filing |
|---|---|---|
| LLC information reporting | Did the domestic disregarded LLC transact with its foreign owner or another related party? | Form 5472 + pro forma Form 1120 |
| Foreign parent income tax | Did the parent conduct a US trade or business, earn ECI, or take a covered treaty position? | Form 1120-F, possibly Form 8833 |
| Foreign parent related-party reporting | Did the foreign corporation itself meet the Form 5472 reporting-corporation rules and transact with a related party? | Form 5472 attached to Form 1120-F when required |

More than one “yes” is possible. Do not collapse the layers into one filing simply because the LLC is disregarded for income-tax purposes.

## Example: foreign company owns an operating US LLC

Assume a UK company owns a US single-member LLC. The LLC receives $50,000 from the parent, pays a $12,000 management fee back to the parent, and operates through employees in the United States.

The LLC has related-party transactions to analyze for Form 5472. The employees can also create a US trade or business for the foreign parent, making Form 1120-F analysis necessary. The management fee raises additional transfer-pricing, sourcing, deduction, and possible withholding questions. The pro forma Form 1120 does not resolve any of those income-tax issues.

This is more complex than a nonresident individual running an online service business entirely from abroad. The [foreign corporate owner guide](/blog/form-5472-foreign-corporate-owner) lists the ownership and identifier records needed for the LLC package.

## What records keep the returns consistent?

Maintain one reconciliation that shows:

1. each legal entity and its EIN or foreign tax identifier;
2. the direction and purpose of every intercompany transfer;
3. service, loan, license, and cost-sharing agreements;
4. amounts in original currency and US dollars;
5. which entity recognized each item in its books; and
6. which filing reports the item and why.

One transfer may affect more than one return, but it should not be duplicated without a legal reason or omitted because another form mentions it.
