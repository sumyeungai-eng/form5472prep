## QUESTION A — Married Person's Allowance (MPA), IRO s.29

### A1. Can the husband claim MPA under salaries tax if the wife has non-salaries income (e.g. rental under Property Tax, sole-proprietorship profits under Profits Tax) but no salaries income?

**PLAIN ANSWER: YES — the statutory condition is narrowly "no *assessable income*" (i.e. no income chargeable to Salaries Tax), so the wife's Property Tax / Profits Tax income is irrelevant to the husband's MPA claim under s.29(1)(b)(i).**

Verbatim text, IRO Cap. 112, s.29 (Married person's allowance):

> **29. Married person's allowance\***
> (1) An allowance (married person's allowance) is to be granted to a person in any year of assessment if—
> (a) the person is, at any time during the year of assessment, married; and
> (b) either of the following conditions is met—
> (i) the person's spouse—
> (A) **did not have assessable income in the year of assessment**; and
> (B) has not made an election under section 41(1) for the year of assessment;
> (ii) the person and the spouse have made an election under section 10(2) for the year of assessment. (Replaced 32 of 2018 s. 6)
> (1A) A married person's allowance is to be granted to a person and the person's spouse in any year of assessment if—
> (a) their marriage subsists at any time during the year of assessment; and
> (b) they have jointly elected to be personally assessed under Part 7. (Added 32 of 2018 s. 6)
> (2) Where an election has been made under section 10(2) a married person's allowance shall be granted to the person chargeable to salaries tax under section 10(3).
> (3) A married person's allowance grantable under this section is—
> (a) an allowance of the prescribed amount; ...
> (4) Where husband and wife are living apart a married person's allowance shall only be granted where the spouse claiming the allowance is maintaining or supporting the other.

Source: https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s29?_lang=en (retrieved 2026-09-01)

**Analysis:** The disqualifying term is "assessable income" — a defined term used throughout Part 3 (Salaries Tax) of the Ordinance for income assessable *to Salaries Tax*, not "total income" (the broader term used in Part 7, Personal Assessment, covering Salaries/Property/Profits Tax combined — see s.42(1)). Section 29 uses "assessable income," not "total income," which is a deliberate and material distinction: the wife having Property Tax or Profits Tax income does **not** give her "assessable income" for s.29 purposes. This textual reading is confirmed by IRD's own plain-language FAQ:

> "If your spouse does not earn any income chargeable to Hong Kong Salaries Tax and has not elected for personal assessment separately in year of assessment 2025/26, you may claim Married Person's Allowance and need not elect joint assessment."

Source: https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/faq/all.htm (FAQ on Allowances, retrieved 2026-09-01)

So: **broader income (rental/business) is irrelevant — only "income chargeable to salaries tax" (assessable income) disqualifies**, matching the narrower reading in the question, not the broader "any chargeable income disqualifies" reading.

### A2. If the wife separately elects Personal Assessment (individually, under s.41(1)) while the husband does not, can the husband still claim MPA under salaries tax?

**PLAIN ANSWER: NO — s.29(1)(b)(i)(B) expressly disqualifies the husband's MPA claim once the spouse "has made an election under section 41(1)" (an individual PA election), unless the couple instead uses one of the joint routes (s.29(1)(b)(ii) via s.10(2) joint salaries assessment, or s.29(1A) via joint PA election) — which the question rules out by stipulating the husband does not elect.**

s.29(1)(b)(i) (quoted above) requires BOTH:
- (A) spouse did not have assessable income, AND
- (B) spouse "has not made an election under section 41(1) for the year of assessment."

Section 41(1) is the *individual* PA election provision:

> **41. Election for personal assessment\***
> (1) An individual—(a) who is of or above the age of 18 years or is under that age if both parents are dead; and (b) who is either ordinarily resident in Hong Kong or a temporary resident, may elect for personal assessment on the individual's total income in accordance with this Part. (Replaced 32 of 2018 s. 9)
> (1A) If—(a) an individual is married and not living apart from the individual's spouse; (b) both of them have income assessable under this Ordinance; and (c) either one or both of them are eligible to make an election under subsection (1), they may jointly make an election for personal assessment.

Source: https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s41?_lang=en (retrieved 2026-09-01)

Since the wife's separate election is necessarily made under s.41(1) (an individual election — s.41(1A) is the *joint* election, which by hypothesis did not happen here), condition s.29(1)(b)(i)(B) fails. Condition (ii) (s.10(2) joint salaries election) also fails since none was made. s.29(1A) (joint PA) also fails since the husband did not jointly elect. **Result: the husband loses MPA eligibility under s.29 entirely for that year** — he would instead get only the basic allowance (s.28), assuming he does not himself elect PA.

Corroborating plain-language IRD guidance (same FAQ as above): "...has not elected for personal assessment separately in year of assessment 2025/26, you may claim Married Person's Allowance" — the converse (has elected separately → may not claim MPA) is the direct implication, matching the statutory text.

### A3. If the husband elects Personal Assessment individually (s.41(1)), what allowance does his PA computation get — basic or married — and on what condition?

**PLAIN ANSWER: The individual election does not by itself change the allowance analysis — it still runs through s.29. If the wife has no assessable income and has not separately elected PA (s.29(1)(b)(i) satisfied), the husband's PA assessment is reduced by the MARRIED person's allowance; otherwise he gets only the BASIC allowance. This is set by s.42A(1)(a), which cross-refers to "such of the allowances prescribed in Part 5 [i.e., ss.28 basic / 29 married / etc.] as may be appropriate."**

Note: s.42B (the old "allowances under personal assessment" provision) is **repealed** (Repealed 43 of 1989 s. 14) — checked live at https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s42B?_lang=en, which returns only "42B. (Repealed 43 of 1989 s. 14)". The allowance mechanics for PA now live in s.42A:

> **42A. Assessment to tax**
> (1) In giving effect to an election under section 41 the assessor shall make a single assessment—
> (a) in the sum of the total income, as reduced under section 42(2) and (5), of the individual making the election; or
> (b) in the case of an election under section 41(1A), in the sum of the joint total income resulting from the aggregation of the total income of the one spouse, as so reduced, with that of the other, as also so reduced,
> **as reduced in each case by such of the allowances prescribed in Part 5 as may be appropriate.**

Source: https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s42A?_lang=en (retrieved 2026-09-01)

Part 5 of the Ordinance is where both s.28 (basic allowance) and s.29 (married person's allowance) sit — "as may be appropriate" means: apply whichever allowance the taxpayer's facts satisfy under those sections, exactly as if computing salaries tax. Mutual exclusivity is confirmed by s.28(2):

> **28. Basic allowance**
> (2) No person may be granted— (a) both a basic allowance and a married person's allowance; or (b) a basic allowance where his or her spouse has claimed a married person's allowance.

Source: https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s28?_lang=en (retrieved 2026-09-01)

So an individual PA election by the husband (s.41(1), not s.41(1A) joint) does **not automatically confer MPA**. It confers MPA only if the underlying s.29(1)(b) condition is independently satisfied on the facts (typically: wife has no assessable income and has not herself separately elected PA under s.41(1)). If instead the wife has assessable income of her own and no joint route was used, the husband's individual PA assessment gets only the basic allowance under s.28.

IRD's own worked example (Example 4, "Joint Assessment & Personal Assessment for Married Couples") shows the MPA of $264,000 being deducted only in the JOINT PA scenario (Mr and Mrs Cheung electing jointly under s.41(1A)/s.29(1A)) — consistent with married allowance flowing from satisfying s.29, not merely from the act of electing PA.
Source: https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/pdf/ind_jae.pdf, p.7 (retrieved 2026-09-01, IRD PDF "Joint Assessment & Personal Assessment for Married Couples")

## QUESTION B — Cross-spouse set-off under a JOINT election

**PLAIN ANSWER: YES in both regimes — one spouse's loss/deduction excess IS set off against the other spouse's income in the same year under a joint election (salaries tax s.10(2)/(3), and joint PA s.41(1A)/s.42). Each spouse is NOT independently floored at zero first in isolation. However, the two regimes reach this result via different statutory mechanics — see note at the end.**

### B(i) — Joint salaries assessment, s.10(2)/(3) with s.12B(2)

> **10. Salaries tax on spouses to be paid separately unless they elect to be jointly assessed**
> (1) In the case of a husband and wife, unless an election is made under subsection (2), salaries tax shall be payable on the net chargeable income of each spouse ... by the spouse to whom the income has accrued.
> (2) Where in any year of assessment a husband and wife ... both have assessable income and—
> (a) **either the husband or wife is entitled to deductions under Part 4A and allowances under Part 5 which, in aggregate, are in excess of his or her net assessable income**; or
> (b) both also have a net chargeable income and the aggregate of the salaries tax which would be payable by them if subsection (1) applies exceeds the salaries tax which would be payable if an election is made under this subsection,
> an election may be made by them ... to be assessed to salaries tax in the manner specified in subsection (3).
> (3) Where an election is made ... salaries tax shall be payable on their **aggregated net chargeable income as ascertained under section 12B(2)** ...

Source: https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s10?_lang=en (retrieved 2026-09-01)

> **12B. Ascertainment of net chargeable income**
> (2) In the case of a person chargeable to salaries tax under section 10(3), that person and his or her spouse **shall have but one net chargeable income**, and it shall be the amount arrived at after deducting from **the aggregate of their net assessable incomes**—(a) such deductions as are under Part 4A allowable to them; and (b) such allowances as are under Part 5 permitted in their case.

Source: https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s12B?_lang=en (retrieved 2026-09-01)

**Mechanism:** salaries tax joint assessment does not compute two separate net chargeable incomes and then floor the negative one at zero — it pools BOTH spouses' net assessable incomes into a single aggregate figure FIRST, then deducts the couple's combined Part 4A deductions and Part 5 allowances from that single pooled total. A spouse whose own deductions/allowances exceed their own net assessable income (the very trigger condition in s.10(2)(a) for being allowed to elect) has that excess automatically absorbed against the other spouse's income as part of the single pooled computation — there is no separate "floor at zero, then combine" step.

### B(ii) — Joint Personal Assessment, s.41(1A) with s.42(5)/(6)

> **42. Calculation of total income**
> (5)(a) Where in any year of assessment the aggregate amount of the deductions under subsection (2)(a) and the loss under subsection (2)(b) for an individual exceeds the total income of the individual—
> (i) ... that part of the excess ... shall be carried forward ...
> (ii) **where the total incomes of the individual and his or her spouse are required to be aggregated under section 42A(1), the amount of such excess shall be reduced as far as can be done by being set off against the total income of his or her spouse for that year of assessment as reduced under subsection (2)**, and that part ... not so reduced shall be carried forward ...
> (b) Where paragraph (a) does not apply and ... the amount of the loss under subsection (2)(b) for an individual exceeds the total income of the individual—
> (i) ... carried forward ...
> (ii) **where the total incomes ... are required to be aggregated under section 42A(1), the amount of such excess shall be reduced as far as can be done by being set off against the total income of his or her spouse** ...
> (7) The amount of any excess set off under subsection (5) against an individual's total income or that of the individual's spouse for any year of assessment shall not be set off for any other year of assessment.
> (10) Where an election is made by a husband and wife under section 41(1A) **the total income (as reduced under subsections (2) and (5)) of each of them shall be separately calculated under this section before both incomes are aggregated under section 42A**.

Source: https://www.elegislation.gov.hk/hk/cap112!en/s42?_lang=en (retrieved 2026-09-01)

**Mechanism (contrast with salaries tax):** under joint PA, each spouse's own total income is calculated SEPARATELY first (s.42(10)) — income minus own Part 4A deductions minus own Part 4 loss. Only the residual EXCESS (i.e., what could not be absorbed by that spouse's own income) is then explicitly set off "against the total income of his or her spouse... as far as can be done" (s.42(5)(a)(ii)/(b)(ii)), before the two (now-adjusted) total incomes are aggregated under s.42A. So this is a genuine cross-spouse set-off of the excess, not a "floor each at zero independently and never let them interact" approach — but the calculation order differs from salaries tax: PA computes per-spouse first, then explicitly cross-sets-off the excess; salaries tax pools everything into one number from the start.

There is also a special extension, s.42(6): if one spouse has NO assessable income at all in the year but has a loss brought forward from a prior PA year, and the couple *would have been* eligible to elect jointly had that spouse had assessable income, and the other spouse has elected PA — the same cross-spouse set-off machinery in s.42(5)(a)(ii)/(b)(ii) applies as if they had jointly elected. (Source: same s.42 page above.)

### Does the answer differ between joint salaries assessment and joint PA?

**Not in substance (both permit cross-spouse set-off of an excess in the same year), but yes in mechanics:**
- Salaries tax joint assessment (s.10(3)/s.12B(2)): single pooled computation from the start — aggregate net assessable incomes, then deduct combined allowances/deductions once. No independent per-spouse floor-at-zero step ever occurs.
- Joint PA (s.41(1A)/s.42(5)/s.42(10)): each spouse's total income (net of their own deductions and losses) is computed separately first; only the excess that a spouse's own income cannot absorb is then explicitly set off against the other spouse's total income "as far as can be done," with any unabsorbed remainder carried forward (not lost) rather than simply floored and discarded.

IRD's own worked example for joint PA (Example 4 in the "Joint Assessment & Personal Assessment for Married Couples" leaflet) shows a positive-income scenario (no loss), so it does not itself illustrate the loss set-off mechanic numerically; the loss set-off rule above is drawn directly from the statutory text of s.42(5)/(6)/(10), which is unambiguous. IRD's "Brief Guide to Personal Assessment" (PAM 37(e)) does show a single-taxpayer loss set-off example (Mr. Lee's business loss vs. his own salaries income, Example 2) illustrating the same-person mechanism that s.42(5)(a)(ii) extends across spouses, but does not include a cross-spouse worked numeric example.
Source: https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/pdf/pam37e.pdf, p.3 (retrieved 2026-09-01)

## Confidence & gaps

- **A1, A2**: High confidence — directly from verbatim s.29 and s.41 text (elegislation.gov.hk), corroborated by IRD's own FAQ plain-language wording. No ambiguity found.
- **A3**: High confidence on the statutory mechanism (s.42A(1)(a) cross-referring to Part 5 "as appropriate", s.28(2) mutual exclusivity). **Not independently confirmed by an IRD worked example of an *individually*-electing married person who fails the s.29 condition and is limited to basic allowance under PA** — this scenario is a straightforward statutory deduction from s.29 + s.42A + s.28 but I could not find an IRD leaflet/FAQ example illustrating it numerically. Treat the mechanism as textually certain, the "no IRD worked example of this specific edge case" as the one gap.
- **B**: High confidence on both salaries-tax joint assessment (s.10/s.12B(2)) and joint PA (s.42(5)/(6)/(10)) — verbatim statutory text obtained directly from elegislation.gov.hk for both. **Gap:** could not locate an official IRD worked numeric example of the cross-spouse loss set-off under joint PA specifically (one spouse profit, other spouse loss, joint PA elected) — the IRD leaflets found show same-person loss set-off (Mr. Lee, PAM 37(e) Example 2) and a joint-PA positive-income example (Mr & Mrs Cheung) but not the two combined. The statutory text of s.42(5)(a)(ii)/(b)(ii) is unambiguous on its own, so this is a corroboration gap, not a substantive doubt.
